This case was a custody fight, not a land dispute. A non-Indian couple in South Carolina sought to adopt Baby Girl, whose birth mother had chosen them. Her biological father, a member of the Cherokee Nation, had agreed to give up his rights but later contested the adoption. He relied on the Indian Child Welfare Act of 1978 (ICWA), the federal law on custody proceedings for Indian children. No treaty was at issue.

The Supreme Court ruled for the adoptive couple. Assuming the father was a "parent" under ICWA, it held that neither the "continued custody" standard in section 1912(f) nor the "breakup of the Indian family" standard in section 1912(d) protected a father who never had custody. It also held that ICWA's adoption-placement preferences did not apply when no other eligible party had sought to adopt.

The decision narrowed how ICWA's protections operate for non-custodial parents. It is a tribal-sovereignty and family-law case rather than a land-rights case, and the record in our data lists no land effect. The Court's reading of ICWA's text was revisited in Haaland v. Brackeen (2023), where the Court rejected constitutional challenges to the Act.
