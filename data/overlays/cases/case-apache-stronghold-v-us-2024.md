Congress's Land Transfer Act directed the federal government to transfer Oak Flat, federally owned land in the Tonto National Forest, to Resolution Copper, which would mine a large copper deposit beneath it. Apache Stronghold, an organization representing certain members of the San Carlos Apache Tribe, sued to stop the transfer under the Free Exercise Clause, the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA), and an 1852 Treaty of Santa Fe it said created an enforceable trust obligation.

The en banc Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of a preliminary injunction, with Judge Collins writing for six judges. Under Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association, a disposition of government real property that does not coerce, discriminate against, or penalize believers does not substantially burden religious exercise. RFRA "subsumed" Lyng's limits. The treaty claim failed because the government's statutory obligation to transfer the land abrogated any contrary treaty obligation. Chief Judge Murguia, joined by four judges, dissented.

The decision makes Lyng's reasoning controlling for RFRA claims over federal land use, while overruling the narrow definition of "substantial burden" in Navajo Nation (2008). Our data says the Supreme Court later denied certiorari.
