The Indian Reorganization Act of 1934 lets the Secretary of the Interior hold land in trust for "Indians," defined as members of a "recognized Indian tribe now under Federal jurisdiction." The Narragansett Tribe had been under Rhode Island's guardianship from 1709, and in 1880 agreed to sell nearly all its remaining reservation land. Federal recognition came in 1983. When the Secretary accepted a 31-acre parcel into trust, Rhode Island and the town of Charlestown objected.

The Supreme Court held that "now" in the statute means 1934, when Congress passed the Act, and that the Narragansett Tribe was not under federal jurisdiction then. The Secretary therefore lacked authority to take the parcel into trust. The Court found the text unambiguous, so it did not defer to the agency's reading.

Carcieri created a threshold question for many trust acquisitions: was the tribe under federal jurisdiction in 1934? It spawned later litigation, including the Mashpee Wampanoag trust-land cases in our dataset.
