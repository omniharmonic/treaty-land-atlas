The Chinook Indian Nation of the lower Columbia River region sought federal recognition through the courts. The Nation asked whether a federal court can recognize an Indian tribe, pointing to language in the Federally Recognized Indian Tribe List Act of 1994 that refers to recognition "by a decision of a United States court." Our data ties the Nation's claim to the unratified Treaty of Tansy Point of 1851; one news report on the case mentions only 1851 treaty negotiations, so that link is unconfirmed.

The Ninth Circuit, in June 2025, held that the List Act's findings do not give courts power to recognize tribes and affirmed dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, treating recognition as a political question reserved to Congress and the executive branch. On March 23, 2026, the Supreme Court denied review of that ruling without comment.

A denial of certiorari is not a ruling on the merits and sets no precedent. It leaves the Ninth Circuit decision in place. The Nation's chairman said the effort will continue through other channels, such as Congress or the Interior Department's administrative process.
