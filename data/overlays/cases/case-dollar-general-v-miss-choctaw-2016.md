A thirteen-year-old member of the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians was placed at a Dollar General store on the Choctaw reservation through the tribe's Youth Opportunity Program. The store sits on land the United States holds in trust for the Band and operates under a lease and business license from the tribe. The boy alleged that the store's manager sexually molested him there. He sued the store's operator, Dolgencorp, in tribal court, which refused to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction. Dolgencorp sued in federal court to stop the tribal case.

The Fifth Circuit held that Dolgencorp's consensual relationship with the boy gave the tribal court jurisdiction under Montana v. United States. The Supreme Court then divided evenly after Justice Scalia's death: "The judgment is affirmed by an equally divided Court." A 4-4 affirmance is not a precedent and sets no national rule.

The result left tribal court jurisdiction over non-member businesses operating on trust land under tribal lease untouched, at least in that circuit. Later decisions on non-member jurisdiction include Plains Commerce Bank (2008) and Cooley (2021).
