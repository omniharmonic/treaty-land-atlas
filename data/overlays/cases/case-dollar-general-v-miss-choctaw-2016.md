A thirteen-year-old member of the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians, called John Doe in the court papers, was placed at a Dollar General store on the Choctaw reservation through the tribe's Youth Opportunity Program, a job-training program. The store sits on land held by the United States in trust for the Band, and operates under a lease agreement with the tribe and a business license issued by the tribe.

Doe alleged that the store's manager sexually molested him while he worked there. In January 2005 he sued the store's operator, Dolgencorp, and the manager in tribal court. Dolgencorp moved to dismiss for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. The tribal court denied the motion, and Dolgencorp then sued in federal court to stop the tribal case.

The Fifth Circuit held that Dolgencorp's consensual relationship with Doe gave the tribal court jurisdiction under Montana v. United States (1981), which sets out when tribes may regulate non-members. The Supreme Court took the case. On June 23, 2016, its entire decision was one sentence: "The judgment is affirmed by an equally divided Court."

The Court gave no reasons and wrote no opinion, so the Fifth Circuit's ruling stood and the tribal court case could go forward.
