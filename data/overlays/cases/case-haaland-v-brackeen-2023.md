Individual non-Indian families who sought to adopt or foster Indian children, with the State of Texas, challenged the Indian Child Welfare Act of 1978 (ICWA). They argued that Congress lacked power to enact it, that its placement preferences violated equal protection, and that its requirements commandeered state officials in violation of the Tenth Amendment. The Fifth Circuit had upheld some provisions and struck others. No treaty was at issue.

In an opinion by Justice Barrett, joined by six other justices, the Court reversed in part. It held that ICWA is consistent with Congress's Article I authority: "Congress's power to legislate with respect to Indians is well established and broad, but it is not unbounded." ICWA is not displaced by state family-law authority where Congress validly legislates. The Court rejected the commandeering and nondelegation claims, and found the remaining challenges, including equal protection, were not properly raised by plaintiffs with standing. Justices Thomas and Alito dissented.

The decision preserved ICWA, leaving questions about Congress's Indian affairs power and the equal protection challenge unresolved. Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl (2013) had interpreted the statute narrowly.
