The Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma owns land in Oklahoma, with other land held in trust for it by the United States. Its former chairman signed a promissory note for $285,000 plus interest, in the Tribe's name, to buy stock from Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. The note said it was signed at Carnegie, Oklahoma, on trust land, though the company said it was signed in Oklahoma City. When the Tribe defaulted, the company sued in state court. The Oklahoma Court of Civil Appeals ruled the Tribe could be sued for off-reservation commercial conduct.

The Supreme Court reversed, 6-3. Justice Kennedy's opinion held that tribes enjoy sovereign immunity from suit on contracts, whether governmental or commercial, on or off a reservation. A tribe may be sued only if Congress has authorized suit or the tribe has waived immunity. The Court conceded that "The wisdom of perpetuating the doctrine may be doubted" but left any change to Congress.

Kiowa sets the baseline for tribal immunity in commercial contexts. Later decisions, including Michigan v. Bay Mills (2014), reaffirmed it while noting Congress's power to limit it.
