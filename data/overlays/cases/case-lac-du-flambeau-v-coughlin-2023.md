This case is about sovereign immunity and bankruptcy, not land. The Lac du Flambeau Band of Lake Superior Chippewa Indians is a federally recognized tribe that wholly owns several business entities. In 2019, one of them, Lendgreen, let Brian Coughlin borrow $1,100 in a high-interest, short-term payday loan. Before he repaid it, Coughlin filed for Chapter 13 bankruptcy, which triggers an automatic stay against further collection efforts by his creditors.

Lendgreen allegedly kept trying to collect. Coughlin asked the Bankruptcy Court to enforce the stay and award damages against Lendgreen, its parent corporations and the Band. The Bankruptcy Court dismissed the suit on tribal sovereign immunity grounds. The First Circuit reversed, concluding that the Code "unequivocally strips tribes of their immunity."

The Supreme Court affirmed in an opinion by Justice Jackson. It held that the Bankruptcy Code expressly abrogates the sovereign immunity of "governmental unit[s]" for specified purposes, and that the Code unambiguously does so for all governments, including federally recognized Indian tribes. The Code's definition of a governmental unit ends with the catchall "other foreign or domestic government." The Court said Congress must make its intent to abrogate "unmistakably clear," and found it did.

Justice Thomas concurred in the judgment, and Justice Gorsuch dissented.
