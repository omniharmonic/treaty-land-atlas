This case is about immunity and bankruptcy, not land. The Lac du Flambeau Band of Lake Superior Chippewa Indians, a federally recognized tribe, owns Lendgreen, a payday-lending business. It made a loan to Brian Coughlin, who then filed for Chapter 13 bankruptcy, which triggers an automatic stay against collection. Lendgreen allegedly kept trying to collect. Coughlin asked the Bankruptcy Court to enforce the stay and award damages. The Court dismissed on sovereign immunity grounds; the First Circuit reversed.

The Supreme Court affirmed, 8-1, in an opinion by Justice Jackson. It held that "The Bankruptcy Code unambiguously abrogates the sovereign immunity of all governments, including federally recognized Indian tribes." The Code's definition of "governmental unit" is comprehensive and includes "other foreign or domestic government." Congress need not use particular words, but its intent must be unmistakably clear. Justice Gorsuch dissented and Justice Thomas concurred in the judgment.

The decision shows that Congress may abrogate tribal immunity through general statutes if the text is clear, and that tribes' off-reservation commercial activity can be reached by bankruptcy law.
