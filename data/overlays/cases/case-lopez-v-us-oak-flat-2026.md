Oak Flat, federally owned land in Arizona's Tonto National Forest, was designated for transfer to Resolution Copper by an act of Congress, over objections from the San Carlos Apache Tribe and Apache Stronghold. An earlier challenge, Apache Stronghold v. United States, ended when the Supreme Court denied certiorari in May 2025, with Justices Gorsuch and Thomas dissenting. A later round was brought by individual Apache women under the name Lopez.

A news report says three Apache women filed an emergency request with the Supreme Court on March 16, 2026, to halt the land transfer. They argued that destroying the site "imposes the most severe burden imaginable" by preventing Apache worship there. The same report says the title transfer was completed on March 13, 2026, after an appeals court refused to block the project, and that the court acknowledged harm to Apache religious practice but saw no viable challenge to Congress's decision.

Our data lists the application, No. 25A1008, as denied on March 19, 2026, but I could not confirm that disposition in a source I opened. The case is part of the line of Lyng and Apache Stronghold decisions about religious claims against federal land use.
