The Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe obtained federal acknowledgement from the Bureau of Indian Affairs in 2007. It then applied to have the Interior Department take land in Mashpee and Taunton, Massachusetts into trust for use as a reservation. The Secretary approved, relying on a second definition of "Indian" in section 479 of the Indian Reorganization Act. Residents of Taunton sued under the Administrative Procedure Act, saying the Secretary lacked authority because the Tribe was not "under federal jurisdiction" in 1934, as Carcieri v. Salazar (2009) requires.

Judge Young of the District of Massachusetts, in July 2016, agreed with the residents. He ruled that the second definition "unambiguously incorporates the entire antecedent phrase," so no deference was due the Secretary, and that the Secretary "lacked the authority to acquire land in trust for the Mashpees, as they were not then under Federal jurisdiction." The matter was remanded to the Secretary.

Our data indicates later proceedings in the District of Columbia and First Circuit, and a certiorari denial in April 2024, but I could not verify those steps. The ruling shows how Carcieri has reshaped trust-land decisions for tribes recognized after 1934.
