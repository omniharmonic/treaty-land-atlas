This is a money-claims case, not a land case. Under the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act, the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin contracted with the Indian Health Service to run a program the government would otherwise have run. The Act promises reimbursement of reasonable contract support costs. Other tribes had won suits over unpaid costs, and the Menominee Tribe then presented its own claims. The agency denied some of them as filed outside the Contract Disputes Act's six-year limit.

The Tribe argued the deadline should be tolled for the nearly two years a class action by other tribes was pending. The Supreme Court held that equitable tolling did not apply. It said a litigant needs both diligence and "some extraordinary circumstance" that prevented timely filing, and treated these as separate elements rather than factors to be weighed together. The Court of Appeals had found no extraordinary circumstance.

The ruling shows the limits of equitable relief for tribes contracting under the self-determination law. It does not change land rights or treaty rights.
