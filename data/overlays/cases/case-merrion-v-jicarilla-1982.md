The Jicarilla Apache Tribe adopted a severance tax on oil and gas produced on its reservation, under a constitution and an ordinance the Secretary of the Interior approved. Merrion and Amoco, which held long-term, Secretary-approved leases to extract oil and gas, sued to stop the tax. A federal trial court said only state and local authorities could tax such production. The Tenth Circuit reversed.

The Supreme Court, 6-3, held for the Tribe. "The power to tax is an essential attribute of Indian sovereignty because it is a necessary instrument of self-government and territorial management." That power does not rest only on the tribe's power to exclude outsiders, but on its general authority to control economic activity within its jurisdiction and to pay for government services. The Court also found no Commerce Clause violation.

Merrion affirmed that tribes can raise revenue from non-members doing business on tribal land, though later cases such as Montana v. United States and Atkinson Trading (2001) narrowed tribal power over non-members on non-Indian land.
