The Mille Lacs Band of Ojibwe sued Mille Lacs County officials over law enforcement on lands within the Band's 1855 reservation. The 1855 Treaty with the Chippewa had ceded land and set apart nine reservations, including lands for the Mille Lacs Band. The Band argued that the reservation was never disestablished, so its police had law-enforcement authority there. A federal district court agreed, in orders of March 2022 and January 2023, and the County defendants appealed.

After Minnesota amended its law in 2023 to give the Band unqualified law enforcement authority over all persons within the 1855 boundaries, the Band argued the appeals were moot. The Eighth Circuit agreed, holding the appeals moot and directing the district court to vacate its orders. Judge Loken's opinion said the reservation question had been "hotly contested but never resolved in the Band's favor for approximately 150 years" and said ambiguities abound, so it should be decided in a live controversy. Judge Erickson concurred only in the mootness holding, and noted the disestablishment questions remain open.

The decision leaves the reservation's status unresolved after earlier cases, including Minnesota v. Mille Lacs (1999) on treaty rights.
