Three appeals, Nos. 25-5185, 25-5189 and 25-5197, challenged the land exchange that would give Oak Flat and a large copper deposit in the Tonto National Forest to Resolution Copper. The plaintiffs were conservation groups, the San Carlos Apache Tribe, and a separate set of Apache plaintiffs.

A three-judge panel of Judges Rawlinson, Milan Smith and Bress affirmed the denial of a preliminary injunction on March 13, 2026, and amended its opinion on April 8. Its summary says the Apache plaintiffs' religious-liberty claim "is foreclosed by precedent," meaning the en banc ruling in Apache Stronghold v. United States (2024). Judge Rawlinson dissented in part, only on the Forest Service appraisal.

On September 29, 2026 the court denied petitions for panel rehearing and rehearing en banc. Judges M. Smith and Bress voted to deny. Judge Rawlinson voted to grant rehearing en banc. The order says the matter "failed to receive a majority of the votes of the non-recused active judges."

Chief Judge Murguia dissented, writing that the court had a chance "to fix our tragic error." Judge Graber, joined by nine other judges, wrote separately that Apache Stronghold was wrong. Judge Bumatay, joined by four judges, also dissented from the denial. Murguia wrote that unless the Supreme Court steps in, a mining project will obliterate Oak Flat.
