The Seminole Nation sued in the Court of Claims over treaty money. Treaties of 1856 and 1866 obliged the United States to pay sums for specified purposes, for schools, a trust fund, and agency buildings. The Seminoles said the government had paid funds to the tribal council and treasurer when it knew the council was corrupt, and had wrongly diverted other money.

The Supreme Court rejected several claims. Some had been released by the 1866 Treaty, some payments satisfied the treaty because schools received the benefit, and the agency-buildings obligation was not breached. But it remanded the claim over 1870 to 1874 payments for findings on whether the council was "corrupt, venal, and false to its trust," whether officials knew, and whether the Nation received any benefit. The government, the Court said, "has charged itself with moral obligations of the highest responsibility and trust" and "should therefore be judged by the most exacting fiduciary standards."

The case is the main source of the federal trust responsibility standard, later cited in cases such as Cobell and United States v. Navajo Nation (2003), which limited it to duties found in statutes and regulations.
