The Indian Gaming Regulatory Act requires states to negotiate in good faith with tribes toward gaming compacts, and lets a tribe sue a state in federal court to compel that duty. The Seminole Tribe of Florida sued Florida under that provision. Florida and its Governor moved to dismiss on state sovereign immunity grounds. The Eleventh Circuit agreed that the Indian Commerce Clause did not give Congress power to abrogate state immunity, and that Ex parte Young did not allow suing the Governor.

The Supreme Court affirmed, 5-4, in an opinion by Chief Justice Rehnquist. It held that Congress clearly intended to abrogate state immunity in the Act, but the Eleventh Amendment "prevents Congress from authorizing suits by Indian tribes against States to enforce legislation enacted pursuant to the Indian Commerce Clause." The Court overruled Union Gas, which had allowed abrogation under the Interstate Commerce Clause.

The decision weakened the Act's enforcement of the duty to negotiate, shifting later disputes to the Interior Department's procedures. It is a leading Eleventh Amendment case beyond Indian law.
