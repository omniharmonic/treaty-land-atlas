The Seneca Nation sought an injunction to stop the Army Corps of Engineers from building the Allegheny Reservoir Project, which would flood a substantial amount of Seneca land in New York. The district court dismissed the complaint. The Nation relied on rights it acquired in the Treaty of Canandaigua of 1794.

The D.C. Circuit, in a per curiam, noted it was undisputed that the flooding would infringe those treaty rights and that Congress can authorize a taking by eminent domain despite a treaty. The only question was whether Congress had shown its intention to do so in a sufficiently clear and specific way. The court found appropriations acts of 1941 and 1957, the hearings and debates, showed Congress knew the Seneca lands would be flooded and that the Nation would not relinquish its rights. One senator said the Government "has the legal right to do what is proposed," but spoke of "moral responsibility." The court concluded: "We think Congress has authorized the taking of these lands." Affirmed.

The Kinzua dam was completed and flooded part of the Allegheny Reservation. The case shows courts accepting congressional abrogation of treaty land protections when intent is shown.
