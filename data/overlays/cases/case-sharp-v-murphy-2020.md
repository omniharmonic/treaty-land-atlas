Sharp v. Murphy was the companion to McGirt v. Oklahoma. Tommy Sharp, Oklahoma's interim warden, was the petitioner, and Patrick Dwayne Murphy was the respondent. The Court was reviewing a judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit.

Both cases turned on whether land the United States promised the Creek Nation in the 19th century remains a reservation for purposes of federal criminal law. As McGirt explains, Oklahoma state courts had rejected any suggestion that the lands remain a reservation, while the Tenth Circuit, in Murphy v. Royal, reached the opposite conclusion.

On July 9, 2020, the Court issued a per curiam order, an opinion issued in the Court's name rather than by one justice. It reads in full: "The judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit is affirmed for the reasons stated in McGirt v. Oklahoma." Justice Gorsuch took no part in the case, and Justices Thomas and Alito dissented.

McGirt, decided the same day, held that for purposes of the Major Crimes Act, land reserved for the Creek Nation since the 19th century remains "Indian country." Justice Gorsuch wrote for a five-justice majority: "Because Congress has not said otherwise, we hold the government to its word."
