Patrick Murphy had been convicted of murder in Oklahoma state court and sentenced to death. The Tenth Circuit held he should have been tried in federal court because the crime occurred in the Creek Reservation, which Congress never disestablished. Oklahoma's warden, Tommy Sharp, took the case to the Supreme Court. It was decided on the same day as McGirt v. Oklahoma.

In a per curiam, the Court wrote only: "The judgment of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit is affirmed for the reasons stated in McGirt v. Oklahoma." Justice Gorsuch took no part in the case. Justices Thomas and Alito dissented.

The order means McGirt's holding, that the Creek Reservation remains Indian country, controlled a murder case, too. The effect on later cases was narrowed in Oklahoma v. Castro-Huerta (2022), which allowed states to prosecute non-Indians for crimes against Indians in Indian country.
