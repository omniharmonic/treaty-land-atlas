Two vehicles collided on a 6.59-mile stretch of North Dakota state highway that runs through the Fort Berthold Reservation. The highway is open to the public and maintained by the State under a federal right-of-way over land held in trust for the Three Affiliated Tribes. Neither driver was a tribal member, though one driver, Fredericks, was the widow of a tribal member with adult children who were members. The other driver worked for A-1 Contractors, a non-Indian company with a subcontract to the Tribes' corporation. Fredericks sued in Tribal Court, which kept jurisdiction.

The Supreme Court, in an opinion by Justice Ginsburg, unanimously held that the tribal court lacked jurisdiction. When an accident happens on a public highway maintained by the State under a federal right-of-way, a civil suit against nonmembers falls under state or federal governance, "absent a statute or treaty authorizing the tribe to govern" it. The Court applied Montana's general rule to this right-of-way and reserved the question for tribal roads.

Strate also held that a tribe's adjudicative jurisdiction over nonmembers is no broader than its regulatory jurisdiction, a rule applied in Hicks (2001) and Plains Commerce Bank (2008).
