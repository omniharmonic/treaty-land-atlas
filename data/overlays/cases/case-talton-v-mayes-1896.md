Talton, a Cherokee, was convicted of murdering another Cherokee in a Cherokee Nation court in Indian Territory and sentenced to hang. He sought habeas corpus from the national jail at Tahlequah, arguing that the grand jury that indicted him was improperly constituted and that this violated his rights under the United States Constitution.

The Supreme Court rejected his challenge. It held that murder by one Cherokee of another within the Nation's jurisdiction is not an offense against the United States but against the Nation's local laws, and that "The Fifth Amendment to the Constitution does not apply to local legislation of the Cherokee nation." Whether a Cherokee statute had been repealed was a question for the Nation's courts alone.

Talton is the basis for the rule that tribal governments are not bound by the federal Bill of Rights, because their power comes from their own sovereignty, not from the Constitution. Congress responded in 1968 with the Indian Civil Rights Act, and Santa Clara Pueblo v. Martinez (1978) then interpreted it.
