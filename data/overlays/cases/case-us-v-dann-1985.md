In 1951 the Shoshone Tribe brought a claim before the Indian Claims Commission seeking compensation for loss of aboriginal title to lands in several Western states. The Commission held that aboriginal title had been extinguished and awarded $26 million, which the Court of Claims affirmed. The award was certified and deposited for the Tribe in an interest-bearing Treasury account. Because of the Tribe's refusal to cooperate, the Interior Secretary had not submitted a distribution plan. The United States then sued members of the Tribe for grazing livestock without a permit. Respondents claimed aboriginal title.

The Supreme Court unanimously held that "payment" under section 22(a) of the Indian Claims Commission Act occurred when the funds were placed in the Treasury account for the Tribe. Waiting for a final distribution plan would frustrate the Act's purpose of disposing of Indian claims with finality. The Court of Appeals' contrary ruling was reversed.

Dann closed off a challenge to the extinguishment of Western Shoshone title.
