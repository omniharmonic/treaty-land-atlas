The Jicarilla Apache Nation's reservation contains natural resources developed under statutes administered by the Interior Department. The United States holds the proceeds in trust for the Tribe. The Tribe sued in the Court of Federal Claims for money damages for mismanagement of those trust funds and asked for government documents. The government claimed attorney-client privilege for some. The claims court applied a "fiduciary exception," under which a private trustee cannot assert that privilege against trust beneficiaries over advice about administering the trust. The Federal Circuit agreed.

The Supreme Court reversed, in an opinion by Justice Alito. It held that the fiduciary exception does not apply to the general trust relationship between the United States and Indian tribes. The Court looked to the exception's common-law roots and found the Indian trust relationship is governed by statutes and regulations, not by common-law trust principles, so the government could keep its privilege.

The decision follows United States v. Navajo Nation (2003) in treating the federal trust responsibility as defined by specific statutes and regulations, which limits tribal breach-of-trust claims.
