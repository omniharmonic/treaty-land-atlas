The Act of July 27, 1866 granted the Atlantic & Pacific Railroad Company a large strip of land in Arizona. The United States, as guardian of the Walapai Indians of Arizona, sued the Santa Fe Pacific Railroad, the successor, to establish that the Walapai held an Indian right of occupancy in part of that land. The question was whether the grant extinguished their claim.

The Supreme Court held that railroad grant lands were subject to any existing Indian right of occupancy until the United States extinguished it through a voluntary cession by the Indians, as section 2 of the Act provided. "Indian title" exists where it is a fact that land was part of a tribe's ancestral home, "definable territory occupied exclusively by that tribe." A tribal claim "need not be based upon treaty, statute, or other formal governmental action." The Court sent the case back to decide whether the right had been extinguished before the railroad was located in 1872.

Santa Fe Pacific states that aboriginal title is a legal right, but that Congress's power to extinguish it is supreme. Tee-Hit-Ton (1955) cited it.
