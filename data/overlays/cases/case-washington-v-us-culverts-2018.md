In the 1854-55 Stevens treaties, western Washington tribes gave up large areas of land and waters and were guaranteed a right to fish off reservation. In the long-running United States v. Washington case, the tribes and the United States later asked the court to order the State to fix culverts, pipes that carry streams under roads, that block salmon from reaching spawning and rearing habitat. The district court found that Washington's barrier culverts violated the treaties and issued an injunction.

The Ninth Circuit affirmed in 2016, holding that in building and maintaining barrier culverts the State violated its obligation to the tribes under the treaties. It upheld an injunction directing the State to correct most of its high-priority barrier culverts within seventeen years, and to fix the rest at the end of their natural life or during other road projects. In 2018 the Supreme Court affirmed by an equally divided Court: "The judgment is affirmed by an equally divided Court." Justice Kennedy took no part.

The 4-4 affirmance is not binding precedent elsewhere, but the Ninth Circuit's ruling stays in force and extends the treaty fishing right to protect salmon habitat from state-built barriers.
