In the agreement of May 1, 1888 with the Gros Ventre and other Indians, the Fort Belknap Reservation in Montana was set aside. Montana became a state in 1889. The United States sued Winters and other non-Indian settlers who had built dams and reservoirs upstream on the Milk River that cut off water to the reservation. The question was whether the agreement included a right to water for irrigation, which the state's prior-appropriation law would not otherwise recognize.

The Supreme Court held there was an implied reservation of a sufficient amount of Milk River water for irrigation, and that Montana's admission to the Union did not affect it. The Government has power to reserve waters and exempt them from appropriation under state law. In the interpretation of agreements with Indians, the Court said, "ambiguities should be resolved from the standpoint of the Indians."

Winters is the foundation of the reserved water rights doctrine. Arizona v. California (1963) later applied it to measure rights by practicably irrigable acreage, and Arizona v. Navajo Nation (2023) concerned limits on federal duties to secure water.
