This case is about pandemic relief money. Title V of the CARES Act set aside $8 billion for "Tribal governments," defined by reference to the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (ISDA). ISDA defines "Indian tribe" to include "any Alaska Native village or regional or village corporation as defined in or established pursuant to" the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (ANCSA), if "recognized as eligible for the special programs and services provided by the United States to Indians." The Treasury Department decided Alaska Native Corporations (ANCs) were eligible, though they are not federally recognized tribes. Several federally recognized tribes sued.

The Supreme Court held 6-3 for the ANCs, in an opinion by Justice Sotomayor. Under the plain meaning of ISDA, ANCs are "Indian tribe[s]" because they were established under ANCSA and are therefore recognized as eligible for its benefits. The tribes' "term of art" reading, equating the clause with federal recognition, failed. Justice Gorsuch dissented with Justices Thomas and Kagan.

The decision shows how ANCSA's land-and-money settlement of 1971, discussed in Alaska v. Venetie (1998), continues to shape which entities count as tribes for federal programs.
