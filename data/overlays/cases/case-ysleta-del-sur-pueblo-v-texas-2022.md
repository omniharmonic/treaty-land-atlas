The Ysleta del Sur Pueblo is a federally recognized tribe in Texas. In 1987 Congress passed the Restoration Act, restoring federal recognition to the Tribe. Section 107 says "gaming activities which are prohibited by [Texas law] are hereby prohibited on the reservation and on lands of the tribe," while denying Texas "civil or criminal regulatory jurisdiction." The Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (IGRA) generally lets tribes offer bingo without state permission if the State allows bingo on some terms. In 2016 the Pueblo began offering electronic bingo, and Texas sued to shut down its bingo operations. The Fifth Circuit held that all of Texas's gaming laws applied.

The Supreme Court, 5-4, in an opinion by Justice Gorsuch, vacated that ruling. It held that "The Restoration Act bans as a matter of federal law on tribal lands only those gaming activities also banned in Texas." Because Texas allows bingo under rules, its bingo laws are regulatory, not prohibitory, so the Pueblo may offer bingo subject to federal law. Chief Justice Roberts dissented.

The decision limits the earlier Cabazon-style regulatory distinction: it follows the statute's line between prohibition and regulation.
