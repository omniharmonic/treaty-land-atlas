Land restoration is a statutory return of land to a tribe. One early authority is section 3 of the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934, headed "Restoration of lands to tribal ownership," which lets the Secretary of the Interior, if he finds it in the public interest, restore to tribal ownership the remaining surplus lands of an Indian reservation.

The Blue Lake Act, Public Law 91-550, was signed on December 15, 1970. It amended section 4 of a 1933 act on the watershed in the Carson National Forest, in New Mexico. The new text declared that the described lands, "upon which said Indians depend and have depended since time immemorial for water supply, forage for their domestic livestock, wood and timber for their personal use, and as the scene of certain religious ceremonials," are to be held in trust for the Pueblo de Taos.

Taos Pueblo's own website describes the signing. It quotes President Nixon: "in 1906 an injustice was done in which land involved in this bill, 48,000 acres, was taken from the Indians involved, the Taos Pueblo Indians." The Pueblo says the signing "restored Taos Pueblo lands" and set a precedent for self-determination.
