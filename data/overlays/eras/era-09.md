The contemporary era is not defined by one law. It is a run of apologies, settlements and returns, some of land, some of authority over it.

On September 8, 2000, Assistant Secretary of Indian Affairs Kevin Gover, speaking at the Bureau of Indian Affairs' 175th anniversary, apologized. The BIA's own release says he extended a formal apology to Indian people for the historical conduct of the agency, which he described as ethnic cleansing and cultural annihilation.

The Land Buy-Back Program for Tribal Nations, created in 2012 under the Cobell settlement, bought fractional interests in allotted land from willing individual owners and placed them in trust for tribes. Interior announced its conclusion on December 4, 2023, saying nearly 3 million acres in 15 states were consolidated and restored to tribal trust ownership, with $1.69 billion paid to more than 123,000 individuals. Interior frames the problem as a legacy of the General Allotment Act of 1887.

In a June 23, 2021 announcement, Interior said all National Bison Range lands, approximately 18,800.22 acres, were transferred to the BIA to be held in trust for the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes. Congress approved this in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (Public Law 116-260). On December 9, 2024, Interior said the administration had signed 400 co-stewardship agreements with tribes, Alaska Native corporations and consortiums, under Joint Secretary's Order 3403, signed in 2021.
