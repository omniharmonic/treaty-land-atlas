On September 13, 1873, at the Los Pinos agency, Felix R. Brunot signed an agreement with chiefs, headmen and men of seven Ute bands. The Ute relinquished to the United States a portion of the reservation set by the 1868 treaty, centered on the San Juan Mountains. The United States agreed to let the Ute hunt on those lands "so long as the game lasts and the Indians are at peace with the white people." It also agreed to hold a perpetual trust fund to produce $25,000 a year.

The agreement's preamble says an earlier negotiation failed "owing to the refusal of said Indians to relinquish their right to any portion of said reservation." The Colorado Encyclopedia says miners had entered the mountains, and that Brunot convinced the Utes it would be better to sell than to lose the land by force with no compensation. It puts the area at 3.7 million acres.

Congress ratified the agreement on April 29, 1874 (18 Stat. 36). The Colorado Encyclopedia says a mining rush followed and that the Utes were force-marched to Utah in 1881.
