On February 28, 1877, Congress ratified an agreement made in the fall of 1876 by commissioners headed by George W. Manypenny with Sioux bands and the Northern Arapaho and Cheyenne. It reduced the Great Sioux Reservation's boundaries, and ended Sioux rights in the Black Hills and the unceded hunting territory. In exchange the Sioux received subsistence rations for as long as needed. The Act said the Sioux were not to be removed to Indian Territory.

The Supreme Court says gold was confirmed in the Black Hills in 1874, and the Army stopped keeping miners out in late 1875. Negotiations for purchase failed, and the agreement was signed by only about 10 percent of adult male Sioux, well below the treaty's three-fourths requirement. The Court of Claims described a "pattern of duress practiced by the Government on the starving Sioux."

In United States v. Sioux Nation (1980), the Supreme Court affirmed that the 1877 Act was a taking requiring compensation, with interest on $17.1 million from 1877. The sources I opened do not say whether the Sioux have accepted the award.
