This is a colonial record from 1752, before the United States existed. The National Archives series includes colonial treaties because United States treaty-making followed a custom set by Great Britain and the colonial governments.

Virginia's governor, Robert Dinwiddie, sent three commissioners, Joshua Fry, Lunsford Lomax and James Patton, to Logg's Town on the Ohio River. His commission says they were to deliver a royal present to the northern Indians in friendship with Virginia and to strengthen the chain of friendship. An editor's footnote adds that the aim was to get the Six Nations to confirm the 1744 Treaty of Lancaster, helping the Ohio Company's plans.

In council the commissioners told the Indians that Virginia held a deed from the Onondaga council at Lancaster, and asked their Ohio brethren to confirm it. Their speaker, the Half King, said they were willing to confirm what their council had done, but had never understood that the Lancaster land reached beyond the hill on the far side of the Allegheny. He added that they lacked full power on the Ohio and must tell their council at Onondaga.

The signed paper nevertheless records the consent of six sachems to the Lancaster deed, and to British settlements on the southern or eastern parts of the Ohio. It says those settlements would go unmolested, and that they would help protect them. It is dated 13 June 1752.
