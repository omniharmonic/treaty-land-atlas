This is a colonial record from 1754. It is the minutes of a congress held at Albany, New York, from 19 June into July 1754, decades before the United States existed. The National Archives series includes colonial records because United States treaty-making followed a custom set by Great Britain and the colonial governments.

New York's lieutenant governor, James De Lancey, hosted commissioners from New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Pennsylvania and Maryland. Benjamin Franklin was among Pennsylvania's. They met the Six Nations, whose speaker Hendrick told them the Six Nations had thought the Covenant Chain broken because they had been neglected for three years. He also said Virginia and Pennsylvania had made paths and built houses in their country without asking.

A separate speech from the River Indians recalled the friendship of their ancestors and the land they gave the first settlers. A speech copied into the minutes says white people said they wanted only the low lands, but now settled the hills too, and asks that the Indians be paid for their lands. The governor answered that if an inquiry showed injury, he would try to get redress.

The delegates also voted to form a plan of union of the colonies, to be established by an Act of Parliament. The pages opened here show the plan debated, but do not show what came of it.
