This 1778 agreement was made at Fort Pitt between commissioners of the United States, then still fighting the Revolutionary War, and the Delaware leaders White Eyes, John Kill Buck Jr. and Captain Pipe. It was a wartime alliance, not a land sale. The Delaware agreed to give U.S. troops "a free passage through their country" to British posts and to supply them, and to join U.S. forces with warriors.

The United States wanted a route to British forts on the lakes. The treaty text says the best way to some of them "is by passing through the country of the Delaware nation." In return the United States agreed to build and garrison a fort for the safety of Delaware families. It also promised to "guarantee to the aforesaid nation of Delawares, and their heirs, all their territorial rights" as long as the Delaware held to the alliance.

Article VI also allowed other friendly nations to join and form a state with the Delaware at its head, with representation in Congress, if Congress approved. The input notes that this was never fulfilled. The sources I opened do not trace what followed; see the linked violation record.
