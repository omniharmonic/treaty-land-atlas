On October 22, 1784, Oliver Wolcott, Richard Butler and Arthur Lee, commissioners of the United States, concluded articles at Fort Stanwix with "the Sachems and Warriors of the Six Nations." The United States agreed to "give peace to the Senecas, Mohawks, Onondagas and Cayugas, and receive them into their protection upon the following conditions."

The conditions were these. Six hostages were to stay with the United States until all prisoners taken in the war were delivered up. The Oneida and Tuscarora nations were to be "secured in the possession of the lands on which they are settled." A line running from near Niagara to the Ohio River became the western boundary of the Six Nations' lands, and they "yield to the United States, all claims to the country west of the said boundary," reserving only six miles square round the fort of Oswego for the United States. The commissioners also promised to order goods for the Six Nations.

EBSCO says the Treaty of Paris of 1783 had ignored Iroquois land claims, the war had divided the Six Nations, and only one sachem signed. The next day Pennsylvania officials secured their own cession of much of northwestern Pennsylvania for $5,000 in continental scrip. EBSCO says the Six Nations council "ultimately rejected the treaty, viewing it as coerced and unrepresentative of their collective will."

EBSCO also says that in 1785 New York officials began a process that eventually extinguished Oneida title to their lands in New York.
