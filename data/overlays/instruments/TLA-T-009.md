On October 22, 1784, three U.S. commissioners met Seneca, Mohawk, Onondaga and Cayuga leaders at Fort Stanwix. The treaty says the United States "give peace to the Senecas, Mohawks, Onondagas and Cayugas, and receive them into their protection upon the following conditions." Those conditions were six hostages until prisoners were returned, a western boundary line, and six miles square around Oswego for the United States. Oneida and Tuscarora possession of their settled lands was secured.

The United States wanted prisoners returned and a new boundary after the war. EBSCO says the Treaty of Paris of 1783 had ignored Iroquois land claims, that the Six Nations arrived divided by the war, and that only one sachem signed. The treaty required hostages to be held until prisoners were delivered.

EBSCO reports that the Six Nations council later rejected the treaty, viewing it as coerced and unrepresentative. The 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua, also in this atlas, later set out the Seneca Nation's boundaries in western New York.
