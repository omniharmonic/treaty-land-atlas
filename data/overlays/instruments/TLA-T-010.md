On January 21, 1785, commissioners of the United States (the treaty is signed by George Rogers Clark, Richard Butler and Arthur Lee) concluded a treaty at Fort McIntosh with the "Sachems and Warriors" of the Wyandot, Delaware, Chippewa and Ottawa nations. The text says the commissioners "give peace" to the four nations "on the following conditions."

Three chiefs, one Wyandot and two Delaware, were to be held until all white and Black prisoners were restored. The nations acknowledged themselves to be under U.S. protection "and of no other sovereign whatsoever." Article III drew a boundary starting at the mouth of the Cuyahoga River, and Article IV allotted the lands inside it to the Wyandot and Delaware "to live and to hunt on," reserving sites for trading posts. Article VI had the signers "acknowledge the lands east, south and west of the lines described in the third article ... to belong to the United States." The United States also reserved Detroit and Michilimackinac, and promised to distribute goods.

The Beaver Area Heritage Foundation, a local-history organization, says "The terms were imposed rather than negotiated." It says the nations were told they had sided against the United States, and that when they reminded the commissioners of a promise that the Ohio would forever be a boundary, the reply was "we claim this country by right of conquest." It adds that "The fact that other nations occupied this country but were not at the treaty was ignored," and that surveyors began running the Seven Ranges in Ohio in September 1785.
