At Fort McIntosh on January 21, 1785, U.S. commissioners made a treaty with Wyandot, Delaware, Chippewa and Ottawa leaders. It gave peace on conditions: hostages until prisoners were returned, acknowledgment of U.S. protection, and a boundary line from the Cuyahoga River through the Muskingum and Great Miami country. The United States allotted the land inside the line to the Wyandot and Delaware to "live and to hunt on," and the signers acknowledged that lands east, south and west of it belonged to the United States.

The Beaver Area Heritage Foundation says the terms were "imposed rather than negotiated," that the commissioners told the nations they had sided against the United States, and that the U.S. reply to objections was "we claim this country by right of conquest." It adds that Pennsylvania wanted clear title to land for Revolutionary War soldiers and that surveys of the Seven Ranges in Ohio began soon after.

The same source says other nations occupied the country but were not at the treaty, and that this was ignored. The boundary was renewed at Fort Harmar in 1789 and replaced at Greenville in 1795, both in this atlas.
