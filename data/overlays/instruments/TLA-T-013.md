On January 10, 1786, U.S. commissioners made a treaty at Hopewell with three Chickasaw leaders named in the text as plenipotentiaries for the nation. The Chickasaw acknowledged U.S. protection "and of no other sovereign whosoever." Article III set the boundary of the lands "allotted to the Chickasaw nation to live and hunt on," running from the Ohio and Mississippi rivers to the Choctaw line. A circle five miles across at the lower Muscle Shoals was reserved for a trading post under U.S. government.

The Mississippi Encyclopedia says the three Hopewell treaties, with the Cherokee, Choctaw and Chickasaw, were nearly identical apart from their boundary articles. The commissioners wanted to impose federal Indian policy on the postwar South and to nullify Spanish influence.

Article IV said any citizen who settled on the land forfeited U.S. protection. The Chickasaw ceded land in 1805 to pay trading debts, in the treaty also in this atlas. The sources I opened for this record do not describe other follow-on events.
