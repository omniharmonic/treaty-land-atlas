On January 10, 1786, at Hopewell on the Keowee River, near Seneca Old Town, Benjamin Hawkins, Andrew Pickens and Joseph Martin concluded a treaty for the United States with Piomingo, "Head Warrior and First Minister of the Chickasaw Nation," Mingatushka and Latopoia. The United States "give peace to the Chickasaw Nation, and receive them into the favor and protection of the said States."

The Mississippi Encyclopedia says a much smaller Chickasaw delegation than the Cherokee or Choctaw arrived, and that the three leaders represented the pro-American faction of their nation. Piomingo questioned Article 1, saying he had taken no American prisoners to surrender, but agreed to Article 3. The commissioners read and explained each article, and the three made their marks on January 10.

Article III set the boundary of "the lands hereby allotted to the Chickasaw nation to live and hunt on," running from the Ohio and Mississippi rivers to the Choctaw line. It reserved a circle five miles in diameter at the lower port of the Muscle Shoals for a trading post "to the use and under the government of the United States." Article IV said a settler on the allotted lands would "forfeit the protection of the United States," and the Chickasaws "may punish him or not as they please."

The Mississippi Encyclopedia says that the three Hopewell treaties were virtually identical apart from their boundary articles, and that none of the delegations recognized U.S. sovereignty over their lands.
