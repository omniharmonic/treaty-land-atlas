On January 9, 1789, Arthur St. Clair, governor of the Northwest Territory, made a treaty at Fort Harmar with Wyandot, Delaware, Ottawa, Chippewa, Potawatomi and Sac leaders. The nations agreed to "renew and confirm" the boundary agreed at Fort McIntosh in 1785 and released all land east, south and west of that line "so far as the said Indians formerly claimed the same." The text records goods worth six thousand dollars delivered at the treaty.

Richland Source reports that the nations at the council hoped for a reservation west of the Muskingum River and north of the Ohio, but St. Clair refused and demanded they accept the Fort McIntosh line. It says he told them he would attack them if they refused, and offered $3,000 in presents.

Many Ohio nations, including the Shawnee, refused to honor the treaty, saying the signers did not speak for them, according to the same source. Warfare increased. The Northwest Indian War ended with the Treaty of Greenville in 1795, which set a new boundary.
