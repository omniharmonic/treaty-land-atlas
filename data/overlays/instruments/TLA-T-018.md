On July 2, 1791, William Blount, governor of the Southwest Territory, signed a treaty of "peace and friendship" at the Holston River with Cherokee chiefs and warriors. The Cherokee acknowledged U.S. protection, agreed not to treat with foreign powers or states, and accepted a new boundary. Article IV says they "release, quit-claim, relinquish and cede" all land to the right of the line. The United States guaranteed "all their lands not hereby ceded."

The Tennessee Encyclopedia says the earlier Treaty of Hopewell did not stop settlers moving onto Cherokee land, and Holston was meant to be more definitive. For a $1,000 annuity, later raised to $1,500, the Cherokee ceded claims east of the Clinch River and north of a line through Kingston. Articles V and VI gave U.S. citizens a road and the Tennessee River, and gave the United States sole control of Cherokee trade.

Further cessions followed at Tellico in 1798, according to the Tennessee Encyclopedia, and in the treaties that followed in this atlas. The linked violation and case records cover the longer history, including Worcester v. Georgia.
