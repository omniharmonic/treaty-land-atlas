This 1792 record is a single article, not a full treaty. It is dated 23 April 1792 at Philadelphia. Henry Knox, Secretary for the Department of War, signed it for the United States.

The article names the Five Nations as the Senecas, Oneidas and the Stockbridge Indians living with them, together with the Tuscaroras, Cayugas and Onondagas. It says the United States would, to promote their happiness, spend 1,500 dollars every year buying clothing, domestic animals and implements of husbandry (farming tools) for them. The money would also encourage skilled craftsmen, called artificers, to live in their villages.

The paper was made official in a particular way. President George Washington's document says the article was stipulated by Knox with the advice and consent of the Senate, and that he, having seen and considered it, did accept, ratify and confirm it. He signed it at Philadelphia on 23 April 1792, and Thomas Jefferson countersigned.

No Native signer appears on the transcript. It gives only the United States' pledge. The transcript does not show how the annual payment was carried out, and it does not mention any land.
