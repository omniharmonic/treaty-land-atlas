On December 2, 1794, Timothy Pickering signed a treaty at Oneida, New York, with chiefs of the Oneida, Tuscarora and Stockbridge nations. The treaty recites that a body of these nations "adhered faithfully to the United States" in the Revolution, were driven from their homes, and had been promised reward. The United States agreed to pay $5,000 for individual losses and services, build a grist mill and saw mill, employ a miller for three years, and pay $1,000 for a church to replace one burned in the war.

The text frames the treaty as keeping wartime promises to allies. It does not describe a land transfer. In return the three nations acknowledged themselves satisfied and "relinquish all other claims of compensation and rewards" for losses and services in the war.
