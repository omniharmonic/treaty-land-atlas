On August 3, 1795, Major General Anthony Wayne signed a treaty at Greeneville, Ohio, with Wyandot, Delaware, Shawnee, Ottawa, Chippewa, Potawatomi, Miami, Eel River, Wea, Kickapoo, Piankashaw and Kaskaskia leaders. It states its purpose was "to put an end to a destructive war." Article III set a general boundary line and the nations ceded all claims east and south of it, plus a list of tracts for U.S. posts, including at Fort Wayne, the Maumee rapids and Detroit.

The e-WV encyclopedia says the treaty followed Wayne's victory at Fallen Timbers on August 20, 1794, and that the nations ceded about two-thirds of present Ohio. The United States paid $20,000 in goods at once and promised $9,500 in goods every year, with the share for each nation named in the text. The text also says the nations could hunt on ceded land and that settlers on the remaining Indian lands could be driven off.

e-WV notes that problems continued as settlers breached the line, and that some leaders, such as Tecumseh, had not signed and did not recognize it. The linked violation record covers later events.
