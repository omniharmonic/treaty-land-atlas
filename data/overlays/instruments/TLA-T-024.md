On May 31, 1796, at New York City, a commissioner appointed by the United States, Abraham Ogden, presided over a treaty between deputies of the Seven Nations of Canada and agents of New York State. The deputies agreed to "cede, release and quit claim to the people of the state of New-York, forever, all the claim, right, or title" of the Seven Nations to lands within the state.

The agents for the state proposed "the compensation" for "the extinguishment of their claim to all lands within the state." The text lists William Constable and Daniel McCormick, purchasers under Alexander Macomb, as parties. The nations kept a tract six miles square at St. Regis, reserved when the state sold land to Macomb, plus one mile square at each of two mills and meadows on the Grass River. New York was to pay 1,233 pounds, 6 shillings and 8 pence, then 213 pounds, 6 shillings and 8 pence every year.
