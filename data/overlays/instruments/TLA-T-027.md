On September 15, 1797, at Genesee, New York, Seneca sachems, chiefs and warriors signed a deed to Robert Morris, a Philadelphia financier, for the right to land in western New York. It was agreed at a treaty "held under the authority of the United States" before commissioner Jeremiah Wadsworth, with a Massachusetts superintendent also present. The price was "one hundred thousand dollars," to be put in Bank of the United States stock held in the President's name for the Seneca.

Morris held the pre-emptive right that Massachusetts had received from New York, and that right would let him buy the land only from the Seneca. The Seneca kept reservations at villages such as Canawaugas, Big Tree and Gardeau and larger tracts along Lake Erie, plus hunting and fishing rights on the sold tract.
