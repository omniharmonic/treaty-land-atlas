On October 2, 1798, two U.S. commissioners made a treaty at Tellico with Cherokee chiefs and warriors. The preamble says the 1791 Treaty of Holston had not been carried out because of "misunderstandings," that settlers had moved onto Cherokee land beyond the boundary before it was marked, and that the treaty aimed at "remedying inconveniences arising to citizens of the United States from the adjustment of the boundary line."

Article IV has the Cherokee "relinquish and cede" land within described lines. The Tennessee Encyclopedia says the cession gave land that settlers had entered between the Clinch River and the Cumberland Plateau and between the Tennessee and Little Tennessee rivers. In return the United States delivered goods worth $5,000 and promised $1,000 of goods yearly in addition to the existing annuity. Two commissioners, one from each side, were to run the line.

The preamble states that settlers were removed from the Indian lands once the 1791 line was marked. The text notes ratification was April 30, 1802, almost four years after signing. Later cessions at Tellico in 1804 and 1805 are in this atlas.
