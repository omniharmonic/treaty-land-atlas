On 24 October 1801, at Chickasaw Bluffs, three United States commissioners (James Wilkinson, Benjamin Hawkins and Andrew Pickens) met the Mingo (a principal leader), principal men and warriors of the Chickasaw nation. The treaty calls itself "of reciprocal advantages and mutual convenience."

In Article I the Chickasaw gave leave to the President to lay out, open and make a wagon road through their land between the settlements of Mero District in Tennessee and Natchez in the Mississippi Territory. The road would be a highway for citizens of the United States and for the Chickasaws. The Chickasaw would appoint two men as assistants, guides or pilots, who would receive reasonable pay. The ferries over water courses on the road would be held to be the property of the Chickasaw nation.

In Article II the commissioners gave the Mingo and his delegation goods worth seven hundred dollars for their trouble and for letting the road be opened. The commissioners also agreed that the President would take such measures as he deemed proper to help the Chickasaws keep all their rights against the encroachments of unjust neighbors, of which he would be the judge.

The treaty took effect once the President ratified it with the Senate's consent. It was ratified on 1 May 1802 and proclaimed on 4 May 1802.
