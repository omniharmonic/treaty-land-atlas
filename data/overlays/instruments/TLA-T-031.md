On December 17, 1801, U.S. commissioners James Wilkinson, Benjamin Hawkins and Andrew Pickens made a treaty at Fort Adams with Choctaw leaders. The Choctaw gave "free consent" to a wagon way through their lands from the northern edge of the Mississippi Territory to Chickasaw land, to be a highway "for ever" for U.S. citizens and the Choctaw. They agreed to retrace the old British-era boundary line, and relinquished land between that line and the Mississippi River, south of the Yazoo.

The United States agreed to remove settlers living beyond that line and demolish their cabins. For the concessions, the commissioners delivered goods worth $2,000 and promised three sets of blacksmith tools.

The treaty text ties it to the Treaty of Hopewell of 1786, which had given the Choctaw peace and protection. A web search suggested the land ceded was about 2.6 million acres, and that the road was meant for the Natchez Trace, but I did not open a source stating either, so neither claim is included. The Choctaw ceded more land at Mount Dexter in 1805, in this atlas.
