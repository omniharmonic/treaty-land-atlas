On June 16, 1802, U.S. commissioners James Wilkinson, Benjamin Hawkins and Andrew Pickens met the kings, chiefs, head men and warriors of the Creek nation. They signed a "treaty of limits" at the commissioners' camp near Fort Wilkinson on the Oconee River.

In Article I the Creek "cede to the United States" all the "tract and tracts of land" inside a described boundary. The line starts at the Appalachee River, a branch of the Oconee, runs down the Oconee to the Ocmulgee, follows the Altamaha, and reaches the mounts marked at the Okefinocau swamp near the head of the St. Marys River. The Creek were recorded as relinquishing "all their right, title, interest and pretensions" to this land forever.

The United States agreed to pay the Creek $3,000 every year, plus $1,000 a year for ten years to the chiefs who administer the government. It also agreed to pay $25,000: $10,000 in goods, $10,000 to satisfy debts owed to the U.S. trading factory, and $5,000 for claims over property taken from U.S. citizens after the Treaty of Colerain. Two sets of blacksmith's tools, and men to work them, were promised for three years.

Article III said garrisons for the frontier would be placed on Creek land "in the manner and on the terms established by the treaty of Colerain."
