On June 16, 1802, Wilkinson, Hawkins and Pickens made a "treaty of limits" with Creek kings, chiefs, head men and warriors near Fort Wilkinson on the Oconee River. Article I says the Creek "cede to the United States" two named tracts, one west of the Oconee near the Appalachee headwaters and one between the Altamaha and the St. Marys river down to the Okefenokee swamp, and relinquish "all their right, title, interest and pretension" to them.

The United States agreed to pay $3,000 a year, $1,000 a year for ten years to the chiefs who governed, and $25,000: $10,000 in goods, $10,000 to satisfy debts owed to the U.S. trading factory, and $5,000 for claims about property taken from citizens. It also promised blacksmiths for three years, and Article III allowed garrisons on Creek land "on the terms established by the treaty of Colerain."
