On 30 June 1802, at Buffalo Creek, New York, the sachems, chiefs and warriors of the Seneca Nation signed a second paper alongside the larger exchange of the same day. The buyers were three individuals: Oliver Phelps of Ontario County, Isaac Bronson of New York City and Horatio Jones of Ontario County. John Tayler was the commissioner appointed by the President to hold the treaty.

For twelve hundred dollars, paid in hand, the Seneca leaders granted, released and forever quit-claimed to the three men the tract known as Little Beard's Reservation in Ontario County. The text bounds it on the east by the Genesee River and Little Beard's Creek, on the south and west by other lands of the buyers, and on the north by Big Tree Reservation. It says it contains two square miles, or twelve hundred and eighty acres.

Three copies were signed, one for the United States, one for the Seneca and one for the buyers. Thirteen marks appear, among them Red Jacket's, Corn Planter's, Young King's and Farmer's Brother's. It was ratified and proclaimed on 7 February 1803.

The text does not explain why the tract was sold or what the Seneca did with the money. It describes a private sale approved at a treaty held under United States authority.
