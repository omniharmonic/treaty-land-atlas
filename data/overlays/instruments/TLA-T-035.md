On October 17, 1802, at Fort Confederation on the Tombigbee River, Brigadier General James Wilkinson signed a "provisional convention" with the Choctaw nation. Choctaw chiefs, head men and principal warriors signed for the lower towns, the upper towns and the six towns. The preamble says they agreed "freely, voluntarily, and without constraint." That is the document's own statement.

Article I let the President retrace and re-mark the old line of limits set between the British king and the Choctaw. It ran from the Chickasawhay River east to the Tombigbee. Two commissioners chosen by the Choctaw from their own number were to go with the U.S. party, and were to be paid one dollar a day.

Article II fixed the line as the boundary. For "one dollar" paid by the United States, the Choctaw released all the land bounded by that line on the north, the Chickasawhay River on the west, the Tombigbee and Mobile rivers on the east, and the U.S. boundary on the south.

Article III let the chiefs of the upper towns alter the old boundary near the mouth of the Yazoo River if it could be done without injury to the nation. The convention took effect only when ratified. The OSU header gives June 20, 1803 for ratification and January 20, 1803 for proclamation, which appear reversed.
