On June 7, 1803, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, made a treaty at Fort Wayne with leaders of the Delaware, Shawnee, Potawatomi, Miami and Kickapoo, and with Eel River, Wea, Piankashaw and Kaskaskia representatives. Article 1 recited that the 1795 Treaty of Greenville had reserved the post of Vincennes, and fixed its boundaries because they were hard to determine. Article 2 gave up U.S. claims to adjoining lands.

Article 3 had the tribes cede "the great salt spring upon the Saline creek" with land up to four miles square, and the United States promised yearly salt to the tribes, not above 150 bushels. Article 4 let the United States locate three tracts on the Vincennes-Kaskaskia road, and one on the road to Clarksville, for houses of entertainment, once certain tribes consented. Article 5 provided for adjusting the boundary if settlements fell on the Indian side.

The text says several tribes joined through agents. The 1804 Delaware treaty referred to a Fort Wayne council in June 1803. The sources I opened do not give acreage or describe pressures.
