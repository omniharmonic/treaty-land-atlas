On June 7, 1803, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, held a treaty at Fort Wayne with the Delawares, Shawnees, Potawatomi, Miamis and Kickapoos. The Eel River, Wea, Piankashaw and Kaskaskia nations were represented by four named agents, Tuthinipee, Winnemac, Richerville and Little Turtle, "properly authorized by the said tribes."

The first article started from the 1795 Treaty of Greenville, which had reserved the post of Vincennes and nearby lands. It said it was hard to fix the exact limits of the tract "as held by the French and British governments," so the treaty set boundaries, starting at Point Coupee on the Wabash. In Article 2 the United States gave up any claim to lands next to that tract.

Article 3 had the tribes "relinquish and cede" the great salt spring on Saline Creek, with land around it not exceeding four miles square. The United States promised each year a quantity of salt for the tribes, not above 150 bushels, to be divided as their general council decided. Article 4 gave the United States the right to locate three tracts on the road between Vincennes and Kaskaskia, and one between Vincennes and Clarksville, for houses where travelers could stay, once the Kickapoo, Eel River, Wea, Piankashaw and Kaskaskia agreed.

Article 5 said that if settlers' claims fell inside the Indian side of the line, the line would be moved to include them, and an equal quantity of land would be given to the tribes at either end of the tract. The treaty gives no acreage.
