On August 13, 1803, at Vincennes, William Henry Harrison made a treaty with the head chiefs and warriors of the Kaskaskia, described in the text as the remains of the Illinois nations. The treaty recites that the Illinois tribes had been reduced to a very small number and were unable "to occupy the extensive tract of country" held by their ancestors. In Article 1 the Kaskaskia "relinquished and cede to the United States all the lands in the Illinois country" they had held or could claim.

They kept about 350 acres near Kaskaskia and the right to locate 1,280 acres elsewhere in the ceded area. The United States took them under its protection, raised their annuity to $1,000, and promised a house for the chief, a fenced field of up to 100 acres, $100 a year for seven years toward a Catholic priest, $300 for a church, and $580 to cover debts.

The text calls that package "a full and ample compensation." The sources I opened do not describe the negotiation or later events.
