On August 18, 1804, at Vincennes, William Henry Harrison made a treaty with the Delaware. The preamble says the Delaware found their annuity from the United States "not sufficient" for their comfort, that "the extensiveness of the country they possess" took hunting parties far from their towns, and that the United States wanted "to connect their settlements on the Wabash with the state of Kentucky." In Article 1 the Delaware "relinquish to the United States forever" the tract between the Ohio and Wabash below the Fort Wayne cession and the Vincennes to Falls of the Ohio road.

They received an added annuity of $300 for ten years, $300 a year for five years for teaching farming and domestic arts, and goods and animals to the value of $400 (plus $800 in goods delivered). Article 4 said the United States would treat the Delaware as owners of lands between the Ohio and White rivers.

The Miami later contested that claim, which led to the 1805 treaty at Grouseland in this atlas.
