On August 27, 1804, at Vincennes, William Henry Harrison made a treaty with the chiefs and head men of the Piankeshaw tribe. In Article 1 the Piankeshaw "relinquishes and cedes to the United States for ever" the land between the Ohio and Wabash below Clark's grant and the Vincennes tract. Article 2 had them acknowledge "explicitly the right of the Kaskaskia tribe to sell" the country that tribe ceded in 1803.

The Piankeshaw received an added annuity of $200 for ten years, paid in money, merchandise, provisions, animals or farm tools at the tribe's option, plus goods worth $700 delivered at the treaty.

The treaty is the second Vincennes treaty of that summer, after the Delaware cession of August 18. The sources I opened do not give acreage, do not describe the council, and do not explain why the Piankeshaw agreed; the text does not state a motive. A later Piankashaw treaty at Vincennes in December 1805 ceded more land and is in this atlas.
