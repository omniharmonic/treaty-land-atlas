On August 27, 1804, at Vincennes, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, signed a treaty with the chiefs and head men of the Piankeshaw tribe. It came nine days after the Delaware treaty at the same town, which ceded land in the same region.

In Article 1 the Piankeshaw "relinquishes, and cedes to the United States for ever" the country between the Ohio and Wabash rivers below Clark's grant. The cession also named the Vincennes tract that had been ceded at Fort Wayne in 1803 and a connecting line drawn parallel to the road from Vincennes to the Falls of the Ohio.

In Article 2 the Piankeshaw "acknowledge explicitly the right of the Kaskaskia tribe to sell" the country the Kaskaskia had lately ceded. The text says that land is separated from Piankeshaw lands by the ridge dividing the Wabash waters from those of Saline Creek and other branches of the Mississippi.

The Piankeshaw received an additional annuity of $200 for ten years, paid at the tribe's option in money, goods, provisions, animals or farm tools. Goods worth $700 were delivered at the signing, and the text calls the whole "full compensation." Article 4 reserved to the United States the right to divide the annuity among families, with a due share for the chiefs. The text states no acreage.
