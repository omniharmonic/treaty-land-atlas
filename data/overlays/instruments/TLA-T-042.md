On October 24, 1804, Daniel Smith and Return J. Meigs, commissioners for the United States, made a short treaty at Tellico garrison with principal Cherokee chiefs. The Cherokee "relinquish and cede" a small tract on the Georgia boundary line, running four miles into Cherokee land, large enough to include "all the plantations in Wafford's settlement."

In return the United States delivered $5,000 in goods or money at signing and promised $1,000 a year in goods or money in addition to the existing annuity. The text notes these were "for, and in consideration of" the cession.

The OSU margin lists the proclamation date as May 17, 1824, about 20 years after signing, which differs from the usual pattern. The sources I opened do not give acreage, explain the 1824 proclamation, or describe any pressure. The input links no violations or cases. Further Cherokee cessions at Tellico and Washington followed in 1805 and 1806, in this atlas.
