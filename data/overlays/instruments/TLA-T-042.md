On October 24, 1804, Daniel Smith and Return J. Meigs, commissioners for the United States, met principal Cherokee chiefs at Tellico garrison, "on Cherokee ground." The treaty has only two articles. The text says the chiefs had considered "certain propositions" from the commissioners and "unanimously agreed."

In Article 1 the Cherokee nation "relinquish and cede" a tract along the boundary line between Georgia and the Cherokee nation. It begins northeast of the most northeast plantation in what the text calls Wafford's settlement. It runs four miles into Cherokee land at right angles to the line, and is drawn wide enough to include "all the plantations in Wafford's settlement."

In Article 2 the United States agreed to deliver $5,000 in goods, or that sum in money at the Cherokee's option, when the treaty was signed. It also promised $1,000 a year in goods or money, in addition to the annuity already agreed. The text says these were "for, and in consideration of" the cession.

The OSU database lists a proclamation date of May 17, 1824, about twenty years after signing. The sources here do not explain that gap, give an acreage, or describe any pressure on the Cherokee.
