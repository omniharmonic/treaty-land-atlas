On November 3, 1804, at St. Louis, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, signed a treaty with chiefs and head men of the "united Sac and Fox tribes." In Article 1 the United States took the tribes into its "friendship and protection," and the tribes agreed to consider themselves under the protection of the United States and "no other power."

Article 2 drew a boundary line. It began on the Missouri River opposite the mouth of the Gasconade, ran to the river the text calls the Jeffreon and down it to the Mississippi, then up the Mississippi to the Wisconsin River, and across to the Illinois River. The tribes ceded "all the lands included within the above-described boundary." The price was goods worth $2,234.50 delivered at signing and an annuity of $1,000 in goods. Article 7 said that as long as the ceded lands stayed U.S. property, the Sauk and Fox could live and hunt on them.

A history from Before Knox says many Meskwaki and Sauk, including the Sauk leader Black Hawk, considered the treaty void because it was "signed in the absence of important leaders and without the consent of the Tribal Council." The same page says Fort Madison, built in 1808 on the side of the Mississippi meant for the Sauk and Meskwaki, called the treaty's legitimacy into question.

In 1816 the Sauk of Rock River signed a treaty with the United States that confirmed the 1804 treaty; it appears elsewhere in the atlas.
