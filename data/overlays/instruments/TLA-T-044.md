On July 4, 1805, Charles Jouett, a U.S. commissioner, made a treaty at Fort Industry on the Miami of the Lake with Wyandot, Ottawa, Chippewa, Munsee, Delaware, Shawnee and Potawatomi sachems and warriors. Article II set a boundary meridian running north from a point on the south shore of Lake Erie 120 miles west of Pennsylvania's western line. Article III had the nations cede lands east of it, bounded south and east by the Greenville Treaty line and north by "the northernmost part of the forty first degree of north latitude."

The United States agreed to pay $1,000 a year forever, $825 and $175 of it at Detroit, to named Wyandot, Munsee and Delaware nations and the Shawnee and Seneca who lived with them. Article V says the cession also settled purchases by the Connecticut Land Company and the proprietors of the "half million acres... called Sufferer's Land," with named sums paid to the Ottawa, Chippewa and some Potawatomi.

The nations could hunt and fish on the ceded land if peaceable. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the negotiation.
