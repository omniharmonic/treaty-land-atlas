On July 4, 1805, at Fort Industry on the Miami of the Lake, a U.S. commissioner made a treaty with Wyandot, Ottawa, Chippewa, Munsee, Delaware, Shawnee and Potawatomi nations. The nations again acknowledged U.S. protection. A boundary meridian ran north from the south shore of Lake Erie, 120 miles west of Pennsylvania's western line, and the nations ceded the land to the east, bounded by the Greenville line.

The United States agreed to pay a yearly sum "forever" of $1,000, delivered at Detroit to Wyandot, Munsee, Delaware and the Shawnee and Seneca living with them. The text also records money from the Connecticut Land Company and the proprietors of "Sufferer's Land," paid to the Ottawa, Chippewa and some Potawatomi, and says the nations kept the right to hunt and fish on the ceded land if peaceable.

This record is the OSU version of the same treaty as TLA-T-044, which carries the same 7 Stat. 87 citation. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the negotiation.
