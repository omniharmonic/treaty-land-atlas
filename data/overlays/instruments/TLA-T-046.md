On July 23, 1805, U.S. commissioners James Robertson and Silas Dinsmoor made an agreement in Chickasaw country with Chickasaw mingo chiefs and warriors. The first article begins: "WHEREAS the Chickasaw nation of Indians have been for some time embarrassed by heavy debts due to their merchants and traders," they agreed to cede a tract from the Ohio River down to the Tennessee, the Duck River and the Natchez road. A one-mile-square tract near Duck River was reserved for a chief.

The United States paid $20,000 "for the use of the nation at large, and for the payment of the debts," $1,000 each to George Colbert and O'Koy, and an annuity of $100 for life to King Chinubbee.

The Chickasaw Nation's website says Colbert and Chinnubbee first told U.S. officials they had no land to sell, until the agent "discovered or revealed" that tribal members owed over $20,000 to trading companies. It gives the cession as 2.25 million acres of hunting land. The Senate ratified it on May 22, 1807.
