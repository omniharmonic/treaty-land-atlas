On July 23, 1805, U.S. commissioners James Robertson and Silas Dinsmoor signed "articles of arrangement" in the Chickasaw country with the mingo chiefs and warriors of the Chickasaw nation. Article I opens: "WHEREAS the Chickasaw nation of Indians have been for some time embarrassed by heavy debts due to their merchants and traders." The Chickasaw were bound by the treaty to cede a tract from the left bank of the Ohio River down to the Tennessee River, up to the mouth of the Duck River, and along the road from Nashville to Natchez. A one-mile-square tract at the mouth of the Duck River was reserved for the chief O'Koy.

In return the United States agreed to pay $20,000 "for the use of the nation at large, and for the payment of the debts due to their merchants and traders." George Colbert and O'Koy were each to receive $1,000, which the text says was granted at the national council's request for services to the nation. King Chinubbee received an annuity of $100 for life. Article IV barred U.S. citizens from settling on part of the cession for three years.

The Chickasaw Nation's own history page says that when U.S. officials first asked Colbert and Chinnubbee for a land cession, the two leaders "said they had no land to sell." It says the U.S. agent then "discovered or revealed" that tribal members owed more than $20,000 to trading companies. The nation gives the cession as 2.25 million acres of hunting land in present-day Kentucky, central Tennessee and northern Alabama. The OSU database records ratification on May 22, 1807.
