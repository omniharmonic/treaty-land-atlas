On August 21, 1805, William Henry Harrison made a treaty at Grouseland, near Vincennes, with the Delaware, Potawatomi, Miami, Eel River and Wea. Article I begins from the Delaware treaty of August 18, 1804, in which the United States had treated the Delaware as owners of the land between the White River, the Ohio and Clark's grant. The text says the Miami, from whom the Delaware derived their claim, "contend" they never meant to convey the soil, and so the Delaware, "for the sake of peace and good neighborhood," released the United States from its guarantee.

In Article II the Miami, Eel River and Wea ceded the land south of a line from the northeast corner of the Fort Wayne tract. The United States added annuities of $600, $250 and $250, plus $500 a year for ten years to the Potawatomi, and delivered $4,000.

Article IV had the United States treat the three tribes as joint owners of the Wabash country above Vincennes and promise not to buy without each tribe's consent.
