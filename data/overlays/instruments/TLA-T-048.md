On October 25, 1805, U.S. commissioners Return J. Meigs and Daniel Smith held a treaty at Tellico with Cherokee chiefs and head men. Article I recognized all former treaties on peace and the prevention of crimes as "continued in force."

In Article II the Cherokee "quit claim and cede to the United States, all the land which they have heretofore claimed" north of a described line. The line starts at the mouth of the Duck River, runs to the Tennessee River opposite the mouth of the Hiwassee, and follows the Tennessee up to the Clinch. The Cherokee kept a small tract at and below the mouth of the Clinch and two sections of one square mile each. The United States also reserved three square miles opposite the Hiwassee, in case the garrisons and factory at South West Point and Tellico needed a new site. The Tennessee Encyclopedia says Meigs and Smith obtained all land north of the Duck River, extended due east to the Tennessee River and including all of the Cumberland Plateau.

In Article III the United States agreed to pay $3,000 in merchandise at once, $11,000 within ninety days of ratification, and an annuity of $3,000. Article IV gave U.S. citizens free use of two more roads through Cherokee country. Two days later the commissioners made a second Tellico treaty, which is recorded separately in the atlas.

The sources here give no acreage and no account of the council.
