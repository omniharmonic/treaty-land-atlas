On October 25, 1805, Return J. Meigs and Daniel Smith made a treaty at Tellico with Cherokee chiefs and head men. Article II has the Cherokee "quit claim and cede to the United States" all land they had claimed north of a line from the mouth of Duck River, up the river, then across to the Tennessee River opposite the Hiwassee, up the Tennessee to the Clinch, and on, reserving tracts at the mouth of the Clinch.

The United States agreed to pay $3,000 in merchandise at once, $11,000 within ninety days of ratification, and a $3,000 annuity. The Tennessee Encyclopedia says the commissioners obtained "all land north of the Duck River" and extended the line due east to the Tennessee, including all of the Cumberland Plateau.

Two days later a second Tellico treaty, in this atlas, gave a mail road and other tracts. Former treaties were recognized as continuing in force. The sources I opened do not describe the council in more detail or give an acreage.
