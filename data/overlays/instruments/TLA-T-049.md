On October 27, 1805, Meigs and Smith made a second treaty at Tellico with Cherokee chiefs and head men. The first article recites that the section of land on which the garrison of South West Point stands, extending to Kingston, "is likely to be a desirable place for the assembly of the state of Tennessee to convene at," as a committee of the legislature had viewed it. The Cherokee "quit claim and cede" it, reserving the ferries, together with the first island in the Tennessee above the mouth of the Clinch.

Article 2 granted citizens of the United States free use of a road from Tellico to the Tombigbee, since the mail was ordered to run from Knoxville to New Orleans through Cherokee, Creek and Choctaw lands. For the cession the United States agreed to pay $1,600 in money or merchandise within ninety days of ratification.

The Tennessee Encyclopedia notes that an additional payment came "a few days later" for the right to operate a mail road. The sources I opened do not give acreage or describe the council.
