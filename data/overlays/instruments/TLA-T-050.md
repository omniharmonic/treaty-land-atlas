On November 14, 1805, Secretary of War Henry Dearborn made a convention in Washington with Creek chiefs and head men. Article I has them "cede and forever quit claim" a tract between the Oconee and Ocmulgee rivers, from the high shoals of the Apalachee to the mouth of the Ulcofauhatche, then down the Ocmulgee to its junction with the Oconee. The Creek also granted a horse path from the Ocmulgee to the Mobile, with boats and houses of entertainment for travelers.

The United States agreed to pay $12,000 a year for eight years and $11,000 a year for the next ten, eighteen payments in all, as consideration for the land, the path, and occupancy of a reserved tract at the Ocmulgee fields.

In 1796, according to the Charlton County Historical Society, the Creek at Colerain had "positively refused to cede" this territory between the Oconee and Ocmulgee to Georgia. The sources I opened do not explain what changed, or describe the 1805 negotiations.
