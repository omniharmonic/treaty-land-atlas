On November 16, 1805, U.S. commissioners James Robertson and Silas Dinsmoor signed a "treaty of limits" at Mount Dexter with the mingoes, chiefs and warriors of the Choctaw nation. In Article I the Choctaw ceded "all the lands to which they now have or ever had claim" lying to the right of a described line. The line begins near the Natchez path to Washington County and runs east across the Pearl River, along the Chickasawhay River toward the Tombigbee, and on to the ridge between the Alabama and Tombigbee waters. The Choctaw kept a tract two miles square at the town of Fuketcheepoonta and a 5,120-acre tract for two daughters of Samuel Mitchell, whom the text names as the children of a Choctaw woman.

In Article II the United States agreed to pay $50,500. Of that, $48,000 was "to enable the Mingoes to discharge the debt due to their merchants and traders," and to pay for depredations on stock and other property by "evil disposed persons" of the Choctaw nation. Another $2,500 went to John Pitchlynn. The United States also promised $3,000 a year in goods.

Article III gave each of three "great medal mingoes," Pukshunubbee, Hoomastubbee and Pooshamattaha, $500 for past services and an annuity of $150 while in office.

Article V said the boundary was to be marked in the presence of three Choctaw appointees, one from each medal district. The OSU record shows the treaty proclaimed on February 25, 1808. The text states no acreage.
