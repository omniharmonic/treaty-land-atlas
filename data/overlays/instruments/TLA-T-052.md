On December 30, 1805, William Henry Harrison made a treaty at Vincennes with the chiefs and head men of the Piankishaw tribe. In Article I the tribe "cedes and relinquishes to the United States for ever" the tract between the Wabash and the land the Kaskaskia ceded in 1803, south of a line from the northwest corner of the Vincennes tract, with one reservation named in the text.

The United States took the Piankishaw under "immediate care and patronage," promised protection as effective as the Kaskaskia enjoyed, and required that the tribe never war on other tribes without U.S. consent. It added an annuity of $300 a year, and the text says that annuity and $1,100 delivered at the treaty are "a full compensation."

This is a later step after the Piankeshaw cession of August 1804, also in this atlas. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the council.
