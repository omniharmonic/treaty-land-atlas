On January 7, 1806, Secretary of War Henry Dearborn made a convention in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Cherokee Nation. Article I has the Cherokee relinquish "all right, title, interest and claim" to the land north of the Tennessee River and west of a line from the Chickasaw Old Fields to the head of Duck River, excepting two tracts: one at Muscle Shoals, and a smaller one on the north bank. They also gave up claims to Long Island in the Holston River.

The United States agreed to pay $2,000 on ratification and $2,000 a year for four years, totaling $10,000. It also promised a grist mill within a year, a machine for cleaning cotton, and $100 a year for the chief Black Fox for life. Article III has the United States promising to use its influence with the Chickasaw about a boundary south of the Tennessee.

The sources I opened do not give acreage, describe the Washington council, or explain what led to the cession.
