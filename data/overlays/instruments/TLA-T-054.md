On November 17, 1807, William Hull, governor of Michigan Territory and sole U.S. commissioner, made a treaty at Detroit with the Ottawa, Chippewa, Wyandot and Potawatomi. The preamble speaks of confirming "the friendship, which happily subsists" and settling "arrangements mutually beneficial." Article I has the nations cede all their right to a described tract starting at the mouth of the Maumee (Miami of the lakes), up to the Au Glaize River, north to a line from the Lake Huron outlet, then east to the Canadian border and down the Detroit River to Lake Erie.

In payment, the United States agreed to pay $10,000 in money, goods, tools or animals, split among the four nations, and a perpetual annuity of $2,400 at Detroit. It also promised two blacksmiths for ten years and a possible commutation of the annuity. Article V lets the nations hunt on the ceded lands.
