On November 25, 1808, William Hull made a treaty at Brownstown, Michigan Territory, with Chippewa, Ottawa, Potawatomi, Wyandot and Shawnee sachems and warriors. The preamble recalls the 1807 Detroit cession and says the lands between the Maumee and the Greenville and Fort Industry lines still belonged to the nations, so the United States "cannot, of right, open and maintain a convenient road" from Ohio to Michigan settlements.

In Article II the nations ceded a 120-foot-wide road from the foot of the Maumee rapids to the western line of the Connecticut Reserve, plus "all the land within one mile of the said road, on each side thereof," for settlements. They also ceded a 120-foot road south from Lower Sandusky to the Greenville line, with the right to take timber. The nations kept hunting and fishing on those lands while the United States held them.

No payment is stated in the text I read. The sources I opened do not describe the council.
