On September 30, 1809, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, made a treaty at Fort Wayne with Delaware, Potawatomi, Miami and Eel River leaders. Article 1 has the Miami and Eel River, "and the Delawares and Putawatimies, as their allies," cede land between the Fort Wayne boundary, the Wabash and a line from Raccoon Creek. A second tract ran south from Fort Recovery. Annuities of $500 each to the Delaware, Miami and Potawatomi and $250 to the Eel River, plus $5,200 in goods, were "full compensation."

The National Library of Medicine's Native Voices timeline says Harrison negotiated to acquire 3 million acres, and that Tecumseh told him the signers "have no authority to sell the land." It says Tecumseh questioned whether leaders could sell land held in common with other peoples, and that he warned Americans not to settle there and organized fighters.

Article 5 required the Wea to agree, and Article 9 asked the Kickapoo; separate treaties in this atlas record their consent.
