On October 26, 1809, William Henry Harrison met the sachems and head warriors of the Wea tribe at Vincennes, Indiana Territory, and signed a short convention. It was the next step after the Treaty of Fort Wayne of September 30, 1809. That treaty had said the Wea's consent was needed to complete the title to the first tract of land the Miami, Eel River, Delaware and Potawatomi were bound by the treaty to cede.

The Wea declared "their full and free consent" to the Fort Wayne treaty with the Delaware, Miami, Potawatomi and Eel River. They also consented to a separate article made the same day with the Miami and Eel River.

In return, "in consideration of the relinquishment made in the first article" of the Fort Wayne treaty, Harrison agreed for the United States to an added annuity of $300 and a present sum of $1,500. He also agreed to a further permanent annuity of $100 "as soon as the Kickapoos can be brought to give their consent to the ninth article" of the Fort Wayne treaty.

The Kickapoo gave that consent on December 9, 1809, in a separate treaty at Vincennes. The OSU record shows this Wea convention was ratified on January 2, 1810, and proclaimed January 25, 1810. The sources here give no acreage and do not describe the council.
