On October 26, 1809, William Henry Harrison made a short convention at Vincennes with the sachems and head warriors of the Wea tribe. The Wea declared "their full and free consent" to the Fort Wayne treaty of September 30 with the Delaware, Miami, Potawatomi and Eel River, and to the separate article made with the Miami and Eel River the same day.

Article 5 of the Fort Wayne treaty had said the Wea's consent was needed "to complete the title" to the first tract ceded, and that they would get goods and a permanent annuity of at least $300. Here the United States agreed to an added $300 annuity and a present sum of $1,500. It also promised a further permanent annuity of $100 once the Kickapoo consented to the ninth article.

This record is the Wea step in the 1809 sequence, together with Kickapoo and Fort Wayne in this atlas. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the council.
