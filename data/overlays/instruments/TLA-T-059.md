On December 9, 1809, William Henry Harrison, governor of Indiana Territory, signed a treaty at Vincennes with the sachems and war chiefs of the Kickapoo. It finished the sequence that began with the Treaty of Fort Wayne of September 30, 1809. In that treaty the Miami, Eel River, Delaware and Potawatomi had offered to cede land on the northwest side of the Wabash "to show their attachment to their brothers the kickapoos." The offer came with a $400 annuity and had "no effect unless the kickapoos will agree to it."

In Article 1 the Kickapoo agreed. The text says the cession in the ninth article of the Fort Wayne treaty "is hereby agreed to by the Kickapoos," with a permanent added annuity of $400 and goods worth $800 as "full compensation."

In Article 2 the Kickapoo ceded a further tract between that land, the Wabash, the Vermilion River, and a line meeting the Vermilion twenty miles from its mouth. The text gives a further annuity of $100 and $700 in goods as full compensation. If the Miami did not approve this second cession, and the United States chose not to take the land without their consent, the United States would be released from paying the $100.

Article 3 applied the Greenville treaty's rules for paying annuities and the right to hunt on the land. The OSU record shows the treaty was ratified on March 5, 1810. The sources opened give no acreage and do not describe the council.
