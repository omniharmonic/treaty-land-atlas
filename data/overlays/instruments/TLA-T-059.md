On December 9, 1809, William Henry Harrison made a treaty at Vincennes with the sachems and war chiefs of the Kickapoo. Article 1 has the Kickapoo agree to the ninth article of the Fort Wayne treaty of September 30 and the cession it contained, with a permanent added annuity of $400 and goods worth $800 as "full compensation."

Article 2 has them cede a further tract between that cession, the Wabash, the Vermilion River and a line meeting the Vermilion twenty miles from its mouth. For that, the text gives a further annuity of $100 and $700 in goods. It says if the Miami do not approve, and the United States does not take possession without their consent, the $100 annuity need not be paid.

The Fort Wayne treaty had said Article 9 would have no effect unless the Kickapoo agreed. A 1816 treaty at Fort Harrison, in this atlas, records the Kickapoo again acknowledging the cession. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the council.
