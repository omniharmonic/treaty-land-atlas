This is a wartime treaty of peace and friendship, signed at Greenville, Ohio, on 22 July 1814, during the War of 1812. William Henry Harrison and Lewis Cass, governor of the Michigan Territory, signed for the United States. The other side was the head men, chiefs and warriors of the Wyandots, Delawares, Shawanoese, Senecas and Miamis.

In Article I, the Wyandots, Delawares, Shawanoese and Senecas, together with the United States, gave peace to the Miami nation. They also extended it to named groups of Potawatomi, Ottawa and Kickapoo.

In Article II, the tribes and bands named engaged to help the United States prosecute the war against Great Britain, and against tribes that continued hostile. They also promised to make no peace with either without the consent of the United States. Each tribe would supply the number of warriors the President, or an officer with authority, required.

In Article III, the Wyandot, the Senecas of Sandusky and Stony Creek, the Delaware and Shawanoes, who the text says kept their fidelity throughout the war, again acknowledged themselves under United States protection and no other power's.

Article IV promised that, if the conditions were faithfully performed, the United States would confirm and establish all boundaries between its lands and those of the Wyandots, Delawares, Shawanoese and Miamies, as they were before the war. The page lists ratification on 13 December 1814 and proclamation on 21 December.
