On 18 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, three United States commissioners (William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau) signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs and warriors of the Potawatomi nation living on the Illinois River. It is one of a series of peace treaties made there in 1815 after the war.

The treaty says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and put the Potawatomi back in every respect on the same footing as before the war. Article 1 provides that every injury or act of hostility on either side would be forgiven and forgot. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between the citizens of the United States and the individuals of the Potawatomi nation.

In Article 3 both sides promised to deliver up all prisoners in their hands to the officer commanding at Fort Clarke on the Illinois River. In Article 4 they recognized, re-established and confirmed every earlier treaty, contract and agreement between the United States and the Potawatomi.

The treaty contains no land cession and sets no boundary. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not say how many prisoners were exchanged or what the Potawatomi leaders thought of the terms.
