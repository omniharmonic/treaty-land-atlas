On 18 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, the same three commissioners, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, signed a treaty of peace and friendship for the United States. The other side was the chiefs and warriors of the Piankashaw tribe or nation. The Piankashaw treaty was signed the same day as the Potawatomi treaty.

The preamble says the parties wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to place the Piankashaw "on the same footing upon which they stood before the war." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Piankashaw nation.

In Article 3 the parties recognized, re-established and confirmed every earlier treaty, contract or agreement between the United States and the Piankashaw. This treaty does not repeat the terms of those earlier agreements, so their terms are found in the earlier treaties themselves.

There is no cession of land and no boundary in the text. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. The text does not identify the war by name and says nothing about how the Piankashaw came to be at Portage des Sioux.
