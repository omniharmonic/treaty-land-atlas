On 19 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship for the United States with chiefs and warriors of the Teton (spelled Teeton in the text) tribe.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship, putting the Teton on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility committed by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Teton tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledge themselves to be under the protection of the United States of America, and of no other nation, power or sovereign. That wording is the same in several other 1815 treaties made at Portage des Sioux with Sioux and other peoples.

The treaty contains no cession of land and no boundary. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not say who spoke for the Teton or how the protection clause was understood by them.
