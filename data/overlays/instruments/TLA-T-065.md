On 19 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, the United States commissioners William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs and warriors of the Sioux of the Lakes. The text calls them a tribe, acting on its behalf.

The preamble says the parties wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to put the Sioux of the Lakes on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 provides that every injury or act of hostility committed by either side would be forgiven and forgot. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors acknowledged themselves and their tribe to be under the protection of the United States and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

Like other treaties made at Portage des Sioux in July 1815, it gives no land, sets no boundary and promises no payment. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. The text does not say which Sioux bands are meant by "of the Lakes."
