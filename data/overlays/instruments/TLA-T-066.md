On 19 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of the Sioux of the river St. Peter's.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to place the tribe on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility committed by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledge themselves to be under the protection of the United States and of no other power, nation or sovereign.

The treaty contains no land cession, no boundary and no payment. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not say how the United States later used the protection clause.
