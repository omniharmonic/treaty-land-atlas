On 19 July 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship for the United States with the chiefs and warriors of the Yancton (Yankton) tribe of Indians.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to put the Yankton on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility committed by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Yancton tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the undersigned chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledge themselves to be under the protection of the United States, and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The text is short and contains no cession of land, no boundary and no payment. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. The signature block names those who witnessed the signing, but the transcript does not describe the Yankton leaders or what they were offered.
