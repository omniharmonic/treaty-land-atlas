On 20 July 1815, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of a tribe or nation the text calls the Mahas. The Oklahoma State database files it as a treaty with the Makah, but the text says Mahas, that is, the Omaha. The Makah are not named.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to put the tribe on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility committed by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe or nation of the Mahas, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors acknowledge themselves and their tribe to be under the protection of the United States and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The treaty cedes no land and sets no boundary. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. Among the signers was Oupaatanga, or the big elk, a principal chief of the Omaha.
