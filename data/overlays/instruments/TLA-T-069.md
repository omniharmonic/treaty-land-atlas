On 2 September 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs, warriors and deputies of the Kickapoo tribe or nation. It came about six weeks after the first Portage des Sioux treaties of July 1815.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship, and to put the Kickapoo on the same footing as before the war. Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Kickapoo nation.

In Article 3 both sides promised to deliver up all prisoners in their hands to the officer commanding at Fort Clarke on the Illinois River, to be restored to their respective nations as soon as practicable. In Article 4 they recognized, re-established and confirmed every earlier treaty, contract and agreement between the United States and the Kickapoo.

The text cedes no land and sets no boundary. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not say which Kickapoo bands sent deputies, or what was in the earlier treaties it confirms.
