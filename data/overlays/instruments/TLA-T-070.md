On September 8, 1815, three U.S. commissioners, William Henry Harrison, Duncan McArthur and John Graham, met sachems, headmen and warriors at Spring Wells, near Detroit. The nations were the Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca, Shawnee, Miami, Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi, living in Ohio, Indiana Territory and Michigan Territory.

The preamble says the Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi, together with certain bands of the Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca, Shawnee and Miami, "were associated with Great Britain in the late war" and wanted to be restored to peace. In Article 1 the United States gave peace to the Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi. In Article 2 it agreed to restore to those three nations "all the possessions, rights, and priviledges, which they enjoyed, or were entitled to, in the year one thousand eight hundred and eleven." They in turn agreed to place themselves again under U.S. protection "and of no other power whatsoever."

Article 3 pardoned chiefs and warriors of the Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca and Shawnee who had kept fighting until the war ended. It cited the fidelity of others in those nations and the "repentance" of the Miami, shown by placing themselves under U.S. protection in 1814. In Article 4 all eight nations agreed to "renew and confirm the treaty of Greenville" of 1795 and all later treaties to which they were parties.

This was a peace treaty, not a new cession. The sources here do not describe the council. The OSU record shows ratification on December 26, 1815.
