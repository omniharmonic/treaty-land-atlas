On September 8, 1815, William Henry Harrison, Duncan McArthur and John Graham met at Spring Wells near Detroit with Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca, Shawnee, Miami, Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi leaders. The preamble says the Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi, with some Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca, Shawnee and Miami bands, "were associated with Great Britain in the late war" and wanted peace. The United States gave peace and restored the possessions and privileges the three nations had in 1811.

Article 3 pardoned chiefs and warriors of the Wyandot, Delaware, Seneca and Shawnee who continued fighting until the war ended, in view of the fidelity of others and the Miami's "repentance." In Article 4 all the parties agreed to "renew and confirm the treaty of Greenville" and all later treaties, which were "again ratified and confirmed."

This was a peace treaty rather than a new cession, and it reaffirmed the Treaty of Greenville and all later treaties the parties had made. The sources I opened do not describe the council.
