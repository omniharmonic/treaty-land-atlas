On 12 September 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the king, chiefs and warriors of the Great and Little Osage tribes or nations.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship, and to put the Osage on the same footing as before the war. Article 1 provides that every injury or act of hostility by either side would be forgiven and forgot. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Osage tribes.

In Article 3 the parties recognized, re-established and confirmed every earlier treaty, contract and agreement between the United States and the Osage tribes or nations. It does not name any earlier treaty.

The text cedes no land, sets no boundary and promises no payment. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not describe who signed for the Little Osage or the Great Osage beyond the signature block, and it does not say what led them to the council.
