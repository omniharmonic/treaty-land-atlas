On September 13, 1815, commissioners William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace at Portage des Sioux with chiefs and warriors of the part of the Sac nation living on the Missouri River. The preamble says this group had "found themselves compelled," when the war began, to separate from the rest of the nation and move to the Missouri, where they had remained loyal to the United States.

In Article 1 they "assent to the treaty" of November 3, 1804, between the United States and the united Sacs and Foxes, and promise to re-establish and enforce it. In Article 2 they promised to remain "distinct and separate from the Sacs of Rock river" and to give them no aid until those Sacs made peace. In return the United States promised them the rights set out in the 1804 treaty and a just share of its annuities.

A history from Before Knox says many Sauk and Meskwaki, including Black Hawk, considered the 1804 treaty void because it was "signed in the absence of important leaders and without the consent of the Tribal Council." This band's confirmation did not settle that dispute. The Sauk of Rock River signed their own confirmation of the 1804 treaty in 1816, which the atlas records separately.
