On September 13, 1815, commissioners William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau made a treaty of peace at Portage des Sioux with chiefs and warriors of the part of the Sac Nation living on the Missouri River. The preamble says this group had "found themselves compelled" at the start of the war to separate from the rest of the nation and move to the Missouri, where they had stayed loyal to the United States.

In Article 1 they "assent to the treaty" of November 3, 1804, with the united Sacs and Foxes and promise to re-establish and enforce it. In Article 2 they promised to remain separate from the Sacs of Rock River and give them no aid until peace was made. In return the United States promised them the rights in the 1804 treaty and a just share of its annuities.

The 1804 treaty is disputed by many Sauk and Meskwaki, according to the Before Knox history. This band's confirmation did not settle the matter for the Rock River group, who made a separate treaty in 1816, also in this atlas.
