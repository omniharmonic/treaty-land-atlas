On September 14, 1815, Clark, Edwards and Chouteau made a treaty of peace and friendship at Portage des Sioux with a Fox king, chiefs and warriors. The parties wished to be placed "on the same footing upon which they stood before the war." Injuries were forgiven, prisoners were to be returned to the commander at Fort Clark on the Illinois River, and perpetual peace was declared.

Article 4 has the Fox "assent to, recognize, re-establish, and confirm, the treaty of St. Louis" of November 3, 1804, "to the full extent of their interest in the same." The United States promised to fulfill that treaty's stipulations in favor of the Fox.

As with the Sauk treaty of the previous day, this was a confirmation, not a new cession. The Before Knox history reports that many Meskwaki and Sauk considered the 1804 treaty void. The sources I opened do not describe the Fox council or what the Fox themselves said.
