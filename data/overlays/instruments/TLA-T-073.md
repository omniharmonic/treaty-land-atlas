On September 14, 1815, commissioners William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship at Portage des Sioux with a Fox king, chiefs and warriors. It came a day after a similar treaty with a Sauk band. The preamble says the parties wished to re-establish peace and to be "placed in all things, and in every respect, on the same footing upon which they stood before the war."

The first three articles were the terms of peace. Every injury or act of hostility by either side was "mutually forgiven and forgot." There would be perpetual peace and friendship between U.S. citizens and the Fox. Both sides would deliver up all prisoners to the officer commanding at Fort Clark on the Illinois River, who would send them home as soon as practicable.

In Article 4 the Fox "assent to, recognize, re-establish, and confirm" the Treaty of St. Louis of November 3, 1804, "to the full extent of their interest in the same." The United States promised to fulfill all that treaty's stipulations in favor of the Fox. This was a confirmation, not a new cession.

A history from Before Knox reports that many Meskwaki and Sauk considered the 1804 treaty void because it was "signed in the absence of important leaders and without the consent of the Tribal Council." The sources opened do not describe the Fox council or say what the Fox themselves said at Portage des Sioux.
