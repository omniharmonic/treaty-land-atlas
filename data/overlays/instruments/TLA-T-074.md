On 16 September 1815, at Portage des Sioux, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the king, chiefs and warriors of the Iaway (Iowa) tribe or nation.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship, and to put the Iowa on the same footing as before the war. Article 1 provides that every injury or act of hostility by either side would be forgiven and forgot. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Iowa tribe.

In Article 3 the contracting parties promised to deliver up all prisoners in their hands to the officer commanding at St. Louis, to be restored to their respective nations as soon as practicable. In Article 4 they recognized, re-established and confirmed every earlier treaty, contract and agreement between the United States and the Iowa.

The text cedes no land, sets no boundary and promises no payment. The page lists ratification on 26 December 1815. It does not say how many prisoners were exchanged or what earlier agreements are meant.
