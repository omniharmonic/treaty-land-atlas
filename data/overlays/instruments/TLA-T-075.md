On 28 October 1815, at St. Louis, two United States commissioners, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of the Kanzas (Kansa) tribe of Indians. William Clark, who signed other treaties in this 1815 series, is not named in this one.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to put the Kansa on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility by either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Kansa tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledge themselves to be under the protection of the United States of America and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The treaty cedes no land and sets no boundary. The Oklahoma State page gives the ratification date as 26 December 1818, which does not match the other 1815 treaties in the series; the other 1815 treaties are listed as ratified 26 December 1815. The cause of the difference is not explained on the page.
