On March 22, 1816, in Washington, George Graham, authorized by the President, signed a treaty with Cherokee chiefs and headmen "duly authorized and empowered by the said nation." Signers included Major Ridge and John Ross.

The preamble explains the cession. The executive of South Carolina had asked the President to extinguish the Cherokee claim to land inside South Carolina's boundaries, "as lately established and agreed upon" with North Carolina. The treaty says the Cherokee nation "is disposed to comply with the wishes of their brothers of South Carolina."

In Article 1 the Cherokee agreed to "cede to the state of South Carolina, and forever quit claim to" a tract. Its bounds run from the east bank of the Chattooga River along the Cherokee boundary to a rock on the Blue Ridge, then about twenty miles on a course the text gives as south 68 and a quarter degrees west to a rock on the Chattooga at 35 degrees north latitude, and down the river to the start. In Article 2 the United States promised that South Carolina would pay the Cherokee nation $5,000 within ninety days after the treaty was ratified. The payment had two conditions: the Cherokee nation had to approve it in council, and South Carolina's executive had to approve the stipulation.

The treaty was ratified on April 8, 1816, according to the OSU record. The same day, the United States signed a second Cherokee convention, covered in a separate record. The sources here give no acreage and do not describe the council.
