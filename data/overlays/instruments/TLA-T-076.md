On March 22, 1816, in Washington, George Graham, for the United States, signed a treaty with Cherokee chiefs and headmen, including Major Ridge and John Ross. The preamble says the executive of South Carolina had asked the President "to extinguish the claim of the Cherokee nation" to land within the state's recently fixed boundaries, and that the Cherokee were "disposed to comply with the wishes of their brothers of South Carolina."

Article 1 has the Cherokee cede to South Carolina a tract between the Chattooga River and a line from a rock on the Blue Ridge. The United States promised that South Carolina would pay the Cherokee $5,000 within 90 days of ratification, provided the Cherokee council approved and South Carolina's executive agreed.

This is one of two Cherokee instruments of the same day in this atlas; the other concerns a Creek-Cherokee boundary and roads. The sources I opened give no acreage and do not describe the council.
