On March 22, 1816, George Graham made a convention in Washington with Cherokee chiefs and headmen. The preamble says doubts existed about the northern edge of the Creek land west of the Coosa River that the Creek ceded at Fort Jackson in 1814, and that a 1806 treaty had recognized a Cherokee claim to lands south of the Tennessee's Big Bend. Article 1 sets a line from the Coosa to the Flat Rock on Bear Creek as the boundary between the lands ceded by the Creek and the Cherokee claim.

Article 2 gave the United States the right to open and use roads and to use rivers and waters within the Cherokee Nation as highways, with the Cherokee to run ferries and public houses. Article 3 had Cherokee commissioners help run the boundary. Article 5 promised the United States would indemnify Cherokee individuals for losses from a military march.

The sources I opened do not give acreage or describe the council. The Fort Jackson cession it refers to is in this atlas.
