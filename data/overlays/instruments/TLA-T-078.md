On May 13, 1816, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau made a treaty at St. Louis with chiefs and warriors of the Sacs of Rock River. The preamble cites the 1814 Treaty of Ghent, which called for ending hostilities with Indian tribes and restoring them to their pre-war footing if they stopped fighting. The text says the Rock River Sacs had declined an invitation to treat and continued hostilities, which "would have justified the infliction of the severest chastisement."

Article 1 has them "unconditionally assent to recognize, re-establish, and confirm" the 1804 Treaty of St. Louis and other contracts. Article 2 places them on the same footing as before the war if they deliver up plundered property by July 1.

According to Before Knox, many Meskwaki and Sauk, including Black Hawk, considered the 1804 treaty void because important leaders and the council were absent.
