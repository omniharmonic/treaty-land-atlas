On 1 June 1816, at St. Louis, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship for the United States. On the other side were chiefs and warriors representing eight bands of the Siouxs, which the text groups into three tribes: the Siouxs of the Leaf, the Siouxs of the Broad Leaf, and the Siouxs who shoot in the Pine Tops.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and put the bands on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." One article forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Another declares perpetual peace and friendship and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

The treaty also contains a land clause. The chiefs and warriors confirmed to the United States every cession of land earlier made by their tribes to the British, French or Spanish government, within the limits of the United States or its territories. Both sides recognized and confirmed every earlier treaty between the United States and the tribes. The chiefs and warriors acknowledged themselves to be under the protection of the United States and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The text names no acreage and no places for the earlier cessions. The page lists a proclamation on 30 December 1816.
