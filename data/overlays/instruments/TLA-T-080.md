On 3 June 1816, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of one part of the Winnebago nation, those residing on the Ouisconsin (Wisconsin) River.

The preamble says these chiefs and warriors, and the portion of the nation they represent, had separated themselves from the rest of the nation, live in a village on the river, and wished to return to friendly relations with the United States. Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side and renews the friendly relations that existed before the late war.

In Article 2 they confirmed to the United States every land cession made by their nation to the British, French or Spanish government within the limits of the United States, and every treaty with the United States, as far as their interest in them extended. In Article 3 they acknowledged themselves under United States protection and that of no other power.

In Article 4 they promised to remain distinct and separate from the rest of their nation, giving it no aid, until peace was also concluded between the United States and the whole Winnebago nation. Article 5 had both sides deliver all prisoners to the officer commanding at Prairie du Chien. The page lists a proclamation on 30 December 1816.
