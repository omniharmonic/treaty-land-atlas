On June 4, 1816, Benjamin Parke, specially authorized by the President, made a treaty at Fort Harrison, Indiana Territory, with chiefs and head men of the Wea and Kickapoo. Article 1 had them again acknowledge peace and friendship. In Article 2 they "acknowledge the validity of, and declare their determination to adhere to, the treaty of Greenville," made in 1795, and all later treaties.

Article 3 had them "explicitly recognize and confirm" the boundary line surveyed and marked for the land on the Wabash and White rivers ceded in 1809. In Article 4 the Kickapoo acknowledged that they had ceded the tract between that line, the Wabash and the Vermilion River, under the 1809 treaty.

It confirms the 1809 cessions recorded in the Fort Wayne, Wea and Kickapoo treaties in this atlas. The sources I opened do not describe the council or give acreage.
