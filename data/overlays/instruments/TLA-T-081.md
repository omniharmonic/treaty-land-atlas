On June 4, 1816, Benjamin Parke, authorized by the President, signed a treaty at Fort Harrison, Indiana Territory, with the chiefs and head men of the Wea and Kickapoo. It added no new land. It confirmed what had been agreed in 1809.

In Article 1 the Wea and Kickapoo "again acknowledge themselves in peace and friendship with the United States." In Article 2 they "acknowledge the validity of, and declare their determination to adhere to, the treaty of Greenville," made in 1795, and all later treaties they had made with the United States.

Article 3 dealt with the line the United States had surveyed and marked for the land on the Wabash and White rivers ceded in 1809. The two tribes "explicitly recognize and confirm" it as "executed conformably to the several treaties they have made."

In Article 4 the Kickapoo chiefs and warriors acknowledged that they had ceded the tract between that line, the Wabash, the Vermilion River and a line meeting the Vermilion twenty miles from its mouth, under the terms of their treaty of December 9, 1809. The Wea and Kickapoo 1809 treaties are recorded separately. The text does not say why the confirmations were requested. It gives no acreage, and no source here describes the council.
