On August 24, 1816, three U.S. commissioners (Ninian Edwards, William Clark and Auguste Chouteau) signed a treaty of peace, friendship and limits with the united Ottawa, Chippewa and Potawatomi living on the Illinois and Milwaukee rivers and the southwestern shore of Lake Michigan.

The treaty's preamble says a "serious dispute" had existed over the right to part of the land the Sauk and Fox ceded to the United States in 1804. The tribes relinquished their claim to that cession south of a line running west from the southern tip of Lake Michigan. They also ceded a separate tract around the Chicago portage and the lower Illinois and Kankakee rivers. In return the United States delivered merchandise and promised goods worth $1,000 a year for twelve years.

The tribes could hunt and fish on the ceded land while it remained U.S. property. The United States gave up its own claim to the 1804 cession north of the line, apart from reserved tracts at the Wisconsin River mouth and elsewhere. The treaty text is the source used here; it does not describe later events.
