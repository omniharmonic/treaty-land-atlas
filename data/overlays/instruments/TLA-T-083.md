On September 14, 1816, Andrew Jackson, David Meriwether and Jesse Franklin signed a treaty with Cherokee delegates at the Chickasaw council house. It set a western boundary for the Cherokee Nation south of the Tennessee River and ran along the ridge between the Tennessee and Tombigbee waters toward the Coosa River.

The treaty text says its purpose was to perpetuate peace and "remove all future causes of dissension" from indefinite boundaries. The Cherokee relinquished all claim to land south and west of the line. In exchange the commissioners offered an annuity of $6,000 for ten years and $5,000 within sixty days as compensation for improvements on the surrendered land. The text also set a council at Turkey's Town and said that if the Cherokee did not assemble, the commissioners could report that as a tacit ratification.

A later section of the document records that the treaty was ratified at Turkey Town "by the whole Cherokee nation in council assembled" on October 4, 1816. The treaty text is the only source used here, so later events are not covered.
