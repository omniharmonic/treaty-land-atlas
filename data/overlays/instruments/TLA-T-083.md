On September 14, 1816, Andrew Jackson, David Meriwether and Jesse Franklin, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty with Cherokee delegates at the Chickasaw council house. The treaty says its aim was "to perpetuate peace and friendship" and "to remove all future causes of dissension which may arise from indefinite territorial boundaries." It would bind the parties once approved by the Cherokee nation and ratified by the United States.

In Article 2 the Cherokee nation acknowledged a western boundary south of the Tennessee River. It starts at Camp Coffee, opposite Chickasaw Island, runs south to the dividing ridge between the Tennessee and Tombigbee waters, then east along that ridge to Well's Creek and the Coosa River. In Article 3 the Cherokee relinquished all claim to lands south and west of the line. The commissioners agreed to an annuity of $6,000 for ten years and $5,000 within sixty days after ratification as compensation for improvements on the surrendered land.

Article 5 set a council at Turkey's Town on September 28. If the Cherokee did not assemble, the commissioners could report that as "a tacit ratification." A note at the end records that the treaty was "ratified at Turkey Town, by the whole Cherokee nation in council assembled," with a signing date of October 4, 1816.

The sources opened give no acreage and do not describe the council or later events.
