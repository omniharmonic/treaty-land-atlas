On September 20, 1816, Andrew Jackson, David Meriwether and Jesse Franklin signed a treaty with the Chickasaw Nation "in council assembled." The treaty text says it aimed "to settle all territorial controversies."

The Chickasaw ceded all right to land north of the Tennessee River. They also relinquished claim to land south of the river and east of a line running from Caney Creek to the Tombigbee River. In return the commissioners agreed to $12,000 a year for ten years and $4,500 for improvements on the surrendered land, paid to Levi Colbert. The treaty reserved several tracts for named Chickasaw individuals, including George Colbert and Appassan Tubby. It states these would revert to the United States if abandoned by the present owners or their heirs.

The treaty text is the only source used here, so it does not say what followed. Two years later, the Chickasaw signed a further treaty ceding western Tennessee and Kentucky (see TLA-T-105).
