On September 20, 1816, Andrew Jackson, David Meriwether and Jesse Franklin signed a treaty with "the whole Chickasaw nation, in council assembled." The treaty says its purpose was "to settle all territorial controversies" and to keep up the peace and harmony between the nations.

In Article 2 the Chickasaw ceded all right or title to lands on the north side of the Tennessee River. They also relinquished claim to territory on the south side, east of a line running from the mouth of Caney Creek up that creek, then south to Gaines's road and southwest along it to the Tombigbee River at the Cotton Gin Port.

In Article 3 the commissioners agreed to pay $12,000 a year for ten years and $4,500 within sixty days after ratification, paid to Levi Colbert, as compensation for improvements. In Article 4 the treaty reserved tracts for named individuals, including George Colbert and Appassan Tubby. They would remain Chickasaw land only while occupied or used by the present holders or their heirs. If abandoned, each tract would "revert to the United States."

The sources here give no acreage and do not describe the council. A further Chickasaw cession in 1818 has its own record in the atlas.
