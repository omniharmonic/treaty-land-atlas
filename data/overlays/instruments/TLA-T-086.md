On 30 March 1817, at St. Louis, William Clark, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty of peace and friendship for the United States with chiefs and warriors deputed by the Menomenee tribe or nation.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to place the Menominee on the same footing as before "the late war." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for their tribe, confirmed to the United States every cession of land earlier made by the tribe to the British, French or Spanish government within the limits of the United States, and every earlier treaty between the United States and the tribe. Article 4 had the parties deliver all prisoners to the officer commanding at Prairie du Chien, to be restored as soon as practicable. In Article 5 they acknowledged themselves to be under the protection of the United States and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The text names no acreage and no places for the earlier cessions. The page lists a proclamation on 26 December 1817.
