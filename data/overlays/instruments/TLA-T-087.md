On 24 June 1817, William Clark and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of the Ottoes tribe of Indians. The signature block lists Ottoes and Missouries.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship, and to put the tribe on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the Ottoes tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledged themselves to be under the protection of the United States of America and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The treaty contains no land cession, no boundary and no payment. The page lists a proclamation on 26 December 1817. The text does not explain why the Missouries appear in the signature block when the opening names only the Ottoes tribe, or what the signers expected from the protection clause.
