On 25 June 1817, William Clark and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of the Poncarar tribe of Indians. The text spells the name that way; the atlas record calls the tribe Ponca.

The preamble says both sides wanted to re-establish peace and friendship and to put the tribe on the same footing as before "the late war between the United States and Great Britain." Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe, and renews the friendly relations that existed before the war.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledged themselves to be under the protection of the United States of America and of no other nation, power or sovereign.

The treaty contains no land cession, no boundary and no payment. The Oklahoma State page header prints the proclamation date as 26 December 1877. The Statutes at Large prints the date as 26 December 1817, so 1877 is a typing error.
