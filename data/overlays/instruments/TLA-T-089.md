On July 8, 1817, Andrew Jackson, Joseph McMinn and David Meriwether signed a treaty at the Cherokee Agency with chiefs of the Cherokee Nation east of the Mississippi and of the Cherokees on the Arkansas River.

The treaty text recites a 1809 promise by the President that those who wished to move west could trade a share of their eastern country for land beyond the Mississippi, and says some Cherokees had already settled on the Arkansas and White rivers. The whole Cherokee Nation ceded lands north and east of described boundaries, beginning at the high shoals of the Appalachee River, "in exchange for other lands," as part of the share due to those on or moving to the Arkansas.

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says "increasing federal and state pressure on the remaining Cherokee led to the Turkeytown Treaty of July 8, 1817," and that the land in the east was exchanged for land in northwest Arkansas. A 1819 convention (TLA-T-106) followed to settle the treaty's terms.
