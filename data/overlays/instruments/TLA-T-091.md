On January 22, 1818, David Brydie Mitchell, U.S. agent for the Creeks, signed a "treaty of limits" with kings, chiefs, head men and warriors of the Creek Nation at the Creek Agency on the Flint River.

The Creeks ceded two tracts in what is now Georgia. The first ran from Goose Creek on the Altamaha River along a line to the Ocmulgee, and the second ran from the high shoals of the Appalachee toward the Chattahoochee. In return the United States agreed to pay $20,000 within the year and $10,000 annually for ten more years, a total the treaty gives as $120,000. It also agreed to supply two blacksmiths and strikers for three years.

The signers include William McIntosh. The treaty text is the only source used here, and the cession numbers in the input data (92 and 93) were not checked. Treaties of 1821, 1825 and 1826 with the Creeks appear elsewhere in this dataset.
