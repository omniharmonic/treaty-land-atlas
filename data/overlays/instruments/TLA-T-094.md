On 20 June 1818, at St. Louis, William Clark and Auguste Chouteau, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with the chiefs and warriors of the Pawnee Republic. It is one of four short Pawnee treaties from June 1818 in this set.

The preamble says the parties wanted to establish peace and friendship. Unlike many treaties from the years just before, it does not mention a war or put the tribe back on a former footing. Article 1 forgives every injury or act of hostility on either side. Article 2 declares perpetual peace and friendship between all citizens of the United States and all individuals of the tribe.

In Article 3 the chiefs and warriors, for themselves and their tribe, acknowledge themselves to be under the protection of the United States of America and of no other nation, power or sovereign. In Article 4 they promise to deliver up to United States authority, to be punished according to law, every individual of the tribe who later violates the treaty's terms.

The treaty cedes no land, sets no boundary and promises no payment. It was signed the day after the Noisy Pawnee treaty. The page lists a proclamation on 17 January 1819. The page prints the proclamation date as 17 January 1819, a different day from the other three; the cause is not explained.
