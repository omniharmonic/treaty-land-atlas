On August 24, 1818, William Clark and Auguste Chouteau signed a "treaty of friendship, cession, and limits" with chiefs and warriors of the Quapaw at St. Louis. The Quapaw acknowledged they were "under the protection of the United States, and of no other state, power, or sovereignty."

They ceded the land from the mouth of the Arkansas up to the Canadian fork, south to the Red River and back to the Mississippi, plus claims east of the Mississippi and north of the Arkansas. They reserved a tract between the Arkansas, Ouachita and Saline rivers. The United States promised $4,000 in goods at signing and $1,000 in goods yearly. Quapaw individuals could hunt on the ceded land.

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says the 1818 reservation was not enough for settlers and territorial officials, who "coveted valuable Quapaw land on the Arkansas River." It reports they pressured the Quapaw into the Treaty of 1824, which ceded the reservation (see TLA-T-123).
