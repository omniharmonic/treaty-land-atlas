On September 17, 1818, Lewis Cass and Duncan McArthur signed a supplementary treaty at St. Mary's, Ohio, with the Wyandot, Seneca, Shawnee and Ottawa. It built on the 1817 treaty made at the foot of the rapids of the Miami of Lake Erie.

Article 1 changed what the 1817 treaty had promised. That treaty had said the United States would grant land by patent to chiefs for their people. This one said those tracts "shall not be thus granted, but shall be excepted from the cession" and "held by them in the same manner as Indian reservations have been heretofore held." They were reserved for the Indians named in the schedule and held "by them and their heirs forever, unless ceded to the United States." A Myaamia Center blog says U.S. senators had objected to land granted in fee simple, and that this treaty offered a kind of renegotiation of how Native landholding was described.

The treaty also added reservations: 55,680 acres and 160 acres for the Wyandot, 16,000 acres for the Wyandot at Solomon's Town and Blanchard's Fork, 12,800 acres for the Shawnee, 8,960 acres for the Shawnee and Seneca at Lewistown, and 10,000 acres for the Seneca. Article 3 barred the named persons from conveying their granted tracts without the President's permission.

Article 4 added annuities forever: $500 to the Wyandot, $1,000 to the Shawnee and the Lewistown Seneca, $500 to the Seneca, and $1,500 to the Ottawa. The sources opened do not describe later removals.
