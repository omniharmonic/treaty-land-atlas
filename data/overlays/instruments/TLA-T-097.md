On September 17, 1818, Lewis Cass and Duncan McArthur signed a supplementary treaty at St. Mary's, Ohio, with the Wyandot, Seneca, Shawnee and Ottawa. It built on the 1817 treaty at the Rapids of the Miami of Lake Erie (see TLA-T-090).

The 1817 treaty had granted tracts to named chiefs for their people. This one provided that those tracts would be "excepted from the cession" and "held by them in the same manner as Indian reservations have been heretofore held." That meant they would be reserved for the Indians named in the schedule and held by their heirs forever, unless ceded to the United States.

The treaty added 55,680 acres for the Wyandots, 16,000 acres for Wyandots at Solomon's Town and Blanchard's Fork, 12,800 acres for the Shawnee, and 8,960 acres for the Shawnee and Senecas at Lewistown. It increased annuities to the Wyandots, Shawnee, Senecas and Ottawas. The treaty text is the only source used here; it does not describe later removals.
