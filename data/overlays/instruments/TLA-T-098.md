On September 20, 1818, Lewis Cass signed a treaty at St. Mary's, Ohio, with chiefs and warriors of the Wyandot tribe. It dealt with land in Michigan Territory.

The Wyandot ceded all the right reserved to them in two tracts, one containing the village of Brownstown and the other the village of Maguaga. The treaty says these held "not more than five thousand acres" and had been reserved for fifty years under an 1809 act of Congress. In exchange the United States agreed to reserve for the Wyandots sections on the south side of the Huron River, in the fourth township south of the base line, containing 4,996 acres.
