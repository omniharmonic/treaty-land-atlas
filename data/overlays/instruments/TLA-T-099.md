On September 25, 1818, Ninian Edwards and Auguste Chouteau signed a treaty at Edwardsville with chiefs and warriors of the Peoria, Kaskaskia, Mitchigamia, Cahokia and Tamarois tribes of the Illinois nation.

The preamble explains the reason. An 1803 treaty at Vincennes had ceded land that was "supposed to include all the land claimed" by those tribes. It did not include land rightfully claimed by the Peoria, who "were not represented" and never received any of the payment. The Peoria were now "disposed to cede all their land," and the parties agreed to confirm the 1803 cession and extend it to cover everyone's claims.

The ceded area runs from the junction of the Ohio and Mississippi rivers up the Ohio, across the divide between the Wabash and Kaskaskia waters, to the Kankakee and Maple rivers, then down the Illinois River to the Mississippi. The stipulations of the 1803 treaty remained binding. The treaty text is the only source used here, so its payment terms and later history are not summarized.
