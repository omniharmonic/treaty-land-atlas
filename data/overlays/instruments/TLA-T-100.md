On September 25, 1818, William Clark, governor of Missouri Territory, signed a treaty at St. Louis with a deputation of chiefs and warriors of the Great and Little Osage. The treaty text says the Osage had been "embarrassed by the frequent demands for property" taken from U.S. citizens by war parties, and that annuity deductions under the 1808 treaty would leave them without payments for years.

To settle this, the Osage ceded the tract from the Arkansas River at Frog Bayou, up the Arkansas and Verdigris to the falls of the Verdigris. The United States agreed to pay its own citizens the proven value of property stolen or destroyed by the Osage since 1814, up to $4,000.

The Kansas Historical Society's Kansapedia says the 1818 treaty was "a response to Euro-American and eastern tribes' intrusions into Osage lands" and led to cessions the United States used to push the Cherokee farther west. It lists the 1818 cession as lands between the White, Arkansas and Neosho-Grand rivers.
