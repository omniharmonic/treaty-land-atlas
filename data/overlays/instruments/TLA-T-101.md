On October 2, 1818, Jonathan Jennings, Lewis Cass and Benjamin Parke signed a treaty at St. Mary's, Ohio, with the Potawatomi nation. The treaty text records no preamble explaining why.

The Potawatomi ceded a country beginning at the mouth of the Tippecanoe River and running up that river, then roughly parallel to the Wabash to the Vermilion River, and down the Vermilion to its mouth. They also ceded all their claim to the country south of the Wabash. The United States agreed to buy any just Kickapoo claim to part of that country and to pay the Potawatomi a perpetual annuity of $2,500 in silver, half at Detroit and half at Chicago.

The treaty also promised land grants to persons named in a schedule, which could not be sold without the President's consent. It is one of several treaties made at St. Mary's in autumn 1818; the Indiana State Library describes that effort as part of purchasing land and moving Native people west of the Mississippi.
