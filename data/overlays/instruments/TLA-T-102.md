On October 2, 1818, Jonathan Jennings, Lewis Cass and Benjamin Parke signed articles with the Wea tribe at St. Mary's, Ohio.

In Article 1 the Wea agreed "to cede to the United State all the lands claimed and owned by the said tribe, within the limits of the states of Indiana, Ohio, and Illinois." In Article 2 they reserved a tract on the Wabash beginning at the mouth of Raccoon Creek, seven miles along the existing boundary line, to be held "as Indian reservations are usually held." Article 3 promised one section of land each to Christmas Dageny and Mary Shields, children of a sister of the Wea chief Jacco. They could not convey it without the President's consent.

In Article 4 the Wea sanctioned the Kickapoo cession made in the second article of the Kickapoo treaty of December 9, 1809. In Article 5 the United States agreed to pay $1,850 a year, added to a former annuity of $1,150, a total of $3,000 a year in silver, paid on the reservation.

A Myaamia Center blog says the Wea treaty was one of four linked negotiations at St. Mary's in autumn 1818, with the Delaware, Potawatomi and Miami.
