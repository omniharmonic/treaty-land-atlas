On October 2, 1818, Jonathan Jennings, Lewis Cass and Benjamin Parke signed articles with the Wea tribe at St. Mary's, Ohio.

The Wea agreed "to cede to the United State all the lands claimed and owned by the said tribe, within the limits of the states of Indiana, Ohio, and Illinois." They reserved one tract on the Wabash beginning at the mouth of Raccoon Creek, to be held "as Indian reservations are usually held." The treaty also promised one section of land each to Christmas Dageny and Mary Shields, children of a sister of the Wea chief Jacco, who could not convey it without the President's consent.

The Wea also sanctioned a 1809 Kickapoo cession. In return the United States agreed to pay $1,850 a year, added to a former annuity of $1,150, for a total of $3,000 in silver, paid on the reservation. The treaty text is the only source used here; no later history was checked.
