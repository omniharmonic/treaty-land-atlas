On October 6, 1818, Jonathan Jennings, Lewis Cass and Benjamin Parke, commissioners of the United States, signed a treaty at St. Mary's, Ohio, with the Miami nation.

The Miami ceded a tract that began at the Wabash River near Raccoon Creek, ran up the Wabash to the reserve near Fort Wayne, and then along the St. Mary's River and other lines to Fort Recovery. They kept several reservations, including one along the Wabash from the Salamonie River to the Eel River. The United States agreed to patent tracts to Jean Baptiste Richardville, principal chief, and to pay a perpetual annuity of $15,000 in silver. It promised a grist mill and saw mill, a blacksmith and gunsmith, farm implements and 160 bushels of salt a year.

A Myaamia Center blog quotes U.S. Indian Department letters from 1817 and 1818 about the plan. An agent suggested Richardville might be offered "a pension, cash in hand, or land reserved for him." The blog says the treaty followed the 1817 Maumee Rapids treaty, which Miami leaders had not signed, and that refusing to sell "simply meant losing the land for nothing."
