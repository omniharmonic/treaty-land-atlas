On October 19, 1818, Isaac Shelby and Andrew Jackson signed a treaty with the whole Chickasaw nation in council. The Chickasaw ceded all claim to the land north of the Tennessee state line, bounded by the Tennessee River, the 35th parallel, the Mississippi and the Ohio. The United States agreed to pay $20,000 a year for fifteen years. At the chiefs' request it also agreed to pay specific debts: $1,115 owed by General William Colbert to a Tennessee captain, and $2,000 owed to a Kentucky captain who had supplied soldiers in 1814.

The Tennessee Encyclopedia says Jackson and Shelby "argued that the land warrants prevented federal action to deter settlement," that the state held many North Carolina land warrants to honor, and that "the Chickasaws agreed to sell the tract for three hundred thousand dollars."

It says the area opened to settlement in 1819, speculators including Jackson founded Memphis, and within six years the purchase held sixteen counties.
