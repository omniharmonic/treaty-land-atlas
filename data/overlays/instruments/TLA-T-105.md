On October 19, 1818, Isaac Shelby and Andrew Jackson, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty with "the whole Chickesaw nation, by their chiefs, head men, and warriors, in full council assembled." The treaty says its aim was "to settle all territorial controversies" and to head off complaints that could disturb the peace.

In Article 2 the Chickasaw ceded "all claim or title" to land north of the Tennessee state line at 35 degrees north latitude. It runs from the Tennessee River west to the Mississippi near the Chickasaw Bluffs, up the Mississippi to the Ohio, and up the Ohio and Tennessee rivers. The United States agreed in Article 3 to pay $20,000 a year for fifteen years. At the chiefs' request it also agreed to pay two debts: $1,115 owed by General William Colbert to a Tennessee captain, and $2,000 owed to a Kentucky captain who supplied soldiers in 1795. Article 4 reserved a tract four miles square around a salt lick for the benefit of "the poor and warriors" of the nation.

The Tennessee Encyclopedia says the Chickasaw had long occupied the tract. It says Tennessee had "a flood of North Carolina land warrants to honor," and that Jackson and Shelby argued the warrants "prevented federal action to deter settlement." It adds that "the Chickasaws agreed to sell the tract for three hundred thousand dollars." The region opened to settlement in 1819, speculators including Jackson founded Memphis, and within six years the Jackson Purchase held sixteen counties.
