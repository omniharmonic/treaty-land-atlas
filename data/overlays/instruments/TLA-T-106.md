On February 27, 1819, Secretary of War John C. Calhoun signed a convention in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Cherokee Nation, "duly authorized and empowered by said nation."

The preamble says "a greater part of the Cherokee nation have expressed an earnest desire to remain on this side of the Mississippi." They wanted the 1817 treaty "finally adjusted" without the delay, trouble or expense of the census it required. The Cherokee therefore offered to cede a tract "at least as extensive as that which they probably are entitled to" under the 1817 treaty. The text says the land ceded was in full satisfaction of all U.S. claims arising from the cession to Cherokee who had gone, or would go, to the Arkansas.

Article 1 ceded all Cherokee lands north and east of a line. It starts on the Tennessee River at the boundary of Madison County, Alabama Territory, follows the Hiwassee and the Blue Ridge, and ends at the Chestatee and Chattahoochee rivers and the Creek boundary. A tract twelve miles square was ceded to the United States in trust, to be sold for a Cherokee school fund. Article 2 promised pay for improvements and a 640-acre reservation to each head of a Cherokee family in the ceded land who chose to become a citizen of the United States. Article 3 granted 640-acre fee-simple reservations to persons on an annexed list.

Article 6 divided the annuity two-thirds to the Cherokee east of the Mississippi and one-third to those west of it.
