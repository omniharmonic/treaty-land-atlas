On February 27, 1819, Secretary of War John C. Calhoun signed a convention in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Cherokee Nation. Its preamble says "a greater part of the Cherokee nation have expressed an earnest desire to remain on this side of the Mississippi" and wanted the 1817 treaty "finally adjusted" without the trouble of a census.

The Cherokee offered to cede a tract at least as large as the one they probably owed under the 1817 treaty. They ceded all lands north and east of a line that starts on the Tennessee River at the Alabama Territory boundary, follows the Hiwassee and the Blue Ridge, and ends at the Chestatee and Chattahoochee rivers and the Creek boundary.

The United States agreed to pay for improvements and to allow a reservation of 640 acres to each head of a family who chose to become a citizen. It also made 640-acre fee-simple reservations to persons on an annexed list, and ceded a twelve-mile-square tract in trust as a Cherokee school fund. The treaty text is the only source used here.
