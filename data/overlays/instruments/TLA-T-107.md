On July 30, 1819, Auguste Chouteau and Benjamin Stephenson, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty at Edwardsville, Illinois, with the principal chiefs and warriors of the Kickapoo tribe.

In Article 1 the Kickapoo ceded "all their land on the southeast side of the Wabash river, including the principal village in which their ancestors formerly resided." They said they had never ceded it before. They also ceded a second tract bounded by the Wabash, the Illinois-Indiana line, the Kankakee River, the Illinois River and the Vincennes tract. The treaty says they claimed a large part of it "by descent from their ancestors" and the rest "by conquest from the Illinois nation, and uninterrupted possession for more than half a century." In Article 3 they promised to remain under U.S. protection "and of no other nation."

In return the United States paid $3,000 in merchandise and ceded them a tract in Missouri Territory beginning at the junction of the Pommes de Terre and Osage rivers. They could never sell it without the President's consent. It also promised $2,000 a year in silver for fifteen years, paid at their town on the Osage River. In Article 7 the United States promised to guarantee them peaceable possession and to keep white people from hunting, settling or intruding. Article 8 provided two boats to carry their property from the Illinois River.

The sources here do not describe the council, give an acreage, or show whether the guarantee held. The OSU record shows the treaty proclaimed on January 13, 1821.
