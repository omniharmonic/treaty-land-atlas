On July 30, 1819, Auguste Chouteau and Benjamin Stephenson signed a treaty at Edwardsville, Illinois, with the principal chiefs and warriors of the Kickapoo tribe.

The Kickapoo ceded all their land southeast of the Wabash River, including the village where their ancestors had lived, and a second tract bounded by the Wabash, the Illinois-Indiana line, the Kankakee and the Illinois rivers. The treaty states they claimed much of this by descent and the rest "by conquest from the Illinois nation." The United States paid $3,000 in merchandise and granted them a tract in Missouri Territory at the junction of the Pomme de Terre and Osage rivers, which they could not sell without the President's consent. It promised $2,000 a year in silver for fifteen years and two boats to carry their property.

The Kickapoo also acknowledged the protection of the United States "and of no other nation." A supplement in 1820 (TLA-T-112) amended the Missouri land article. The treaty text is the only source used here.
