On August 30, 1819, Benjamin Parke signed a treaty at Fort Harrison, Indiana, with chiefs, warriors and head men of the Kickapoos of the Vermilion.

The Kickapoo agreed to cede "all the lands which the said tribe has heretofore possessed, or which they may rightfully claim, on the Wabash river, or any of its waters." Article 2 describes their claimed boundary, running along the Vincennes tract line, ridges toward the Vermilion River, Pine Creek and the Wabash. The tribe gave up an existing $1,000 annuity. In return the United States agreed to pay $2,000 a year in specie for ten years and paid $3,000 on the spot, which the treaty treats as full compensation.

Article 4 says: "As the said tribe contemplate removing from the country they now occupy," the annuity would be paid at a place to be agreed. The 1820 convention that appears on the same Statutes at Large pages shows an annuity moved to Kaskaskia and $2,000 advanced to help them leave the Wabash. The treaty text is the main source.
