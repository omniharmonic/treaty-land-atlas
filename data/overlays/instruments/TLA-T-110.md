On June 16, 1820, Lewis Cass, as commissioner, signed a treaty at Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan Territory, with the Chippeway tribe. Henry R. Schoolcraft, "mineralogist to the expedition," is among the witnesses.

The Chippewa ceded a tract beginning at the Big Rock in the St. Mary's River, on the boundary with British Upper Canada. It ran down the river to the Little Rapid and back from the river far enough "so as to include sixteen square miles of land." The tribe acknowledged receiving a quantity of goods "in full satisfaction" for the cession.

The United States promised to secure to the Indians a perpetual right of fishing at the falls of St. Mary's and a place of encampment convenient to the fishing ground, on the ceded tract. The encampment could not interfere with any military work or private rights. The treaty text is the only source used here, and the treaty does not describe what followed.
