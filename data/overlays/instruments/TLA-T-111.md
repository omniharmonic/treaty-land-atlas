On July 6, 1820, Lewis Cass, commissioner for the United States, signed articles of a treaty at L'Arbre Croche and Michilimackinac, Michigan Territory, with the Ottawa and Chippewa nations.

The treaty is brief. The Ottawa and Chippewa ceded the Saint Martin Islands in Lake Huron, described as "containing plaster of Paris, and to be located under the direction of the United States." They acknowledged receiving a quantity of goods "in full satisfaction of the above cession." The goods are not itemized or valued in the text.

The treaty became binding after ratification by the President and Senate, and was proclaimed March 8, 1821. The text names Ottawa and Chippewa chiefs among the signers. It is the only source used here, so it does not explain why the United States wanted these islands beyond the mention of plaster of Paris, nor does it describe what followed.
