On July 19, 1820, Auguste Chouteau and Benjamin Stephenson signed a treaty at St. Louis with chiefs and warriors of the Kickapoo. It is "supplementary to, and amendatory of" the Edwardsville treaty of July 30, 1819 (see TLA-T-107).

The only change is to the sixth article. The United States, "in addition to three thousand dollars worth of merchandise" paid that day, ceded to the Kickapoo a tract in Missouri Territory. It begins at the junction of the Pomme de Terre and Osage rivers, follows the dividing ridge between the Osage and White river waters, and runs to the Osage line and Nerve Creek. The new wording says the land would be possessed "in like manner" as the lands the Kickapoo ceded in Illinois and Indiana.

The 1819 article had barred the Kickapoo from selling the land without the President's consent; the amended text quoted here does not repeat that proviso. The treaty text is the only source used here.
