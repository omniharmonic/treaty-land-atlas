On August 11, 1820, Benjamin Parke signed a treaty at Vincennes with chiefs, warriors and head men of the Wea tribe.

The Wea ceded and relinquished "all the lands reserved by the second article" of their October 2, 1818 treaty at St. Mary's. That was the reservation on the Wabash they had kept when they ceded their other claims in Indiana, Ohio and Illinois (see TLA-T-102). The United States paid $5,000 in money and goods, which the parties considered "a full compensation."

Article 3 says "it is contemplated by the said Tribe, to remove from the Wabash," so the annuity from the 1818 treaty would be paid to them at Kaskaskia in Illinois. The treaty text is the only source used here; where the Wea went afterward is not covered by it.
