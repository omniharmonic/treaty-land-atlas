On 5 September 1820, at Vincennes, Benjamin Parke, commissioner for the United States, made a short convention with the chiefs, warriors and head men of the Tribe of Kickapoos of the Vermilion. It changes how an earlier treaty was carried out. It does not cede land.

In Article 1 the parties agreed that the annuity secured to the tribe by the treaty of 30 August 1819 would hereafter be paid at Kaskaskia, in the state of Illinois.

The preamble to Article 2 says the tribe was about leaving its settlements on the Wabash and had asked for help to remove. Parke, for the United States, paid and advanced the tribe two thousand dollars, and the receipt is acknowledged in the text. That sum was to be an equivalent, in full, for the annuity due to the tribe under the 1819 treaty for the year 1821.

So the practical effect, according to the text, was that the tribe received two thousand dollars in 1820 in place of its 1821 annuity, and later annuities would be paid at Kaskaskia. The page lists a proclamation on 8 January 1821.

The 1819 treaty was not opened, so its terms, including the size of the annuity and the land it concerned, are not described here.
