On October 18, 1820, Andrew Jackson and Thomas Hinds signed a treaty "of friendship, limits, and accommodation" with the Choctaw Nation near Doak's Stand on the Natchez Road. The preamble says the President wished to promote Choctaw "civilization" through schools and to exchange "a small part of their land here" for country beyond the Mississippi. It also says the state of Mississippi wanted part of Choctaw land.

The Choctaw ceded a tract in Mississippi. The United States ceded to the Choctaw a tract between the Arkansas and Red rivers. Individual warriors who chose to move west were promised a blanket, kettle, rifle, ammunition and corn.

The Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History says the treaty ceded cotton lands east of the Mississippi "for approximately thirteen million acres" in southeastern Oklahoma, and that most families did not move until the 1830 Dancing Rabbit Creek treaty. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says the 1825 Choctaw treaty later set the Choctaw line near Fort Smith.
