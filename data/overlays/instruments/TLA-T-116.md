On January 8, 1821, Daniel Forney and David Meriwether, for the United States, signed a treaty at Indian Spring with chiefs, head men and warriors of the Creek Nation. The Creeks ceded land east of the Flint River, running up the Flint and then to the Chattahoochee, but kept 1,000 acres around the Indian Spring and 640 acres on the Ocmulgee that included General McIntosh's improvements. They also kept several small tracts while the occupants stayed, and a tract for the United States agency on the Flint.

The United States agreed to pay $10,000 at signing, $40,000 after ratification and further annual payments over fourteen years. As a further payment, it agreed to pay Georgia whatever balance was due from the Creeks to Georgia citizens, up to $250,000. Georgia would release the Creeks from claims for property taken or destroyed before 1802.

The treaty text is the only source used here. The Creeks ceded more Georgia land in a separate 1825 treaty that was later annulled (see TLA-T-125).
