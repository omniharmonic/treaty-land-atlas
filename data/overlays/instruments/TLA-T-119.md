On 3 September 1822, at Fort Armstrong, Thomas Forsyth, agent of Indian affairs, made an agreement with the chiefs, warriors and head men of the United Sac and Fox tribes, acting for themselves and their tribes.

It deals with a single promise from the treaty signed at Saint Louis on 3 November 1804. The ninth article of that treaty says the United States would, at a convenient time, establish a trading house or factory where members of the tribes could be supplied with goods at a more reasonable rate than they had been accustomed to. The stated aim was to stop the abuses and impositions practised on the tribes by private traders.

In 1822 the chiefs, warriors and head men accepted one thousand dollars, paid in merchandise from the United States' factory. In exchange they exonerated, released and forever discharged the United States from that obligation. The text says the ninth article is, from the date of the agreement, "abrogated and of no effect."

The paper cedes no land. The page lists a proclamation on 13 February 1823. It does not say why the United States was released from the promise, and it does not record the Sac and Fox view of the exchange.
