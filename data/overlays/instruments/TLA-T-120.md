On September 18, 1823, chiefs and warriors of the Florida tribes signed a treaty with United States commissioners near St. Augustine. Article I says they "appealed to the humanity" of the United States and promised to stay under its protection. In return they ceded "all claim or title" to the whole territory of Florida except a district allotted to them.

Article II says the tribes "will hereafter be concentrated and confined" within described boundaries, and keeps the line at least fifteen miles from the Gulf coast. The United States promised to protect them, to supply implements, stock and $6,000, and $5,000 a year for twenty years, plus rations for twelve months and $4,500 for abandoned improvements.

A Florida historical marker says the tribes were to occupy a four million acre reservation of the interior peninsula, and that "failure on both sides to comply with the terms of this and later treaties" led to the Second Seminole War (1835-1842). The marker says the war decimated the Seminole and Miccosukee and led to the surrender of most survivors for transportation west.
