On September 18, 1823, chiefs and warriors of the Florida tribes signed a treaty with the United States. A Florida historical marker says the leaders of the Seminole and Miccosukee met government officials on the banks of Moultrie Creek, near St. Augustine, and signed after twelve days of negotiation.

In Article 1 the chiefs and warriors wrote that they "have appealed to the humanity" of the United States and promised to stay under its protection and no other power's. They ceded "all claim or title" to the whole territory of Florida except a district assigned to them. Article 2 says the tribes "will hereafter be concentrated and confined" within set boundaries.

The United States promised protection, $6,000 in tools and livestock, and $5,000 a year for twenty years. It promised twelve months of rations, $4,500 for those forced to abandon improvements, and $1,000 a year for twenty years for a school. Article 9 records that the chiefs objected that the land held too little good soil. It says the north line would move north if officials judged the land insufficient.

The marker says the tribes were to occupy a four million acre reservation, and that "failure on both sides to comply with the terms of this and later treaties" led to the Second Seminole War (1835-1842). It says the war decimated both tribes and led most survivors to surrender for transportation west.
