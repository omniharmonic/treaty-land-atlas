On August 4, 1824, William Clark signed a treaty in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Sauk and Fox tribes. The preamble states the aim to "perpetuate peace and friendship" and remove causes of dissension from undefined boundaries.

The Sauk and Fox ceded all their claims within Missouri, between the Mississippi and Missouri rivers and a line running from the Kansas River north one hundred miles and east to the Mississippi. A small tract between the Des Moines and Mississippi rivers was set aside for the "half-breeds" of the two nations. They acknowledged the lands east and south of the lines belonged to the United States and agreed not to hunt or settle there after January 1, 1826. In return they were to receive $1,000, $500 a year to each tribe for ten years, a blacksmith and farm help.

The Iowa History Daily page says Congress repealed the "Half-Breed Tract" provision in 1834, after the Black Hawk War and the Black Hawk Purchase.
