On August 4, 1824, William Clark signed a treaty in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Ioway tribe, including deputies Ma-hos-kah (White Cloud) and Mah-ne-hah-nah (Great Walker).

The Ioway ceded all lands they claimed within Missouri, between the Mississippi and Missouri rivers and a line running north from the Kansas River to the northwest corner of the state and east to the Mississippi. In return the United States paid $500 in cash or merchandise that year and $500 a year for ten years, plus a blacksmith, farming tools and cattle.

The chiefs acknowledged that the lands east and south of the described lines belonged to the United States and that none of their tribe would settle or hunt on them after January 1, 1826. They also acknowledged being "under the protection of the United States" and agreed not to hold treaties with foreign powers, states or individuals. The Iowa History Daily page says the Iowa leader Mahaska visited Washington that day and that the visit produced "the first cession of lands in Iowa" by treaty.
