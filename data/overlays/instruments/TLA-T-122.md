On August 4, 1824, William Clark, Superintendent of Indian Affairs, signed a treaty in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Ioway Tribe. The Ioway deputies named in the treaty are Ma-hos-kah (or White Cloud) and Mah-ne-hah-nah (or Great Walker).

The Ioway ceded all claims to land within the State of Missouri lying between the Mississippi and Missouri rivers. The boundary ran from the mouth of the Kansas River north one hundred miles to the northwest corner of Missouri, then east to the Mississippi. In return, the United States agreed to pay $500 in cash or merchandise that year and $500 a year for ten more years. It also promised a blacksmith, farming tools, cattle and help with farming.

In Article 3 the chiefs acknowledged that the lands east and south of those lines belonged to the United States, and that none of their tribe would settle or hunt there after January 1, 1826, without special permission. In Article 4 they acknowledged the Ioway to be "under the protection of the United States of America, and of no other sovereign whatsoever," and agreed not to treat with foreign powers or individual states.

The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on January 18, 1825. The treaty does not state the acreage involved.
