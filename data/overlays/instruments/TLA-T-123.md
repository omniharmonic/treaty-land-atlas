On November 15, 1824, the Quapaw Nation signed a treaty with the United States in Arkansas Territory. The Quapaw ceded all claim to the land in the reservation set in 1818 (see TLA-T-096), between the Arkansas, Ouachita and Saline rivers.

The treaty says the Quapaw "will hereafter be concentrated and confined to the district of country inhabited by the Caddo Indians, and form a part of said Tribe," and must start moving before January 20, 1826. The United States agreed to pay $500 to each of four head chiefs for losses from leaving their farms, $4,000 in goods, and $1,000 a year for eleven years, and to supply food and transport money. It reserved two sections for James Scull to settle a $7,500 debt the Quapaw owed him.

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says settlers and territorial officials "put pressure on the Quapaw, who signed the Treaty of 1824," and that the move to Caddo country in early 1826 "met with disaster."
