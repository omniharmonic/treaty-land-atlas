On November 15, 1824, the Quapaw Nation signed a treaty at Harrington's, in the Territory of Arkansas, with Commissioner Robert Crittenden for the United States. The Quapaw were recorded as ceding "all claim or title" to lands in Arkansas territory. The boundary ran from the Arkansas River opposite the Post of Arkansas southwest to the Ouachita, up the Ouachita to the Saline Fork, and back to the Arkansas at Little Rock. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says settlers and territorial officials who wanted valuable Quapaw land on the Arkansas River "put pressure on the Quapaw."

Article 4 says the Quapaw "will hereafter be concentrated and confined to the district of country inhabited by the Caddo Indians, and form a part of said Tribe." They were to start moving before January 20, 1826. The United States promised $500 to each of four head chiefs for the losses of leaving their farms, $4,000 in goods, $1,000 a year for eleven years, and corn, meat and salt for six months. Two sections were reserved to James Scull against a $7,500 debt the Quapaw owed him.

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says the Quapaw moved to Caddo country in early 1826 "but met with disaster." Flooding destroyed crops, and starvation killed sixty people. It says "by 1830, all of the Quapaw had returned to Arkansas."
