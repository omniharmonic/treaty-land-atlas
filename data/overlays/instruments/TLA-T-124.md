On January 20, 1825, Secretary of War John C. Calhoun signed a convention in Washington with Choctaw chiefs and head men. It amended the 1820 Treaty of Doak's Stand (see TLA-T-115). The preamble says the land granted west of the Mississippi in 1820 "embraces a large number of settlers, citizens of the United States," and that the President wished to obviate the resulting difficulties.

The Choctaw ceded the part of that western land east of a line running south from a point one hundred paces east of Fort Smith on the Arkansas River to the Red River. The line was to be the permanent boundary, and the United States agreed to remove settlers on the west side. In return the United States agreed to pay $6,000 a year forever, applied to schools for twenty years, and $6,000 a year for sixteen years in place of land sales.

The Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History says funding for Choctaw schools later "came from annuity payments for land ceded in an 1825 treaty."
