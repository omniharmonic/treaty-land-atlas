On June 2, 1825, William Clark signed a treaty at St. Louis with chiefs, head men and warriors of the Great and Little Osage. The Osage ceded all their claims to lands within Missouri and Arkansas Territory and to lands west of them, north and west of the Red River and south of the Kansas River, east of a line drawn from the head of the Kansas through the Rock Saline.

The treaty reserved a tract for the Osage "so long as they may choose to occupy the same," beginning twenty-five miles west of the Missouri line and fifty miles wide. The United States agreed to pay $7,000 a year for twenty years and supply 600 cattle, 600 hogs, poultry, oxen, carts, tools and a blacksmith, and to build a house for each of four principal chiefs. It made reservations for people of mixed ancestry.

Kansapedia says the first 1825 treaty "led to Osage land cessions in present-day Oklahoma" and that, over 31 years, Osage territory diminished "by more than 75 percent."
