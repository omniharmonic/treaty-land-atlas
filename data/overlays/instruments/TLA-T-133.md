On July 18, 1825, Brigadier General Henry Atkinson and Major Benjamin O'Fallon, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty with chiefs and warriors of the Ricara (Arikara) Tribe. The Statutes at Large spell the name "Ricara."

The opening says the aim was "to put an end to an unprovoked hostility on the part of the Ricara Tribe of Indians against the United States, and to restore harmony." The commissioners agreed to give peace to the tribe, "the Chiefs and Warriors thereof having first made suitable concessions for the offence." The text does not say what the offence was.

Article 1 promises "a firm and lasting peace." In Article 2 the tribe admitted that it resides within the territorial limits of the United States, acknowledged its supremacy, and claimed its protection. It also admitted the right of the United States to regulate trade and intercourse with it. In Article 3 the United States agreed to receive the tribe into its friendship and protection. Trade was to take place at places the President designated, and only with licensed American citizens. The tribe promised to protect traders and to hand over unlicensed foreigners.

The treaty contains no land cession and states no acreage. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation date of February 1826 (the OCR is partly garbled).
