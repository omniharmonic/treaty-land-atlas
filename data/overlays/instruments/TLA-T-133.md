On July 18, 1825, Brigadier General Henry Atkinson and Indian agent Benjamin O'Fallon signed a treaty with chiefs and warriors of the Ricara (Arikara) tribe. It was one of a series of 1825 treaties made along the upper Missouri River.

The preamble states the aim of putting an end to "unprovoked hostility" by the Ricara against the United States and restoring harmony. Article 1 sets "a firm and lasting peace." In Article 2 the tribe admitted that it resides within the United States and acknowledged U.S. supremacy and the right to regulate trade and intercourse. Article 3 took the tribe under the protection of the United States.

Later articles limited trade to places named by the President and to authorized American citizens. The treaty contains no land cession and states no acreage. The treaty text is the only source used here; the 1823 conflict that the preamble alludes to is not described in it.
