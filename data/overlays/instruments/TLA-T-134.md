On 30 July 1825, at the Lower Mandan Village, Brigadier General Henry Atkinson and Major Benjamin O'Fallon, Indian agent, signed a treaty for the United States with the chiefs and warriors of the Belantse-etea or Minnetaree tribe.

The text opens with a complaint: acts of hostility had been committed against United States citizens by some restless men of the tribe. Article 1 therefore promises a firm and lasting peace and a friendly intercourse. In Article 2 of the treaty, the Belantse-etea or Minnetaree tribe admit that they live within the territorial limits of the United States, acknowledge its supremacy, and claim its protection, and they admit the right of the United States to regulate all trade and intercourse with them. In return the United States agrees to receive them into its friendship and under its protection, and to extend such benefits and acts of kindness as the President thinks convenient, just and proper.

Trade is to happen only at places the President designates, with traders the United States licenses. For injuries by individuals there is to be no private revenge; chiefs must deliver accused persons to be punished under United States law. The tribe also promises never to supply guns or ammunition to any nation not in amity with the United States.

The treaty cedes no land. The page lists a proclamation on 6 February 1826.
