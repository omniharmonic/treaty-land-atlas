On August 19, 1825, William Clark and Lewis Cass signed a treaty at Prairie du Chien with the Sioux, Chippewa, Sauk and Fox, Menominee, Iowa, Winnebago and part of the Ottawa, Chippewa and Potawatomi.

The treaty text says the United States regretted that wars had long been carried on between the Sioux and Chippewas and others, and invited the tribes "to promote peace among these tribes, and to establish boundaries among them and the other tribes who live in their vicinity." Article 1 declares perpetual peace and Article 2 sets the line between the Sauk and Fox and the Sioux. Other articles fix further lines.

The Treaties Matter website says the Native delegates pointed out that the game they hunted did not recognize boundaries. It says the treaty was a compromise, and one effect was to "clear the title" to lands, "making it easier to purchase specific tracts" from single nations. It adds that within three years the United States was purchasing land inside the 1825 boundaries.
