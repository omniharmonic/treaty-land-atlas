On November 7, 1825, William Clark signed a convention at St. Louis with chiefs of the Shawnee Nation residing in Missouri. The preamble says the Shawnee had held land near Cape Girardeau under permission from the Spanish government in 1793. It says the Delawares abandoned it in 1815, and that the Shawnee "did remove" under an assurance of other lands, after which U.S. citizens took over their improvements.

The Shawnee ceded their claim to the land between the St. Come River and Cape Girardeau. In exchange the United States agreed to give a tract west of Missouri, 25 miles by 100 miles, within the land lately purchased from the Osage (see TLA-T-126), also for Shawnee in Ohio who might move west. It also agreed to pay $14,000 for losses and removal costs.

The Kansas Historical Society's Kansapedia says that in 1825 the Shawnee near Cape Girardeau "were removed from their homes by the United States government," that relocation began in 1826, and that the reservation was 1.6 million acres.
