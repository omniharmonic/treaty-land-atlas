On November 7, 1825, William Clark, Superintendent of Indian Affairs, signed a convention at St. Louis with chiefs and head men of the Shawnee Nation residing in Missouri. The preamble tells a history. The Shawnee had settled near Cape Girardeau under a permission from the Spanish government, given to the Shawnees and Delawares on January 4, 1793. The Delawares abandoned the tract in 1815. The Shawnees, "under an assurance of receiving other lands in exchange, did remove," after making valuable improvements that "were taken possession of by the citizens of the United States."

In Article 1 the Shawnee ceded their claim to the land between the River St. Come and Cape Girardeau. In Article 2 the United States agreed to give them, and Shawnee in Ohio who might move west, a tract west of Missouri within the land bought from the Osage in June 1825. The text calls it "fifty (50) miles square," but its boundaries are 25 miles by 100 miles. The United States also agreed to pay $14,000 for losses and the costs of removal.

The Kansas Historical Society says the Shawnee near Cape Girardeau "were removed from their homes by the United States government" and given land south of the Kansas River. It says relocation began in 1826 and the reservation covered 1.6 million acres.
