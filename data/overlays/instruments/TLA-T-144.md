On January 24, 1826, Secretary of War James Barbour signed a treaty in Washington with chiefs and head men of the Creek Nation. Its preamble says "a great majority of the Chiefs and Warriors" had protested the Indian Springs treaty of February 12, 1825, and that it was signed by persons having "no sufficient authority to form treaties, or to make cessions."

Article 1 declares the 1825 treaty "null and void." In Article 2 the Creeks ceded all their land in Georgia east of the middle of the Chattahoochee River and a second tract on the river's western bank. The United States agreed to pay $217,600, to be divided among the chiefs and warriors, and a perpetual annuity of $20,000. A supplemental article of March 31, 1826 adjusted the lines and added $30,000.

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says federal investigators agreed with the Creek complaint, but "the Creeks did not, however, have their territory restored in the new treaty."
