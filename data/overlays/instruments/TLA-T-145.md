On August 5, 1826, Lewis Cass and Thomas L. McKenney signed a treaty at Fond du Lac of Lake Superior with the Chippewa tribe. The preamble explains that, because of the "remote and dispersed situation" of the Chippewa, full deputations of their bands had not attended Prairie du Chien in 1825, which would leave that treaty of "doubtful obligation" for bands not represented.

The Chippewa assented to the 1825 treaty and agreed to send a deputation to Green Bay in 1827 to fix the line with the Winnebago and Menominee. Article 3 grants the United States "the right to search for, and carry away, any metals or minerals from any part" of their country, but "this grant is not to affect the title of the land, nor the existing jurisdiction over it."

The treaty also granted 640 acres to each listed person of Chippewa descent, on the St. Mary's River, and set annuities. The treaty text is the only source used here.
