On October 23, 1826, Lewis Cass, James B. Ray and John Tipton signed a treaty near the mouth of the Mississinewa River in Indiana with the chiefs and warriors of the Miami Tribe.

In Article 1 the Miami were recorded as ceding "all their claim to land in the State of Indiana, north and west of the Wabash and Miami rivers," along with the cession of the St. Mary's treaty of October 6, 1818. Article 2 set aside reservations for the tribe, including fourteen sections at Seek's village and thirty-six sections at Flat Belly's village. The State of Indiana could lay out a canal or road through any of them. Article 3 granted tracts to persons named in a schedule, who could not convey them without the President's consent.

The commissioners had delivered goods worth $31,040.53 and promised $26,259.47 more. Article 4 says "payment for these goods shall be made by the Miami tribe out of their annuity, if this treaty be not ratified by the United States."

The United States promised an annuity of $35,000 in 1827 and $30,000 in 1828. After that, a permanent annuity of $25,000 would be paid "as long as they exist together as a tribe." It also promised wagons, oxen, houses, cattle, hogs and iron. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on January 24, 1827.
