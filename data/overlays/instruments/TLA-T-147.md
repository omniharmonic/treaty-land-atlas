On October 23, 1826, Lewis Cass, James B. Ray and John Tipton signed a treaty near the mouth of the Mississinewa River, Indiana, with chiefs and warriors of the Miami tribe.

The Miami ceded "all their claim to land in the State of Indiana, north and west of the Wabash and Miami rivers" and of the 1818 cession. The treaty reserved several tracts for the tribe, including fourteen sections at Seek's village and thirty-six sections at Flat Belly's village. It allowed the State of Indiana to lay out a canal or road through the reservations.

The commissioners had delivered goods worth $31,040.53 to the Miami, with $26,259.47 more promised. The treaty says that if it was not ratified, "payment for these goods shall be made by the Miami tribe out of their annuity." It also set annuities of $35,000 in 1827 and $30,000 in 1828 and then a permanent annuity. The treaty text is the only source used here, so the full terms are not summarized.
