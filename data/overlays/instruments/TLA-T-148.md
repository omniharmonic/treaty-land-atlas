On August 11, 1827, Lewis Cass and Thomas L. McKenney signed a treaty at Butte des Morts, on the Fox River in the Territory of Michigan. The other parties were the Chippewa, Menomonie and Winebago tribes.

Article 1 says the southern boundary of the Chippewa country "was left undefined by the treaty concluded at Prairie du Chien" in 1825, because some principal Menomonie chiefs had not attended. The tribes now agreed a line running northeast from the Plover Portage of the Ouisconsin River to Wolf River, and on to Green Bay.

Article 2 records "much difficulty" over the dealings of the Menomonie and Winebago with the tribes of New York. It refers the whole matter to the President, whose decision "shall be final." Article 3 has the Menomonie and the United States agree on the boundaries of a tract at Green Bay, "the jurisdiction and title of which are hereby acknowledged to be in the United States." The President could adjust the lines if they interfered with just claims of the New York Indians.

The commissioners distributed goods worth $15,682 "in consideration of the liberal establishment of the boundaries." Article 6 reserved the right of the United States to punish those responsible for "the recent outrages at Prairie du Chien, and upon the Mississippi." The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on February 23, 1829.
