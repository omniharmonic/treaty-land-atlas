On August 11, 1827, Lewis Cass and Thomas L. McKenney signed a treaty at Butte des Morts on the Fox River with the Chippewa, Menominee and Winnebago tribes.

Article 1 says the southern boundary of the Chippewa country "was left undefined by the treaty concluded at Prairie du Chien" in 1825, because some principal Menominee chiefs did not attend. It then defines the line, running northeasterly from the Plover Portage of the Wisconsin River to Green Bay.

Article 2 notes "much difficulty" over the Menominee and Winnebago dealings with the New York Indians, and refers the matter to the President, whose decision would be final. It also defines a tract at Green Bay whose jurisdiction and title are acknowledged to be in the United States. In Article 4 the commissioners distributed goods worth $15,682 for the "liberal establishment of the boundaries." Article 6 reserved the United States' right to punish Winnebagoes for recent "outrages." The treaty text is the only source used here.
