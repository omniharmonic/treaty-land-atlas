On November 15, 1827, Thomas L. McKenney and John Crowell signed an agreement at the Creek Agency with Little Prince and other Creek chiefs and head men.

The preamble recalls the January 1826 Treaty of Washington, which was meant to cede all Creek land inside Georgia's chartered limits. Surveys had since shown that its lines did not take in all of it, and it says "the President of the United States having urged the Creek Nation further to extend the limits." The chiefs agreed to cede all remaining lands owned or claimed by the Creek Nation in Georgia.

The United States agreed to pay $27,491 on approval and ratification and on the sanction of a council of the Creek Nation or additional chiefs' signatures. It also allowed $15,000 more on account of the cession. The text makes the agreement binding as the act of the Creek Nation only if a council sanctions it.
