On November 15, 1827, Thomas L. McKenney and John Crowell signed articles of agreement at the Creek Agency. They signed for the United States, with Little Prince and other chiefs and head men of the Creek Nation.

The preamble looks back to the Treaty of Washington of January 24, 1826. That treaty was meant to cede all Creek land inside Georgia's chartered limits. It "having been since ascertained" that its lines, and a supplementary article, did not cover all of it, and "the President of the United States having urged the Creek Nation further to extend the limits," the chiefs, "being desirous of complying with the wish of the President," ceded "all the remaining lands now owned or claimed by the Creek Nation" within Georgia's chartered limits.

In return the United States agreed to pay $27,491 once the agreement was approved and ratified and had also been sanctioned by a council of the Creek Nation, or by added signatures of chiefs "as shall constitute it the act of the Creek Nation." It also agreed to allow $15,000 more, of which $5,000 was for the education and support of Creek children at a school in Kentucky.

The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on March 4, 1828. The agreement does not state how many acres were involved.
