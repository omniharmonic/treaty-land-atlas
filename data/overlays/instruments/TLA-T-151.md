On February 11, 1828, John Tipton, for the United States, signed a treaty at the Wyandot village near the Wabash, Indiana, with the Eel River or Thorntown party of Miami.

The party ceded their claim to a reservation of about ten miles square at their village on Sugartree Creek, which had been reserved by the 1818 Miami treaty (see TLA-T-104). They agreed not to burn houses or fences and to "remove to the five mile reservation on Eel River by the fifteenth day of October next."

The commissioner had delivered $2,000 in goods. If the Senate ratified, the United States would pay $8,000 in goods, build twelve log houses, clear forty acres, and supply a wagon, oxen, horses and provisions. If the treaty were not ratified, the party agreed to pay for the goods by deduction from their annuity. The treaty also paid trader Peter Langlois $1,000 in silver and $3,000 in goods for earlier supplies, and appropriated $1,000 a year for five years to educate Indian youths. The treaty text is the only source used here.
