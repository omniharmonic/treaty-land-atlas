On September 20, 1828, Lewis Cass and Pierre Menard signed a treaty with the Potawatomi at the Missionary Establishments on the St. Joseph River of Lake Michigan, Michigan Territory.

The Potawatomi ceded two tracts. The first began at the mouth of the St. Joseph River and ran up the river, to the 19th mile tree on the northern boundary of Indiana, and to Lake Michigan. The second began at a line east from the southern end of Lake Michigan and ran south ten miles toward Flat Belly's reservation and Seek's village.

The United States agreed to an added permanent annuity of $2,000 and an annuity of $1,000 for twenty years, $30,000 in goods, $10,000 in goods and $5,000 in specie in 1829, and $7,500 for houses, fences, animals and labourers. It also agreed to deliver tobacco, iron and steel annually and to apply $1,000 a year to education. The treaty text is the only source used here.
