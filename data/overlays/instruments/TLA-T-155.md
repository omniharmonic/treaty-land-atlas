On July 29, 1829, commissioners John McNeil, Pierre Menard and Caleb Atwater signed a treaty at Prairie du Chien, Michigan Territory, with the united Chippewa, Ottawa and Potawatomi nations of the waters of the Illinois, Milwaukee and Manitowoc rivers.

The nations ceded two tracts. The first began at the Winnebago village on Rock River and ran down to the line west from Lake Michigan to the Mississippi, then up the Mississippi to the reservation at the mouth of the Wisconsin River. The second began on the west shore of Lake Michigan about twelve miles north of Chicago and ran west to Rock River and back east to the Fox River of the Illinois.

The United States agreed to pay $16,000 a year forever in specie at Chicago, $12,000 in goods in October, fifty barrels of salt a year, and to keep the blacksmith's shop at Chicago. The treaty reserved sections for named chiefs and bands and granted tracts to descendants of Indians. The treaty text is the only source used here.
