On August 1, 1829, commissioners John McNeil, Pierre Menard and Caleb Atwater signed a treaty at Prairie du Chien, Michigan Territory, with the Winnebago nation.

The Winnebago ceded "all their right, title, and claim" to a country beginning on the Rock River at the mouth of the Pee-kee-tol-a-ka, running north to the road from the Blue Mound to the Wisconsin and Fox portage, and down the Wisconsin to the Mississippi. The United States agreed to pay $18,000 a year in specie for thirty years and to give $30,000 in goods, tobacco, salt, three blacksmith shops, oxen and a cart for thirty years.

At the Winnebago's request, the United States also agreed to pay $23,532.28 to persons listed in a schedule for claims against the Winnebago that they acknowledged as justly due. The treaty provided land grants for descendants located "without the mineral country."
