On August 3, 1829, John McElvain signed articles at Little Sandusky, Ohio, with a band of Delaware Indians on the Sandusky River.

The band ceded the three-mile-square tract next to the Wyandot reservation that had been reserved for them by the 1817 treaty at the Rapids of the Maumee (see TLA-T-090). The band agreed "to remove to and join their nation on the west side of the Mississippi" on or before January 1 of the next year, when peaceable possession was to be given to the United States.

The United States agreed to pay $3,000: $2,000 in hand and $1,000 for horses, clothing, provisions and other items to help the journey. The Kansas Historical Society's Kansapedia says that by 1831, "after several relocations by the United States government," the Delaware were settled at the junction of the Kansas and Missouri rivers. The treaty text and Kansapedia are the only sources used here.
