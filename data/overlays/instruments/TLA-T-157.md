On August 3, 1829, John McElvain, authorized by the President, signed articles of agreement at Little Sandusky, Ohio, with a band of Delaware Indians on the Sandusky River. The agreement was for the cession of a reservation in Ohio.

The band was recorded as ceding "the tract of three miles square, adjoining the Wyandot reservation upon the Sandusky river." That tract had been reserved for their use by the treaty of the Rapids of the Maumee, signed September 29, 1817. The band also engaged "to remove to and join their nation on the west side of the Mississippi, on the land allotted to them," on or before January 1 of the next year. On that date, "peaceable possession" of the reservation was to be given to the United States.

The United States agreed to pay $3,000. Two thousand dollars was paid in hand, and the other $1,000 was to buy horses, clothing, provisions and other useful articles to help them on their journey. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on January 2, 1830.

The Kansas Historical Society's Kansapedia describes the Delaware nation as a whole. It says that by 1831, "after several relocations by the United States government," the Delaware were settled at the junction of the Kansas and Missouri rivers in present-day northeastern Kansas. It does not mention this Ohio band.
