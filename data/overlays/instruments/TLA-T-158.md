On September 24, 1829, a supplementary article to the Delaware treaty of October 3, 1818, made at St. Mary's in Ohio, was agreed at the White River in Missouri. The 1818 treaty had provided that the United States would find the Delaware a country west of the Mississippi as their permanent residence.

The supplementary article says the Delaware Nation "are now willing to remove, on the following conditions, from the country on James fork of White river in the State of Missouri." They would go to the country in the fork of the Kansas and Missouri rivers. That country extended up the Kansas River to the Kansas line and up the Missouri to Camp Leavenworth. The United States pledged "the faith of the government" to guarantee them quiet possession of it forever.

The United States agreed to furnish forty horses, six wagons and ox-teams, tools, provisions for the journey and for a year after arrival, and a grist and saw mill. It would pay an added permanent annuity of $1,000 for their full relinquishment of the Missouri country. Thirty-six sections of the best land there were to be sold for a schools fund.

The article was "concluded in part only." A chief or warrior from each town would explore the country, and sign if they approved. The proclamation was on March 24, 1831. Kansapedia says that by 1831 the Delaware were settled at the junction of the Kansas and Missouri rivers.
