On September 24, 1829, a supplementary article to the Delaware treaty of October 3, 1818 was concluded at the White River, Missouri. The 1818 treaty had promised the Delaware a country west of the Mississippi as their permanent residence.

The supplementary article says the Delaware Nation "are now willing to remove, on the following conditions, from the country on James fork of White river in the State of Missouri" to the country in the fork of the Kansas and Missouri rivers, up the Kansas to the Kansas line and up the Missouri to Camp Leavenworth. The United States pledged to secure that country to them forever.

It agreed to furnish forty horses, six wagons and ox-teams, tools and provisions, to build a grist and saw mill, and to pay an additional permanent annuity of $1,000. Thirty-six sections were to be sold for a schools fund. The article was concluded in part only; a deputation was to explore the country and sign if they approved. Kansapedia says the Delaware were settled at the Kansas and Missouri junction by 1831.
