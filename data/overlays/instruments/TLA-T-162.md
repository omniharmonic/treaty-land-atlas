On February 28, 1831, the United States and the principal chiefs and warriors of the Seneca of the Sandusky River signed a treaty at Washington. The Seneca ceded their Ohio lands, about 40,000 acres from reservations made in 1817 and 1818.

The treaty text says the Seneca had earnestly solicited the President to arrange an exchange of their Ohio land for land west of the Mississippi. The United States commissioner, James B. Gardiner, negotiated it. In return the United States promised about 67,000 acres west of the Mississippi next to the Cherokee lands and the Missouri boundary, removal costs, one year of provisions, mills, a blacksmith shop, and presents of rifles, blankets and tools.

The Ohio land was to be sold by the United States, with advances repaid from the sales and any remainder held as a tribal fund paying 5 percent. The sub-agent who helped, Henry C. Brish, also received a 160-acre grant in the treaty. The sources used here do not describe the later history of the western land.
