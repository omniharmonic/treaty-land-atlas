On July 20, 1831, commissioner James B. Gardiner and agent John McElvain signed a treaty for the United States with the principal chiefs and warriors of a mixed band of Seneca and Shawnee living near Lewistown, Ohio. The band ceded two tracts in Logan County: the 48-square-mile reserve of the 1817 treaty and an 8,960-acre reserve from the 1818 treaty.

The treaty carried out the Act of Congress of May 28, 1830, the Indian Removal Act. Its preamble says the band expressed willingness and anxiety to move west to secure a more permanent home. In return the United States promised a tract of about 60,000 acres west of the Mississippi, removal costs, a year of provisions, a sawmill, a blacksmith shop, and a $6,000 advance for improvements.

The ceded Ohio land was to be sold. After deducting costs, including 70 cents per acre, the remainder would produce a 5 percent annuity. Individual grants went to James McPherson, Henry H. McPherson and interpreter Martin Lane. The sources used here do not describe later events.
