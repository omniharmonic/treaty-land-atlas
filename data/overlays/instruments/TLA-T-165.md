On August 30, 1831, special commissioner James B. Gardiner signed a treaty for the United States with two Ottawa bands in Ohio: the band at Blanchard's Fork and Oquanoxa's village, and the band at Roche de Boeuf and Wolf Rapids. The first ceded about 21,760 acres and the second about 28,157 acres of Ohio reservations.

The treaty was meant to carry out the 1830 act that allowed the President to offer Ohio tribes land west of the Mississippi. The first band was promised 34,000 acres in the west, a $2,000 advance, removal and a year of provisions. The second band was offered 40,000 acres if it chose to move; the text says it was not willing at that time to agree to removal.

Sale proceeds were to repay debts, including individual debts the treaty lists, with 70 cents per acre deducted for costs. The page does not describe the negotiations, so whether consent was freely given cannot be judged from it. The sources used here do not describe later events.
