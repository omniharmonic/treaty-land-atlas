On March 24, 1832, Lewis Cass, specially authorized by the President, signed a treaty at Washington City with the Creek tribe. The Creeks ceded all their land east of the Mississippi River. The text gives no acreage. The United States wanted the Creeks to move west and join their countrymen, and the treaty says no Creek could be compelled to emigrate.

The treaty paid $12,000 a year for five years and then $10,000 a year for fifteen, $100,000 to pay debts and for relief, and removal costs plus a year's subsistence. Ninety principal chiefs could each select one section and each family head a half section, reserved from sale for five years, with patents in fee simple to those who stayed. Article V provided for removing intruders from the ceded country.
