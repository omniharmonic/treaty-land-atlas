On May 9, 1832, James Gadsden, for the United States, signed a treaty at Payne's Landing on the Ocklawaha River in Florida Territory with chiefs and head men of the Seminole. The preamble says the Seminoles were "willing" for a delegation of chiefs, among them Jumper, Charley Emartla and Sam Jones, to examine the Creek country west of the Mississippi at United States expense. The articles would bind both sides only if they were satisfied with that country and with the Creeks' disposition to reunite with them.

In Article 1 the Seminole relinquished "all claim to the lands they at present occupy in the Territory of Florida." They agreed to emigrate to the country assigned to the Creeks and be received as part of the Creek nation. The United States agreed to pay $15,400 for improvements. It would also investigate and settle claims for slaves and other property alleged to be stolen, up to $7,000. Removal was to be completed within three years of ratification.

The Seminole Nation Museum says a delegation of seven chiefs toured the western land and on March 28, 1833 signed what they believed to be a statement that it was suitable. It says many chiefs stated they "had not committed to moving their people" and were "coerced through force and misinterpretation into signing." It says the refusal of most Seminoles to leave led to the Second Seminole War, from December 1835 to 1842.
