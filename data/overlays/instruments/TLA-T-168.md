On May 9, 1832, commissioner James Gadsden signed a treaty at Payne's Landing on the Ocklawaha River, Florida Territory, with Seminole chiefs and headmen including Jumper, Charley Emartla and Sam Jones. The Seminole relinquished all claim to the Florida lands they occupied and agreed to move west of the Mississippi to Creek territory, joining the Creek nation. The text states no acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $15,400 for improvements on the ceded lands and other sums, including up to $7,000 to settle claims over alleged stolen slaves and property. A delegation of chiefs was first to examine the Creek country at United States expense, and the Seminole would move only if satisfied. Removal was to be finished within three years. The treaty describes removal as the President's recommendation.

The Seminole Nation Museum says many chiefs later stated they had not committed to moving their people, some said they were pressured into signing, and the refusal of most Seminoles to leave led to the Second Seminole War, which it dates from December 1835 to 1842.
