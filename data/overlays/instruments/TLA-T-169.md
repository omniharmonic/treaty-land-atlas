On September 15, 1832, Major General Winfield Scott and Illinois Governor John Reynolds signed a treaty for the United States with the Winnebago nation at Fort Armstrong, Rock Island, Illinois. The Winnebago ceded their lands south and east of the Wisconsin River and the Fox River of Green Bay.

In exchange the United States granted them part of the Neutral Ground west of the Mississippi River. It also promised an annuity of $10,000 in specie for 27 years, a school near Fort Crawford or Prairie du Chien, farm help, and payments for physicians. The page gives no acreage for either tract.

The treaty was signed in the months after the Black Hawk War. Article IX required the Winnebago to hand over eight named people accused of attacks on American citizens and soldiers, and said the annuity would be suspended until they did. The page itself does not mention the war. The sources used here do not describe later events.
