On October 24, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, Missouri, for the United States with the Kickapoo. The Kickapoo ceded their lands under the 1822 Treaty of Edwardsville and all other claims to land in Missouri.

In exchange the United States assigned a permanent reservation southwest of the Missouri River, next to the Delaware line. A supplemental article, signed at Fort Leavenworth on November 26, 1832, fixed the boundaries to include at least 1,200 square miles. The treaty also paid an 1832 annuity of $18,000, of which $12,000 was directed to pay tribal debts, and $5,000 a year for 19 years. Smaller sums paid for a blacksmith, a mill, a church, a school and farm help.

Article XIII required the Kickapoo to remove to the new land with as little delay as possible. The treaty page does not describe the circumstances of the negotiation. The sources used here do not describe later events.
