On October 26, 1832, commissioners Jonathan Jennings, John W. Davis and Marks Crume signed a treaty on the Tippecanoe River for the United States with the chiefs, headmen and warriors of the Potawatomi. The Potawatomi ceded their title to lands in Indiana bounded by Lake Michigan, the Michigan road and the Tippecanoe River, among other lines. The page gives no acreage.

The United States agreed to pay a $20,000 annuity for twenty years, $100,000 in goods, a further $30,000 in goods in 1833, and $62,412 in tribal debts listed in a schedule. Article II set aside reservations for named bands, totalling 80 sections, the largest being 36 sections for the band of Aub-be-naub-bee. The treaty also provided for a sawmill and for help with any future emigration.
