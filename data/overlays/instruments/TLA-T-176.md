On October 26, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, St. Louis County, Missouri. On the other side were chiefs, warriors and counselors of the Delaware and Shawnee bands formerly at Cape Girardeau. They ceded all their lands in Missouri and any claims for lost property and improvements. The page states no acreage.

The preamble says the Delawares had suffered losses from leaving Cape Girardeau through various causes, and the treaty compensated them. They received livestock, ground preparation, mill and school money, merchandise, and $12,000 held to pay tribal debts. The Shawnees received $800 in cash, $400 in clothing and horses, $500 for removal, and one year of provisions after relocation. The treaty funded the move of Shawnee bands from Arkansas to lands on the Kansas River.

Shawnee relocation payments depended on their removal. The text describes no threats, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
