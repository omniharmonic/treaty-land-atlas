On October 27, 1832, commissioners Jonathan Jennings, John W. Davis and Marks Crume signed a treaty on the Tippecanoe River, Indiana, for the United States with Potawatomi chiefs and warriors of the St. Joseph, Cold Water, Wabash and Elkhart bands. The Potawatomi ceded their title to lands in Indiana, Illinois and Michigan Territory south of the Grand River. The page gives no total acreage but lists many reserved tracts, mostly in sections, for bands and individuals.

The United States agreed to pay $15,000 a year for twelve years, $32,000 in goods after signing, and $10,000 in goods the next spring. It also agreed to pay claims and debts, $2,000 a year for the education of Indian youth, and money for horses.
