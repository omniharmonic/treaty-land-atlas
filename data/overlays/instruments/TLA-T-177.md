On October 27, 1832, commissioners Jonathan Jennings, John W. Davis and Marks Crume signed a treaty on the Tippecanoe River, Indiana, for the United States. The other party was the chiefs and warriors of the Potowatomies of the State of Indiana and Michigan Territory.

In Article 1 they ceded "their title and interest to lands in the States of Indiana and Illinois, and in the Territory of Michigan, south of Grand river." The treaty states no acreage. Article 2 set aside reservations from the cession for named bands. These included ten sections for the band of Che-Chaw-kose, sixteen for the band of Ash-kum and Wee-si-o-nas, and four for each of several others. It also reserved the reservation at Po-ca-gan's village and one for Potowatomies at Notta-we-sipa under earlier treaties. Article 3 granted tracts to named individuals.

In Article 4 the United States agreed to pay $15,000 a year for twelve years, and $32,000 in goods soon after signing. It also agreed to pay $10,000 in goods the next spring at Notta-wa-si-pa, to be paid to that band and used to pay their just debts. Those debts, listed in a schedule, amounted to $20,721. It promised $2,000 a year for educating Indian youths for as long as Congress thought proper.

The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on January 21, 1833. The treaty does not describe the negotiation or what followed.
