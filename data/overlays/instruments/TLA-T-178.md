On October 27, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, St. Louis County, Missouri, for the United States. The other parties were the Kaskaskia and Peoria tribes. With the Michigamia, Cahokia and Tamarois bands they "formerly composed the Illinois nation of Indians." The Kaskaskia and the bands united with them were "desirous of uniting with the Peorias" on lands west of Missouri.

In Article 1 the Kaskaskia ceded the lands granted to them by the 1803 treaty of Vincennes. They kept about 350 acres near Kaskaskia for Ellen Decoigne, a chief's daughter. In Article 2 they gave up a $1,000 permanent annuity and a salt annuity. In Article 3 the Peoria ceded all their claims to land reserved or assigned to them in Illinois or Missouri.

In Article 4 the United States gave the combined tribes "one hundred and fifty sections of land forever, or as long as they live upon it as a tribe," on the waters of the Osage River. Article 5 set an annuity of $3,000 for ten years.

Article 6 records that the Peoria "assert in Council" that they never understood the fifth article of the 1818 Edwardsville treaty as ceding their claims to land in Missouri, where they had lived more than sixty years. They "demand an equivalent." The commissioners agreed to payments, carts, oxen, log houses and a year of provisions to quiet the claims. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on February 12, 1833.
