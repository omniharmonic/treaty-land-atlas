On October 27, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, Missouri, for the United States with the Kaskaskia and Peoria, joined by the Michigamia, Cahokia and Tamarois bands. The treaty says the Kaskaskia and the bands united with them wished to join the Peorias on lands west of Missouri.

The Kaskaskia ceded lands granted under the 1803 Treaty of Vincennes, keeping about 350 acres near Kaskaskia for Ellen Decoigne, and gave up a $1,000 permanent annuity and their salt annuity. The Peorias ceded claims to lands reserved in former treaties. The United States granted the combined tribes 150 sections on the Osage River west of Missouri and agreed to pay $3,000 a year for ten years, with further payments, goods, stock and houses.

The text shows the Peorias saying they never understood the fifth article of the 1818 Treaty of Edwardsville as ceding their Missouri claims, and that they now demanded an equivalent. The sources used here do not describe later events.
