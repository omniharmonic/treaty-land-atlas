On October 29, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, St. Louis County, Missouri, for the United States. The other parties were the chiefs, warriors and counsellors of the Piankeshaw and Wea tribes.

In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding "all their right, title and interest to and in lands within the States of Missouri and Illinois," and confirmed earlier treaties. In Article 2 the United States ceded to the two tribes for permanent residence "two hundred and fifty sections of land." The tract lay within the survey of the lands set apart for the Piankeshaws, Weas and Peorias. It was bounded on the east by Missouri's western line for fifteen miles and was "intended to include the present villages of the said Piankeshaws and Weas."

Article 3 paid the Piankeshaw "a full equivalent" for their claim for salt annuities, for improvements on the Missouri lands they left, and for horses lost in moving. That meant cattle, hogs and farm tools worth $500 a year for five years, plus $750 for farming help and $200 at signing. Article 4 paid the Wea $500 in stock and tools, plus $200 at signing, for improvements and lost horses. It also gave aid and a year of provisions for the Wea living in Indiana to join the rest. Article 5 supported a shared blacksmith shop for five years.

The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on February 12, 1833.
