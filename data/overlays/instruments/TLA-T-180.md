On October 29, 1832, commissioners William Clark, Frank J. Allen and Nathan Kouns signed a treaty at Castor Hill, Missouri, for the United States with the chiefs, warriors and counselors of the Piankeshaw and Wea. The tribes ceded all their claimed interest in lands in Missouri and Illinois. The treaty was proclaimed on February 12, 1833.

In return the United States granted them 250 sections for permanent residence, within the survey area set aside for the Piankeshaws, Weas and Peorias. The page states the sections but no acreage. The treaty also paid sums described as equivalents for the Piankeshaw claim to salt annuities, for improvements on lands they left, and for horses lost on relocation. The Wea received help for the Wea group in Indiana to rejoin the tribe and a year of provisions after arrival.

A shared blacksmith shop was funded for five years. The page describes no coercion, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
