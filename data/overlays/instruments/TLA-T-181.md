On December 29, 1832, commissioners Henry L. Ellsworth and John F. Schermerhorn signed an agreement at the Seneca agency on the head waters of the Cowskin River, for the United States. The other party was the chiefs and headmen of the "United Nation" of the Senecas and Shawnee. The preamble recalls the Lewistown treaty of July 20, 1831, and says the mixed band of Senecas and Shawnees "removed from their homes in Ohio" to the lands assigned west of the Mississippi.

It says the two groups "have lately formed a confederacy" and were "anxious to unite as one Tribe or Nation." They wanted all their land on the east side of the Neosho, or Grand, River, which divided it.

In Article 1 the United Tribe ceded the land granted to them on the west side of the Neosho River by the 1831 treaties. In Article 2 the United States agreed to grant a tract east of the river, bounded on the east by Missouri's west line and on the south by the Cherokee line. It would contain sixty thousand acres, not counting the land the Sandusky Senecas already held. It would be split into two patents, north for the Ohio mixed band and south for the Sandusky Senecas. The patents would be in fee simple, but "the lands shall not be sold or ceded without the consent of the United States."

The United States also agreed to build a grist mill, saw mill and blacksmith shop, and to pay $1,000 for losses on the journey west. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on March 22, 1833.
