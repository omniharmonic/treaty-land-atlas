On December 29, 1832, commissioners Henry L. Ellsworth and John F. Schermerhorn signed a treaty at the Seneca agency on the Cowskin River for the United States with the chiefs and headmen of the combined Seneca and Shawnee United Nation. The preamble says the Sandusky Senecas and the Lewistown Seneca-Shawnee band, who had already moved from Ohio under the 1831 treaties, had formed a confederacy and wanted one location.

The tribes ceded the land they held west of the Neosho (Grand) River under those treaties. In exchange they received about 60,000 acres east of the Neosho, bordering Missouri, the Cherokee lands and the Sandusky Senecas' land. The tract was divided into two patents, and the land could not be sold or ceded without the consent of the United States.

The United States also paid $1,000 for claims for forage and property lost during the move west and agreed to build mills and a blacksmith shop. The page describes no coercion.
