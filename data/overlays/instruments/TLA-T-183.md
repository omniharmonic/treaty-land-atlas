On February 14, 1833, commissioners Montfort Stokes, Henry L. Ellsworth and John F. Schermerhorn signed a treaty at Fort Gibson for the United States with chiefs and headmen of the Muskogee (Creek) Nation. The United States assigned the Creeks a country west of the Mississippi, described by rivers and survey lines. The treaty gives no acreage and no price.

The text says the land would be the common property of the whole Creek nation, that the Seminoles would be located on part of it, and that it replaced the land provided under the 1826 treaty. A patent in fee simple was promised after ratification. The treaty settled a boundary dispute between the Creeks and the Cherokees that had been referred to the President.

The United States agreed to pay a fair price for improvements the Creeks had to leave because of the new boundary, and provided $1,000 a year for education, smiths, mills and saws. The page does not describe how the agreement was negotiated.
