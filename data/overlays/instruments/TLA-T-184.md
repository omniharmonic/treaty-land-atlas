In February 1833 Commissioner George B. Porter signed a treaty at Maumee, Ohio, for the United States with the chiefs and headmen of a band of Ottawa living on reserves on the Miami of Lake Erie. Article I has the band cede all its land on either side of the Miami River of Lake Erie. The page gives no total acreage, but it describes two earlier reservations, one of 34 square miles and one four miles square.

The United States agreed to pay $29,440, which the band directed to its debts to named creditors. Article III also mentions an $18,000 claim the band had from an 1831 treaty, to be paid when that deficiency was settled. Article II reserved specific tracts out of the cession for individuals, including chiefs and several non-Native residents.

The band also agreed to remove from all the ceded lands. The page does not say how the negotiation was conducted or whether the band faced pressure, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
