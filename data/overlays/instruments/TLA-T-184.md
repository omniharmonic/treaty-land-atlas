On February 18, 1833, George B. Porter, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at Maumee, Ohio, with the chiefs and head men of the Band of Ottawa Indians residing on the Indian Reserves on the Miami of Lake Erie. The preamble recalls two reservations. One was thirty-four square miles granted by the 1817 treaty at the Rapids of the Miami. The other was four miles square on Miami Bay, reserved in the 1807 Detroit treaty. The band was living on both.

In Article 1 the band ceded all its land on either side of the Miami River of Lake Erie or on Miami Bay, in both reservations "or to which they have any claim." The page gives no total acreage. Article 2 set aside tracts from the cession for individuals. Among them were 320 acres for the chief Au-to-kee at the mouth of the river and 800 acres for the Navarre family. Smaller tracts went to other chiefs and to residents such as Robert A. Forsyth and John E. Hunt, "at the especial request of the said band."

In Article 3 the United States agreed to pay $29,440, applied "by direction of the said band" to its debts to named creditors. The band agreed "to remove from all the lands herein ceded." The treaty also notes the band's claim that $18,000 was due under the 1831 treaty. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on March 22, 1833.
