On May 13, 1833, John F. Schermerhorn, Commissioner of Indian Affairs West, signed a treaty at New Gascony, Arkansas Territory, for the United States with the chiefs and warriors of the Quapaw. It supplements the 1824 treaty, which had required the Quapaw to relocate to Caddo territory.

The preamble says the Red River site repeatedly flooded, destroyed crops and was unhealthy, with nearly a quarter of the people dying. It says the Caddo would not absorb the Quapaw and settlers had taken the Quapaw's improvements. The Quapaw, it says, saw no alternative but to perish if they stayed.

The Quapaw relinquished their lands on Bayou Treache, on the Red River. The United States agreed to convey 150 sections west of the Missouri state line, between the Seneca and Shawnee lands. It would pay $4,180 in Quapaw debts, $2,000 a year in annuities for twenty years, and pay for cabins, education, tools and removal costs. The page gives no acreage in acres.
