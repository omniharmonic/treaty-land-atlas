On June 18, 1833, commissioner James Gadsden signed a treaty at Pope's, Fayette County, Florida Territory, for the United States with Apalachicola chiefs and warriors, including Mulatto King and Tustenuggy Hajo. A second, parallel agreement was signed the same day with Econchatimico and his followers.

The chiefs gave up the reservation made for them under the 1823 Treaty of Camp Moultrie on the Apalachicola River. In return the United States agreed to grant a section and a half to each of the two chiefs, and Econchatimico received three sections on the Chattahoochee River. The page gives no total acreage. If the chiefs chose to move to Arkansas, the United States would pay $3,000 for the relinquished reservation.

The agreements cleared the way for Seminole removal under the 1832 Treaty of Payne's Landing. If the chiefs stayed, United States protection would end once the other Seminole bands had moved, and they would fall under Florida's government and laws. The text says they relinquished their rights voluntarily.
