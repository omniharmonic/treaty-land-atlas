On June 18, 1833, commissioner James Gadsden signed a treaty at Pope's, Fayette County, Florida Territory, for the United States. The other parties were chiefs and warriors of the Apalachicola, including Mulatto King and Tustenuggy Hajo. The text says they "voluntarily relinquish" the privileges they held under the 1823 treaty at Camp Moultrie. They surrendered their reservation on the Apalachicola River. A parallel agreement the same day gave up the Chattahoochee reservation of Econchatimico.

In Article 2 the United States agreed to convey by patent, within three years, a section and a half of land to each of the two chiefs, and three sections to Econchatimico. They could sell the land and move with the consent of the Florida Territory's executive. But "should they remain on their lands," the United States would, once Blunt's band and the Seminoles had migrated, "withdraw the immediate protection hitherto extended" to them. The chiefs would then become "subject to the government and laws of the territory of Florida."

Article 3 continued their share of a $5,000 annuity while the Seminoles remained in the territory. Article 4 offered an alternative. If they chose to join the Seminoles in the Arkansas country by surrendering their grants, the United States would pay $3,000 for the relinquished reservation.

The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on April 12, 1834. The treaty states no acreage for the reservations.
