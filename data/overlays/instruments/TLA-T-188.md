On September 21, 1833, Henry L. Ellsworth, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at the Otoe Village on the Platte River with the united bands of Otoes and Missourias.

In Article 1 they ceded "all their right and title" to the lands lying south of a line. It began on the Little Nemohaw River at the northwest corner of the land reserved at the 1830 Prairie du Chien treaty for certain half-breeds. It ran west along the Little Nemohaw to its head branches and then due west "as far west, as said Otoes and Missourias, have, or pretend to have any claim." The page gives no acreage.

In return the United States agreed to continue their $2,500 annuity and their $500 farming-tool annuity for ten years from July 15, 1840. It also agreed to $500 a year for schools for five years, a horse mill and two farmers for five years, and $1,000 in stock.

Article 7 made the mill, school, farmers and stock conditional. They would not be provided until the tribes located themselves "in such convenient agricultural districts, as the President may think proper," and payments would not continue if they abandoned that location. In Article 8 the tribes declared their "entire willingness to abandon the chase for the agricultural life." They also agreed to refer disputes with other tribes to an arbiter. The Statutes at Large record a proclamation on April 12, 1834.
