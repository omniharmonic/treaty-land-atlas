On September 21, 1833, commissioner Henry L. Ellsworth signed a treaty at the Otoe Village on the Platte River for the United States with the united bands of Otoes and Missourias. The tribes ceded their claimed land south of a line beginning at the Little Nemaha River and running west to the limit of their claims. The page does not give an acreage. The treaty was proclaimed on April 12, 1834.

In return the United States agreed to continue a $2,500 annuity for ten years from July 15, 1840, plus a $500 annuity for farming tools, $500 a year for education for five years, a horse mill, two farmers for five years, and stock and goods. These farming, school and mill benefits depended on the tribes settling in districts the President chose, and payments could stop if they left.

In Article VIII the tribes declared their willingness to give up hunting for farming. The page describes no coercion, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
