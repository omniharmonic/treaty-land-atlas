On October 9, 1833, commissioner Henry L. Ellsworth signed a treaty at the Grand Pawnee village on the Platte River for the United States with the chiefs and headmen of four confederated Pawnee bands: the Grand Pawnees, Pawnee Loups, Pawnee Republicans and Pawnee Tappaye. The Pawnees gave up their claims to all land south of the Platte River. The page states no acreage.

In return the United States promised $4,600 in goods a year for twelve years, split among the bands, agricultural implements for five years, $1,000 a year for ten years for schools, blacksmith services, farmers, and about $1,000 in livestock per tribe, to be handed over when the President judged it safe. The treaty states its aim as advancing Pawnee welfare and introducing farming.

Many benefits depended on the Pawnees settling in agricultural districts and staying there year-round. Article X releases the United States from several stipulations if they do not. The page does not describe coercion directly, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
