On 24 May 1834, at Washington, John H. Eaton signed articles for the United States with a delegation of the Chickasaw nation. The text calls them articles "proposed by the Commissioners ... in pursuance of the request made, by the Delegation" and says the Chickasaws "are about to abandon their homes."

The agreement revises the 1832 Treaty of Pontotoc. Article 5 changes the reservations to be granted in fee (full ownership): four sections to heads of families of ten or more, three for five to nine, two for fewer, with an added section for those owning more than ten slaves and half a section for ten or fewer. Other articles gave sections to unmarried adults, half sections to orphans, and special grants to named chiefs, the agent and two lawyers.

Reservations could be sold only after certification, by at least two of seven named persons, that the owner could manage their affairs, and with the President's approval. Article 11 says the rest of the Chickasaw country would be sold as public land, first at not less than $1.25 an acre and falling over time, with the proceeds invested and the interest paid to the Chickasaws.

If the Chickasaws found a home west of the Mississippi, the United States would protect them there, let them sell invested stock to buy land, and furnish guides and a year of supplies. The page lists a proclamation on 1 July 1834.
