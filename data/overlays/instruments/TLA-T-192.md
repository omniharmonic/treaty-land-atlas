On October 23, 1834, commissioner William Marshall signed a treaty at the Forks of the Wabash, Indiana, for the United States with the chiefs and warriors of the Miami. The Miami ceded several tracts in Indiana, all part of reserves made in the 1818 and 1826 treaties. They included 36 sections at Flat Belly's village, about 23,000 acres along the Wabash River and several smaller tracts. The page gives no total acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $208,000: $58,000 within six months of ratification, $50,000 for tribal debts, and $100,000 in annual installments of $10,000. It added $1,500 for horses the Miami said whites had stolen. It would value Miami buildings on ceded land and provide comparable improvements on sites the chiefs chose. Named individuals received patents for tracts from the ceded lands.

The Senate amended the treaty, and the Miami assented to the amendments in November 1837. The page does not describe pressure or the circumstances of negotiation.
