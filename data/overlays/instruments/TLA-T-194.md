On December 10, 1834, William Marshall, commissioner for the United States, signed a short treaty at a camp on the Tippecanoe River in Indiana. The other party was Muck Rose, a chief of the Potawatomi (spelled Potawattamie in the text), and his band.

Article 1 has the chief and his band cede six sections of land. These sections had been reserved for them by the second article of the October 26, 1832 treaty with the Potawatomi on the Tippecanoe River. The treaty states no acreage. In Article 2 the band was bound by the treaty to yield peaceable possession of the sections to the United States within three years of the date of ratification of the 1832 treaty.

For this the United States stipulated to pay four hundred dollars in goods at signing, which the treaty acknowledges as received, and "an annuity of one thousand dollars for two years." The treaty was proclaimed on March 16, 1835. The text does not describe how the negotiation went or what happened to the band afterward.
