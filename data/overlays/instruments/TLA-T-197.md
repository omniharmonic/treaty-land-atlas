On July 1, 1835, commissioner Jehiel Brooks signed a treaty at the agency house in the Caddo nation, Louisiana, for the United States with the chiefs, headmen and warriors of the Caddo. The Caddo gave up their territory between the Sabine and Red rivers, bounded by the United States-Mexico boundary line and by the Red River, Pascagoula Bayou, Bayou Wallace, Lake Wallace and Cypress Bayou. The text gives no acreage, only "more or less." Four leagues were carved out for the Grappe heirs, and one section for Larkin Edwards.

The United States agreed to pay $80,000 in total: $30,000 in goods and horses at signing, $10,000 in money within a year, and $10,000 a year for four years. In Article II the Caddo promised to remove at their own expense out of the boundaries of the United States within one year, and never to return to settle there.
