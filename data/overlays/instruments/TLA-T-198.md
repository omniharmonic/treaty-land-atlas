On August 24, 1835, United States commissioners Montfort Stokes, M. Arbuckle and F. W. Armstrong signed a treaty at Camp Holmes with the Comanche, the Wichita and associated bands. The Cherokee, Muscogee, Choctaw, Osage, Seneca and Quapaw also took part. The treaty contains no land cession.

Its articles set perpetual peace and friendship among the parties and United States citizens. Citizens were to have free passage through Indian country on their way to or from Mexico, with compensation for stolen horses or property under stated conditions. The treaty also confirmed the right to hunt and trap in the Great Prairie west of the Cross Timber, required payment for damage to traders' goods, and provided presents to the signing nations.

The text says the nations entered the treaty freely and willingly, and the page shows nothing otherwise. Because no land changed hands in it, this is not a cession in Royce's sense. The sources used here do not describe what followed.
