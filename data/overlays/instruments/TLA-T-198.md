On August 24, 1835, the United States signed a treaty at Camp Holmes, near the Canadian River in the Muscogee nation. The commissioners were Governor M. Stokes, Brigadier General M. Arbuckle and F. W. Armstrong. Representatives of the Comanche and Witchetaw (Wichita) nations and their associated bands signed. Chiefs of the Cherokee, Muscogee, Choctaw, Osage, Seneca and Quapaw nations were also parties, and the treaty aimed to bring peace among all of them.

The treaty contains no land cession. Article 1 sets perpetual peace and friendship. In Article 3, the Comanche and Witchetaw nations agreed that United States citizens could pass through their settlements or hunting ground without molestation on their way to Mexico or back. The United States guaranteed them full indemnification for stolen horses or property, if the property could not be recovered and proof showed a United States citizen stole it within the United States. Article 4 gave all parties free permission to hunt and trap in the Great Prairie west of the Cross Timber.

Article 8 says the Comanche and Witchetaw had freely and willingly entered the treaty, the first they had made with the United States or any of the other parties. They were to receive presents after signing, and the United States asked nothing in return except to remain at peace. The treaty was proclaimed on May 19, 1836.
