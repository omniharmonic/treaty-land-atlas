On March 26, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at a camp on Turkey Creek Prairie, Indiana, for the United States with Mesquawbuck, a Potawatomi chief acting for himself and his band. The band ceded four sections of land that had been reserved for them under an 1832 treaty on the Tippecanoe River. The page does not state the acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $2,560 in specie at the next annuity payment after ratification, and to cover the costs of making the treaty. In Article 4 the band agreed to remove to the country west of the Mississippi provided for the Potawatomi nation by the United States, within two years. The Senate struck out Article 6.

The treaty is one of several 1836 cessions of Indiana reserves with a removal clause. The page describes no pressure or circumstances surrounding the agreement, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
