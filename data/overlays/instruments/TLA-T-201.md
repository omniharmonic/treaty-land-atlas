On March 28, 1836, Henry R. Schoolcraft signed a treaty at Washington for the United States with the Ottawa and Chippewa nations, with a supplemental article signed on March 31. The nations ceded a large tract described by boundaries, covering lands and islands not reserved. The page gives no total acreage. Reservations were held in common for five years only, unless the President allowed longer, and included tracts of 50,000 acres at Little Traverse Bay, 20,000 acres at Grand Traverse Bay and 70,000 acres at the Pierre Marquette River.

Payments included a $30,000 annuity for 20 years, funds for education, missions, tools and medical services, $150,000 in goods and provisions on ratification, $300,000 to pay debts, and a $150,000 fund for people of mixed descent. Under Article 8 a deputation was to select a homeland southwest of the Missouri River, and the United States would remove the Indians at its expense when they wished.

The treaty was proclaimed on May 27, 1836. The input links it to Michigan v. Bay Mills (2014), which this entry did not examine.
