On March 29, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty on the Tippecanoe River, Indiana, for the United States with Waukewa's band of the Potawatomi. The page names the son of Wau-ke-wa Che-cose as the chief signing for the band. The band ceded four sections of land reserved for them under an earlier treaty. The page gives no acreage, and no land was exchanged.

The United States agreed to pay $2,560 in specie at the first annuity payment after ratification and to cover the expenses of making the treaty. The band agreed to vacate the land within three months and to remove to the country provided for the Potawatomi nation west of the Mississippi River within two years.

Article 6 was struck out by the Senate. The page describes no coercion or negotiation dynamics, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
