On March 29, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty on the Tippecanoe River in Indiana. The other party was the only son of Wau-ke-wa Che-cose, a Potawatomi chief (spelled Pottawatamy in the text), and his band. The atlas labels this "Waukewa's Band."

Article 1 has the chief and his band cede the four sections of land reserved for them by the second article of an earlier treaty with the Potawatomi. The treaty states no acreage. In Article 2 they were bound by the treaty to yield peaceable possession within three months of signing. They were also bound by the treaty to remove, within two years, to the country provided for the Potawatomi nation west of the Mississippi River.

The United States agreed to pay $2,560 in specie, meaning coin, at the first annuity payment after ratification, and to pay the necessary expenses of making the treaty. The Senate struck out Article 6, whose text the page does not show. The treaty was proclaimed on June 4, 1836. The treaty text does not describe how the negotiation went, and the sources used here do not say what became of the band.
