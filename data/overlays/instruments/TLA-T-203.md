On April 11, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at a camp on the Tippecanoe River in Indiana. The other parties were Pau-koo-shuck, the oldest son of Aub-ba-naub-ba, and the head men of Aub-ba-naub-ba's band of Potawatomi (spelled Potawattimie in the text).

In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding to the United States the thirty-six sections of land reserved for them by the second article of the October 26, 1832 treaty with the Potawatomi on the Tippecanoe River. The treaty states no acreage. The United States agreed in Article 2 to pay $23,040 in specie, meaning coin: one half at the first annuity payment after ratification and the other half at the next.

In Article 3, Pau-koo-shuck and his band were bound by the treaty to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River provided for the Potawatomi nation by the United States, within two years. The Senate struck out Article 4, whose text the page does not show. The treaty was proclaimed on May 25, 1836. The treaty text does not say how the negotiation went, and the sources used here do not say what happened to the band afterward.
