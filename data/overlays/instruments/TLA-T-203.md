On April 11, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at a camp on the Tippecanoe River, Indiana, for the United States with Pau-koo-shuck, the oldest son of Aub-ba-naub-ba, and head men of Aub-ba-naub-ba's Potawatomi band. The band ceded 36 sections of land that had been reserved for them under the 1832 treaty. The page gives no acreage; at 640 acres to the section this would be about 23,040 acres, but that is a calculation, not a figure in the source.

The United States agreed to pay $23,040 in specie, half at the first annuity payment after ratification and half at the next. In Article 3 the band agreed to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River within two years, to land the United States would provide for the Potawatomi nation.

The Senate struck out Article 4. The page describes no coercion, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
