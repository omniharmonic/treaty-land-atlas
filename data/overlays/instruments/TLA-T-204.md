On April 22, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at the Indian Agency in Indiana for the United States with the Potawatomi chiefs and headmen O-kah-mause, Kee-waw-nay, Nee-boash and Mat-chis-jaw and their bands. They ceded ten sections of land that had been reserved for them under the 1832 treaty along the Tippecanoe River. The page does not state the acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $6,400 for the cession, at the first annuity payment after ratification. The treaty also provided for the removal of the bands west of the Mississippi River within two years. The removal article is partly garbled in the page's transcription, and the Senate struck out Article 4.

The treaty belongs to a run of 1836 cessions of reserved Indiana sections with removal clauses. The page records only the signed terms and signatures. It describes no pressure or circumstances, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
