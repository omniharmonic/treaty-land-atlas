On April 22, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at the Indian Agency in Indiana. The other parties were O-kah-mause, Kee-waw-nay, Nee-boash and Mat-chis-jaw, chiefs and head men of the Potawatomi (spelled Patawattimie in the text), and their bands.

In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding ten sections of land reserved for them by the second article of the October 26, 1832 treaty with the Potawatomi on the Tippecanoe River. The treaty states no acreage. Article 2 has the United States pay $6,400 at the first annuity payment after ratification. Article 5 has it cover the necessary expenses of making the treaty.

In Article 3 the chiefs, head men and their bands were bound by the treaty to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River provided for the Potawatomi nation by the United States, within two years. The Senate struck out Article 4, whose text the page does not show. The treaty was proclaimed on May 25, 1836. The treaty text does not describe how the negotiation went, and the sources used here do not say what happened to the bands afterward.
