On April 22, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at the Indian agency in Indiana. The other parties were Nas-waw-kee and Quash-quaw, chiefs and head men of the Potawatomi (spelled Patawattimie in the text), and their bands.

In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding three sections of land reserved for them by the second article of the October 26, 1832 treaty with the Potawatomi on the Tippecanoe River. The treaty states no acreage. In Article 2 the United States agreed to pay nineteen hundred and twenty dollars, or $1,920, at the first annuity payment after ratification. Article 5 has it pay the necessary expenses of the treaty.

In Article 3 the bands were bound by the treaty to give possession of the three sections and to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River provided by the United States for the Potawatomi nation, within two years of the signing date. The Senate struck out Article 4, and the page does not show what it said. The treaty was proclaimed on May 25, 1836. The treaty text does not describe how the negotiation went, and the sources used here do not say what happened to the bands afterward.
