On April 23, 1836, John A. Bryan, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty with William Walker, John Barnett and Peacock, chiefs and principal men of the Wyandot tribe in Ohio, acting for the tribe. A delegation of chiefs had come to Washington. Article 1 ceded a strip of land five miles in extent on the east end of the Wyandot reservation in Crawford County, Ohio. It also ceded one section in Cranberry Swamp on Broken Sword Creek, set out in an 1817 treaty, and 160 acres that replaced an equal quantity set apart in a supplemental 1818 treaty.

Under Article 2 the land was to be surveyed like other public lands and sold at a time and place the President directed. The money went back to the tribe. Article 5 allowed up to $20,000 of the proceeds, at the chiefs' direction, for rebuilding mills, repairing roads, schools and other public objects, with the rest distributed among tribe members like annuities. Article 4 charged the costs of the treaty, including the delegation's expenses, to the sale proceeds.

Article 8 said that if the Indians were not satisfied with the prices during the sale, the Register and Receiver would close it on the chiefs' written application and report to the War Department. The treaty was proclaimed on May 16, 1836. The treaty text does not describe the negotiation, and the sources used here do not say how the sale went.
