On April 23, 1836, commissioner John A. Bryan signed a treaty at Washington for the United States with William Walker, John Barnett and Peacock, chiefs and principal men of the Wyandot in Ohio. The treaty was proclaimed on May 16, 1836. The Wyandot ceded a strip five miles long on the east end of their reservation in Crawford County, Ohio. They also ceded one section in Cranberry Swamp on Broken Sword Creek, set out in an 1817 treaty, and 160 acres that replaced a quantity set apart in a supplemental 1818 treaty.

The lands were to be surveyed and sold at public sale. Up to $20,000 of the proceeds could be used, at the chiefs' direction, for mills, roads, schools and other improvements, and the remainder distributed among tribal members. Article 8 let the chiefs halt the sale in writing if they were dissatisfied with prices.
