On May 9, 1836, commissioner Henry R. Schoolcraft signed a treaty at Washington for the United States with the chiefs of the Swan Creek and Black River bands of the Chippewa in Michigan. The bands gave up four tracts in southeastern Michigan: a three-mile square on Swan Creek, about 1,760 acres near Salt Creek, a quarter section at the mouth of the Au Vaseau, and two sections near the mouth of the Black River, about 8,320 acres in all. They had held these reservations since an 1807 treaty at Detroit.

The preamble says the bands wished to fix their residence at a place more favourable to their welfare and sent their chiefs to Washington to arrange it. In Article 4 the United States agreed to furnish 8,320 acres west of the Mississippi, northwest of St. Anthony's Falls. It also promised the net proceeds of sales of the ceded land, $1,000 annuities for ten years, an advance of $2,500 and goods worth $4,000.
