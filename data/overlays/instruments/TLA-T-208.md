On August 5, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at a camp near Yellow River, Indiana, for the United States with Potawatomi chiefs and headmen including Pe-pin-a-waw, No-taw-kah and Mac-kah-tah-mo-ah. The bands ceded 22 sections that the 1832 Tippecanoe treaty had reserved for them. The page gives no acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $14,080 in specie, part of which could pay tribal debts verified by a commissioner. Article 3 says the bands agree to remove west of the Mississippi River within two years. Twenty-six chiefs and headmen signed with marks.

An editor's note on the Forest County Potawatomi's copy of the treaty says Menominee and others wrote at least three letters protesting that they had not attended the council or signed, and that the treaty was invalid. Menominee's name is not on the treaty. The input data links this treaty to the 1838 Trail of Death, which this entry's sources do not cover in detail.
