On September 17, 1836, William Clark, Superintendent of Indian Affairs, signed a treaty at Fort Leavenworth for the United States with the Ioway tribe and the Missouri band of Sac and Fox. The tribes gave up all claims to the strip of land between the State of Missouri and the Missouri River. The page states no acreage. The treaty was proclaimed on February 15, 1837.

The United States agreed to pay a $7,500 present in money. The tribes received about 400 sections on the south side of the Missouri River, between the northern boundary of the Kickapoo lands and the Grand Nemahar River. The Sac and Fox took the lower half and the Ioway the upper half. The United States also promised houses, cleared farmland, a farmer, blacksmith, schoolmaster, interpreter, tools, a year of rations, a ferry, livestock and a mill, and $500 and $400 toward relocation.

The text says the ceded lands could never be made available for Indian purposes and that extending the state line would make a natural boundary between whites and Indians. The tribes are recorded as well satisfied.
