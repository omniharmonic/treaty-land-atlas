On September 20, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at Chippewanaung, Indiana, for the United States with the Potawatomi chiefs Me-mat-way, brother of To-i-sa, and Che-quaw-ka-ko, and their band. The band ceded ten sections of land that the 1832 treaty had reserved for them along the Tippecanoe River. The page states no acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $8,000 by May 1 of the following year. Some of that sum could go toward the band's debts, once a United States commissioner verified them as valid. The United States also agreed to cover the costs of making the treaty. In Article 3 the band agreed to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River within two years.

The page shows only the signed terms, the signatures by mark and the witnesses. It describes no coercion or pressure, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
