On September 22, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at Chippewanaung, Indiana, for the United States with Mo-sack, a Potawatomi chief, and his band. The band ceded four sections of land that had been reserved for them under an earlier 1832 treaty. The page does not state a total acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $3,200 by May 1 of the following year. Certain debts of the band, once verified by a commissioner appointed by the United States, would be deducted from that sum. The United States also agreed to cover the costs of making the treaty. The band agreed to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River, set aside for the Potawatomi nation, within two years.

Like its neighbours of September 1836, this treaty combines a cession of a reservation with a removal clause and a debt-payment clause. The page describes no pressure or objections from the band, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
