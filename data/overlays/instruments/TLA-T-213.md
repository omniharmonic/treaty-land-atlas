On September 22, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at Chippewanaung, Indiana, with Mo-sack, a chief of the Potawatomi (spelled Potawattimie in the text), and his band.

In Article 1 the chief and his band ceded four sections of land reserved for him and his band by the second article of the October 1832 treaty with the Potawatomi on the Tippecanoe River. The treaty states no acreage. In Article 2 the United States agreed to pay $3,200 on or before the first of May following. At the request of the chief and band, Article 4 had a commissioner appointed by the United States pay debts of the band that he found just, deducted from that sum. Article 5 had the United States pay the expenses of making the treaty.

In Article 3 the chief and his band agreed to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River provided for the Potawatomi nation by the United States, within two years. The treaty was proclaimed on February 16, 1837. The text does not describe how the negotiation went, and the sources used here do not say what happened to the band afterward.
