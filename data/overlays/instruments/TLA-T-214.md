On September 23, 1836, commissioner Abel C. Pepper signed a treaty at Chippewanaung, Indiana, for the United States with the chiefs and leaders of the Potawatomi of the Wabash. The Potawatomi ceded all their remaining land in Indiana, which the 1832 treaty had set aside as reservations for several bands. The reservations totalled 42 sections. The page states no acreage.

The United States agreed to pay $1.25 per acre, a total of $33,600 in specie, by May 1, 1837. Debts the tribe owed were to be paid from this sum, and the tribe acknowledged an $800 debt to Hamilton and Comperet. The Potawatomi agreed to move west of the Mississippi within two years, and the United States agreed to cover the costs of making the treaty.

The page describes no coercion, negotiation pressure or circumstances of signing. It is one of the last of the 1836 Indiana reserve cessions in this series, and the sources used here do not describe later events.
