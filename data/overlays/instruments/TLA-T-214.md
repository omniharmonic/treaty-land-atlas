On September 23, 1836, Abel C. Pepper, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at Chippewanaung, Indiana, with the chiefs, warriors and headmen of the Potawatomi of the Wabash. In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding all the land of the tribe in Indiana that the 1832 treaty had designated as reservations. The treaty lists six groups: the bands of Kin-krash (four sections), Che-chaw-kose (ten), Ash-kum and Wee-si-o-nas (sixteen), We-saw (four), Mo-ta (four) and Mi-no-quet (four). It totals the list at 42 sections and states no acreage.

In Article 2 the United States agreed to pay one dollar and twenty-five cents per acre, or $33,600, in specie (coin), on or before May 1, 1837. At the chiefs' request, Article 4 had a commissioner pay the debts of the Wabash Potawatomi that he found just, deducted from that sum. A note after the signatures says the chiefs acknowledged owing Hamilton and Comperet $800 and asked that it be paid from the money. Article 5 had the United States pay the expenses of the treaty.

In Article 3 the chiefs, warriors and headmen were bound by the treaty to remove to the country west of the Mississippi River provided for the Potawatomi nation by the United States, within two years. The treaty was proclaimed on February 18, 1837. The text does not describe the negotiation, and the sources used here do not say what followed.
