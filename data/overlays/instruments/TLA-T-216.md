On September 28, 1836, the confederated Sac and Fox tribes signed a treaty with U.S. commissioner Henry Dodge at a treaty ground opposite Rock Island. They ceded to the United States the reservation of 400 sections that the 1832 treaty had set aside for them.

The preamble says the tribes wanted additional means of support and a way to pay their creditors. The United States agreed to pay $30,000 in June 1837 and $10,000 a year for ten years, to pay up to $48,458.87 1/2 of debts certified as justly owed, and to deliver about 200 horses. The text does not describe how the council itself went, and the sources I read do not report pressure.

Article 6 required the tribes to leave the ceded land by November 1, 1836, and barred any band from planting, fishing or hunting there afterward. Further Sac and Fox cessions followed: 1,250,000 acres in 1837, and in 1842 all remaining claims west of the Mississippi, with an agreement to move to land on the Missouri River or its waters.
