On October 15, 1836, agents John Dougherty and Joshua Pilcher met the chiefs of the Otoes, Missouries, Omahaws and the Yankton and Santee bands of Sioux at Bellevue on the Upper Missouri. Like four other 1836 agreements, this one dealt with land between the State of Missouri and the Missouri River.

Article 1 has the signers "for ever cede relinquish and quit claim" all their right, title and interest in that land, south of a line running due west from the northwest corner of Missouri to the river. It frees the United States from any guarantee under the 1830 treaty of Prairie du Chien.

Article 2 sets a present of $4,520 in merchandise, shared among the tribes. The text says this was partly to compensate for leaving their fall hunts and traveling several hundred miles to attend. The page headline reads "$4,500"; the article itself says four thousand five hundred and twenty dollars.

Article 3 adds food and farm help. The Otoes and Missouries were to get five hundred bushels of corn, because their move had left them short of means to live through the spring. The Omahaws were to have one hundred acres broken up and fenced near their village. The treaty was proclaimed on February 15, 1837.
