On November 30, 1836, Indian agent Lawrence Taliaferro held a convention at St. Peters with the chiefs, braves and principal men of the Wahpaakootah, Susseton and Upper Medawakanton tribes of Sioux. It is the last of four 1836 conventions on the same strip of land between the State of Missouri and the Missouri River.

The text repeats the common background. The 1830 treaty of Prairie du Chien said the country was to be assigned to tribes living there or others the President might place there. The 1836 document says it was "desirable" that the lands become part of Missouri, with the earlier terms kept until the Indians involved gave assent.

The signers then agreed to "forever cede, relinquish and quit claim" all their right, title and interest, and freed the United States from any guarantee under the earlier treaty. The text gives as their reasons that the lands could never serve Indian purposes and that settling Indians there would bring collisions with citizens.

In return the United States agreed to furnish presents of five hundred and fifty dollars in goods, and the text says receipt was acknowledged. Seventeen leaders signed with marks. The treaty was proclaimed on February 18, 1837.
