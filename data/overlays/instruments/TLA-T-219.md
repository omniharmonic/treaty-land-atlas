On January 14, 1837, at Detroit, the Saginaw tribe of the Chippewa nation and U.S. commissioner Henry R. Schoolcraft signed a treaty. The tribe ceded a series of named tracts in Michigan, from 640 acres to 40,000 acres, on the Au Sable, Flint, Cass, Shiawassee, Saginaw and other waters.

Under Article 3 the lands were to be surveyed and offered for sale like other public lands, with the net proceeds invested for the tribe. Article 4 directed sums from that fund to pay debts, including $40,000 for debts incurred since the Treaty of Ghent. The text also says the tribe would be permitted to remove and join related tribes, or to move to a site west of the Mississippi that the United States would help secure, with the costs repaid from land proceeds.

Article 2 let the tribe live on two of the tracts for five years, with a $500 penalty for white settlers. The treaty gave the tribe a path to leave Michigan, but the sources I read do not say what happened to that plan.
