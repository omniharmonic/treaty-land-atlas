On January 17, 1837, at Doaksville near Fort Towson, Choctaw chiefs and commissioners and four Chickasaw delegates signed articles of convention. The Chickasaw were allowed to form a district inside the Choctaw country, called the Chickasaw district of the Choctaw Nation, with equal representation in the Choctaw general council.

The agreement was between the two nations, not with the United States. The Chickasaw Nation's media page says Chickasaw representatives had traveled west of the Mississippi three times without finding land to suit them, and that after Removal the Chickasaw found themselves under the Choctaw Nation's laws and constitution. The Chickasaw agreed to pay the Choctaw $530,000 for the rights, $30,000 at once and the rest invested. President Jackson sent the convention to the Senate, which approved it in February 1837.

The Chickasaw Nation describes the result as a trying time for its people. The district's boundaries are set out in Article 2 of the text.
