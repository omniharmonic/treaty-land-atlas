On February 11, 1837, in Washington, commissioner John T. Douglass signed articles with several Potawatomie chiefs, including Chee-chaw-kose and Quiquito. The chiefs spoke for their bands, who had held land in Indiana.

Article 1 has the chiefs approve two 1836 treaties, dated August 5 and September 23, that ceded certain Indiana lands. Those lands had been reserved to these bands in October 1832 treaties. The chiefs ceded their interest and agreed "to remove to a country that may be provided for them by the President of the United States, southwest of the Missouri river, within two years from the ratification of this treaty."

The United States made promises in return. Article 3 says it would convey by patent a tract on the Osage River southwest of the Missouri, remove the Potawatomi there, give them one year's subsistence after arrival, and pay the expenses of the treaty and the delegation in Washington. Article 4 has the United States buy the "five sections in the prairie, near Rock Village" reserved for Quiquito for $4,000.

The treaty was proclaimed on February 18, 1837. The page does not show whether or when the promised land was patented.
