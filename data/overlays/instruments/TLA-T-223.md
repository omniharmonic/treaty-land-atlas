On July 29, 1837, at St. Peters, Wisconsin Territory, Governor Henry Dodge and chiefs and headmen of the Chippewa nation signed a treaty. The Chippewa ceded a large tract between the Mississippi and the Chippewa and Wisconsin river country, described by boundary lines in Article 1.

In return the United States agreed to pay yearly for twenty years: $9,500 in money, $19,000 in goods, and sums for blacksmiths, farmers, provisions and tobacco. Article 5 guaranteed the Chippewa the privilege of hunting, fishing, and gathering wild rice on the ceded lands, but only during the pleasure of the President. The Supreme Court later noted that the treaty did not mention removal and was consistent with the government's aim of purchasing land.

In 1850 the President ordered the Chippewa removed from the ceded land. In Minnesota v. Mille Lacs Band (1999) the Supreme Court held that the Chippewa kept the hunting, fishing and gathering rights the 1837 treaty guaranteed.
