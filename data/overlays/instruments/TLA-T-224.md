On September 29, 1837, in Washington, Joel R. Poinsett, authorized by the President, and a delegation of Dakota (Sioux) chiefs and braves signed a treaty. The chiefs ceded all their land east of the Mississippi River and all their islands in the river.

The United States agreed to invest $300,000 in state stocks, paying at least 5 percent a year; to pay $110,000 to relatives of the signers with at least one quarter Sioux blood; to apply $90,000 to the tribe's debts; and to provide annuities in goods, farm tools and provisions for twenty years. The Senate struck Article 3. The Minnesota Historical Society says the Dakota delegation in Washington was pressured and eventually agreed to give up the lands for annual payments, and that a chief later said the whites had deceived the Dakota leaders.

The sources I read do not trace the full aftermath of this cession; the Dakota's 1851 cessions in Minnesota followed.
