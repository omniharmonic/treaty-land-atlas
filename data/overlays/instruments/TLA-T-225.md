On October 21, 1837, in Washington, Commissioner of Indian Affairs Carey A. Harris and the confederated Sac and Fox signed a treaty. The tribes ceded a tract of 1,250,000 acres lying west of and next to the land they had conveyed in the treaty of September 21, 1832, and any rights they might claim in the land ceded on July 15, 1830.

In return the United States agreed to survey the land, pay $100,000 toward the tribes' debts, provide $28,500 in goods, build two gristmills, spend $24,000 on breaking up and fencing ground, and invest $200,000 in state stocks at not less than 5 percent. The text says the debts were those found to be justly due and admitted by the Indians; it does not describe how the council went.

The 1837 cession added to the land the Sac and Fox had already ceded. In 1842 they ceded all remaining claims west of the Mississippi River and agreed to move.
