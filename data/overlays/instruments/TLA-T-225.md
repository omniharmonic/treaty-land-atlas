On October 21, 1837, Carey A. Harris, Commissioner of Indian Affairs, signed a treaty at Washington with the confederated Sacs and Foxes, through their chiefs and delegates. In Article 1 the tribes ceded two things. One was a tract containing 1,250,000 acres, lying west of and adjoining the tract they had conveyed in the treaty of September 21, 1832. The other was any right or interest in the land ceded on July 15, 1830 that they might claim under the wording of that treaty's first article.

In Article 2 the United States agreed to survey the ceded land, pay up to $100,000 of the tribes' debts that were found justly due and admitted by the Indians, and deliver $28,500 in goods. It also agreed to build two grist mills and support two millers for five years, to spend $24,000 breaking up and fencing ground on the land they kept, and to invest $200,000 in State stocks at not less than five per cent.

Article 4 says the Sacs and Foxes agreed to remove from the ceded tract within eight months of ratification. Keokuck's village was excepted: they could retain it for two years. Article 5 had the United States pay the expenses of the negotiation and of the chiefs' and delegates' trip. The treaty was proclaimed on February 21, 1838. The text does not describe how the council went, and the sources used here do not say what followed.
