On October 21, 1837, Carey A. Harris, authorized by the President, signed a treaty at Washington with the Sacs and Foxes of Missouri, through their chiefs and delegates. In Article 1 they made three cessions. The first was all right or interest in the country between the Missouri and Mississippi rivers, up to the Sac and Fox-Sioux boundary line described in the August 19, 1825 treaty. The second was the right to locate for hunting or other purposes on lands ceded in the July 15, 1830 treaty. The third was all claims under several treaties, including that of September 17, 1836, for which no appropriations had been made. The treaty states no acreage.

In Article 2 the United States agreed to pay $160,000. Of that, $2,500 was for merchandise and $100 for their interpreter, Jacques Mettez, and the balance of $157,400 was to be invested to earn not less than five per cent. The interest was to pay for a blacksmith's establishment ($1,000 a year), a farmer and tools ($1,600), and a teacher and school ($770), with the remaining $4,500 delivered in money or merchandise.

Article 3 had the United States pay the expenses of the negotiation and trip. The treaty was proclaimed on February 21, 1838. The text does not say how the council went, and the sources used here do not say what followed for this band.
