On November 23, 1837, Joshua Pilcher, a U.S. Indian agent specially authorized by the President, signed a short treaty at St. Louis with the Ioway Indians, through their chiefs and delegates. In Article 1 the Ioway ceded to the United States all the right and interest in the land ceded by the July 15, 1830 treaty that they might be entitled to claim under the wording of that treaty's second article. The treaty states no acreage. It is a release of a claim to land already ceded under the 1830 treaty.

In Article 2 the United States agreed to pay $2,500 in horses, goods and presents when they signed the treaty at St. Louis. Article 3 had the United States pay the expenses of the negotiation and of the chiefs and delegates signing, to Washington and home. Article 4 made the treaty binding on the parties once ratified by the United States.

The treaty was proclaimed on February 21, 1838. Four Ioway delegates signed with marks. The text does not describe how the council was conducted, and the sources used here do not say what followed for the Ioway.
