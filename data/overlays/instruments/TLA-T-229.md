On November 23, 1837, at St. Louis, Indian agent Joshua Pilcher and four Ioway delegates signed a short treaty. The Ioway ceded all the right and interest in the land ceded by the July 15, 1830 treaty, to the extent they might claim it under the wording of that treaty's second article.

The United States agreed to pay $2,500 in horses, goods and presents when they signed in St. Louis. The United States also paid the expenses of the negotiation and of the delegates' trip to Washington and home. The treaty became binding when ratified by the United States.

This is a release of a claim under an earlier treaty, not a new boundary. The sources I read do not describe how the council was conducted or what followed for the Ioway.
