On February 3, 1838, Carey A. Harris, Commissioner of Indian Affairs, signed a treaty at Washington with the First Christian and Orchard parties of the Oneida residing at Green Bay, through their chiefs and representatives. In Article 1 they were recorded as ceding to the United States all their title and interest in the land set apart for them in the February 8, 1831 and October 27, 1832 treaties with the Menominee. These are the Wisconsin Oneida, not the New York Oneida who signed other treaties that year.

From that land the United States reserved a tract of 100 acres for each individual, laid out to include all their settlements and improvements near Green Bay (Article 2). The treaty gives no total acreage. Under Article 3 the United States agreed to pay the Orchard party $3,000 and the First Christian party $30,500, of which $3,000 could pay for a church and parsonage. The text says the total of $33,500 was to reimburse money the Oneida had spent and to pay their chiefs and agents for buying and securing title to the land.

The treaty turned a larger shared tract into per-person reserves. It was proclaimed on May 17, 1838. The treaty text does not describe the council, and the sources used here do not say what followed for the Oneida in Wisconsin.
