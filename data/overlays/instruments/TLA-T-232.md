On February 3, 1838, in Washington, Carey A. Harris and the First Christian and Orchard parties of the Oneida residing at Green Bay signed a treaty. They ceded all their title and interest in the land set apart for them in the 1831 and 1832 treaties with the Menominee.

From the cession the United States reserved to them 100 acres for each individual, drawn to include all their settlements and improvements near Green Bay. The United States agreed to pay the Orchard party $3,000 and the First Christian party $30,500, of which $3,000 could pay for a church and parsonage. The text says the total of $33,500 was meant to reimburse money the Oneida had spent and to pay their chiefs and agents for buying and securing title to the land.

The treaty converted a larger tract into per-person reserves. The sources I read do not describe later events for the Oneida in Wisconsin.
