On October 19, 1838, at the Great Nemaha sub-agency, agent John Dougherty and the chiefs and headmen of the Ioway tribe made a treaty of cession.

Article 1 has the Ioway cede "all right or interest in the country between the Missouri and Mississippi rivers" within a boundary drawn in an 1825 treaty, to the extent recognized there, along with claims under treaties made since by the United States with the Sacs and Foxes. They were also recorded as giving up claims under treaties of 1824, 1830 and 1836, except for what the 1836 treaty had secured for them: two hundred sections of land and a list of goods and services.

Article 2 sets the price. The United States agreed to pay $157,500, to invest it, and to guarantee an annual income of at least five percent. Part of that income would go for a blacksmith shop, farming help and education under the President's direction, and the balance would be paid to the tribe in money or merchandise each October.

Article 3 adds ten houses on land the Ioway chose. The treaty was proclaimed on March 2, 1839.
