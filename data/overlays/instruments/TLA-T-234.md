On November 6, 1838, at the Forks of the Wabash in Indiana, commissioner Abel C. Pepper and the Miami tribe signed a treaty. The Miami ceded a tract south of the Wabash River described by boundary lines, plus three reservations from the 1818 St. Marys treaty and one at Seek's village from the 1826 treaty. The tribe reserved a tract supposed to contain ten square miles for the band of Me-to-sin-ia.

The United States agreed to pay $335,680: $60,000 after ratification and the rest in ten yearly installments, after claims were paid. The treaty names claims of J. B. Richardville and Francis Godfroy and sets up a commissioner to investigate other claims against the tribe, set aside $150,000 for them, at the tribe's request. A Miami account says the nation's stance on removal began to shift with this treaty, and that rising debts were a pressing issue.

The 1838 treaty did not require removal. In 1840 the Miami ceded their remaining Indiana land and agreed to move west.
