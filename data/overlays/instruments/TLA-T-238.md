On September 3, 1839, at Stockbridge, Wisconsin Territory, commissioner Albert Gallup and the Stockbridge and Munsee tribes, formerly of New York, signed a treaty. They ceded the east half of the 46,080-acre tract laid out for them east of Lake Winnebago under the 1832 treaty with the Menominee. The ceded half is stated as 23,040 acres.

The United States agreed to pay one dollar per acre. The text says part of the tribes wanted to remove west and others wanted to stay. The emigrating party received $8,767.75 for its share of the land and $3,879.30 for improvements, and the remaining $10,392.95 was paid to and invested for the 342 people who stayed, with $6,000 set as a school fund.

The treaty split the tribes' holdings between those leaving and those staying. A further Stockbridge treaty in 1848 sold the remaining township on the east side of Lake Winnebago.
