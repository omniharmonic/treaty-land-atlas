On September 3, 1839, Albert Gallup, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at Stockbridge in Wisconsin Territory with the Stockbridge and Munsee tribes, formerly of New York, who lived on Lake Winnebago. In Article 1 they ceded the east half of the 46,080-acre tract laid off for them on the east side of the lake under the October 27, 1832 treaty with the Menominee. The ceded half was to contain 23,040 acres. The United States agreed to pay one dollar per acre.

The treaty records that part of the tribes wanted to remove west and the others to stay. Article 2 paid the emigrating party $8,767.75 for their share of the whole tract. Article 3 paid them $3,879.30 for their improvements, for a total of $12,647.05 to the emigrants. The remaining $10,392.95 was paid to and invested for the 342 people who stayed at Stockbridge. Of that, $6,000 became a school fund.

Article 6 let up to three people explore the country west, with their costs borne by the emigrants. It also said the United States would pay the emigrants' removal costs west of the Mississippi and a year of subsistence when they asked. The treaty was proclaimed on May 16, 1840. The treaty text does not describe the negotiation, and the sources used here do not say what followed.
