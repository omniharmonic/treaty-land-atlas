On November 28, 1840, at the Forks of the Wabash, Samuel Milroy and Allen Hamilton, acting unofficially as commissioners, and Miami chiefs, warriors and headmen signed a treaty. The Miami ceded "the residue of the Big Reserve," described as all of their remaining lands in Indiana.

The United States agreed to pay $550,000. Of that, $300,000 was set aside to pay the tribe's debts, after a commissioner reviewed claims, and the remaining $250,000 was to be paid in twenty yearly installments. The treaty names payments of $25,000 to John B. Richardville and $15,000 to the executor of Francis Godfroy. A Miami account describes the nation's rising debts and says white Americans were surrounding the Miami and encroaching on the land they still held.

The treaty required the Miami to move to the country assigned west of the Mississippi within five years, with the United States paying the costs and rations for twelve months. The treaty assigned a western tract estimated at 500,000 acres.
