On March 17, 1842, at Upper Sandusky, Ohio, commissioner John Johnston and the Wyandott Nation signed a treaty. The Wyandot ceded the residue of the large reserve in Crawford County, Ohio, which was all their remaining land in the state, about 109,144 acres, and their Wyandotte Reserve in Michigan, about 4,996 acres.

In return the United States granted a tract of 148,000 acres west of the Mississippi, promised a perpetual annuity of $17,500, and agreed to pay for their improvements. The chiefs agreed to remove their whole people west of the Mississippi. The Wyandot Nation of Kansas says Johnston, sent as agent in 1841, firmly believed removal was in the Wyandot's best interest, and that the Wyandot voted on March 17, 1842 to accept most of his proposals.

The same source says 664 people left the Grand Reserve on July 12, 1843. In 1850 the Wyandot gave up their claim to the 148,000-acre tract for $185,000.
