On May 20, 1842, at Buffalo Creek, New York, U.S. commissioner Ambrose Spencer and chiefs, headmen and warriors of the Seneca nation signed a treaty. It was made with an indenture between the Seneca and Thomas Ogden and Joseph Fellows, the holders of the 1838 purchase rights, and it responds to the Treaty of Buffalo Creek of January 15, 1838.

The text recites that in 1838 the Seneca had granted four reservations to Ogden and Fellows for $202,000, including Buffalo Creek (estimated at 49,920 acres) and Cattaraugus. In 1842 Ogden and Fellows agreed that the Seneca could keep the Cattaraugus and Allegany Reservations. The Buffalo Creek and Tonawanda tracts were to be given up to Ogden and Fellows, with payments to owners of improvements.

The Tonawanda Seneca contested the 1838 treaty as to their lands, according to New York court-history summaries. Those summaries say a later settlement let the Seneca give up their Kansas rights and use federal funds to purchase their lands from the Ogden company.
