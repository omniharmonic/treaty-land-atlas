On October 11, 1842, John Chambers, commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at the Sac and Fox agency in Iowa Territory with the confederated tribes of Sacs and Foxes, represented by their chiefs, headmen and braves. In Article I they were recorded as ceding forever all the lands west of the Mississippi River in which they had any claim, title or interest. They reserved the right to occupy for three years the part west of a north-south line running from the "painted or red rocks" on the White Breast fork of the Des Moines River. The treaty states no acreage.

In Article II the United States agreed to pay each year five per cent interest on $800,000 and to pay the debts in an attached schedule, $258,566.34. It also agreed that the President would assign a tract on the Missouri River or its waters as a permanent home for them and their descendants. The tribes were to pay for moving the blacksmith and gunsmith shops, except for the part the United States already owed under earlier treaties.

In Article III the Sacs and Foxes were bound by the treaty to move west of the line by May 1 of the next year. They were bound by the treaty to move to the assigned land when their chiefs consented, and to move at their own expense if they had not left within three years. The United States agreed to furnish provisions for the journey. The treaty was proclaimed on March 23, 1843. The sources used here do not describe the removal itself.
