On October 11, 1842, at the Sac and Fox agency in Iowa Territory, commissioner John Chambers and the confederated Sac and Fox signed a treaty. The tribes ceded forever all the lands west of the Mississippi River to which they had any claim, reserving the right to occupy for three years the part west of a line running north-south from the painted or red rocks on the White Breast fork of the Des Moines River.

The United States agreed to pay yearly 5 percent interest on $800,000 and to pay the debts listed in an attached schedule, $258,566.34. The President would assign a tract on the Missouri River or its waters as a permanent home. The tribes agreed to move west of the line by May 1 of the next year and to the assigned land when the chiefs consented, and to move at their own expense if they stayed past three years.

The treaty converted a large Iowa cession into a promise of land elsewhere. The sources I read do not trace what followed.
