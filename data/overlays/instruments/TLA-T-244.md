The treaty of January 4, 1845, was made at the Creek Agency by United States commissioners with the Creek and Seminole tribes. It deals with where Seminoles would live and who governed them.

The preamble recalls that a Creek treaty of 1833 had said Seminoles would be a constituent part of the Creek nation. Many had settled in the Creek country, but others, "a large portion of the tribe," had refused. They said they would not submit to Creek laws and feared losing property to Creek authorities. The United States complained that those outside had settled on land secured to other tribes and committed depredations.

Article 1 gave Seminoles the right to settle in a body or separately anywhere in the Creek country, make their own town regulations subject to the general control of the Creek council, in which they would be represented, and have no distinction made between the two tribes except in managing money. Article 2 required Seminoles not yet there to remove "immediately" after ratification and settle permanently.

In Article 4 the United States, noting Creek dissatisfaction with their 1833 boundaries, agreed to an extra $3,000 yearly for education for twenty years. The treaty was proclaimed on July 18, 1845.
