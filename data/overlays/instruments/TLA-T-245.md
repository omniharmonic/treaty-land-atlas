On January 14, 1846, at the Methodist Mission in the Kansas country, U.S. commissioners Thomas H. Harvey and Richard W. Cummins and the Kansas tribe signed a treaty. The tribe ceded 2,000,000 acres on the east part of its country, running the full 30-mile width and west for quantity.

The United States agreed to pay $202,000. Of that, $200,000 was funded at 5 percent with the interest paid yearly for thirty years, and the remaining $2,000 covered treaty expenses, $400 to the Methodist mission for improvements, and a mill. The Kaw Nation's history describes earlier annuities as seldom delivered or owed to traders, and says railroad, town and land speculators coveted the land.

The Kaw Nation says the 1846 treaty reduced its territory to 256,000 acres at Council Grove. The 1859 treaty took Council Grove and a federal act of May 27, 1872 moved the Kanza to a 100,137-acre site in what became Oklahoma.
