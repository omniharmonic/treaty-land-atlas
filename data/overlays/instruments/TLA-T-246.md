On May 15, 1846, U.S. commissioners P. M. Butler and M. G. Lewis met chiefs, counsellors and warriors of the Comanche, Caddo, Wichita, Waco and several other tribes at Council Springs, in Robinson County, Texas, near the Brazos River.

Article 1 has the signers acknowledge themselves "to be under the protection of the United States, and of no other power, state, or sovereignty whatever." Article 2 gives the United States "the sole and exclusive right of regulating trade and intercourse" with them, and bars unlicensed traders. Articles 3 and 5 are marked "stricken out" on the page.

Other terms covered prisoners. The tribes were to deliver up white persons and negroes held by them, for which the United States would pay a fair compensation, while prisoners taken from them by Texas or the United States were to be returned to them. The treaty also set rules on punishing murder, robbery and horse stealing under state or territorial law.

In return the United States agreed to presents of goods worth ten thousand dollars, and blacksmiths, school-teachers and permission for preachers to reside among them. It was proclaimed on March 8, 1847. The page shows no land cession.
