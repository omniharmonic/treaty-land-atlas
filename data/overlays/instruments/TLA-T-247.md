The 1846 treaty was concluded at the agency on the Missouri River near Council Bluffs on June 5 and at Pottawatomie Creek near the Osage River on June 17. Three U.S. commissioners and the various bands of the Pottawatomie, Chippewa and Ottawa signed. The tribes sold all lands they claimed north of the Missouri in Iowa Territory and near the Osage River, without affecting earlier grants and reservations.

The United States agreed to pay $850,000 and to grant 576,000 acres, thirty miles square, on both sides of the Kansas River. That tract was the eastern part of the land the Kansa had ceded by the January 1846 treaty. The Potawatomi were to pay the United States $87,000 for it, out of the $850,000, and to remove within two years of ratification. The text set aside $30,000 for removal costs and $50,000 to settle their affairs and pay debts before leaving.
