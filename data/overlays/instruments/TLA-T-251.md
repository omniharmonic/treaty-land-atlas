On August 21, 1847, at Leech Lake, U.S. commissioners Isaac A. Verplank and Henry M. Rice and the Pillager band of Chippewa signed a treaty. The Pillager band sold and ceded the country within boundaries running from the south end of Otter Tail Lake along the Sioux-Chippewa boundary to Long Prairie River, up the Crow Wing and Leaf Rivers, and back to the starting point.

Article 3 said the ceded country would be held by the United States as Indian land until otherwise ordered by the President. In return the United States agreed to give the band yearly for five years a list of goods, including blankets, cloth, prints, twine, kettles, tobacco and salt.

The cession was one of a set in 1847 that the 1848 Menominee treaty names as the Menominee's new home, apart from land assigned to the Winnebago. The sources I read do not describe how the Pillager council was conducted.
