On August 21, 1847, Isaac A. Verplank and Henry M. Rice, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty at Leech Lake with the Pillager Band of Chippewa Indians, through their chiefs, head-men and warriors. In Article 2 the Pillager band sold and ceded all the country within boundaries beginning at the south end of Otter Tail Lake. The line ran south along the Sioux-Chippewa boundary to the Long Prairie River, up that river to the Crow Wing River, up the Crow Wing to the Leaf River, and back to the start. The treaty states no acreage.

Article 3 says the ceded country would be held by the United States as Indian land until otherwise ordered by the President. In Article 4 the United States agreed to furnish the band each year for five years a list of goods: Mackinaw blankets, cloth, prints, twine, thread, combs, needles, looking-glasses, vermilion, kettles, 500 pounds of tobacco and five barrels of salt. At the first payment the band was also to get 200 beaver traps and 75 north-west guns as a present.

The treaty was proclaimed on April 7, 1848. The treaty text does not describe how the council went, and the sources used here do not say what followed.
