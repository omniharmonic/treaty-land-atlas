On August 6, 1848, at Fort Childs near the head of Grand Island on the Platte River, Lieutenant-Colonel Ludwell E. Powell, commanding a battalion of Missouri Mounted Volunteers en route to Oregon, signed for the United States. He and the chiefs and head men of four Pawnee bands (Grand Pawnee, Pawnee Loups, Pawnee Republicans and Pawnee Tappage) signed an agreement and convention.

The Pawnee ceded a tract starting five miles west of the post, running north to the crest of the bluffs, east to the end of Grand Island (supposed about sixty miles), and back to the river. The United States had paid, through a quartermaster, $2,000 in goods and merchandise on signing. The United States also got the right to use hard timber on Wood River. The Pawnee promised friendship and not to molest white citizens.

The OSU page lists it as ratified January 8, 1849. The sources I read do not describe how the council was conducted or its effects.
