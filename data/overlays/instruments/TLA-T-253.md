On October 18, 1848, at Lake Powawhaykonnay, Wisconsin, U.S. commissioner William Medill and the Menomonee tribe signed a treaty. The Menominee ceded, sold and relinquished all their lands in the State of Wisconsin, wherever situated.

In return the United States gave them as a home the country ceded by the Chippewa in the treaties of August 2 and August 21, 1847 that was not assigned to the Winnebago, guaranteed to contain not less than 600,000 acres. It agreed to pay $350,000, including $30,000 to the chiefs to prepare for removal, $40,000 to mixed-blood persons, and $20,000 for their removal and $20,000 for subsistence, which the tribe agreed to bear itself.

An 1854 treaty text records "great unwillingness on the part of said Indians to remove" west of the Mississippi to Crow Wing and says the President consented to their locating temporarily on the Wolf and Oconto Rivers.
