On November 24, 1848, at Stockbridge, Wisconsin, U.S. commissioners and the Stockbridge tribe signed an agreement. The Stockbridge renounced benefits under an 1843 act of Congress, acknowledged themselves under the protection and guardianship of the United States, and sold and relinquished the township of land on the east side of Lake Winnebago that the 1831 Menominee treaty had granted them.

The United States agreed to pay $16,500 to settle affairs and prepare a new home, $14,504.85 for improvements, and $3,000 for the expenses of the sachem and headmen since 1843. $16,500 was to be invested at 5 percent. The tribe could stay on the land for one year after ratification and would then remove to the country set apart for them or other land west of the Mississippi they could secure, with removal costs paid.

A supplemental article addressed the tribe's claims for lands in Indiana and Wisconsin that it said it had lost without its consent.
