On September 9, 1849, John M. Washington, governor of New Mexico and lieutenant-colonel commanding U.S. troops in New Mexico, and Indian agent James S. Calhoun signed an agreement with Mariano Martinez, head chief, and Chapitone, second chief, for the Navajo tribe. The text has the Navajo acknowledge that the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo placed them under the exclusive jurisdiction and protection of the United States.

The treaty promised perpetual peace, made U.S. trade and intercourse laws binding on the Navajo, required the return of captives and stolen property by October 9, 1850, allowed free passage through Navajo territory, and said the United States would establish military posts and trading houses. Article 9 left it to the United States to designate and adjust the Navajo's territorial boundaries. An NPS history says Washington came within eight or nine miles of Canyon de Chelly, and that his men killed the Navajo leader Narbona during an argument over a stolen horse.

The same history says the Navajo were raiding again, the treaty broken, before Washington reached Santa Fe.
