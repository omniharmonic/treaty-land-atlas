On December 30, 1849, James S. Calhoun, Indian agent at Santa Fe, signed articles with principal and subordinate chiefs representing the Utah (Ute) tribe. The Ute acknowledged themselves lawfully and exclusively under U.S. jurisdiction and to its power and authority they "unconditionally submit."

The treaty ended hostilities, required the return of captives and stolen property by March 1, 1850, and extended U.S. trade and intercourse laws over the Ute. Article 4 said the territory occupied by the Utahs was annexed to New Mexico until the government of the United States ordered otherwise. Article 5 allowed free passage through Ute territory, Article 6 provided for military posts and agencies, and Article 7 left it to the United States to limit the Ute's boundaries.

The treaty ceded no named tract. It claimed U.S. jurisdiction over Ute territory. The sources I read do not describe how the signers were chosen or what followed.
