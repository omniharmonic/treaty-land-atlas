On December 30, 1849, James S. Calhoun, Indian agent at Santa Fe, signed a treaty as United States commissioner with twenty-nine named principal and subordinate chiefs, representing the Utah (Ute) tribe. In Article I the Utah tribe acknowledged that they were lawfully and exclusively under the jurisdiction of the United States and that "to its power and authority they now unconditionally submit."

Article II ended hostilities and set perpetual peace. Article III required the return of captives and stolen property at Abiquiu by March 1, 1850. Article IV extended the laws regulating trade and intercourse with Indians over the Utahs and annexed the territory they occupied to New Mexico, "until the Government of the United States shall otherwise order." Article V allowed free passage through their territory. Article VI provided for military posts, agencies and trading houses.

In Article VII the Utahs agreed that the United States should, at its earliest convenience, designate, settle and adjust their territorial boundaries. They also bound themselves not to leave their accustomed homes unless an agent permitted it, and to confine themselves to the limits assigned. The treaty cedes no named tract. It was ratified and proclaimed on September 9, 1850. The treaty text does not say how the signers were chosen or what followed.
