On April 1, 1850, in Washington, a U.S. commissioner and Wyandot delegates signed a treaty. The Wyandot released all claim to the 148,000 acres that the March 17, 1842 treaty had agreed to assign to them west of the Mississippi.

The United States agreed to pay $185,000, at $1.25 an acre. Of that, $100,000 was to be invested in U.S. stocks at 5 percent, the interest to be paid to the Wyandot, for the purpose of enabling them to pay their just debts, including what was due to the Delawares for the purchase of their lands. The remaining $85,000 was to be paid to the nation or on its drafts. The United States also paid the expenses of the negotiation and of the delegation.

The treaty settled the 1842 promise of a tract by payment. The sources I read do not describe how the nation used the funds.
