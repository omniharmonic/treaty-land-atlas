On July 23, 1851, at Traverse des Sioux on the Minnesota River, commissioners Luke Lea and Alexander Ramsey and the Sisseton and Wahpeton bands of Dakota signed a treaty. The bands ceded all their lands in Iowa and all their lands in Minnesota Territory east of a line running from the Red River of the North to the Sioux River. The Minnesota Historical Society says that with the Treaty of Mendota it opened twenty-four million acres to settlers.

The United States agreed to pay $1,665,000, with $275,000 for the chiefs' debts and removal, and the rest mostly held in trust at 5 percent. The Senate struck Article 3, which had defined the reservation. Treaties Matter quotes the historian Lucille Kane saying that the traders looked to the funds the Indians would receive for their lands.

MNopedia says the treaties were a step toward the loss of the Dakota homeland and the US-Dakota War of 1862.
