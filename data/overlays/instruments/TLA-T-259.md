On August 5, 1851, at Mendota in Minnesota Territory, commissioners Luke Lea and Alexander Ramsey and the Mdewakanton and Wahpekute bands of Dakota signed a treaty. The bands ceded all their lands and all claim to any lands whatever in Minnesota Territory or Iowa.

The United States agreed to pay $1,410,000, including $220,000 for the chiefs' engagements and removal, $30,000 for schools and farms, and $1,160,000 held in trust with 5 percent interest for fifty years. The Senate struck Article 3. MNopedia says the Dakota owed Henry Sibley and other traders a great deal of money, and that some sources suggest leaders and agents were bribed into signing the traders' papers.

MNopedia says the two 1851 treaties were a step toward the loss of the Dakota homeland and the US-Dakota War of 1862. The forty-five chiefs of the lower bands signed the amended treaty on September 4, 1852.
