On August 5, 1851, Luke Lea and Alexander Ramsey, commissioners for the United States, signed a treaty at Mendota in Minnesota Territory with the Mdewakanton and Wahpekute bands of Dakota. In Article 2 the bands were recorded as ceding and relinquishing all their lands and all their claim to any lands whatever in Minnesota Territory or Iowa. In Article 4 the United States agreed to pay $1,410,000. That included $220,000 to the chiefs to settle their affairs and move, and $30,000 for schools, mills and farms. The other $1,160,000 was to stay in trust, with five per cent interest paid for fifty years. The Senate struck out Article 3.

The Minnesota Historical Society says the commissioners told the Mdewakanton and Wahpekute that, with the upper bands' land sold, they would be surrounded by white colonists and eventually forced to give up their land. It says the Dakota owed Henry Sibley and other traders a great deal of money. In November 1851 the leaders of the two bands signed traders' papers, and "some sources suggest that tribal leaders and their agents were bribed."

MNopedia says the two 1851 treaties were "a step towards the loss of their homeland and the US-Dakota War of 1862." The Senate approved them, with changes, on June 23, 1852, and forty-five chiefs of the lower bands signed the amended treaty on September 4, 1852. The OSU page shows the treaty proclaimed on February 24, 1853.
