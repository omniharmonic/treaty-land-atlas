On June 22, 1852, in Washington, commissioner Kenton Harper and three Chickasaw commissioners signed a treaty that dealt mainly with money and land sales, not a new cession. The page shows it ratified August 13, 1852, and proclaimed February 24, 1853.

Article 1 has the Chickasaw "acknowledge themselves to be under the guardianship of the United States" and says a U.S. agent would continue to live among them. Article 2 says the sale of land ceded under the 1832 treaty had been costing more than it brought in, so the unsold remainder was to be sold as soon as practicable in the way least expensive to the Chickasaws.

Article 3 sent the Chickasaw claim to a four-mile-square reservation on the Sandy River in Tennessee to the Secretary of the Interior, who would decide what, if anything, was owed, at no more than $1.25 an acre. Article 4 ordered an accounting of their money from the 1832 and 1834 treaties, with their exceptions decided by the Secretary.

Article 5 has the United States keep the national fund "in trust" and invested, with interest paid yearly. Article 7 barred paying any claim from that fund unless the Chickasaw General Council allowed it first.
