On July 27, 1853, Thomas Fitzpatrick, Indian agent and sole commissioner for the United States, signed a treaty at Fort Atkinson, in the Indian Territory, with the Comanche, Kiowa and Apache tribes, described as inhabiting the territory south of the Arkansas River. In Articles 1 and 2 peace was to be perpetual between the United States and the tribes, and among the tribes themselves. In Article 5 they agreed to stop warlike incursions into the Mexican provinces and to restore captives taken from there.

In Article 3 the tribes recognized the right of the United States to lay off and mark out roads or highways, make reservations of land needed for them, locate depots, and establish military and other posts within their territories. EBSCO's research summary says the treaty aimed to ease white passage westward and "facilitate the building of a transcontinental railroad through Indian lands." The treaty does not cede land by name.

In Article 6 the United States agreed to deliver $18,000 a year for ten years, with five more if the President thought it advisable. The payment was for the tribes' agreements, for the losses they might suffer from the travel of United States citizens through their territories, and for their support. In Article 7 the United States promised to protect them from depredations by its people and compensate them for any injuries. Article 8 let annuities be withheld for violations. The treaty was ratified on April 12, 1854 and proclaimed on February 12, 1855.
