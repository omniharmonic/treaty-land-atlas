On September 10, 1853, at Table Rock near the Rogue River, Superintendent Joel Palmer and agent Samuel Culver signed a treaty with Chief Jo-aps-er-ka-har and other headmen of the Rogue River tribe. They ceded their lands in the upper Rogue Valley. They were allowed to "occupy temporarily" a tract at the foot of the Table Rocks as an Indian reserve until the President chose a permanent home. The United States agreed to pay $60,000, but kept $15,000 to pay for settlers' property destroyed in the war.

The treaty followed a ceasefire after a summer of fighting around Jacksonville. The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians' history says the treaty covered three language groups and that the ancestors of its members were forced to sign seven treaties over two years, ceding the area between the Columbia River and the Siskiyous. The United States wanted the valley open to miners and settlers.

The Senate ratified the treaty on April 12, 1854, and it was proclaimed in 1855. Fighting resumed in 1855. The Siletz history says the Table Rock reserve was found unworkable, and the Rogue Valley peoples were removed to the Coast (Siletz) Reservation in 1856.
