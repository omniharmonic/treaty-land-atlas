On September 19, 1853, nine days after the Table Rock treaty, Superintendent Joel Palmer signed a treaty with the Cow Creek band of Umpqua Indians at Cow Creek in the Umpqua Valley, Oregon Territory. The band ceded and relinquished its claim to a described tract. It could occupy a smaller tract on Cow Creek as an Indian reserve until the President selected a permanent home.

The United States agreed to pay $12,000: $1,000 in goods, and $11,000 in twenty annual installments of $550. It also promised two dwelling houses, a fenced and plowed five-acre field, and farms if the President chose. The band agreed to let travelers pass and to protect U.S. agents.

The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians' history says the Cow Creek and Rogue River treaties moved together through ratification. Both were ratified on April 12, 1854 and proclaimed on February 5, 1855. It adds that the Senate left the choice of a permanent reservation to the President, and that the ancestors of the Siletz peoples were forced to sign seven treaties ceding about 15 million acres. The government later chose to gather scattered bands onto the Coast (Siletz) Reservation. The input lists no linked violations or cases.
