On March 15, 1854, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and seven chiefs of the "confederate tribes of Ottoe and Missouria Indians" signed a treaty. The tribes agreed to cede to the United States "all their country west of the Missouri River," except a strip ten miles wide on the Big Blue River. They promised to vacate the ceded country within a year after the United States made provision, and to give up claims under earlier treaties.

The United States agreed to pay $20,000 a year for three years, then smaller sums in stages over the following decades, plus $20,000 for the move and $4,000 at once for provisions and farming. The Kansas Historical Society describes Manypenny's spring 1854 negotiations in the Kansas and Nebraska territories as a push to buy tribal land, with the surplus to be resold to railroad companies, settlers and speculators. It adds that this ignored a clause in the Kansas-Nebraska Act not to interfere with Indian treaties.
