On May 10, 1854, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and Shawnee delegates signed a treaty for the "now united tribe" of Shawnee. They ceded the tract west of Missouri that a federal deed had conveyed to the tribe in 1844. The treaty describes it by metes and bounds as "estimated to contain sixteen hundred thousand acres, more or less." The United States ceded back 200,000 acres, to be selected near the Missouri line and divided among individuals and families, with small tracts for mission schools and churches.

The United States agreed to pay $829,000, including $700,000 in seven annual installments. The Kansas Historical Society says Manypenny's 1854 treaties promoted allotment to individual members, with excess land bought by the government and sold to railroad companies, settlers and speculators. It adds that this ignored a clause in the Kansas-Nebraska Act and opened many Kansas lands to settlement.

The Society records that the 1887 Dawes Act formalized allotment and that many holdings were lost by mortgage, lease or sale. Manypenny later wrote that he "committed a high crime." The sources I read do not describe what followed for the Shawnee in particular.
