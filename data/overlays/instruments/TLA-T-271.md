On May 18, 1854, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and five delegates of the Sacs and Foxes of Missouri signed a treaty. They ceded the lower half of the strip on the Missouri River assigned to them and the Ioways in 1836, "saving and reserving fifty sections, of six hundred and forty acres each," to be selected in the western part and set off by metes and bounds. If the delegates and agent could not find that much in one body, they could extend the selection onto public land to the west or northwest, and any part chosen outside the reservation reduced the reserve by the same amount.

In payment the United States agreed to pay $48,000 in installments, starting with $15,000 in October 1854 and 1855. The Kansas Historical Society says Manypenny's 1854 treaties led 11 Kansas tribes to cede nearly 13 million acres, ignoring a Kansas-Nebraska Act clause, and opened Kansas to settlers.

The Society also lists a Sac and Fox reservation in Brown County among the four federally recognized reservations in Kansas today.
