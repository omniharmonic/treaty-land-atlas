On May 30, 1854, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and delegates of the Kaskaskia, Peoria, Piankeshaw and Wea signed a treaty. The treaty first recognized the four tribes, which had recently "united themselves into a single tribe." The tribe then ceded the tracts assigned to the groups in treaties of October 1832. They reserved "a quantity of land equal to one hundred and sixty acres for each soul in said united tribe," according to a schedule, plus ten sections held in common.

A section of 640 acres went to the American Indian Mission Association. The United States agreed to pay $66,000 in six annual installments, with $2,000 each for education and for settling affairs.

The Kansas Historical Society says Manypenny's 1854 treaties led 11 Kansas tribes to cede nearly 13 million acres and favored allotment: 160 acres per person on the tribal rolls, with the excess bought by the government for resale to railroad companies, settlers and speculators. It records that many allotted tribal lands were later mortgaged, leased or sold to whites after the 1887 Dawes Act. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on August 2, 1854.
