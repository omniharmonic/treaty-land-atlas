On June 5, 1854, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and five Miami delegates signed a treaty, with five Miami living in Indiana present and assenting. The Miami ceded the tract assigned to them in Kansas in 1841, "bounded on the east by the State of Missouri" and "estimated to contain five hundred thousand acres." They excepted and reserved 70,000 acres for homes and a section of 640 acres for school purposes. The ceded and reserved land would be surveyed, and Miami living on the lands would select individual holdings of 200 acres per person.

The United States agreed to pay $200,000 in installments and an invested fund. The treaty also relieved the United States of paying a permanent annuity of $25,000. The Kansas Historical Society says Manypenny's 1854 treaties promoted allotment, with excess property bought by the government and sold to railroad companies, settlers and speculators, and that he later regretted it, writing that he had "committed a high crime."

The Society adds that the 1887 Dawes Act formalized allotment and that many tribal holdings were mortgaged, leased or sold to whites. The sources I read do not follow the Miami specifically.
