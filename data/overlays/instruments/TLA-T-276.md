This agreement was made on November 4, 1854, at Doaksville, near Fort Towson, in the Choctaw country. Unusually, the parties were two Native nations, not a Native nation and a United States commissioner. Five commissioners named by the Choctaw tribe and five named by the Chickasaw tribe signed. It was made "subject to the approval of the President and the Senate of the United States."

The preamble explains that a convention made at the same place on January 17, 1837, had set up a Chickasaw district within the Choctaw nation. "Difficulties have arisen" over the boundary line between that district and the other Choctaw districts, and the tribes wanted "no longer" to have a dispute about it.

Article 1 replaces the 1837 boundaries. It describes a new line starting on the north bank of the Red River at the mouth of Island Bayou, running northwest along that bayou, then north to the Canadian River, west along the Canadian to the one hundredth degree of west longitude, and south to the Red River. A small offset was to keep Allen's or Wa-pa-nacka academy two miles inside the Chickasaw district.

Article 2 had the Chickasaws hire a surveyor and pay all costs of marking the eastern line by August 1, 1855. A commissioner appointed by the chief of each Choctaw district would supervise. The page shows it ratified February 28, 1855 and proclaimed April 10, 1855.
