On November 18, 1854, at a council ground opposite the mouth of Applegate Creek on the Rogue River, Joel Palmer signed an agreement with chiefs and headmen of the Quil-si-eton and Na-hel-ta bands of the Chasta, the Cow-nan-ti-co, Sa-cher-i-ton and Na-al-ye bands of the Scoton, and the Grave Creek band of the Umpqua. They ceded all of their country, described by landmarks south of the Rogue River to the Oregon boundary. They agreed to "remove to such portion of the Table Rock reserve" as the superintendent chose, or to any reserve the President directed.

The United States agreed to pay $2,000 a year for fifteen years and to spend a further $6,500 on provisions. It also set aside $5,000 for claims of settlers for lost property.

The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians' history says the ancestors of its members were forced to sign seven treaties in two years, ceding about 15 million acres. The Siletz history says Superintendent Palmer then wrote that the Table Rock reservation could not be permanent and that the Rogue Valley tribes would have to remove to Siletz also. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 3, 1855.
