On November 29, 1854, at Calapooia Creek in Douglas County, Oregon Territory, Joel Palmer signed an agreement with Napesa (Louis), head chief, and other chiefs of the confederated bands of the Umpqua and the Calapooias residing in the Umpqua Valley. They ceded all their country, from the Cascade crest to the divide between the Umpqua and Coos rivers. A smaller area was held as an Indian reservation for them and any other bands the government assigned there, and they could be removed from it if that became expedient.

For the cession the United States agreed to annual payments over twenty years, falling from $3,000 to $1,000 a year, plus $10,000 for comfort and subsistence. It also provided for allotments sized by family, from 20 acres for a single adult.

The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians' history says Superintendent Palmer organized the removal of the Umpquas, Kalapuyas and Molallas in January 1856 to a temporary camp on the south fork of the Yamhill River, now Grand Ronde. It adds that the ancestors of its members were forced to sign seven treaties in two years, and that the government chose to concentrate scattered tribes on one reservation away from the settlers. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 3, 1855.
