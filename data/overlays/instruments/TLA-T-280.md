This short agreement, made at Nebraska City on December 9, 1854, is a supplement to a treaty of March 15, 1854. Agent George Hepner signed for the United States. The chiefs and headmen of the confederate Ottoe and Missouria tribes signed with marks.

The preamble explains the problem. The earlier treaty had the tribes cede all their country west of the Missouri River except a strip ten miles wide on the waters of the Big Blue River. After exploring it, the tribes found "that they had been mistaken as to the location thereof." Much the larger part, nearly all of it, lay west of the Big Blue and lacked enough timber. The tribes were dissatisfied, and the United States was "desirous of removing all cause of complaint."

The single article changes the starting point of the reservation to a point five miles due east of the old one. From there the line runs west twenty-five miles, north ten miles, then back east. That ground was to be the "reservation and home" of the two tribes in place of the one first described.

The page lists the record as 10 Stat. 1130 and 11 Stat. 605. It was ratified February 28, 1855, and proclaimed April 10, 1855.
