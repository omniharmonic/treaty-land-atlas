The Willamette Valley treaty was made at Dayton, Oregon Territory, between Superintendent Joel Palmer and chiefs of the confederated bands of Kalapuya (Calapooias) and others. Palmer and the first chiefs signed on January 4, 1855; more bands added their assent on January 9, 10 and 22.

Article 1 has the bands cede "all their right, title, and claim to all and every part of the country" inside boundaries running from the Columbia River south, west to the Coast Range summit, along the Calapooia Mountains and back north along the Cascade summit. They could stay inside the ceded country on temporary reserves until a permanent home was designated, and then agreed to vacate and move to it.

Article 2 promised annual payments that decline over twenty years: $10,000 a year for five years, then $8,000, $6,500 and $5,500. It added $50,000 for goods, stock, tools, mills, shops, schools and removal costs. Article 3 added a physician, school-teacher, blacksmith and farming superintendent for five years after removal.

Article 4 let the President survey the permanent home into lots of twenty to eighty or more acres by family size and issue patents that could not be sold or leased for more than two years. Ratified March 3, 1855, proclaimed April 10, 1855.
