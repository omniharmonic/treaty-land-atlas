On January 31, 1855, in Washington, Commissioner George W. Manypenny and six Wyandott chiefs and delegates signed a treaty. Article 1 provided that "their organization, and their relations with the United States as an Indian tribe shall be dissolved and terminated on the ratification of this agreement." The Wyandotts were declared citizens of the United States, subject to the laws of the United States and of Kansas Territory. Those who wanted to keep their Indian status could apply to commissioners. The tribal land was to be divided among members.

The treaty's own premise was that the Wyandott "having become sufficiently advanced in civilization" were "desirous of becoming citizens." This fits the Kansas Historical Society's account of Manypenny's 1854-55 policy of allotting land to individuals and selling the surplus to settlers.

The 1867 treaty with the Seneca and others later said that a portion of the Wyandottes, "although taking lands in severalty, have sold said lands, and are still poor." That treaty set apart land ceded by the Senecas as a home for Wyandottes who chose to "begin anew a tribal existence." The OSU treaty database lists ratification on February 20, 1855.
