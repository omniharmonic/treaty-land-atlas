On June 11, 1855, at Camp Stevens in the Walla Walla Valley, Isaac Stevens and Joel Palmer signed a treaty with chiefs and delegates of the Nez Perce, who lived partly in Oregon and partly in Washington. The Nez Perce were recorded as ceding their country and kept a reservation for their exclusive use. They secured fishing "at all usual and accustomed places" and hunting rights, and the United States promised $200,000. Encyclopedia.com says they were recorded as ceding most of 13 million acres of ancestral territory in exchange for a guarantee that 7.5 million acres would remain a reservation.

HistoryLink says the Nez Perce were divided at the council, with Lawyer favoring a treaty and Looking Glass opposed. Stevens used promised gifts and threats of force, and refused a delay the Indians requested.

Encyclopedia.com reports that gold was found on Nez Perce land in the early 1860s, settlers rushed in, and an 1863 treaty, called the Thief Treaty, reduced the reservation to 780,000 acres, excluding Chief Joseph's Wallowa Valley home territory. Joseph's band was ordered out in 1877, leading to the Nez Perce War.
