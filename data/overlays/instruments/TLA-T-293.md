On June 25, 1855, at Wasco near The Dalles, Superintendent Joel Palmer signed a treaty with the chiefs and head men of the confederated bands of the Walla Walla and the Wasco, living in Middle Oregon. They were recorded as ceding their country, bounded by the Columbia River, the Cascade crest, the 44th parallel and the Blue Mountains. A reservation was set apart "until otherwise directed by the President." The treaty secured the exclusive right to fish in streams on the reservation and fishing at other "usual and accustomed stations" in common with citizens. The United States promised annual payments falling from $8,000 to $2,000 over twenty years.

The Oregon Blue Book says the tribes were recorded as ceding ten million acres while reserving rights to fish, hunt, gather berries and dig roots, and that the Warm Springs Reservation is now 644,000 acres.

A 2019 Senate report says that in 1865 a supplemental treaty restricted off-reservation rights, and it recommended a bill to nullify the 1865 treaty and leave the 1855 treaty intact. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 8, 1859.
