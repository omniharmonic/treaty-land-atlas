Governor Isaac Stevens signed the Treaty of Olympia with chiefs, headmen and delegates of the Quinault and Quileute. HistoryLink says the Quinault signed on July 1, 1855, and the Hoh and Quileute on January 25, 1856. The tribes ceded the coast between the Makah lands and the Chehalis-Quinault ridge. Article 2 provided that tracts "sufficient for their wants" would be selected by the President and surveyed later, and that the tribes would move there within a year of ratification. The United States promised $25,000 in declining payments and secured fishing at "usual and accustomed grounds and stations."

The treaty did not name a reservation. HistoryLink reports Congress ratified it in 1859. The National Park Service says Stevens pressured Puget Sound tribes into signing treaties in 1854 and 1855, and the Supreme Court's 1979 opinion notes that the interpreter used a "chinook jargon" with a simple 300-word vocabulary.

The treaty's fishing clause was among those at issue in Washington v. Fishing Vessel Association (1979).
