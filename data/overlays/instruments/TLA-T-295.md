On July 16, 1855, at Hell Gate in the Bitter Root Valley, Governor Isaac Stevens signed a treaty with chiefs and delegates of the confederated Flathead, Kootenay and Upper Pend d'Oreille tribes, who were declared a nation under head chief Victor. They ceded a tract that began on the Rocky Mountain crest at the 49th parallel. A general reservation was reserved, with the Bitter Root Valley to be surveyed and possibly set aside for the Flathead. The United States promised $120,000 and secured fishing "at all usual and accustomed places."

Char-Koosta News, the Salish and Kootenai tribal newspaper, says Stevens wanted to acquire large areas of tribal land for settlement and railroad expansion, that interpreters were present but language differences led to misunderstandings, and that leaders negotiated "amid pressure from U.S. officials." It describes an "eventual, though not total, forced relocation to the Flathead Reservation" and says Chief Victor defended the Bitterroot Valley as his people's homeland.

The newspaper calls the treaty a loss of millions of acres that also recognized certain rights and created a reservation. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 8, 1859.
