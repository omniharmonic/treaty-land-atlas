On July 31, 1855, at Detroit, commissioners George W. Manypenny and Henry C. Gilbert signed a treaty with the Ottawa and Chippewa of Michigan, parties to the 1836 treaty. The United States agreed to withdraw from sale unsold public lands for the bands at Sault Ste. Marie, Grand Traverse, Little Traverse, Mackinac and elsewhere, and to give allotments of 40 or 80 acres to members. Article 3 released the United States from liability under former treaties. Article 5 provided that the "tribal organization" would be dissolved except as needed to carry out the agreement.

A 1994 House committee report says the Ottawa pressed Washington for a new treaty that ended a removal threat that had hung over them since 1837. It says "the 1855 Treaty guaranteed that the United States would not force the Ottawa/Odawa to leave their Michigan homes."

The same report says it was among the earliest "allotment" treaties, and that the allotment provision "was used, through mismanagement and fraud, to take most Ottawa/Odawa land." A federal court in 1979 held that the treaty did not terminate the bands' tribal status.
