On August 2, 1855, at Detroit, commissioners George W. Manypenny and Henry C. Gilbert signed an agreement with the Chippewa Indians of Sault Ste. Marie. Article 1: "The said Chippewa Indians surrender to the United States the right of fishing at the falls of St. Mary's and of encampment, convenient to the fishing-ground, secured to them by the treaty of June 16, 1820." The agreement dealt with a single fishery and campsite rather than a land cession by named tract.

Article 2 provided that a commissioner would value the Indians' interest in the fishery and that the award would be paid like annuities, "in full satisfaction for the right hereby surrendered," with one-third going to half-blood relatives if the Indians wished. Article 3 gave the chief Oshawwawno an island in the St. Mary's River in fee simple.

The treaty text does not say why the United States wanted the site. The Sault Tribe's history says that treaties of 1820 and 1836 and a later pattern of settlers entering northern Michigan prompted the 1855 treaties, and that its people did not lose their sovereignty or ancestral hunting and fishing rights on ceded lands.
