On August 2, 1855, at Detroit, commissioners George W. Manypenny and Henry C. Gilbert signed an agreement with the Chippewa Indians of Sault Ste. Marie. Article 1: "The said Chippewa Indians surrender to the United States the right of fishing at the falls of St. Mary's and of encampment, convenient to the fishing-ground, secured to them by the treaty of June 16, 1820." The agreement dealt with a single fishery and campsite rather than a land cession by named tract.

Article 2 provided that a commissioner would value the Indians' interest in the fishery and that the award would be paid like annuities, "in full satisfaction for the right hereby surrendered," with one-third going to relatives of mixed ancestry if the Indians wished. Article 3 gave the chief Oshawwawno an island in the St. Mary's River in fee simple.

The treaty text does not say why the United States wanted the site. The Sault Tribe's history does not discuss this fishing agreement. It says that after settlers violated the 1836 treaty, the chiefs signed the 1855 Treaty with the Ottawa and Chippewa, and that its people came to understand the treaties did not end their sovereignty or their ancestral right to hunt and fish on the ceded lands and waters.
