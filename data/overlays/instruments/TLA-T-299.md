On October 17, 1855, at a council ground on the Upper Missouri near the mouth of the Judith River, commissioners A. Cumming and Isaac Stevens signed a treaty with chiefs of the Blackfoot Nation (Piegan, Blood, Blackfoot and Gros Ventres), the Flathead Nation, and the Nez Perce. The treaty set perpetual peace. Article 3 made a large area of the Blackfoot territory "a common hunting-ground for ninety-nine years." Article 4 set aside territory for the Blackfoot alone, and Article 8 allowed roads, telegraph lines and military posts. The United States promised annual payments.

Trail of the Great Plains Tribes, a Great Falls history site, says Stevens was charged with making peace along a northern railroad route, and that tribal groups attended with limits, since the Crow were absent. It says Stevens's opening remarks about replacing buffalo with cattle showed the parties' lack of understanding, and asks how well the Blackfeet understood what they signed.

The treaty did not cede a named tract. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on April 15, 1856.
