On December 21, 1855, at Dayton, Oregon Territory, Joel Palmer signed an agreement with the chiefs and head men of the Molala (Molel) tribe. The Molala ceded their claim to a tract of Oregon Territory that began at Scott's Peak and ran along the eastern edge of the lands bought in 1853 and 1854 from the Cow Creek, the Umpqua and Calapooia, and the Chasta, Scoton and Grave Creek bands, up to the Cascade crest. Article 3 provides that the Indians would remove to a reservation. The United States agreed to bear the expense of removal and to give rations.

Article 2 secured to the Molala the rights and privileges promised by treaty to the Umpqua and Calapooia. Article 6 set aside $12,000 to buy out white settlers' titles and improvements in the Grand Round Valley, where the Indians were to be encamped. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 8, 1859.

The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians' history says the ancestors of its members were forced to sign seven treaties and that in January 1856 Palmer removed the Umpquas, Kalapuyas and Molallas to a temporary camp on the south fork of the Yamhill River, now Grand Ronde. That tribal history ties the removal policy to a plan to confederate scattered tribes on one reservation away from settlers.
