On February 5, 1856, at Stockbridge, Wisconsin, the Stockbridge and Munsee tribes ceded and relinquished "all their remaining right and title in the lands at the town of Stockbridge" and the 72 sections in Minnesota set aside for them in 1848, along with several sums and claims. The United States agreed to select and give them a tract in Wisconsin "near the southern boundary of the Menomonee reservation," large enough for 80-acre lots for heads of families and 40-acre lots for others. It also agreed to pay $41,100 for improvements, $20,550 for removal, and $18,000 for stock, tribal debts and settling their affairs.

The treaty's preamble recites decades of dispute: acts of Congress in 1843 and 1846 tried to allot their Lake Winnebago lands, "dissensions" persisted, and part of the tribe refused to be bound. The treaty was a settlement of that history.

The Stockbridge-Munsee Community's timeline records that the 1856 treaty, signed by Sachem Ziba T. Peters, established the reservation in Shawano County, Wisconsin. It adds that lumber barons claimed part of the reservation in 1871. The new tract came from a Menominee cession made that same month.
