On September 24, 1857, at Table Creek, Nebraska Territory, commissioner James W. Denver signed a treaty with the chiefs of the four confederate Pawnee bands: the Grand Pawnees, Pawnee Loups, Pawnee Republicans and Pawnee Tappahs. They agreed to "cede and relinquish to the United States all their right, title, and interest in and to all the lands now owned or claimed by them," except a reservation thirty miles long by fifteen miles wide on both banks of the Loup Fork of the Platte River. The United States agreed to pay $40,000 a year for five years, then $30,000 a year as a perpetual annuity, and to build manual-labor schools.

HistoryNet says that in spring 1857 a settler shot a young Pawnee chief and newspapers called for the tribe's removal from settlement lands. Sioux raids on the reservation continued, and in the 1870s the Pawnee moved to Indian Territory.

The Pawnee Nation's historical marker, quoting the Nebraska State Historical Society, says the Pawnee moved to Indian Territory in 1875, ending their settlement in Nebraska. HistoryNet adds that three groups left between 1873 and 1875.
