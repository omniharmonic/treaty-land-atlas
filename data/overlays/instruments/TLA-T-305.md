On November 5, 1857, at the meeting house on the Tonawanda reservation in Genesee County, New York, Commissioner Charles E. Mix signed a treaty with five men authorized by the Tonawanda Band of Seneca, including Ely S. Parker. The preamble recites that the 1838 Treaty of Buffalo Creek and the 1842 treaty had conveyed the Buffalo Creek and Tonawanda reservations to Thomas Ludlow Ogden and Joseph Fellows, and that the treaties "remain unexecuted" as to Tonawanda. The Tonawanda Band surrendered all claims to Kansas lands and to removal.

The United States agreed to pay $256,000. Article 3 allowed the band to buy from Ogden and Fellows "the entire Tonawanda reservation" or any part, with the United States paying for it out of that sum at an average of no more than $20 an acre.

PBS's biography of Ely Parker says the 1838 and 1842 treaties threatened to remove the Senecas to Kansas, and that Parker "led treaty negotiations that allowed the Tonawanda Senecas to buy back about two-thirds of their reservation land." The treaty ended the removal threat for this band.
