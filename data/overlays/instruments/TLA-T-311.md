On July 16, 1859, at the Sac and Fox agency, Commissioner David Crawford signed a treaty with delegates of the Swan Creek and Black River Chippewas and the Munsee or Christian Indians. The preamble says the Swan Creek band had claimed a share of the benefits of the 1855 Detroit treaty "under a misapprehension" of its terms, which covered only Chippewa still in Michigan. The United States agreed that the reservation of 8,320 acres, or thirteen sections, in Franklin County, Kansas Territory would benefit the part of the band living there.

The Munsee, who "have expressed a desire to unite with said band of Chippewas," were united with them on the reservation. Members would receive lands in severalty, not more than 40 acres to a head of family or child and 80 to an unmarried adult. Article 2 provided for the sale of the residue of the land.

The Munsee Tribe in Kansas says the Munsee confederated with the Swan Creek and Black River bands through the treaty, and that the two peoples created a joint tribal council to track annuity payments.
