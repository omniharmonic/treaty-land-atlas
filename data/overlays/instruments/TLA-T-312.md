On October 1, 1859, at the Sac and Fox agency in Kansas Territory, commissioner Alfred B. Greenwood signed a treaty with Keokuk and other chiefs of the confederated Sacs and Foxes of the Mississippi. The treaty says the tribe had "more lands than are necessary for their occupancy and use" and wished to abolish tenure in common. A rectangular part of the reservation "to contain about one hundred and fifty-three thousand and six hundred acres" would be retained, with 80 acres assigned to each member. The rest would be sold in parcels of up to 160 acres to the highest bidder, and the proceeds used for houses, tools and stock. Article 5 provided for paying the Indians' debts.

The Franklin County Kansas History Portal says the negotiations were initiated by the U.S. government "to decrease the size of the Sac and Fox reservation." It records a Sac and Fox leader, Shaw-Paw-Kaw-Kah, saying: "I sign under protest, knowing in my own heart that there is no good in it for the Indians."

The same portal's account has him warning that "We will eventually have to surrender this diminished reservation." The treaty was ratified on June 27, 1860 and proclaimed on July 9, 1860, according to the OSU page.
