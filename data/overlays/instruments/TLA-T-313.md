In October 1859, at the Kansas agency, Commissioner Alfred B. Greenwood and chiefs and headmen of the Kansa (Kaw) agreed to divide their reservation. A tract nine miles by fourteen miles stayed with the tribe, and members would receive allotments of up to forty acres each. The rest of the reservation would be sold.

The treaty's own recital says the Kansa "having now more lands than are necessary for their occupation and use" wished to end holding land in common. Sale proceeds were to pay for houses, tools and stock, and to settle the tribe's debts. The land was to go to the highest bidder for cash in parcels of no more than 160 acres. Settlers who had improved land before December 2, 1856 could buy their claims at set prices.

The text shows a sale and allotment plan, not removal. Allotted tracts could not be sold except to the United States or other tribal members, and white people could not live on the reservation without permission. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on June 27, 1860.
