In May 1860, at Sarcoxieville on the Delaware reservation in Kansas Territory, Commissioner Thomas B. Sykes and Delaware chiefs agreed to survey the reservation and divide it. Each member of the tribe would receive an eighty-acre tract, inalienable except to the United States or other Delawares. Small tracts were also reserved around the mill, school, council house, missions and agency.

The treaty says the Delawares believed a railroad would raise the value of their lands. It gave the Leavenworth, Pawnee and Western Railroad Company the first right to buy the remaining land at an appraised price of no less than $1.25 per acre. The company would also get a perpetual right of way over allotted land for just compensation. Patents were tied to building the road, and unsold land would revert to the nation if the company defaulted.

The text shows railroad promoters and the federal government shaping the terms. The next Delaware instrument, a July 1861 document that appears in this atlas, deals with this railroad purchase.
