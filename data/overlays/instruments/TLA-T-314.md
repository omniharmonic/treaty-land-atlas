In May 1860, at Sarcoxieville on the Delaware reservation in Kansas Territory, Commissioner Thomas B. Sykes and Delaware chiefs were bound by the treaty to survey the reservation and divide it. Each member of the tribe would receive an eighty-acre tract, inalienable except to the United States or other Delawares. Small tracts were also reserved around the mill, school, council house, missions and agency.

The treaty says the Delawares believed a railroad would raise the value of their lands. It gave the Leavenworth, Pawnee and Western Railroad Company the first right to buy the remaining land at an appraised price of no less than $1.25 per acre. The company would also get a perpetual right of way over allotted land for just compensation. Patents were tied to building the road, and unsold land would revert to the nation if the company defaulted.

The OSU treaty database lists ratification on July 27, 1860. A July 1861 Delaware agreement, also in this atlas, recorded the railroad company's mortgage of 100,000 acres of Delaware land to secure its bonds.
