On March 6, 1861, at the Great Nemaha agency in Nebraska Territory, U.S. agent Daniel Vanderslice signed an agreement with four delegates of the Sac and Fox of Missouri and seven delegates of the Iowa. The Sac and Fox ceded their reservation, estimated at 32,098 acres. It was to be surveyed and sold at auction in parcels of up to 160 acres.

In exchange, the Iowa ceded to the United States, for the Sac and Fox, the part of the Iowa reservation west of Nohearts Creek. That tract became the Sac and Fox "permanent home," and the rest remained Iowa land. The treaty also settled an $800 claim by trader Joseph Tesson with a 160-acre grant and gave named chiefs individual tracts with patents.

The text shows a land swap between two tribes arranged by the United States, with the cession proceeds going to sales. Ratification took until February 1863.
