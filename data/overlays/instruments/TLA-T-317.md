On July 2, 1861, at Leavenworth City, the United States and the Delaware tribe reached an agreement about the surplus lands covered by the May 1860 treaty. The Leavenworth, Pawnee and Western Railroad Company had agreed to buy those lands. The company's agent, Thomas Ewing Jr., executed twenty-nine bonds totaling $286,742.15 for the appraised value, payable in ten years with six percent interest.

The text says the company pledged and mortgaged 100,000 acres of Delaware land to the United States to secure those bonds. If the company failed to build the railroad sections it had promised, the 1860 treaty made it forfeit the unpatented lands, which the United States could take and sell for the Delawares' benefit.

This instrument shows how the 1860 land sale to a private railroad was financed and secured. The pledged land totaled 156 1/4 sections, or 100,000 acres. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on August 6, 1861, and proclamation on October 4, 1861.
