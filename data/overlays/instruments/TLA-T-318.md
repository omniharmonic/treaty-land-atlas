On November 15, 1861, Commissioner William W. Ross and chiefs, braves and headmen of the Potawatomi agreed to dispose of part of their Kansas reservation, which the treaty says held 576,000 acres. The reservation had been acquired under an 1846 treaty for $87,000. Members who wanted separate farms could receive eighty acres each. Those who preferred common ownership could hold land together. The remainder was to be sold for the tribe's benefit.

The treaty gave the Leavenworth, Pawnee and Western Railroad the first chance to buy the surplus land at $1.25 per acre. It had to build sections of road within set years, and patents would issue only as sections were finished. The President could also grant fee-simple patents to male allottees judged "sufficiently intelligent and prudent."

The text shows allotment, sale and a railroad option, not a removal. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on April 15, 1862. It records proclamation on April 19, 1862.
