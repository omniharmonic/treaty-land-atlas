The Kansa (Kaw) and the United States made this agreement at the Kansas Agency, Kansas, on March 13, 1862, to amend a treaty of October 5, 1859. That treaty had been ratified by the President on November 17, 1860, with a Senate amendment that the Kansa chiefs accepted on October 4, 1860.

Article 1 deals with settlers. People who settled on the diminished reserve between December 2, 1856 and October 5, 1859 were to have the value of their improvements appraised by the Secretary of the Interior. Certificates of indebtedness issued "by said tribe" would cover the appraisals, up to $15,000 in total. Similar certificates covered earlier settlers' claims, up to $14,421, and claims approved under the 1859 treaty's fifth article, up to $36,394.47. All such certificates would be "receivable as cash" in payment for land bought or entered on the part of the first assigned reservation outside the diminished reserve.

Article 2 records that the tribe, wishing to thank missionary and teacher Thomas S. Huffaker, asked the Secretary to convey him the half-section on which he had lived since 1851, if he paid the appraised value at not less than $1.75 an acre.

The Kansa chiefs were bound by the treaty to Senate amendments on February 26, 1863. The treaty was proclaimed March 16, 1863.
