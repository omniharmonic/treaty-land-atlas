On June 28, 1862, at the Kickapoo Agency in Kansas, the United States and the Kickapoo agreed to survey the reservation, which was held under an 1854 treaty. Members could take land in severalty: a half section for each chief, a quarter section for each other head of a family, and forty acres for each other person. Those who preferred common ownership could hold land together. The remainder, after special reservations, was to be sold for the tribe's benefit.

The Kickapoo, the treaty says, thought the government and the public wanted railroads pushed west "in the direction of the gold mines in Colorado Territory." It gave the Atchison and Pike's Peak Railroad Company the privilege of buying the whole surplus at $1.25 per acre, on condition that it build the line in stages and post bonds. Patents would issue only as sections were completed.

The treaty also said that if a majority of adult males in the common-holding group chose to move to Indian country south of Kansas, a new home would be bought for them. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 13, 1863.
