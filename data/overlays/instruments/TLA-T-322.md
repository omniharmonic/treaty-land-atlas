This treaty was made in Washington on March 11, 1863. Commissioner of Indian Affairs William P. Dole and Clark W. Thompson signed for the United States. Henry M. Rice and a long list of chiefs and headmen signed for the Chippewa of the Mississippi and the Pillager and Lake Winnibigoshish bands.

Article 1 ceded to the United States the reservations "known as Gull Lake, Mille Lac, Sandy Lake, Rabbit Lake, Pokagomin Lake, and Rice Lake," set out in the 1855 treaty. One exception was a half-section including the mission buildings at Gull Lake, granted in fee simple to the Reverend John Johnson.

In return, Article 2 set aside a new reservation for the Chippewas of the Mississippi, with boundaries beginning one mile south of the southern end of Leech Lake. Article 3 extended existing annuities ten years, and provided $20,000 toward settling claims for depredations committed in 1862.

Article 4 promised to clear and break acres for each band and build a house for each chief. Article 12 said the Indians need not move until the United States had met Articles 4 and 6, and that the Mille Lac Indians would not be compelled to remove, owing to their "good conduct," while they did not molest whites. Ratified March 13, 1863; proclaimed March 19, 1863.
