On June 9, 1863, at Lapwai, the United States and Nez Perce headmen signed a treaty in which the tribe relinquished the lands reserved in 1855, except a smaller reservation near the Clearwater River and Lapwai. The Nez Perce were to move onto it within a year of ratification. The treaty was ratified in April 1867.

The National Park Service says miners were already in the Orofino area and Lewiston was growing. Congress chose to draft a new treaty so that the areas where settlers lived "were no longer a part of the reservation at all," rather than enforce the 1855 ban on white settlement. The Park Service adds that negotiators looked for one "head chief" to speak for all Nez Perce, though each band regarded itself as autonomous.

Lawyer and other pro-treaty headmen signed. The US government assumed this bound all Nez Perce. Chief Joseph, Chief White Bird and other "non-treaty" headmen thought they would not be held to an agreement they did not make. The Park Service says the treaty "planted the seeds of conflict" that grew into the 1877 war.
