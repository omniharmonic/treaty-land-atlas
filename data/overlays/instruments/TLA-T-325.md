On July 30, 1863, at Box Elder in Utah Territory, Brigadier General P. Edward Connor and Governor James Duane Doty signed an agreement with chiefs of the northwestern bands of the Shoshone. It re-established peace and adopted the terms of the Fort Bridger treaty, including free travel routes. It raised annuities by $5,000 and recorded that Pokatello's country was bounded by Raft River and the Portneuf Mountains.

The text says the bands had been "reduced by the war to a state of utter destitution," and the United States gave $2,000 in provisions and goods at signing. Article 5 adds that nothing in the treaty admitted any "other or greater title or interest" than existed under Mexican law.

In Northwestern Bands of Shoshone Indians v. United States (1945), the bands sued for about $15 million for roughly 15 million acres, arguing the treaty recognized their title. The Supreme Court held that suit was allowed only for rights the treaty acknowledged, found the claim did not arise under the treaty, and affirmed.
