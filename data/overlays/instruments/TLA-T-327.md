On October 2, 1863, at Old Crossing on the Red Lake River, commissioners Alexander Ramsey and Ashley C. Morrill signed a treaty with chiefs, headmen and warriors of the Red Lake and Pembina bands of Chippewa. The bands ceded all their right, title and interest in lands they claimed in Minnesota and Dakota Territory, within boundaries starting at the Lake of the Woods.

The United States agreed to pay $20,000 a year for twenty years, with up to $5,000 yearly held for farming, education and goods. It also set aside $100,000 to pay for "depredations" on traders and for exactions levied on a steamboat company on the Red River, and to help the bands pay their debts. The treaty granted homesteads of 160 acres to adult mixed-blood men who met its conditions, and 640-acre tracts for chiefs Moose Dung and Red Bear.

An 1899 Supreme Court case, Jones v. Meehan, concerned title to the Moose Dung tract. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 1, 1864.
