On October 12, 1863, at Tuilla Valley in Utah Territory, the United States and the Shoshone-Goship (Kumumbar) bands signed a treaty of peace and friendship. The bands agreed to end hostilities against emigrant trains, mail and telegraph lines and citizens, and to keep routes of travel "forever free and unobstructed."

Article 4 allowed the Goship country to be explored for gold, silver and other minerals, with mines worked and mining and farming settlements formed "wherever they may be required." Mills and timber use were also allowed. Article 5 records the boundaries as the bands defined them. Article 6 authorized the President to make reservations when he thought it "expedient" for the bands to give up their roaming life, and they agreed to move to whatever reservation he indicated.

In return the United States promised annuities, which the bands accepted as full compensation for lost game and the rights they conceded. The text contains no explicit cession of title. The OSU treaty database lists ratification on March 7, 1864.
