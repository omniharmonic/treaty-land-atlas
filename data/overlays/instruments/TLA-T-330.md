Signed in Washington on April 12, 1864, these articles supplement the treaty made at the Old Crossing of Red Lake River, Minnesota, on October 2, 1863. Commissioners Clark W. Thompson and Ashley C. Morrill signed for the United States. Red Lake and Pembina chiefs, headmen and warriors signed with marks.

Article 1 has the bands "agree and assent to the provisions" of the Old Crossing treaty as the Senate amended it by a resolution dated March 1, 1864.

Article 2 swaps the annuity set in the earlier treaty for annual payments, for as long as the President chose, of $10,000 to the Red Lake band and $5,000 to the Pembina band, distributed per capita. Article 3 adds yearly spending for fifteen years, $8,000 for the Red Lake band and $4,000 for the Pembina band, on goods, provisions and farming tools. Article 4 adds a blacksmith, physician, miller and farmer for fifteen years, and Article 5 a sawmill.

Article 6 changes how a $100,000 fund would be used: $25,000 to the chiefs for provisions and clothing, with the remaining $75,000 going first to claims of injured traders and steamboat owners, and the remainder toward the bands' debts since 1859. Article 7 provides scrip for mixed-bloods in place of land. It was proclaimed April 25, 1864.
