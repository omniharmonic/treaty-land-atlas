On May 7, 1864, at Washington, Commissioner William P. Dole and superintendent Clark W. Thompson signed a treaty with the Chippewa chief Hole-in-the-day and Mis-qua-dace, acting for the Chippewas of the Mississippi and the Pillager and Lake Winnibigoshish bands. The Gull Lake, Mille Lac, Sandy Lake, Rabbit Lake, Pokagomin Lake and Rice Lake reservations, set in the 1855 treaty, were ceded to the United States. A few sections went in fee simple to named chiefs and a missionary.

In return the United States agreed to set apart a new reservation for the Chippewas of the Mississippi, described by boundaries around the Leech Lake area. The United States agreed to pay $7,500 for clearing and breaking land on it. It also promised $20,000 toward settling claims for "depredations committed by said Indians in 1862," $10,000 to the chiefs, and $5,000 to Hole-in-the-day for his burned house and furniture.

The treaty names the 1862 events only as "depredations." The OSU treaty database lists ratification on February 9, 1865, and proclamation on March 20, 1865.
