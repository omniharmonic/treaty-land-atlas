On March 6, 1865, at Washington, the Omaha ceded a tract from the north side of their Nebraska reservation, stretching from the Missouri River west to the reservation line. They agreed to vacate the ceded land immediately after ratification. The United States agreed to pay $50,000 for goods, stock, buildings and improvements, plus $7,000 for damages from the occupancy and timber use by the Winnebago while they stayed temporarily on Omaha land.

The Omaha also agreed to divide the remaining reservation among members in severalty, citing a wish to promote "settled habits of industry." Two days later the United States set it apart as a home for the Winnebago, under the treaty described next in this atlas.

The Omaha cession thus served to settle another displaced nation on Omaha land. The pages opened here do not describe the Omaha view or later events, so those are not covered.
