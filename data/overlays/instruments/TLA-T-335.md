On March 8, 1865, at Washington, the Winnebago ceded their reservation in Dakota Territory at Usher's Landing on the Missouri River. In return the United States set apart, as their "future home," the tract the Omaha had ceded two days earlier, in Nebraska Territory.

The United States agreed to remove the tribe and its property to the new home and to feed them for a year. It promised a steam sawmill with a gristmill, and 100 acres broken and fenced for each band. It also promised guns, horses, cows, oxen, wagons and tools, agency buildings, a school and a house for each chief. The text says this was so the Winnebagos might be as well situated "as they were when they were moved from Minnesota."

The treaty thus records a second removal, from the Dakota Territory reserve to Nebraska, after an earlier one from Minnesota. The pages opened here do not describe conditions at either place or how the tribe regards the treaty, so those are not covered.
