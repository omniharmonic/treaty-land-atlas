On March 8, 1865, in Washington, the Winnebago tribe ceded to the United States their reservation in Dakota Territory, at Usher's Landing on the Missouri River. In return the United States set apart for them, "forever," the tract the Omaha tribe had ceded on March 6, 1865, in the Territory of Nebraska.

The United States agreed to remove the tribe and its property to the new home and to feed the tribe for one year after arrival. It promised a steam sawmill with a gristmill, and 100 acres broken and fenced for each band, with seed to plant them. It also promised $2,000 worth of guns, 400 horses, 100 cows, twenty yoke of oxen, wagons and other goods. It promised an agency building, a school house and a house for each chief.

The treaty says these terms were meant so that the Winnebagos "may be as well situated as they were when they were moved from Minnesota." The text itself therefore records a second relocation, following an earlier one from Minnesota. The treaty text does not describe conditions at either place, and no source here here gives the tribe's own account, so neither is covered.
