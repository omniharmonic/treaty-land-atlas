On March 10, 1865, at Washington, the Ponca ceded the part of their 1858 reservation west of a range line, estimated at 30,000 acres. The United States ceded fractional township 31 north, range 7 west, and the Niobrara River islands in front of it, to the Ponca. The treaty says the United States did this "by way of rewarding them for their constant fidelity" and to return their "old burying-grounds and corn-fields." The Ponca, not the United States, were to pay any settlers' improvement claims.

NET Nebraska Studies says the treaty also meant to move the Ponca away from Sioux attacks. It says the 1868 Fort Laramie treaty with the Sioux mistakenly included Ponca land, and that the commissioners probably had forgotten the 1865 treaty.

The atlas links this treaty to the Ponca removal to Indian Territory in 1877 and to Standing Bear v. Crook (1879). The pages opened here do not describe those events, so they are not covered here.
