On August 12, 1865, in the Sprague River Valley, Oregon, superintendent J. W. Perit Huntington signed with chiefs and headmen of the Woll-pah-pe tribe of Snake Indians. The tribe ceded and relinquished the country it occupied, described from Snow Peak in the Blue Mountain range. It agreed to "remove forthwith" to the reservation set up by the 1864 treaty with the Klamath, Modoc and Yahooskin Snake.

No member could leave without written permission from the agent or superintendent. The tribe agreed not to sell arms or ammunition to hostile tribes. The United States promised $5,000 to fence, break and cultivate land, plus seeds, tools, animals and first-year subsistence, and shared use of the agency's physician, mechanics, farmers, teachers, mills and schools.

The treaty also allowed annuities to be withheld from any Indian who brought liquor onto the reservation. The text records removal, confinement to the reservation and the loss of the tribe's land base. The pages opened here do not give historical context or later events.
