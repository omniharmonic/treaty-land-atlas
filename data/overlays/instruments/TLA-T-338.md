On September 29, 1865, at Canville Trading Post in Kansas, Commissioner D. N. Cooley and superintendent Elijah Sells signed with chiefs of the Great and Little Osage. The treaty says the Osage had more land than they needed, that payments under earlier treaties had ceased, "leaving them greatly impoverished," and that they wished to improve their condition by selling surplus land.

They sold a strip fifty miles wide along the east side of their reservation for $300,000, held in the Treasury at five percent interest. The land was to be surveyed and sold, including land granted to Kansas for railroad construction. They also ceded a twenty-mile strip off the north side, to be held in trust and sold for the tribe's benefit at not less than $1.25 per acre.

The wording shows a sale made under financial need. The pages opened here do not describe later events or the Osage Nation's account, so those are not covered.
