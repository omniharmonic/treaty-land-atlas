On October 10, 1865, a United States commission met chiefs and head-men of the Minneconjon band of Dakota (Sioux) at Fort Sully, Dakota Territory. Commissioners were Governor Newton Edmunds, Edward B. Taylor, Major-General S. R. Curtis, Brigadier-General H. H. Sibley, Henry W. Reed and Orrin Guernsey. This is one of a series of separate treaties with Sioux bands that fall.

In Article 1 the band acknowledged itself "subject to the exclusive jurisdiction and authority of the United States." They promised to stop hostilities against its citizens and to use their influence, and physical force if needed, to keep other Sioux bands or nearby tribes from hostile acts. Article 2 had them stop attacks on other tribes unless attacked first, and Article 3 sent quarrels between tribes to the President for a decision.

Article 4 is the main trade. The band would "withdraw from the routes overland" already or later established through their country. The United States would pay $10,000 a year for twenty years in articles chosen by the Secretary of the Interior, as long as the band kept the treaty. Article 5 promised protection to individuals who chose to settle on band land for farming.

The treaty was ratified March 5, 1866, and proclaimed March 17, 1866. Two more chiefs came into the council on October 20 and asked to sign.
