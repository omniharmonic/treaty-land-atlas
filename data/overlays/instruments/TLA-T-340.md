On October 14, 1865, a United States commission met chiefs and head-men of the Lower Brule band of Dakota (Sioux) at Fort Sully, Dakota Territory. Commissioners were Governor Newton Edmunds, Edward B. Taylor, Major-General S. R. Curtis, Brigadier-General H. H. Sibley, Henry W. Reed and Orrin Guernsey.

As in the other Fort Sully treaties, Article 1 has the band acknowledge itself subject to the jurisdiction and authority of the United States, and Article 4 has it "withdraw from the routes overland" in exchange for $6,000 a year for twenty years, paid in articles chosen by the Secretary of the Interior.

This one is different in Article 6. The Lower Brule were to settle on a permanent reservation "at or near the mouth of the White River, to include Fort Lookout," twenty miles along the Missouri and ten miles deep. Once at least fifty lodges or families occupied it and took up farming, the United States would give $25 per family a year for five years, as a common fund for stock and implements, which stayed United States property. It would also employ a blacksmith and farmer, could build roads through, and could open schools. No white person other than officials could stay without being admitted to the band.

Article 7 let the Two Kettles band settle next to them. Ratified March 5, 1866; proclaimed March 17, 1866.
