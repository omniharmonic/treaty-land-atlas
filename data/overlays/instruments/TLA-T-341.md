On October 14, 1865, on the Little Arkansas River in Kansas, seven U.S. commissioners, including William S. Harney, Kit Carson and William W. Bent, signed with chiefs and headmen of the Arapaho and Cheyenne of the Upper Arkansas. Article 2 set apart a reservation between the Arkansas River and the Cimarron River. The tribes relinquished claims to the rest of the country, especially the land north of the Platte forks, which they said they had never given up.

Article 6 states that the United States wished "to express its condemnation of, and, as far as may be, repudiate the gross and wanton out-rages" at Sand Creek on November 29, 1864. It granted 320 acres to each of named chiefs, including Black Kettle, and promised further compensation for lost property.

The treaty followed the 1861 Fort Wise treaty and Sand Creek, which the National Archives links to disputes over Fort Wise. It was ratified in 1866 and later replaced by the Medicine Lodge treaty. The pages opened here do not describe what happened to these grants.
