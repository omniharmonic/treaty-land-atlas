This short agreement was signed on the Little Arkansas River in Kansas on October 17, 1865. It supplements a treaty with the Cheyenne and Arapaho made three days earlier, on October 14, 1865, at the same council ground. Commissioners for the United States included John B. Sanborn, William S. Harney, Kit Carson and William W. Bent.

The preamble explains that the Apache, who had been "confederated with the Kiowa and Comanche tribes," wanted to dissolve that confederation and join the Cheyenne and Arapaho. Those two tribes were willing to receive the Apache "on an equal footing," and the United States commissioners gave their assent.

Article 1 unites the three and says the United States "will hereafter recognize" them as the confederated bands or tribes of Cheyenne, Arrapahoe and Apache. Article 2 says everything the October 14 treaty required of the United States or of the Cheyenne and Arapaho would now apply to the three confederated tribes, as if the treaty had been made with all of them.

The page does not repeat the terms of the October 14 treaty, which was not opened. This agreement was ratified May 22, 1866, and proclaimed May 26, 1866. Chiefs of each tribe signed with marks, including Black Kettle and Little Raven.
