On October 19, 1865, a United States commission met chiefs and head-men of the Blackfeet band of Dakota (Sioux) at Fort Sully, Dakota Territory. Commissioners were Governor Newton Edmunds, Edward B. Taylor, Major-General S. R. Curtis, Brigadier-General H. H. Sibley, Henry W. Reed and Orrin Guernsey. The band is a Sioux band, not the Blackfeet Nation of Montana.

Article 1 has the band acknowledge itself subject to United States jurisdiction and promise to stop hostilities against its citizens. Later articles have the band end attacks on other tribes unless attacked first, and send disputes with other tribes to the President for a decision.

Article 4 is the trade. The band was to "withdraw from the routes overland already established or hereafter to be established, through their country." In return the United States agreed to pay $7,000 a year for twenty years, in articles chosen by the Secretary of the Interior, so long as the band faithfully kept the treaty.

Article 5 says any Senate amendment would be final and binding on the band as part of the treaty, as if the chiefs had agreed to it. This text has no farming or reservation article, unlike some of the sister treaties. It was ratified March 5, 1866, and proclaimed March 17, 1866.
