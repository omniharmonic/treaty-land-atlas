On November 15, 1865, at the Warm Springs Agency in Oregon, Superintendent J. W. Perit Huntington signed articles with chiefs and head-men of the confederated tribes and bands of Middle Oregon. They amend a treaty of June 25, 1855.

Article 1 says the 1855 treaty had let the tribes "fish, hunt, gather berries and roots, pasture stock, and erect houses" on ceded lands outside the reservation. The United States side says this was abused by Indians living away from the reservation. The 1865 text has the tribes relinquish all those rights. Article 2 has them agree to remain on the reservation. Article 3 lets the superintendent or agent give short written permits to leave. Article 4 says violators lose their share of annuities. In exchange, Article 5 gave $3,500 for teams, implements and seeds, and Article 6 promised an allotment to each head of family.

A Senate committee report in 2019 says a 1997 Forest Service report concluded that the tribal leaders' signatures on this supplemental treaty "were obtained by fraudulent means." The committee said the bill S. 832 would nullify the supplemental treaty and leave the 1855 treaty intact. The OSU database notes that the 116th Congress nullified the treaty in 2020.
