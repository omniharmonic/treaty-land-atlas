On April 7, 1866, at Washington, the Bois Forte band of Chippewa ceded and "forever relinquish[ed]" all their claim to lands east of the 1854 boundary line, especially the area near Lake Vermilion where they lived as a reservation. They also ceded all claims to lands west of that line or anywhere else in the United States.

In return the United States promised to set apart, within one year after ratification, a reservation of not less than 100,000 acres, with a specific lake to be included if practicable, and a township on the Grand Fork River. It agreed to pay $30,000 on ratification and to build a blacksmith shop, school, chiefs' houses and an agency building. Annuities would be paid for twenty years, on the reservation if practicable.
