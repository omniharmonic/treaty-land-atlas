On April 28, 1866, at Washington, commissioners Dennis N. Cooley, Elijah Sells and Ely S. Parker signed a treaty with Choctaw and Chickasaw delegates. In Article 3 the Choctaws and Chickasaws ceded to the United States the "leased district," the territory west of the 98th meridian, for $300,000. The money was to be held in trust until the two nations passed laws giving people of African descent, formerly enslaved among them, the rights of citizens and forty acres each.

If they did not do so within two years, the money would be held for freedmen the United States removed from the nations. The treaty also let railroad companies build lines through the nations, allowed the United States to settle up to 10,000 "Kansas Indians" in their districts, and provided for survey and division of land east of the 98th meridian.

The Department of the Interior's 2022 testimony says the Five Tribes' 1866 treaties arose after the Civil War, and that the freedmen provisions differed in each. The pages opened here do not describe the Choctaw or Chickasaw views or later events.
