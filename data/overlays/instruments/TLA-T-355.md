On April 28, 1866, in Washington, U.S. commissioners Dennis N. Cooley, Elijah Sells and Ely S. Parker signed a treaty with commissioners of the Choctaw and Chickasaw Nations. The nations agreed that slavery and involuntary servitude would never again exist among them, except as punishment for crime.

In Article 3 they ceded to the United States "the territory west of the 98° west longitude, known as the leased district." The United States agreed to pay $300,000, but to hold it in trust at not less than five percent interest. The money would be released once the nations' legislatures passed laws giving formerly enslaved people of African descent, and their descendants, the rights of citizens, including suffrage, and forty acres each. If no such laws came within two years of ratification, the money would be held for those freedpeople whom the United States removed from the nations.

The treaty also allowed the United States to settle up to ten thousand "Kansas Indians" in the two nations, with the same rights as Choctaws and Chickasaws. It also made a general amnesty for past offenses against U.S. laws. The Department of the Interior reports that the Five Tribes' 1866 treaties treated freedmen differently. The sources here do not describe whether the Choctaw and Chickasaw legislatures passed the laws, or either nation's own account.
