On July 4, 1866, at the Delaware Agency in Kansas, the United States and the Delaware Indians agreed to the tribe's removal. The preamble says Congress had made it the duty of the President "to provide by treaty for the removal of the Indian tribes from the State of Kansas," that the Delawares "have expressed a wish" to move to the Indian country between Kansas and Texas, and that the Missouri River Railroad Company wanted to buy the reservation.

The Secretary of the Interior could sell the remaining reservation to that company, or to other responsible buyers, in a body for not less than $2.50 an acre. The United States would secure the Delawares the value of land earlier sold to the Leavenworth, Pawnee and Western Railroad. Adult Delawares with allotments could choose, free from restraint, to leave the tribe and become citizens, and their lands would be excluded from the sale.

The treaty combines a federal removal policy, a railroad purchase and a citizenship option. The pages opened here do not describe the Delawares' own views or events after removal.
