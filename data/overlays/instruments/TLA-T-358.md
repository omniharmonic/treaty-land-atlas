On July 19, 1866, at Washington, the United States and the Cherokee Nation signed a new treaty. Article 1 declared void the "pretended treaty" the Cherokee Nation made with the Confederate States in 1861, which the Cherokee council repudiated in 1863. Article 9 states that freedmen and free colored persons in the country, and their descendants, "shall have all the rights of native Cherokees."

Article 16 allowed the United States to settle friendly Indians in the Cherokee country west of the 96th meridian, at up to 160 acres per person, paid for at a price agreed or set by the President. Article 17 ceded in trust the Kansas land the Cherokees had been sold in the 1835 treaty, to be appraised at not less than $1.25 an acre and sold.

In The Cherokee Tobacco (1871), the Supreme Court held that an 1868 internal revenue law applied in the Cherokee Nation despite Article 10 of the 1866 treaty, saying that "one or the other must yield."
