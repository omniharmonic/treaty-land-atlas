On February 18, 1867, in Washington, the Sacs and Foxes of the Mississippi signed a treaty with Commissioner of Indian Affairs Lewis V. Bogy and other U.S. officials. Five chiefs signed for the tribes: Keokuk, Che-kus-kuk, Uc-quaw-ho-ko, Mut-tut-tah and Man-ah-to-wah.

In Article 1 the tribes were recorded as ceding the unsold part of their diminished Kansas reserve, which the treaty says held about 86,400 acres. In Article 2 they were recorded as ceding the unsold part of their old reservation that was to be sold for their benefit under the 1860 treaty. The United States agreed in Article 3 to pay one dollar an acre for the whole, "about one hundred and fifty-seven thousand acres," less land set apart for individuals. It also agreed to pay the tribe's outstanding scrip debt of $26,574, plus interest. After deducting removal, subsistence and settlement costs, the money was to be added to the tribe's invested funds at five percent interest.

In Article 6 the United States agreed, "in consideration of the improvements upon the said reservation," to give the tribes a new home in the Indian country south of Kansas and south of the Cherokee lands, up to 750 square miles. A commission appointed by the Interior Secretary was to select it with delegations from the tribes. The sources here do not describe the move itself or the tribes' own account.
