On February 18, 1867, at Washington, the Sacs and Foxes of the Mississippi, represented by Keokuk and four other chiefs, signed with Commissioner Lewis V. Bogy and other U.S. officials. The tribes ceded the unsold part of their diminished Kansas reserve, which the treaty says held about 86,400 acres, and the unsold part of their old reservation. Together the United States estimated about 157,000 acres, less individual allotments.

The United States agreed to pay one dollar an acre for the whole, and to pay the tribe's outstanding scrip debt of $26,574 plus interest. Sale proceeds, after removal and settlement costs, were to be added to the tribe's invested funds. In return for the improvements, the United States also agreed to give the Sacs and Foxes a new home "in the Indian country south of Kansas," up to 750 square miles, selected by commissioners with tribal delegates.
