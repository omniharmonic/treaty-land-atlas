On February 19, 1867, at Washington, Commissioner of Indian Affairs Lewis V. Bogy and special commissioner William H. Watson signed with chiefs of the Sisseton and Wahpeton bands of Dakota. The preamble says that after the 1862 outbreak by other Sioux bands, these bands kept their obligations to the United States. It says Congress, "in confiscating the Sioux annuities and reservations," made no provision for them, leaving some "homeless wanderers."

The bands ceded the right to build wagon roads, railroads, mail stations and telegraph lines across lands they claimed. In return the treaty set apart a permanent reservation near Lake Traverse, in consideration of that cession, their services and the confiscation of their annuities, reservations and improvements.

In DeCoteau v. District County Court (1975), the Supreme Court said the Lake Traverse reservation, created by the 1867 treaty, was terminated by an 1891 Act ratifying an 1889 agreement. The Court held state courts have jurisdiction over unallotted lands within the 1867 borders.
