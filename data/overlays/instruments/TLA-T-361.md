On February 23, 1867, in Washington, U.S. commissioners led by Lewis V. Bogy signed a single agreement with the Senecas, Mixed Senecas and Shawnees, Quapaws, Confederated Peorias, Kaskaskias, Weas and Piankeshaws, Miamis, Ottawas and certain Wyandottes. The preamble says some members living in Kansas wanted to move to the Indian country south of the state. Others wanted to end tribal relations and become citizens. Tribes already in the Indian country had been "driven from their reservations early in the late war" and were willing to sell part of their land for relief.

The Senecas ceded a strip of 20,000 acres for $20,000. The Senecas who shared a reserve with the Shawnees ceded its north half, about 30,000 acres, for $24,000, paid to the Senecas. The Shawnees ceded about 12,000 acres at one dollar an acre, and the Quapaws ceded two tracts at set prices per acre. The Senecas agreed to separate from the Shawnees and unite with the Senecas of the 1831 treaty.

Article 13 set apart for the Wyandottes, as their future home, the land the Senecas ceded in Article 1, to be owned in common. Article 16 sold the west part of the Shawnee reservation to the Ottawas at one dollar an acre. Article 23 has the Peoria group agree to sell their Kansas allotments and move to new homes in the Indian country within two years. The sources here do not give the tribes' own accounts.
