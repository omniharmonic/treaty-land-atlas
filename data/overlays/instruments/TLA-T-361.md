On February 23, 1867, at Washington, Commissioner Lewis V. Bogy and other U.S. officials signed a single agreement with the Senecas, Mixed Senecas and Shawnees, Quapaws, Confederated Peorias, Kaskaskias, Weas and Piankeshaws, Miamis, Ottawas and Wyandottes. The preamble says some members living in Kansas wished to move to the Indian country south of the state, others wished to end tribal relations and become citizens, and tribes already in the Indian country had been "driven from their reservations early in the late war" and were willing to sell part of their lands for relief.

The Senecas ceded a 20,000-acre strip. The Seneca-Shawnee reserve was split, with about 30,000 acres ceded for $24,000. The Shawnees ceded about 12,000 acres, and the Quapaws ceded land in Kansas and a further tract, while Senecas and Shawnees separated. Other articles covered the Peoria, Miami, Ottawa and Wyandotte groups.
