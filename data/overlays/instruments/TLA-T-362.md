On February 27, 1867, at Washington, the United States and the Potawatomi agreed to relocation planning. The preamble says the Pottawatomies "believe that it is for the interest of their tribe that a home should be secured for them in the Indian country south of Kansas." A commission, with Potawatomi delegates, would select a reservation of up to thirty miles square. If it was on land the government had bought from the Creeks, Seminoles or Choctaws, the price would not exceed the government's cost.

The treaty also set terms for selling the tribe's remaining Kansas lands to a railroad company at a stated price per acre. The Prairie band, which did not move, would receive its own share rather than an interest in the new reservation. The reservation, once chosen, would never be included in any state or territory unless an Indian Territory was organized.
