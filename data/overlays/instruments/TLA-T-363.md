On March 19, 1867, at Washington, Louis V. Bogy and other U.S. officials signed with chiefs of the Chippewas of the Mississippi, including Hole-in-the-Day. The Chippewas ceded their Minnesota lands secured under the second article of their 1865 treaty, estimated at about two million acres. They kept a reserved tract that includes White Earth Lake and Rice Lake and holds thirty-six townships.

In return the United States agreed to pay $5,000 for school buildings, $4,000 a year for ten years and longer for schools, $10,000 for a sawmill with a gristmill, and $5,000 to help build houses for people who moved to the new reservation. Land outside the earlier reservation but inside the new one would count as part payment.

The treaty replaced the 1865 arrangement with a reservation west of the earlier lands. The pages opened here do not state the reasons for the cession, any pressure on the signers, or what followed, so those points are not covered.
