On October 21, 1867, at Medicine Lodge Creek, the same U.S. commissioners who signed with the Kiowa and Comanche signed a separate treaty with chiefs of the Kiowa, Comanche and the Apache tribe. Article 1 had the Apaches "confederate and become incorporated" with the Kiowa and Comanche and accept as their permanent home the reservation described in the Kiowa-Comanche treaty. They pledged to make no permanent settlement outside it.

Article 2 had the Kiowa and Comanche agree that the benefits of the earlier treaty, including physicians, teachers, farmers, blacksmiths, agency buildings, mills and schools, would be shared equally with the Apache.

The treaty adds one more nation to the Kiowa-Comanche reservation. The Oklahoma Historical Society says the Medicine Lodge treaties settled the Southern plains tribes on reservations in Indian Territory. Later events, including the Jerome Agreement and Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock (1903), are described in separate entries for the Kiowa-Comanche treaty and the Jerome Agreement.
