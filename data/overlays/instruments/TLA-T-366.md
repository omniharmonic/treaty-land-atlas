On October 28, 1867, at Medicine Lodge Creek, the same U.S. commissioners signed a treaty with Cheyenne and Arapaho chiefs. Article 2 set apart a reservation bounded by the 37th parallel (the southern line of Kansas), the Cimarron River and the Arkansas River, for their "absolute and undisturbed use and occupation." Article 11 had the tribes relinquish the right to occupy permanently any territory outside the reservation. They kept the right to hunt south of the Arkansas "so long as the buffalo may range thereon."

The tribes agreed to withdraw opposition to the Smoky Hill River railroad and not to oppose other railroads that did not cross the reservation. The treaty also said no white settlements would be permitted in the old 1865 Little Arkansas reservation for three years.

The Oklahoma Historical Society says the treaty settled these tribes on reservations in Indian Territory. The atlas links this instrument to the later Fort Reno military reserve carved from the reservation, which I did not verify.
