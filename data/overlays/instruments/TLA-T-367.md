On March 2, 1868, at Washington, Commissioner Nathaniel G. Taylor, Colorado Governor Alexander C. Hunt and Kit Carson signed with representatives of seven Ute bands: Tabaquache, Muache, Capote, Weeminuche, Yampa, Grand River and Uintah. Article 2 set apart a district of western Colorado, west of the 107th meridian, for the Utes' "absolute and undisturbed use and occupation." Article 3 had the Utes release "all claims and rights in and to any portion of the United States or Territories" outside it. Two agencies were to be built, and the treaty extended provisions of an 1863 Tabeguache treaty to all the bands.

Article 16 said no cession of reservation land was valid unless signed by at least three-fourths of the adult male Utes.

The Colorado Encyclopedia says the treaty put the San Juan Mountains inside the reservation, "off limits to non-Indians," but prospectors entered anyway after minerals were found. That led to the 1873 Brunot Agreement, and the Utes were force-marched to Utah in 1881.
