On March 2, 1868, in Washington, Commissioner of Indian Affairs Nathaniel G. Taylor, Colorado Governor Alexander C. Hunt and Kit Carson signed a treaty with representatives of seven Ute bands. The bands were the Tabaquache, Muache, Capote, Weeminuche, Yampa, Grand River and Uintah. Article 1 extended the provisions of the 1863 Tabeguache treaty to the other bands.

Article 2 set apart a district of Colorado Territory from the 107th meridian west to the territory's western boundary. It was reserved for "the absolute and undisturbed use and occupation" of the named bands. Article 3 had the Utes relinquish "all claims and rights in and to any portion of the United States or Territories" outside it. Article 4 provided for two agencies, one on White River and one on the Rio de los Pinos.

Article 16 said no cession of reservation land would be valid unless signed by at least three-fourths of the adult male Utes interested in it.

The Colorado Encyclopedia says the 1868 treaty put the San Juan Mountains inside a reservation that took in almost the entire western third of Colorado. It says prospectors and miners entered anyway, and that the 1873 Brunot Agreement took 3.7 million acres of the reservation. It adds that the Utes were eventually force-marched to Utah in 1881.
