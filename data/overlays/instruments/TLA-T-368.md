On April 27, 1868, in Washington, Commissioner of Indian Affairs Nathaniel G. Taylor and eight Cherokee commissioners signed a supplemental article to the Cherokee treaty of July 19, 1866. The Cherokee commissioners included Lewis Downing and Elias C. Boudinot. Article 17 of the 1866 treaty had ceded in trust the "so-called Cherokee neutral lands" in Kansas, which this text says contained 800,000 acres, more or less.

The preamble tells a dispute. Interior Secretary James Harlan had contracted on August 30, 1866, to sell the land to the American Emigrant Company, a Connecticut corporation. Secretary Orville H. Browning, "regarding said sale as illegal and not in conformity with said treaty," made a contract on October 9, 1867, with James F. Joy of Detroit.

The 1868 article settled it. The American Emigrant Company would assign its contract to Joy, who would take on its obligations, and his 1867 contract would be cancelled. Within ten days of ratification, $75,000 would be paid to the Secretary of the Interior as trustee for the Cherokee Nation. Joy and the company would take only what remained after "actual settlers" had received the lands they were entitled to. The sources here do not give the Cherokee Nation's view or say what happened afterward.
