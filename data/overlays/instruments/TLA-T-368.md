On April 27, 1868, at Washington, Commissioner Nathaniel G. Taylor and eight Cherokee commissioners, including Lewis Downing and Elias C. Boudinot, signed a supplemental article to the 1866 Cherokee treaty. Article 17 of that treaty had ceded in trust the "so-called Cherokee neutral lands" in Kansas, which this treaty says contained 800,000 acres, more or less.

The Interior Secretary had contracted in 1866 to sell the land to the American Emigrant Company. A later Secretary, Orville H. Browning, regarded that sale as illegal and in 1867 contracted with James F. Joy of Detroit. The 1868 article settled the dispute by having the Emigrant Company assign its contract to Joy, who would take on its duties, while his 1867 contract was cancelled. The land was to be sold subject to the rights of "actual settlers."
