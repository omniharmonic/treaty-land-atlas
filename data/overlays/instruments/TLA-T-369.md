On April 29, 1868, at Fort Laramie, Lieutenant General William T. Sherman and other U.S. commissioners signed a treaty with bands of the Sioux Nation and the Arapaho. The Supreme Court says the treaty "was concluded at the culmination of the Powder River War of 1866-1867," in which Sioux leaders, led by Red Cloud, fought to protect their lands from settlers. The Senate ratified it on February 16, 1869, and it was proclaimed on February 24, 1869.

Article 2 set apart a district bounded by the Missouri River, the northern line of Nebraska, the 104th meridian and the 46th parallel for the "absolute and undisturbed use and occupation" of the Sioux. Article 16 declared the country north of the North Platte River and east of the Big Horn Mountains "unceded Indian territory." It promised that the military posts there would be abandoned and the road to the Montana settlements closed.

Article 12 said no cession of reservation land would be valid unless signed by at least three-fourths of the adult male Indians occupying or interested in it.

The Court's 1980 opinion tells what followed. The Black Hills, inside the reservation, held gold and silver. The Army made an expedition into the Hills in 1874. In 1877 Congress passed an Act implementing a new "agreement" and so, the Court said, "in effect, abrogated the Fort Laramie Treaty." The Court held that the 1877 Act was a taking requiring just compensation, with interest on a principal sum of $17.1 million.
