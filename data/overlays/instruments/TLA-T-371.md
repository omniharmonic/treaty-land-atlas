On May 10, 1868, at Fort Laramie, U.S. commissioners signed with chiefs and headmen of the Northern Cheyenne and Northern Arapaho. Article 2 had the tribes accept for their permanent home some portion of either the Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho reservation set by the Medicine Lodge treaty, or the reservation set for the Brule and other Sioux by the April 1868 Fort Laramie treaty.

The tribes relinquished all right, claim and interest in all territory outside the two reservations, "except the right to roam and hunt while game shall be found in sufficient quantities to justify the chase." They also agreed not to build permanent homes outside those reservations.

The treaty gave the Northern Cheyenne and Northern Arapaho no reservation of their own. The pages opened here do not describe what followed, so later events, including where the two nations eventually settled, are not covered.
