On May 10, 1868, at Fort Laramie, U.S. commissioners signed a treaty with chiefs and headmen of the Northern Cheyenne and Northern Arapaho. Article 2 had the tribes accept for their permanent home some portion of either of two reservations. One was the reservation for the Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho set by a treaty at Medicine Lodge Creek. The other was the reservation set for the Brule and other Sioux by the April 29, 1868, Fort Laramie treaty.

The tribes surrendered all right, claim and interest in territory outside those reservations, "except the right to roam and hunt while game shall be found in sufficient quantities to justify the chase." They agreed not to build permanent homes outside the reservations. Within one year they were to attach themselves permanently to one of three agencies: near Medicine Lodge Creek, on the Missouri River near Fort Randall, or the Crow agency near Otter Creek on the Yellowstone. Part of the people could join one agency and part another, as each chose.

The treaty thus gave the Northern Cheyenne and Northern Arapaho no reservation of their own. Article 8 required the signatures of at least a majority of the adult male Indians for any cession of reservation land.
