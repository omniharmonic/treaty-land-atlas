On June 1, 1868, at Fort Sumner, New Mexico, General William T. Sherman and Colonel Samuel F. Tappan signed with Navajo chiefs and headmen. Article 2 set apart a reservation, bounded on the north by the 37th parallel and including Canyon de Chelly, for the Navajo's "use and occupation." The Navajo agreed to relinquish the right to occupy any territory outside it, keeping the right to hunt on contiguous unoccupied lands. The treaty provided that their removal from Fort Sumner would be directed by the military commander.

In Arizona v. Navajo Nation (2023), the Supreme Court says the United States "forcibly moved many Navajos from their original homeland" to Bosque Redondo, and that the 1868 treaty "put an end to all war" and created a reservation on their original homeland.

The Court held the treaty reserved the water needed for the reservation but did not require the United States to take affirmative steps to secure water. The Navajo Nation's own history was not opened.
