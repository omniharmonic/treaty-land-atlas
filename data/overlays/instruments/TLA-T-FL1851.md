On September 17, 1851, U.S. commissioners D. D. Mitchell and Thomas Fitzpatrick signed a treaty near Fort Laramie with chiefs and headmen of several northern Plains nations. In Article 2 the nations recognized the right of the United States to establish roads and military and other posts within their territories. Article 5 set out the boundaries of the territories of the Sioux, Cheyenne and Arapaho, Crow and other nations.

In Article 3 the United States pledged to protect the nations against depredations by U.S. citizens. The nations did not surrender the privilege of hunting, fishing or passing over any of the described tracts. Article 7 provided $50,000 a year in goods for ten years, with up to five more years at the President's discretion.

A Kappler footnote says the Senate ratified the treaty on May 24, 1852 with an amendment cutting the annuity from 50 to 10 years, and that the tribes assented, the last being the Crows in 1854. In Montana v. United States (1981) the Supreme Court said the 1851 treaty did not by its terms convey any land to the Crow Tribe.
