On September 17, 1851, at Fort Laramie, commissioners D. D. Mitchell and Thomas Fitzpatrick met the chiefs, headmen and braves of the Sioux, Cheyenne, Arapaho, Crow, Assiniboine, Gros Ventre, Mandan and Arikara nations. Article 1 had the nations agree to "abstain in future from all hostilities whatever against each other."

In Article 2 the nations "recognize the right of the United States Government to establish roads, military and other posts, within their respective territories." In Article 3 the United States bound itself to protect them against depredations by its own people. Article 5 set out the boundaries the nations recognized as their territories. It added that they did not "abandon or prejudice any rights or claims they may have to other lands" and did not "surrender the privilege of hunting, fishing, or passing over" them. Article 7 promised $50,000 a year for ten years, with up to five more at the President's discretion, in provisions, merchandise, domestic animals and farm tools.

A footnote in the database says the Senate ratified the treaty with an amendment cutting the annuity from fifty years to ten, subject to the tribes' acceptance, and that all tribes except the Crows assented. In Montana v. United States (1981), the Supreme Court said the treaty "did not by its terms formally convey any land to the Indians at all."
