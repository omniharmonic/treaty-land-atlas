On May 28, 1851, at Dent's and Vantine's Crossings on the Stanislaus River, O. M. Wozencraft, acting for the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs, captains and head men of the Iou-ol-umnes, We-chillas and other tribes. It was never ratified, and it is one of 18 negotiated in California in 1851 and 1852. In Article 1 the bands acknowledge the United States as sole and absolute sovereign of the soil and territory Mexico ceded. Article 4 sets apart districts for them along the Tuolumne and Stanislaus Rivers, and Article 9 sets aside a tract for Captain Cornelius and his people, not as a grant in fee simple.

The National Archives' Prologue says these treaties aimed to extinguish Native title: the Indians ceded title and accepted reservations, and the government pledged to pay and to set the reservations aside. The commissioners assumed the groups were tribes; today most are known to have been villages.

The Senate rejected all 18 treaties in closed session in 1852 and imposed an injunction of secrecy. Indian title was left unresolved. The Indian Claims Commission later decided in favor of California Indians who sought compensation for lands taken without payment.
