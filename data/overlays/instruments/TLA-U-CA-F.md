On July 18, 1851, at Camp Union, near the Yuba River, O. M. Wozencraft, acting for the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs, captains and head men of the Das-pia, Ya-ma-do and other tribes. It was never ratified, and it is one of 18 negotiated in California in 1851 and 1852. In Article 1 the bands acknowledge the United States as sovereign of the territory Mexico ceded. Article 3 sets apart a district beginning on Bear River at the western line of Camp Far West, running twelve miles up the stream and north to the Yuba River. Article 4 promises 500 beef cattle and 200 sacks of flour while they moved.

The National Archives' Prologue says these treaties aimed to extinguish Native title: the Indians ceded title and accepted reservations, and the government pledged to pay and to set the reservations aside. The commissioners assumed the groups were tribes; today most are known to have been villages.

The Senate rejected all 18 treaties in closed session in 1852 and imposed an injunction of secrecy. Indian title was left unresolved. The Indian Claims Commission later decided in favor of California Indians who sought compensation for lands taken without payment.
