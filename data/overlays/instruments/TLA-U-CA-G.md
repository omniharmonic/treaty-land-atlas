On August 1, 1851, at Bidwell's Ranch, on Chico Creek, O. M. Wozencraft, acting for the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs, captains and head men of the Mi-chop-da, Es-kuin and other tribes. It was never ratified, and it is one of 18 negotiated in California in 1851 and 1852. In Article 1 the bands acknowledge the United States as sovereign of the soil and territory Mexico ceded. Article 3 sets apart a district beginning on the Feather River, two miles above Hamilton, and following the boundaries of Neal's, Hensley's and Bidwell's land grants. Article 4 promises 200 beef cattle and 75 sacks of flour.

The National Archives' Prologue says these treaties aimed to extinguish Native title: the Indians ceded title and accepted reservations, and the government pledged to pay and to set the reservations aside. The commissioners assumed the groups were tribes; today most are known to have been villages.

The Senate rejected all 18 treaties in closed session in 1852 and imposed an injunction of secrecy. Indian title was left unresolved. The Indian Claims Commission later decided in favor of California Indians who sought compensation for lands taken without payment.
