On August 20, 1851, at Camp Lu-pi-yu-ma on the south side of Clear Lake, Indian agent Redick McKee made a treaty with the Ca-la-na-po, Ha-bi-na-po, Da-no-ha-bo, Mo-al-kai and other tribes. In Article 3 they were bound by the treaty to "relinquish, cede, and forever quit claim" to the United States all right, title, claim or interest in lands or soil in California. Article 4 set apart a reservation beginning where a spur of Mount McKee meets Clear Lake. It was guaranteed to the tribes and to other tribes the United States might later move there from the Russian River valley or elsewhere.

Article 5 promised 100 head of beef cattle and 200 sacks of flour in 1851 and in each of 1852 and 1853. Article 7 promised a farmer with two assistants, a carpenter, a blacksmith and teachers. This reservation is the one the Camp Fernando Feliz treaty of two days later sends the Russian River peoples to.

None of it took effect. It was one of 18 treaties made in California in 1851 and 1852. The National Archives' Prologue magazine says the senators found the commissioners' appointments irregular, were unsure whether Mexico had recognized native land title, and heard strong objections from white Californians. The Senate "rejected the treaties and, following Senate rules, imposed an injunction of secrecy on them." With the treaties rejected, Indian title was left unresolved. Prologue adds that Indians of California later sought compensation for lands taken without payment in a case before the Indian Claims Commission. It reports that "the case was decided in favor of the Indians."
