On August 22, 1851, at Camp Fernando Feliz, on the Russian River, Redick McKee, acting for the United States, signed a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs, captains and head men of the Sai-nell, Yu-ki-as, Mas-su-ta-ka-ya and Po-mo tribes. It was never ratified, and it is one of 18 negotiated in California in 1851 and 1852. In Article 3 the tribes relinquish, cede and quit claim to the United States all their interest in lands or soil in California. Article 4 has them agree to move within one year, or when the United States required, from the Russian River to the Clear Lake reservation set up in the Camp Lu-pi-yu-ma treaty, with the United States defraying the costs.

The National Archives' Prologue says these treaties aimed to extinguish Native title: the Indians ceded title and accepted reservations, and the government pledged to pay and to set the reservations aside. The commissioners assumed the groups were tribes; today most are known to have been villages.

The Senate rejected all 18 treaties in closed session in 1852 and imposed an injunction of secrecy. Indian title was left unresolved. The Indian Claims Commission later decided in favor of California Indians who sought compensation for lands taken without payment.
