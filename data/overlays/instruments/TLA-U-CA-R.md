On November 4, 1851, at a camp in Scott's Valley, Shasta County, Indian agent Redick McKee made a treaty of peace and friendship with chiefs, captains and head men representing the Upper Klamath, Shasta and Scott's River Indians. The text counts their villages as twenty-four, nineteen and seven. In Article 3 they were bound by the treaty to "sell, cede, relinquish, and forever quit claim" to the United States all their interest in the lands they occupied and in all other lands in California. Article 4 set apart a reservation around Scott's Valley. Article 5 had the tribes move there within two years, or sooner if the Indian agent required.

Article 7 promised 200 head of beef cattle and 200 sacks of flour in each of 1852 and 1853, plus blankets and clothing. Article 9 promised to fence 1,000 acres. Article 10 kept a one-mile belt on the eastern and southern sides of the reservation within the jurisdiction of the United States.

None of it took effect. It was one of 18 treaties made in California in 1851 and 1852. The National Archives' Prologue magazine says the senators found the commissioners' appointments irregular, were unsure whether Mexico had recognized native land title, and heard strong objections from white Californians. The Senate "rejected the treaties and, following Senate rules, imposed an injunction of secrecy on them." With the treaties rejected, Indian title was left unresolved. Prologue adds that Indians of California later sought compensation for lands taken without payment in a case before the Indian Claims Commission. It reports that "the case was decided in favor of the Indians."
