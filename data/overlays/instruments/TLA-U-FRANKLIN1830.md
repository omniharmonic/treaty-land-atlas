On August 31, 1830, John H. Eaton, Secretary of War, and General John Coffee signed articles at Franklin, Tennessee, with Chickasaw chiefs and headmen. The OSU copy says they were authorized to act for the whole nation. The Chickasaw ceded their lands east of the Mississippi River north of a defined line, up to Tennessee's southern border. The United States would provide a comparable country west of the Arkansas Territory and pay $15,000 a year for twenty years after removal.

The treaty was conditional. If a suitable replacement country could not be found and approved, the text says the treaty and all its provisions would be null and void.

Chickasaw.tv says a delegation led by Levi Colbert went west in October 1830, found no acceptable land, and the Senate never approved the treaty, so it became void. The Chickasaw ceded their whole homeland east of the Mississippi in the 1832 Treaty of Pontotoc Creek instead. Chickasaw.tv also states the Chickasaw were forced to cede their lands.
