On September 23, 1805, Lieutenant Zebulon Pike of the U.S. Army, acting as agent, signed a short agreement at the mouth of the St. Peters (Minnesota) River with Sioux chiefs. The Sioux Nation granted the United States "for the purpose of the establishment of military posts" nine miles square at the mouth of the St. Croix, and a strip nine miles on each side of the Mississippi from below the Minnesota confluence up to include the falls of St. Anthony. The text gives "full sovereignty and power" over those districts.

The payment in Article 2 was left blank in the document. The Senate committee later filled it as $2,000, or goods of that value, and the Senate consented to ratification on April 16, 1808. The Sioux were to keep rights to "pass, repass, hunt or make other uses of the said districts" as before.

The OSU note says no proclamation by the President can be found, but that more than 25 years after the Senate's approval War Department correspondence spoke of the cessions "as an accomplished fact." The sources I opened do not describe the council.
