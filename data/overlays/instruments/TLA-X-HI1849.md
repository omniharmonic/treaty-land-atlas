This is a treaty between the United States and the King of the Hawaiian Islands, not with a Native nation inside the United States. It was signed at Washington on December 20, 1849, by Secretary of State John M. Clayton and James Jackson Jarves, accredited as the King's special commissioner. The preamble speaks of the "relations of good understanding which have hitherto so happily subsisted between their respective states."

Article 1 provides for "perpetual peace and amity between the United States and the King of the Hawaiian Islands, his heirs and his successors." Article 2 sets "reciprocal liberty of commerce and navigation," with no higher customs duties on one country's goods than on any other country's, and a most-favored-nation clause. Article 7 gave U.S. whale ships access to the ports of Hilo, Kealakekua and Hanalei, as well as Honolulu and Lahaina. Article 8 gave each country's citizens the right to travel and reside in the other.

The Statutes at Large page records that ratifications were exchanged at Honolulu on August 24, 1850, and that the treaty was proclaimed on November 9, 1850. Article 16 set a term of ten years, continuing until twelve months after either side gave notice to end it.
