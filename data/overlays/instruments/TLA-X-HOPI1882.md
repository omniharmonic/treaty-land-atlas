On December 16, 1882, President Chester A. Arthur signed an executive order for a tract in Arizona Territory, between the 110th and 111th meridians. The order withdrew it "from settlement and sale" and set it apart "for the use and occupancy of the Moqui and such other Indians as the Secretary of the Interior may see fit to settle thereon." The presidency.ucsb.edu copy heads it "Moqui (Hopi) Reserve." It is a unilateral executive act, not a treaty or an agreement with the Hopi.

A 2005 Senate committee report says the order set aside approximately 2.5 million acres. It says that a small but indeterminate number of Navajos lived on part of the land at the time, and that the Hopi and Navajo Tribes have disputed title and occupancy "throughout the 1890s and up to the present."

In the 1940s the Secretary of the Interior set up grazing districts, with District Six, about 631,000 acres, for the Hopi. Congress authorized a suit in 1958. In Healing v. Jones (1962, affirmed 1963), the court gave the Hopi the exclusive right to District Six and gave both tribes joint and equal rights to the rest, which became the Joint Use Area. Congress then passed the 1974 Act (P.L. 93-531) to promote a settlement through partition and land exchanges.
