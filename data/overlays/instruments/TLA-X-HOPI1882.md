On December 16, 1882, President Chester A. Arthur signed an executive order withdrawing a rectangular tract in Arizona Territory, between the 110th and 111th meridians and 35 degrees 30 minutes to 36 degrees 30 minutes north, "from settlement and sale." The land was "set apart for the use and occupancy of the Moqui and such other Indians as the Secretary of the Interior may see fit to settle thereon." Moqui is the older name for the Hopi.

No treaty or agreement with the Hopi is involved; the order is a unilateral executive act. A Senate committee report states the order set aside approximately 2.5 million acres, and that a small number of Navajos lived on part of the land at the time.

The same report says the order's wording led to conflicting Hopi and Navajo claims. In Healing v. Jones (1962, affirmed 1963), the court gave the Hopi exclusive rights to one district and both tribes joint and equal rights to the rest, which became the Joint Use Area. Congress then passed the Navajo and Hopi Indian Land Settlement Act of 1974. The Hopi Tribe's own account was not opened.
