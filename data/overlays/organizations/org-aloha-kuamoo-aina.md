Aloha Kuamoʻo ʻĀina (AKA) is a federally registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit, in its own words, founded by the Beamer ʻohana, Kanaka ʻŌiwi cultural practitioners and lineal descendants of Ke Aliʻi Manono. It is led by Keola Beamer and Dr. Kamana Beamer. Hawaiʻi has its own legal context, separate from U.S. federally recognized tribes.

AKA's About page says the land is a historic battle site connected to the 1819 conflict known as Kaua ʻAi Noa, or the Battle of Kuamoʻo, over the traditional kapu religious system. It says the site was purchased for conservation on December 31, 2015, protecting it in perpetuity, and is now owned by AKA. Its donate page says that on that date the Trust for Public Land and AKA shared that the historic 47-acre property was purchased and permanently protected.

The donate page lists the funders. They include the State Legacy Lands Fund at $3,000,000 and the Office of Hawaiian Affairs at $500,000, followed by foundations, businesses and individuals with smaller gifts.

AKA states what it intends. It says it envisions the land as a center of education for peace, justice and reconciliation, transforming a former place of conflict into a refuge for place-based learning that integrates Native Hawaiian and Indigenous cultures. It says it will engage the local community in planning for the land's future and aims to restore the cultural, historical and ecological integrity of the landscape. The donate page invites tax-deductible gifts through DonorBox or Venmo.
