The Amah Mutsun Land Trust (AMLT) describes itself as an initiative of the Amah Mutsun Tribal Band and the vehicle by which the Band accesses, protects and stewards lands integral to its identity and culture. Its history page says the Tribal Council decided in 2005 that the people should re-engage in stewardship of their ancestral lands. The Land Trust was established in 2013, with Sempervirens Fund providing fiscal sponsorship, and it became a fully incorporated 501(c)(3) in 2015.

AMLT's site gives its stewardship area as stretching from Año Nuevo in the north, along the Santa Cruz Mountains to Monterey Bay, south to the Salinas River and inland to include the Pajaro and San Benito watersheds.

On land return, AMLT's home page announces that by the end of 2025 it will acquire a 50-acre property in San Juan Bautista, which it calls the first time the Band has regained full access rights to land in its traditional territory since its forced removal over 225 years ago.

The Land Trust's donate page lists goals including conserving culturally sensitive sites, stewarding lands in its territory, a two-year Mutsun land stewardship corps program and a youth summer camp.
