The California Indian Basketweavers' Association (CIBA) is a nonprofit based in Woodland, California. ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer lists it under EIN 68-0290749, with tax exemption issued in October 1993. CIBA's site says it grew from a 1986 effort that began with a small group of women weavers and held a first informal gathering of weavers in June 1991. It says its membership is now nearly 1,000 people.

CIBA's work is cultural, not a land purchase program. Its own history page says that Mission, Spanish, Mexican and American systems cut weavers off from traditional gathering territories and that some families found former gathering sites inaccessible because of public and private land laws. In its vision statement, CIBA lists a goal of "increasing California Indian access to traditional cultural resources on public and tribal lands and traditional gathering sites."

The same statement lists working with public agencies, monitoring and discouraging pesticide use in gathering areas, and raising public awareness. The site also carries pages on cultural fire and pesticides.

CIBA's pages describe no land it holds or has returned. Its role in land return, as its pages describe it, is advocating for access and healthy gathering areas.
