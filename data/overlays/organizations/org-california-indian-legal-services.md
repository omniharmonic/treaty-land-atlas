California Indian Legal Services (CILS) is a nonprofit law office based in Sacramento. Its website says it was founded in 1967 and credits the combination of George Duke of California Rural Legal Assistance and "an inspired David Risling of the Hoopa Tribe." ProPublica lists it under EIN 94-1676390, with tax exemption issued in January 1968.

CILS says it offers "free or low-cost representation to both Native individuals and tribes in matters of federal Indian law." Its site also lists special projects, including a California water rights study and a veterans military discharge upgrade project, along with community education and outreach.

For results, CILS makes broad claims on its own site. It says it has "successfully left our mark in defending the most important rights of Indians and tribes in hundreds of major cases." It also reports dedicating 34,799 hours to advocacy in 2022. A testimonial on its site from Greg Sarris, chairman of the Federated Indians of Graton Rancheria, says CILS authored the bill signed by President Clinton on December 27, 2000, which recognized the Graton Rancheria as a federally recognized tribe.

These are the organization's own descriptions.
