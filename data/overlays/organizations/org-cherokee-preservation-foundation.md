The Cherokee Preservation Foundation is an independent nonprofit foundation based in Cherokee, North Carolina. Its site says it was established as part of the Second Amendment to the Tribal-State Compact between the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians (EBCI) and the State of North Carolina. It says it is funded by gaming revenues generated by the Tribe and is not associated with any for-profit gaming entity. ProPublica lists it under EIN 56-2272253, with tax exemption issued in August 2002. Bobby Raines is listed as executive director.

The foundation states its mission as preserving Cherokee culture, protecting the natural environment, and creating economic opportunities for the EBCI and neighbors in western North Carolina. It makes grants rather than holding or returning land.

Land-related work appears in its environmental preservation programs. One is Revitalization of Traditional Cherokee Artisan Resources (RTCAR), which it describes as a grantmaking program helping the EBCI restore the traditional balance in using natural resources such as river cane, white oak and clay. Another is Generations Qualla, a program on sustainability and stewardship.

The foundation's Impact Report lists dollar totals awarded by category, including more than $54 million in cultural preservation and more than $10 million in environmental preservation. These are the foundation's own figures; this record did not check them against its tax filings.
