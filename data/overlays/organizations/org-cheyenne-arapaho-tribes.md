The Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes are a tribal government based in Oklahoma. Their official website lists Reggie Wassana as governor and Hershel Gorham as lieutenant governor.

One land question is Fort Reno, west of Oklahoma City. News9 reports that the site was part of the tribes' reservation until an 1883 executive order by President Chester Arthur set apart a 9,493-acre military reservation. It says that after the military no longer needed the land, the roughly 9,500 acres went to the U.S. Department of Agriculture rather than back to the tribes. News9 reports that the tribes' leadership says the land is rightfully theirs and should be returned, and that farm bill language bars the transfer. Rep. Frank Lucas calls its use for agricultural research the "highest and best use for the property."

A second matter is in Colorado. The City of Boulder says it worked with Arapaho and Cheyenne tribal representatives on a draft stewardship plan for open space where Fort Chambers likely stood. Men trained there before the Sand Creek Massacre of November 29, 1864. The plan, titled "Heal the Land; Heal the People," keeps the land city-owned, with continuing tribal guidance.

Neither matter is a completed return of title to the tribes.
