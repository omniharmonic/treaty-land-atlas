The Conservation Fund is a national conservation nonprofit based in Arlington, Virginia. ProPublica lists it with tax exemption issued in July 1985. Its mission page says it has been buying at-risk land for more than 40 years and has worked in all 50 states to conserve more than 9 million acres. It is not Indigenous-led.

Its role in land return appears in Maine, in a project called Tributary Land Returns. Maine Public reports that the Conservation Fund bought a parcel of nearly 3,400 acres near Bridgewater from a local forester in 2025. It held the land for the Mi'kmaq Nation until fundraising for the $1.9 million parcel was complete. The Bangor Daily News quotes Tom Duffus of the Conservation Fund saying, "Indigenous land return projects are a cornerstone of our conservation efforts."

The Conservation Fund is one of seven non-Indigenous organizations in the project. It also contributed to the Houlton Band of Maliseet Indians' 2025 purchase of 1,327 acres in Monticello, with First Light and The Nature Conservancy, though amounts were not disclosed.

Maine Public describes the overall effort as a $50 million campaign to return more than 50,000 acres to the five Wabanaki tribes. The Conservation Fund is one partner among several, and the tribes and the Wabanaki Commission direct which parcels are chosen.
