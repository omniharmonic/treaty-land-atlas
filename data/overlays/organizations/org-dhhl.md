The Department of Hawaiian Home Lands (DHHL) is a State of Hawaii agency, not a charity. Its website says President Warren G. Harding signed the Hawaiian Homes Commission Act on July 9, 1921, and that through the Act the United States set aside approximately 200,000 acres for a permanent homeland for Native Hawaiians. The department quotes Prince Kuhio as saying the way to rehabilitate the Hawaiian people was to place them back upon the soil.

DHHL says it is responsible for 203,981 acres of trust lands, 10,045 homestead leases, and 47,086 lease applications by 29,451 Native Hawaiian beneficiaries. The gap between leases and applications is the waitlist the agency says it is working to shorten. In an April 2025 release, DHHL announced plans to issue more than 2,600 leases by the end of 2025 and said it was actively developing 29 projects.

The department is led by a nine-member Hawaiian Homes Commission, appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Senate; at least four members must have not less than one-fourth Hawaiian blood. Kali Watson is chairman, appointed by Governor Josh Green and confirmed in March 2023.

DHHL is not a land-return organization in the Land Back sense. It leases homestead lots to qualified beneficiaries rather than transferring title.
