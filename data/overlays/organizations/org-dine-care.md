Diné Citizens Against Ruining our Environment (Diné C.A.R.E.) says it is a nonprofit on the Navajo Nation that works with Navajo communities affected by energy and environmental issues. ProPublica lists it in Window Rock, Arizona, under EIN 86-0670809, with tax exemption issued in May 1995. Its site names Robyn Jackson as executive director.

The group's own history says it began in 1988 as a small community organization to stop a medical waste incinerator and dump in Dilkon, Arizona. It says the tribal government had approved the plan, and the group organized the community and defeated it. Its listed later work includes co-founding the Indigenous Environmental Network in 1990, opposing a proposed asbestos dump in 1991, and stopping what it calls reckless timber cutting in the Chuska Mountains in 1994.

Its mission statement says it advocates for traditional teachings by protecting life within the Four Sacred Mountains and promotes sustainable use of natural resources. The group says it is a membership organization by and for the Diné, and that its work is mostly paid for by foundation grants.

Diné C.A.R.E. defends land from harm. It is not a land-return group, and its pages describe no land it has bought or returned.
