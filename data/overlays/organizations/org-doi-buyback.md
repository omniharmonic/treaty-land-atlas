The Land Buy-Back Program for Tribal Nations was a federal program run by the U.S. Department of the Interior. It came out of the Cobell v. Salazar settlement, which provided a $1.9 billion Trust Land Consolidation Fund to be spent within ten years. The Secretary of the Interior established the program in 2012, and it began purchasing in December 2013.

The problem it targeted is called fractionation. Interior explains that the General Allotment Act of 1887 divided reservation land among individual tribal members. When an owner died, ownership passed to many heirs, so a single tract can have hundreds of owners. Interior gives the example of a tract on the Lac Courte Oreilles Reservation with more than 1,200 owners.

Under the program, owners willing to sell could sell their fractional interests for fair market value. The purchased interests were placed into trust for the tribes. Interior says this increased tribal trust land bases for conservation, stewardship, economic development or other uses the tribes consider beneficial.

Interior reports that the program consolidated more than 800,000 interests, equal to 2.3 million acres, across 50 locations in 15 states. The ten-year implementation period ended November 24, 2022. This is a closed federal program, not a charity.
