Dream of Wild Health (DWH) is an intertribal nonprofit serving the Minneapolis-Saint Paul Native American community. Its site says it began in 1998 as a program of Peta Wakan Tipi, a nonprofit founded in 1986, and that founder Sally Auger retired in 2011. ProPublica lists it in Minneapolis under EIN 41-1632662, with tax exemption issued in August 1989. Its staff page names Neely Snyder (St. Croix Ojibwe) as executive director.

DWH says it owns a 30-acre farm in Hugo, Minnesota. Its project timeline says DWH acquired an original 10-acre farm between 2004 and 2019, and that in March 2020 it purchased a 20-acre property. The farm site says that purchase expanded the farm from 10 acres to 30. This is a Native-led organization buying land for a farm, not a federal or treaty-based return.

The farm grows organic Indigenous foods, including corn, beans and squash from seeds the group says a Potawatomi elder gave it. DWH reports growing more than 26,000 pounds of produce in 2023, distributed through a weekly food share and partner Native organizations in the Twin Cities. Its capital campaign describes a Center for Native American Food Sovereignty and a farm incubator program offering land and tools to emerging Native farmers.

The produce, pounds and campaign goals are DWH's own statements.
