The Great Lakes Indian Fish and Wildlife Commission (GLIFWC) describes itself as made up of its staff and 11 member tribes. Together, its site says, they "advocate for Ojibwe treaty rights and natural resources stewardship." The member bands are in Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin. ProPublica lists GLIFWC in Odanah, Wisconsin, under EIN 39-1468447, with tax exemption issued in June 1985.

GLIFWC says its membership reflects the bands that signed the 1836, 1837, 1842 and 1854 treaties with the United States. The area those treaties cover is called the Ceded Territory. The site says the signing tribes reserved their ability to live a traditional life on their homelands, and that the treaties recognize the bands' right to self-govern and to regulate off-reservation harvests.

The site says the 1983 decision in Lac Courte Oreilles v. Voigt led to the formal establishment of GLIFWC's structure, mission and tribal membership. Its programs include harvest registration, seasonal harvest regulations and maps.

On land, GLIFWC's Land Conservancy page explains how conservation easements can create pathways for tribal access to privately owned land. It is guidance for landowners, not a program in which GLIFWC buys or holds land.
