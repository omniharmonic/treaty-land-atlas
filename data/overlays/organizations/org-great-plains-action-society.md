Great Plains Action Society (GPAS) calls itself an Indigenous-led grassroots nonprofit working across Iowa and eastern Nebraska. Its About page says it became an official nonprofit in 2017, began to form during the Dakota Access Pipeline resistance in 2016, and switched to 501(c)(3) status in 2022. ProPublica lists it in Des Moines under EIN 88-3978480, with tax exemption issued in December 2022.

The About page names Sikowis Nobiss (Nehiyaw/Saulteaux, George Gordon First Nation) as founder and executive director. It lists a board president, Keely Driscoll (Meskwaki and Ho-Chunk).

GPAS describes four initiatives: protecting the sacred, land defense (climate and environment), representation, and civic engagement. Its home page says its mission is to build Indigenous power and presence in Iowa and eastern Nebraska "through reMatriation, healing justice and a regenerative economy." The About page says Iowa "needs to reMatriate prairie."

GPAS lists a land and healing steward and a land defense organizer among its staff. Its tactics include mutual aid, art, education and non-violent direct action.  This record found no completed land acquisition or return by GPAS on the pages read. Its documented work is advocacy, organizing and land defense.
