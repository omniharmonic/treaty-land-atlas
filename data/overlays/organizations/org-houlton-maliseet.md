The Houlton Band of Maliseet Indians is a federally recognized tribe in Aroostook County, Maine, and one of the five Wabanaki tribes in the Tributary Land Returns project. The Bangor Daily News names it with the Mi'kmaq Nation, the two Passamaquoddy communities and the Penobscot Nation.

The County, a local news outlet, reported in February 2025 that a 1,327-acre parcel along the North Branch of the Meduxnekeag River in Monticello had been returned to the Maliseet people after a two-year effort by the tribe and conservation groups. The land adjoins the tribe's Wilderness Pines Campground. Tribal Business News says First Light and The Nature Conservancy were partners, and that the Conservation Fund contributed too; amounts were not disclosed.

Susan Young, the tribe's real estate and natural resources director, said in a release that the parcel "represents the largest of the tribe's woodlots." The tribe's initial plans include more hunting, gathering and cultural activities, with long-term timber management for forest health and possible tribal income. The parcel includes three and a half miles of river frontage where the tribe has done stream restoration for cold-water fish.

The tribe said it plans to file a fee-to-trust application with the Bureau of Indian Affairs to convert the parcel to trust land.
