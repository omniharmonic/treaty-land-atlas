Hui Aloha ʻĀina Momona is a nonprofit on Oʻahu's windward side. ProPublica lists it in Kaneohe, Hawaii, under EIN 27-3930989, with tax exemption issued in October 2011. Its website says it uses indigenous practices to help the community put native foods on dinner tables, with a goal of restoring food culture and security in Hawaiʻi.

The group describes its work as organic farming, workshops on traditional Hawaiian food preparation, healing the land and feeding people. Its pages mention events such as wrapping thousands of laulau, weeding taro patches and harvesting taro seedlings for community distribution. A current campaign addresses the coconut rhinoceros beetle, a threat to coconut palms.

Its main land-related claim is on its home page. It says that after a year-long campaign that began with an eviction letter in June 2021, it "successfully secured (with the help of the community) the subsistence farm site we have been leasing for the last 8 years." It says it is now working toward a subsistence farm school.

The site does not say who owns the farm site now or how it was secured, so it is not described here as a land return. It also does not name the group's leaders.
