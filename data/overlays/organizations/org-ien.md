The Indigenous Environmental Network (IEN) says it was established in 1990 and formed by grassroots Indigenous peoples and individuals to address environmental and economic justice. Its mission statement calls IEN "an alliance of Indigenous Peoples" that protects the sacredness of the Earth Mother from contamination and exploitation. ProPublica lists the tax ID 38-3653476 under the name Indigenous Educational Network Of Turtle Island, in Bemidji, Minnesota, with tax exemption issued in July 2014.

IEN's About page lists its activities as building the capacity of Indigenous communities and tribal governments, running an informational clearinghouse, organizing campaigns and direct actions, and convening local, regional and national meetings. It says it supports Indigenous communities and youth mainly in North America and, more recently, globally.

Its website currently features a monthly "Keep It In The Ground" report, a campaign against a pipeline permit, a report on banking and climate, and mapping tools for oil, gas and petrochemical threats. It also lists a news item on tribal nations seeking protection of a sacred pipestone site.

IEN is an advocacy and organizing network. Its pages describe no land bought or returned by IEN, and do not name its leaders.
