The Indian Land Tenure Foundation (ILTF) describes itself as "a national, community-based organization serving American Indian nations and people in the recovery and control of their rightful homelands." ProPublica lists it in Little Canada, Minnesota, under EIN 41-2014273, with tax exemption issued in October 2001. Its leadership page says Howard D. Valandra, a Sicangu Lakota and enrolled member of the Rosebud Sioux Tribe, returned in 2025 to serve as president, and that an 11-member board is made up of Indian landowners, tribal representatives and others committed to Indian land issues.

ILTF's stated mission is that land within the original boundaries of every reservation, and other areas where tribes retain aboriginal interest, is in Indian ownership and management. It pursues this through four strategies: education, cultural awareness, economic opportunity and legal reform. It also says it educates landowners about land management, ownership and transfer, noting problems such as fractionated ownership, where one parcel has many heirs.

As a community foundation, ILTF accepts contributions to support grantmaking and program initiatives. It publishes educational materials, estate-planning resources for landowners and annual reports.

ILTF funds and supports land recovery by others; it is not known to hold land itself. Its results are described by the organization in its annual reports.
