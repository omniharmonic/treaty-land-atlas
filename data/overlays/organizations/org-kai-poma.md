Kai Poma is a nonprofit founded by representatives of the Sherwood Valley Band of Pomo Indians, Round Valley Indian Tribes and Coyote Valley Band of Pomo Indians, three tribes with ancestral ties to the Mendocino County coast, according to KRCR. ProPublica lists Kai Poma in Willits, California, under EIN 88-0548030, with tax exemption issued in March 2022. Its own website was a "coming soon" page when fetched.

KRCR reported on July 13, 2026 that a 136-acre beach property near Westport, Blues Beach, would be transferred from the state to Kai Poma. Caltrans described it as the first transfer of land managed by Caltrans and owned by California to a Native nonprofit. The California Transportation Commission approved the transfer on June 26. The Mendocino Voice reported that legislation by Sen. Mike McGuire, signed by Gov. Gavin Newsom in 2021, allowed Caltrans to transfer the beach at no cost to a qualified nonprofit.

Kai Poma Chairman Eddie Knight said preserving and restoring the beach supports tribal practices such as "harvesting food, harvesting medicine, and safeguarding sites of historical and spiritual significance." Governor Newsom said the land will be managed under the stewardship of local tribes.

Reports differ on timing. KRCR says the land will be transferred; The Mendocino Voice says it has been transferred, as Caltrans announced. This record could not confirm a recorded deed.
