McKenzie River Trust is a nonprofit land trust formed in 1989 to protect habitats and scenic lands in the McKenzie River basin. Its home page says its service area now covers much of western Oregon, including coastal headlands from Reedsport to Lincoln City, and that it has protected more than 10,000 acres. ProPublica lists it in Eugene under EIN 93-1029808, with tax exemption issued in September 1995. It is not an Indigenous-led organization.

Its own site reports that on November 14, 2024, the Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians regained ownership of 27 acres of their ancestral homelands at Cape Foulweather on the central Oregon coast. The Trust calls it the capstone of a multi-year collaboration to protect the land's ecological, cultural and scenic values.

In October 2026, Underscore Native News, republished by OPB, reported that the Siletz Tribe is buying a nearly seven-acre parcel south of Yachats. A partner land trust said it was appraised and purchased for $120,000, with support from McKenzie River Trust and the Nature Conservancy. Conservation Director Eli Tome described the Trust's role as real estate transactions and fundraising, and said it used flexible funding so the title carries no restriction. The article said the transaction was still ongoing but could be finalized within the week.

The tribe's Natural Resources Director, Angela Sondenaa, said the land was part of the tribe's ancestral territory and the coast reservation.
