The Mechoopda Indian Tribe of Chico Rancheria says on its own website that it is a federally recognized tribe of 560 members governed by an elected Tribal Council. Its ancestral village was on Little Butte Creek, less than four miles south of downtown Chico.

In September 2022, Chico State reported the first land return of its kind in California. Ownership and management of the 93-acre Butte Creek Ecological Preserve passed from Chico State Enterprises to the tribe under a zero-dollar agreement approved by the California Wildlife Conservation Board. The agreement was enabled by Assembly Bill 379. The university said no buildings can be built on the land, and that Chico State faculty and staff keep research access under an MOU.

In 2026, press and trade reports describe a second return of roughly 450 acres in the Big Chico Creek watershed, in two properties next to the Big Chico Creek Ecological Reserve. A local real estate firm says escrow closed on May 5 and that the tribe's total holdings rose to about 3,000 acres. It says the former owners, who lost their homes in the 2024 Park Fire, chose to transfer the land back.

The firm says the tribe's crew will use traditional ecological knowledge and prescribed burns to restore the fire-hit land. That is the broker's account, not a tribal statement.
