The Mi'kmaq Nation, a Wabanaki tribe with lands in Aroostook County, Maine, received a land return of nearly 3,400 acres near Bridgewater, Maine Public reported on June 26, 2026. The parcel has forests, wetlands and three miles of frontage on North Whitney Brook. The Conservation Fund bought it from a local forester the year before and held it for the Nation until fundraising for the $1.9 million parcel was complete.

Maine Public says the parcel will more than double the tribe's land holdings. The Bangor Daily News gives its Mi'kmaq name as Mesgilg Maqamigew, or "big land," and reports that the Nation will keep public ATV, snowmobile, bicycle and pedestrian access on a former railroad bed that crosses it.

Chief Sheila McCormack said in a news release, "This property will give our people a place for outdoor recreation, a place to teach our children how to hunt and fish." Maine Public says the tribe hopes to use it for hunting, fishing and basket-weaving. Former Vice Chief Richard Silliboy told the Bangor Daily News he would like walking trails and a few cabins, and that the land will eventually be placed in a federal land trust, which can take years.

It is the sixth completed project in the Tributary Land Returns effort.
