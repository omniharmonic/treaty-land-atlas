NANA Regional Corporation is not a tribal government or a charity. Its own website says it is a for-profit Alaska Native Corporation, formed as a result of the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (ANCSA), which Congress passed in 1971. Alaska is a distinct legal case: ANCSA settled aboriginal land claims by giving land and money to Native-owned corporations rather than creating reservations.

The Act itself states Congress's aim. Its first finding says "an immediate need for a fair and just settlement of all claims by Natives and Native groups of Alaska." The Act's land provisions direct that land be selected and conveyed to Village Corporations and Regional Corporations.

NANA says it is owned by more than 15,500 Iñupiaq shareholders who live in or have roots in Northwest Alaska. Its region covers 38,000 square miles, largely above the Arctic Circle, and includes 11 communities, among them Kotzebue and Kivalina. It says that in 1976, 10 of the 11 village corporations merged with NANA; Kotzebue kept its own corporation.

On land, NANA says it manages its lands to protect subsistence activities, including land use permitting, ANCSA land conveyances and access issues. It runs a trespass program to protect its lands. Its board chair is listed as Lee "Big Boy" Staheli of Kiana.
