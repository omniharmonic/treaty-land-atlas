The Nanticoke Indian Association is the nonprofit of the Nanticoke Indian Tribe in Delaware. ProPublica lists it in Millsboro under EIN 51-0261316, with tax exemption issued in June 1987. Its mission statement says it aims to enhance and promote the welfare of the Nanticoke Indian community and to preserve the tribe through conservation, education and cultural awareness programs. Its leadership page lists Chief Avery "Leaving Tracks" Johnson and Assistant Chief Farrah Norwood-Stigall.

The tribe's website carries an article excerpt, by Jon Hurdle of Delaware Public Media and dated November 24, 2021, about a land return. It says a 31-acre cornfield on the edge of Millsboro is the first ancestral parcel the community has ever owned. The tribe took ownership in October 2021 after The Conservation Fund, a land-preservation nonprofit, bought the land and donated it. The parcel adjoins the Nanticoke Indian Center, where the tribe's existing parcel is about one acre.

According to the article, the Fund declined to say how much it paid but said the price was less than the $999,000 listing. Mount Cuba Center and the Delaware Open Space Council provided funding. The sellers, the Harmon and Draine families, accepted a slightly lower bid than a competing one because they wanted to protect the land by transferring it to the tribe.

A State of Delaware easement allows tribal activities but permanently prevents residential development.
