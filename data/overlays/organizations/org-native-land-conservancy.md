The Native Land Conservancy, based in Mashpee, Massachusetts, calls itself the first Native American-led land conservation group east of the Mississippi. Its Our Story page says it was founded in 2012. It quotes founder Ramona Peters, who speaks as a Wampanoag, saying she founded it "to see if people would be inclined to give some land back."

Its Land Holdings page says it holds title to more than 295 acres across 11 towns in Massachusetts, plus 8.5 acres on an island in Lake Superior in Wisconsin. Most holdings began as gifts from landowners. In 2023 the group bought 3.3 acres at Aquinnah, and says it holds that land until the Aquinnah Land Initiative can raise funds to buy it.

The group also holds conservation easements on land it does not own. One is 776 acres in central Alabama cared for by a Maskoke community. In 2016 it signed a five-year cultural respect agreement with the Dennis Conservation Land Trust, which it describes as the first of its kind on the East Coast and later renewed as a perpetual easement.

ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists Native Land Conservancy Inc of Mashpee as a 501(c)(3), with tax exemption issued in June 2014.
