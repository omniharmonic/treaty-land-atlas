Native Hawaiian Legal Corporation (NHLC) calls itself a nonprofit public interest law firm representing Native Hawaiians. Its history page says it was formed in 1974, as part of a grassroots effort, after Native Hawaiians had trouble getting leases under the Hawaiian Homes Commission Act while those lands were increasingly leased to people the Act was not meant to benefit. ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists it as a Honolulu 501(c)(3), tax exemption issued June 1993.

NHLC runs six legal programs. Two of them concern land and places: one protects family homestead and land rights against lease cancellation and adverse possession, and one protects culturally significant places (wahi pana). Its service pages say it holds state officials to ceded-lands trust duties and helps Hawaiian Homes beneficiaries with lost applications, lease cancellations and lot-size cuts. For 2025 it reports more than 560 matters and 170 cases managed.

One recent case is East Maui water. An NHLC senior staff attorney wrote on October 8, 2026 that the Board of Land and Natural Resources denied Nā Moku Aupuni o Koʻolau Hui a contested case hearing three times, on the 2016, 2025 and 2026 permits, and that the Environmental Court each time later found the Board had violated the law. That is NHLC's own account of a case still in progress.
