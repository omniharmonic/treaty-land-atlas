The Nolumbeka Project is a 501(c)(3) in Massachusetts. It describes itself as a welcoming, inclusive, non-tribal organization, and says its volunteer board includes several members of mixed Native American heritage. Its stated work is educational events with Indigenous presenters, an archive for tribes and educators, and protecting land, especially the ancient multi-tribal villages of which Wissatinnewag is a part.

Its land record is the Wissatinnewag property in the Connecticut River Valley. Its history page says that on March 30, 2001, the Friends of Wissatinnewag, Inc., which later blended into the Nolumbeka Project in 2013, bought the property after years of negotiation. The page says it had been targeted for a Walmart and was the last undeveloped piece of an ancient Indian village.

The group paid $780,000 for 61 acres, well under the original asking price of $2 million. Twenty acres were sold to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and ownership of the other 41 acres went to the Nolumbeka Project. These figures come from the organization's own history.

ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists The Nolumbeka Project Inc as a 501(c)(3), tax exemption issued April 2011. Donors should know that the organization says it does not determine who is Native and seeks tribal and non-tribal input, so tribal consent for specific claims should be checked.
