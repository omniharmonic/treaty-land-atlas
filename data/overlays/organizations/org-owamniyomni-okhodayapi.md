Owámniyomni Okhódayapi is a Dakota-led nonprofit in Minneapolis. Its About page says it was founded in 2016 by Paul Reyelts and Mark Wilson, who are not described as Native, as the St. Anthony Falls Lock & Dam Conservancy. It was renamed Friends of the Lock & Dam, then Friends of the Falls in 2020, and later took its Dakota name. The page says the organization is Dakota-led with a Native American majority board. Shelley Buck, an enrolled member of the Prairie Island Indian Community, is its president.

Its project is the Upper Lock site. The About page says the lock and the land around it are owned by the federal government through the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and that the lock closed to commercial navigation in 2015. The vision is to transform five acres there into a place of healing, restoration, education and connection. The organization is designing the project in coordination with the Minneapolis Park and Recreation Board. It says it follows the vision of the four Dakota Nations in Minnesota.

The page describes a conveyance process involving the Dakota Nations, the City, the Park Board and the Corps. It does not say the land has been transferred. It lists planned seasons of work, with cultural and environmental restoration set for 2026 to 2027.

ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists the organization as a Minneapolis 501(c)(3), tax exemption issued May 2016.
