Save California Salmon (SCS) says it began in 2017 and is a 501(c)(3) Indigenous-led nonprofit. Its About page says it was formed after the 2002 Klamath River fish kill, in which, it says, federal water diversions led to the death of over 65,000 salmon. ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists it in Orleans, California, with tax exemption issued in July 2022.

SCS describes its mission as restoring and protecting Pacific salmon, their watersheds and the communities that depend on them. It says it works through policy advocacy, education, creative expression and grassroots organizing, and that it lifts up Indigenous Traditional Ecological Knowledge. The home page currently asks visitors to speak up on a Bay Delta water plan before the state's Water Board.

The site says the board is made up of tribal leaders, fishermen, educators and scientists from the watersheds it works in. Its team page lists members such as Allie Hostler of the Hoopa Valley Tribe, Malissa Tayaba of the Shingle Springs Band of Miwok Indians, and Sammy Gensaw, a Yurok fisherman.

SCS says it was part of the historic removal of dams on the Klamath River in 2024, and that salmon have since returned to over 300 river miles. These are the organization's own statements; this entry did not read an outside report. SCS works on water and fish, not on land transfers.
