Tanka Fund's mission, as stated on its Who We Are page, is to return buffalo to Native American lands, lives and economies. The page says it is a 501(c)(3) that is Native American-led and staffed, and that since 2014 it has provided animals, grants and technical assistance to Native-owned or managed family- and community-based buffalo ranches. It says it works with Native ranchers of all tribal affiliations across the United States. Its team page lists Dawn Sherman, a member of the Lakota, Shawnee and Delaware tribes, as Executive Director.

The page reports that its rancher partners now represent more than 2,500 head of buffalo over 100,000 acres of land, and that it aims to reach 500,000 acres and 5,000 buffalo or more within five years. The first is a current figure and the second is a goal. Both are the organization's own.

Tanka Fund does not buy land for tribes. It supports the people who graze buffalo on land they already own or manage. Its home page tells of a Rosebud Reservation ranch bringing buffalo back after more than 125 years, with 40 buffalo now and room for 65. Its Young Native Leaders Network provides training and partnerships for young ranchers.

ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists Tanka Fund of Kyle, South Dakota, as a 501(c)(3) with exemption issued in September 2019. The board page lists Ron Brownotter, a Standing Rock buffalo rancher, as vice president.
