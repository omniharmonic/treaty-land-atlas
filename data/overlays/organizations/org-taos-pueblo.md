Taos Pueblo's website calls it one of the oldest living and continuously inhabited communities in the United States, and says its multi-storied adobe homes are estimated to be over a thousand years old. Its About page describes a government in which the Governor cares for the traditional pueblo village and the War Chief cares for the land. A Tribal Council made up of men who have served as War Chiefs or Governors serves for life.

The page tells the Blue Lake story in the Pueblo's words. It says that in 1906 the lake was taken by Theodore Roosevelt and placed in the National Forest registry, that it is revered as a sacred area, and that its return capped a long history of struggle. Its list of cultural milestones includes "Blue Lake returned to Taos in 1970."

The federal law is Public Law 91-550, enacted in December 1970. Its first section amends an Act of May 31, 1933 and provides that described lands "upon which said Indians depend and have depended since time immemorial for water supply, forage for their domestic livestock, wood and timber for their personal use, and as the scene of certain religious ceremonials" are declared to be held by the United States in trust for the Pueblo de Taos.

The text read here did not state acreage, so this entry does not repeat the record's 48,000 acres. The Pueblo's site is inconsistent on when Taos became a National Historic Landmark (1960 on one page, 1965 on another).
