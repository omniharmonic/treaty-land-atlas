Wabanaki REACH, formally Maine-Wabanaki REACH, says it supports the self-determination of Wabanaki people through education, truth-telling, restorative justice and restorative practices in Wabanaki and Maine communities. Its history page traces it to an ICWA Workgroup formed after a 1999 federal review found Maine out of compliance with the Indian Child Welfare Act. In 2008 the group decided to set up the Maine Wabanaki-State Child Welfare Truth and Reconciliation Commission and took the name REACH. The page says the Commission was seated in 2013, spent 27 months on its work, and interviewed over 150 people.

REACH says it operated under an outside fiscal sponsor for years before incorporating on April 1, 2018 and becoming a 501(c)(3). ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists Maine-wabanaki Reach of Old Town, Maine, with exemption issued March 2019.

Its board page lists Passamaquoddy and other Wabanaki members, including one who has worked for the Passamaquoddy Tribe and Maine Coast Heritage Trust on land return. A blog post on its site describes a land purchase made by Niweskok, a separate Wabanaki group, free of a conservation easement. That land belongs to Niweskok, not REACH.

REACH's own programs are educational and healing programs, a Wabanaki Studies institute and truth-seeking work. The sources here show no land acquisitions by REACH itself.
