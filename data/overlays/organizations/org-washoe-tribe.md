The Washoe Tribe's own programs are not described here; see its website. The land return comes from partner and news accounts.

On February 11, 2026, the Northern Sierra Partnership announced the return of 10,274 acres in the Northern Sierra to the ownership and care of the Washoe people. The land, formerly Loyalton Ranch, is northeast of Lake Tahoe and about 20 miles north of Reno. It is now the WélmeltiɁ Preserve, owned by the Waší·šiw Land Trust, which the Partnership describes as a Washoe-led nonprofit dedicated to returning ancestral lands to the Washoe People of Nevada and California.

The Partnership says it and the Feather River Land Trust began working with the Tribe in 2021 and had the property under contract in late 2023. It reports $6.9 million raised, including $5.5 million in public funding from the Wildlife Conservation Board and $1.4 million in private donations. KOLO-TV reported on April 8, 2026 that the Waší·šiw Land Trust completed the purchase and called it the largest tribal land return ever completed in the Sierra Nevada.

Washoe Tribe Chairman Serrell Smokey is quoted saying the land purchase is "good medicine for our people." The land is owned by the land trust, which is separate from the Tribe's government.
