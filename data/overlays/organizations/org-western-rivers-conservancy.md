Western Rivers Conservancy (WRC) is a nonprofit land trust in Portland, Oregon, working on rivers in nine western states. It is not Indigenous-led. Its staff page lists Nelson Mathews as President. ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer lists it as a 501(c)(3) with exemption issued in February 2002. WRC says it has protected over 227,000 acres along more than 260 rivers.

WRC's strongest tribal result is Blue Creek. WRC says it and the Yurok Tribe set up a long-term partnership in 2009 to buy 47,097 acres along the lower Klamath River and Blue Creek from Green Diamond Resource Company. Its Yurok partner page says WRC bought 47,000 acres of privately owned land and returned it to the Yurok, doubling the tribe's land holdings and creating the Blue Creek Salmon Sanctuary and the Yurok Tribal Community Forest. Its project page says that in May 2025, after a 23-year partnership, WRC and the Tribe completed the largest single "land back" deal in California history. WRC says it raised $60 million for the project, in part through carbon offset credits.

WRC's Tribal Nation Partners page also describes helping the Esselen Tribe of Monterey County regain a 1,199-acre ranch in 2020, which it says was the first land ever returned to the tribe since the Spanish displaced it. These descriptions are WRC's own.

A gift to WRC is not a gift to any tribe.
