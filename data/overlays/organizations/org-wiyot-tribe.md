The Wiyot Tribe's official website describes the tribe's home as the Humboldt Bay region of California, where it says Wiyot people have lived for thousands of years. Its Tribal Government page states the tribe's mission: to exercise tribal rights and promote the common welfare, to establish the supreme law of the Tribe, to provide for and protect its sovereign right to exercise self-government pursuant to its own laws, to protect and develop its lands and resources, and to promote and safeguard its aboriginal rights.

The tribe's website says the tribe established the Wiyot Sacred Site Fund to purchase back portions of Indian Island as they become available, along with other sites of religious or cultural significance. The tribe's Natural Resources department lists a Tuluwat Project, an environmental education program, fisheries and other environmental projects.

A major return to the tribe came from the City of Eureka. North Coast Journal reported that the City Council voted unanimously on December 4, 2018 to direct staff to finalize the transfer of 200 acres of Indian Island to the Wiyot Tribe, and that a ceremony of return was scheduled for October 21, 2019. Active NorCal reported that the city carried out the transfer in a ceremony on October 21, 2019. Those reports are outside accounts of a tribal land return, not statements by the tribe.

The tribe's site lists a tribal office phone number but no donation page. Contact the tribe before assuming any giving channel exists.
