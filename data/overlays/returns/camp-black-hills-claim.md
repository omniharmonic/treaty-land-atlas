The Black Hills of South Dakota were part of the Great Sioux Reservation set up by the Fort Laramie Treaty of April 29, 1868, in which the United States pledged the area would be set apart for the absolute and undisturbed use and occupation of the Sioux. In the Act of 1877, Congress took the Black Hills.

The Sioux Nation brought the claim for generations. In United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (decided June 30, 1980), the Supreme Court held that the 1877 Act effected a taking of tribal property, which implied an obligation to make just compensation, including interest. The lower court had set the principal at $17.1 million, the 1877 fair market value, with 5 percent annual interest from 1877.

The money has not been collected. PBS NewsHour reported in 2011 that $102 million was set aside in 1980 and that the trust's value had grown well beyond $1 billion, but that the Sioux had never collected. It reported the tribes say the land was never for sale and that accepting the funds would be tantamount to a sales transaction. In PBS's words, former special trustee Ross Swimmer said: "They didn't want the money. They wanted the Black Hills." PBS also quotes a Sioux leader saying some tribal members want to accept the money.
