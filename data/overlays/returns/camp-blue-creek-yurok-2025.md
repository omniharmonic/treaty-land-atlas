The Yurok Tribe, California's largest federally recognized tribal nation, now owns 47,097 acres on the eastern side of the lower Klamath River in Northern California. Native News Online reported the land deal, announced by the Tribe, Western Rivers Conservancy (WRC) and two California state agencies, in June 2025. The land is now managed as the Blue Creek Salmon Sanctuary and Yurok Tribal Community Forest.

WRC acquired or facilitated the transfer of the lands from Green Diamond Resource Company in several phases between 2009 and 2017, then conveyed them to the Tribe in stages. The final phase, 14,968 acres, officially transferred on May 30, 2025. Native News Online reports a total cost of $56 million, of which just $8 million came from direct public grants. The rest came from private foundations, corporate and individual giving, carbon credit sales and a federal tax credit program.

Tribal Chairman Joseph L. James said the partnership of more than two decades forges a sustainable future for the fish, forests and people that honors both ecological integrity and cultural heritage. The release describes the lands as important habitat for salmon and a cold-water refuge, and says carbon credit revenue will support the project over the long term.

High Country News calls it the largest tribal land return in California history and says it more than doubles the Tribe's landholdings.
