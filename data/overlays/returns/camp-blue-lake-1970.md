Taos Pueblo's own page states that on December 15, 1970, President Richard Nixon signed Public Law 91-550, restoring Taos Pueblo lands. The Pueblo's page and Nixon's remarks agree on the number: 48,000 acres, which Nixon said were taken from the Taos Pueblo Indians in 1906. The Pueblo says the signing led to the unhindered continuation of its traditional culture and set a precedent for self-determination for all American Indian people.

The National Archives traces the path. In 1903 the Secretary of Agriculture asked for a forest reserve in New Mexico, and in 1906 President Theodore Roosevelt made the withdrawal permanent, making the land a national forest. The Commissioner of Indian Affairs and the local superintendent petitioned for its return in 1912 and 1918 and were rebuffed. A 1927 cooperative agreement and a 1940 permit gave limited protections but did not satisfy the Pueblo.

In 1965 the Indian Claims Commission ruled the Pueblo entitled to nearly $300,000 for the land. The Pueblo proposed waiving the award in exchange for the land around Blue Lake. The House passed transfer bills in 1966, 1967 and 1968; H.R. 471 passed the Senate 70 to 12 on December 2, 1970.

At the signing, Nixon said the bill represented justice rather than a gift, and respect for religion, and marked a new direction of self-determination rather than termination.
