The Esselen Tribe of Monterey County regained its first ancestral homelands since it was displaced by the Spanish four centuries ago, according to a Western Rivers Conservancy (WRC) press release dated July 27, 2020. The release says the Tribe, WRC and the California Natural Resources Agency completed a conservation project on the preceding Friday that placed the 1,199-acre Adler Ranch, in Big Sur about 20 miles south of Monterey, into Esselen ownership.

The release explains the mechanism. When the ranch went up for sale, WRC set out to purchase it to protect a stretch of the Little Sur River, a steelhead stream, and old-growth redwoods. WRC and the Tribe then raised money to transfer the ranch to the Tribe. In 2019 the partners applied to the California Natural Resources Agency, which later awarded the Tribe $4.5 million so it could purchase the land from WRC.

The Esselen Tribe says what it intends. Tribal Chairman Tom Little Bear Nason said the land will allow the Tribe to protect its traditions and preserve the cultural heritage of the historic tribes of the region. The release says the Esselen plan to build a traditional village and to allow other tribes in the area, including the Rumsen, Ohlone and Amah Mutsun, to use the property. It describes the property as a fraction of the Tribe's former territory but enough to reinvigorate tribal culture, hold ceremonies, and teach tribal members and the public.
