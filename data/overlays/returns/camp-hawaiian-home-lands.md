Hawaiian Home Lands is a program of leasing land to Native Hawaiians. It is a different legal context from federally recognized tribal land: it comes from an act of Congress and is run by the State of Hawai'i, not from a treaty or a reservation.

The Department of Hawaiian Home Lands (DHHL) says Congress passed the Hawaiian Homes Commission Act, 1920, and President Warren Harding signed it on July 9, 1921 (42 Stat. 108). DHHL says that through the Act the United States set aside approximately 200,000 acres to establish a permanent homeland for native Hawaiians. It quotes Prince Kuhio, the leader of the effort, writing that the only method to rehabilitate the race was to place them back upon the soil.

Under the Act, DHHL says, native Hawaiians are defined as individuals having at least 50 percent Hawaiian blood. The department provides 99-year homestead leases at an annual rental of $1, which a 1990 state law allowed to be extended to an aggregate of 199 years. Leases are for residential, agricultural or pastoral purposes.

As of the DHHL page read for this record, the department is responsible for 203,981 acres of trust lands, 10,045 homestead leases, and 47,086 lease applications by 29,451 native Hawaiian beneficiaries. DHHL lists housing development among its priorities, citing Act 279's $600 million. Its own page, not an independent source, supplies these figures.
