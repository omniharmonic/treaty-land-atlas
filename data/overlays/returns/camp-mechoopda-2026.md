The Mechoopda Indian Tribe of Chico Rancheria in Butte County, California, has reportedly taken back roughly 450 acres of its ancestral land. Hoodline, a local news aggregator, reported on June 9, 2026, citing Action News Now, that two properties beside the Big Chico Creek watershed and the Big Chico Creek Ecological Reserve closed in June. The sellers are not named in the source read.

The purchase was helped along by California's Tribal Nature-Based Solutions program, an initiative of the California Natural Resources Agency that directs funding to tribal land returns. The Governor's April 2024 announcement describes that program as supporting ancestral land return, restoration and wildfire resilience. Hoodline says state application records showed the tribe originally proposed a broader purchase of about 658 acres from several willing sellers, and that the 450 acres appear to be an initial closing within that larger plan.

Hoodline reports that tribal leaders cast the deal as a return of stewardship to Mechoopda ancestors, not just a real estate transaction. It also notes that in 2022 California State University, Chico transferred 93 acres in Big Chico Creek Canyon to the tribe under a conservation and stewardship agreement.

This record rests on one secondary source. The state's own announcement could not be opened for verification.
