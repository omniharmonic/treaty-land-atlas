A bill to expand the Miccosukee Tribe's land in Florida's Everglades did not become law. Native News Online reported on January 2, 2026 that President Donald Trump vetoed H.R. 504, the Miccosukee Reserved Area Amendments Act, which passed both chambers of Congress without opposition. The bill would have amended the Miccosukee Reserved Area Act to formally include the tribe's historic Osceola Camp and direct the Interior Department to work with the tribe to protect homes and infrastructure from flooding.

Grist reports the bill passed on December 11 and was vetoed on December 30, 2025, and that the House failed to override on Thursday, January 8, 2026. It describes the transfer as 30 acres of Everglades land. Native News Online says the veto came "on Monday," which would be December 29, so the veto date differs between the two outlets. The Miccosukee are recognized as a tribe by the federal government and, Grist says, must seek land additions through Congress rather than the Interior Department.

Trump's veto message said the tribe had actively sought to obstruct immigration policies, referring to its lawsuit over the "Alligator Alcatraz" detention center. Miccosukee Chairman Talbert Cypress said in a statement that the bill was about protecting people, homes and a historic community, and not about immigration. He added that the tribe would pursue protections by other means.
