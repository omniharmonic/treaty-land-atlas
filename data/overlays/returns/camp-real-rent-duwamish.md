Real Rent is a voluntary program, run by supporters of the Duwamish Tribe, that asks people who live and work in Seattle to make rent payments to the Tribe. Its website says the Tribe has yet to be justly compensated for its land, resources and livelihood. It is not a land return and moves no land.

The Real Rent site says 100 percent of the money goes to Duwamish Tribal Services (DTS), a nonprofit run by the Duwamish Tribe. It says DTS provides social, educational, health and cultural services and operates the Duwamish Longhouse and Cultural Center, a free museum, event space and community center. The site suggests payers choose an amount that feels meaningful, for example $54 a month as a reminder of 54,000 acres of homeland.

The site makes a claim about history. It says that in 1971, 1,000 Duwamish members were paid $64 each for their land, so about $64,000 in total for 54,000 acres. That figure is the program's own. The 1855 Treaty of Point Elliott, which the Duwamish and other tribes signed on January 22, 1855, ceded lands to the United States in Article 1, and was ratified March 8, 1859. The site also says the Tribe does not have federal recognition and invites visitors to sign a petition for it.
