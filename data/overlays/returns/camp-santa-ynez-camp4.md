Camp 4 is a ranch of roughly 1,400 acres along Highway 246 in the Santa Ynez Valley, California. The Santa Ynez Band of Chumash Indians says it bought the land from Fess Parker in 2010 to provide tribal housing for its members, only 17 percent of whom lived on the original reservation. The Band says it plans 143 homes and a small Tribal Hall on the land.

The route to trust status was contested. Noozhawk reported on February 13, 2019 that U.S. District Judge Stephen Wilson granted summary judgment to a local landowner challenging a 2017 Bureau of Indian Affairs decision to take Camp 4 into trust, declaring that decision null and void and sending the matter back to the agency. The suit argued an official lacked authority to issue the final decision.

The Band's own press release of December 17, 2019 says the Senate voted to pass the National Defense Authorization Act, which includes language directing the Department of the Interior to take 1,427 acres, known as Camp 4, into federal trust for the Band. The Band says it had pursued both the administrative and legislative routes. Its release says Senator Feinstein's office cited the Band's agreement with Santa Barbara County and a perpetual exclusion of gaming on Camp 4. The Band's reservation page states Camp 4 was placed into trust through federal legislation on December 20, 2019, increasing the reservation tenfold.
