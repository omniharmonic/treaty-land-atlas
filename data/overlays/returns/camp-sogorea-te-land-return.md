Sogorea Te' Land Trust works in the East Bay of California, which it describes as traditional Lisjan Ohlone land. Its website says the Land Trust cultivates what it calls rematriation, defined as restoring a people to their rightful place in sacred relationship with their ancestral land, and that its work is made possible by the leadership of Indigenous women and culture keepers.

The Land Trust's page on rematriation lists the sites it has worked on. It says Lisjan, in East Oakland, is a traditional village site and the first piece of land rematriated by Sogorea Te' within Lisjan territory. It says 'Ookwe Park is the first park returned to the care of the Confederated Villages of Lisjan and Sogorea Te' Land Trust, Rammay is a small garden in West Oakland, and Rinihmu Pulte'irekne is 3.8 acres of land in the Oakland hills. Ištune is a house it bought in August 2020, with 14 days to raise $750,000. The sources here do not give acreage for most sites or a total.

The Land Trust funds its work in part through the Shuumi Land Tax, a voluntary annual contribution that non-Indigenous people living on Lisjan Ohlone territory make. Shuumi means gift in Chochenyo. The Land Trust states that the Ohlone community in the East Bay has no land base and is not federally recognized.

It says that with community support it built the first ceremonial arbor in Lisjan territory in more than 250 years and rematriated multiple sites, including one house, to Indigenous stewardship.
