On December 28, 2016, President Barack Obama used the Antiquities Act to create Bears Ears National Monument in southeastern Utah. The proclamation says the land "is profoundly sacred to many Native American tribes, including the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, Navajo Nation, Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah Ouray, Hopi Nation, and Zuni Tribe," and that "traditional ecological knowledge" held by Native peoples is "itself, a resource to be protected."

The proclamation reserved "all lands and interests in lands owned or controlled by the Federal Government" within its boundaries, which "encompass approximately 1.35 million acres." It withdrew those federal lands from entry, sale and disposition under the public land laws, from location under the mining laws, and from mineral and geothermal leasing, subject to valid existing rights.

It also created a new kind of tribal role. A Bears Ears Commission was established "to provide guidance and recommendations" on management plans, made up of "one elected officer each from the Hopi Nation, Navajo Nation, Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah Ouray, and Zuni Tribe."

The Bears Ears Inter-Tribal Coalition's own site says the five tribes formed the Coalition in July 2015 and sent President Obama a Tribally-led proposal dated October 15, 2015. It describes the Commission as a model of collaborative management grounded in Tribal sovereignty. The monument was reduced in 2017, restored in 2021, and reduced again in 2026, as the later entries on this site describe.
