When a tribe wants land taken into federal trust, it applies to the Bureau of Indian Affairs. This "fee-to-trust" process rests on section 5 of the Indian Reorganization Act and on rules at 25 CFR part 151. The Bureau published a final rule revising those rules in the Federal Register on December 12, 2023, effective January 11, 2024.

The Bureau's summary says it had developed extensive experience since the regulations were first promulgated in 1980. Relying on that experience and input from multiple stakeholders, the rule "makes the fee-to-trust process more efficient, simpler, and less expensive to support restoration of Tribal homelands."

The rule itself gives the reason. It says the average time to receive a final fee-to-trust decision was then about 985 days. It also created a new category, the "initial Indian acquisition," meant to support tribes that do not currently have land held in trust. And it kept the IRA's central limit: the rule explains how the Bureau judges whether a tribe was under federal jurisdiction in 1934, using conclusive, presumptive and probative evidence.

Rules like this one can be revised by later administrations.
