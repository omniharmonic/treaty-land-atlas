In 1956, Congress passed an act "Relating to the Lumbee Indians of North Carolina." It gave the people a name but withheld federal status. It said nothing in the Act "shall make such Indians eligible for any services performed by the United States for Indians because of their status as Indians." For nearly seventy years, that sentence left the Lumbees in limbo, recognized by the state but not on the federal list.

The change came in the National Defense Authorization Act, Public Law 119-60. Its section 8803, the Lumbee Fairness Act, amended the 1956 law. The new text says: "Federal recognition is extended to the Lumbee Tribe of North Carolina." Federal laws of general application to Indians and federally recognized tribes, including the Indian Reorganization Act, now apply to the Tribe and its members.

The Act also deals with land. It authorizes the Secretary of the Interior to take land into trust for the Tribe if the land lies within Robeson, Cumberland, Hoke or Scotland Counties in North Carolina.

Spectrum News reported that President Trump signed the measure on a Thursday, December 18, 2025, and that the Tribe had worked toward recognition for more than a century. It reported that the state of North Carolina has recognized the Lumbees since 1885, and that the Tribe has about 60,000 members.
