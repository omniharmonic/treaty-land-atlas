The 2013 Violence Against Women Act let participating tribes prosecute non-Indians for a narrow set of domestic-violence crimes. The 2022 reauthorization, Public Law 117-103, signed March 15, 2022, broadened that power. Section 804 renamed the jurisdiction "special Tribal criminal jurisdiction" and changed the heading of section 204 of the Indian Civil Rights Act from "crimes of domestic violence" to "covered crimes."

The codified definition lists nine covered crimes: assault of tribal justice personnel, child violence, dating violence, domestic violence, obstruction of justice, sexual violence, sex trafficking, stalking, and violation of a protection order. The Act defines "assault of Tribal justice personnel" as force or threatened force against an individual acting for or serving the tribe in preventing, investigating, prosecuting or adjudicating a covered crime, or in detaining or supervising people charged or convicted.

The law also pays for it. It authorizes $25,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2023 through 2027, for reimbursements to tribes and for training, technical assistance, data collection and evaluation of the criminal justice systems of participating tribes. Of that sum, not more than 40 percent may go to reimbursements.

The Act also made Alaska tribes designated under a separate pilot (subtitle B of title VIII) eligible for reimbursement of their costs. The sources here do not say how many tribes have used the new authority.
