At Fort Stanwix on October 22, 1784, United States commissioners gave "peace to the Senecas, Mohawks, Onondagas and Cayugas" on conditions. Article II secured the Oneida and Tuscarora in the lands where they were settled. Article III drew a boundary line and said the Six Nations "shall and do yield to the United States, all claims to the country west of the said boundary." East of the line, they were to be secured in peaceful possession of their lands.

The dispute is over who had the right to agree. Wikipedia reports that Joseph Brant told the commissioners the delegates were "not authorized, to stipulate any particular cession of lands," and that he left early. It says Cornplanter and Aaron Hill signed. It also says the Six Nations council at Buffalo Creek refused to ratify, denying its delegates had power to give away such large tracts. Only that one tertiary source supports these points here. On January 9, 1789, the Treaty of Fort Harmar renewed and confirmed the Stanwix engagements and repeated the western boundary. It was made with Arthur St. Clair for the United States.

The treaty database lists the Cayuga, Oneida, Onondaga, Seneca, Mohawk and Tuscarora nations among successors. The sources read here show no court remedy for the 1784 cession. Later New York land claims are a separate story.
