The Treaty of New Echota was signed on December 29, 1835 (7 Stat. 478, proclaimed May 23, 1836). In Article 1 the Cherokee Nation "cede relinquish and convey to the United States all the lands owned claimed or possessed by them east of the Mississippi river" for five million dollars. The treaty also promised land in the west. Article 7 said the Cherokee "shall be entitled to a delegate in the House of Representatives of the United States whenever Congress shall make provision for the same."

The treaty text speaks of "the chiefs and head men and people of the Cherokee nation in general council." The North Carolina State Library's NCpedia describes it differently. It says "a small, unauthorized group of about 100 Cherokee leaders (known as the Treaty Party)" signed it. Principal Chief John Ross, it says, collected more than 15,000 signatures asking the Senate not to ratify. The Senate approved the treaty by one vote. The treaty gave two years to leave. In May 1838, President Van Buren sent troops under Gen. Winfield Scott to round up Cherokees and hold them in camps and stockades.

Between October 1838 and March 1839 the Cherokee moved west. NCpedia says an estimated 4,000 of about 15,000 died. The sources read here do not say whether Congress has ever provided the Article 7 delegate seat.
