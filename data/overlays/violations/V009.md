The Treaty of Payne's Landing (May 9, 1832; 7 Stat. 368; proclaimed April 12, 1834) had the Seminole agree to give up their claim to lands in Florida and move west to the Creek country. The preamble made the deal depend on a trip. A delegation of chiefs would examine the western land, and the agreement would bind only "should they be satisfied" with it. Article 7 said the Seminole "will remove within three (3) years after the ratification of this agreement."

On March 28, 1833, at Fort Gibson, the delegation signed a statement. The treaty records that they "have expressed themselves satisfied" with the land in a letter of March 1833. The United States treated that letter as meeting the condition.

The National Park Service says that "Many Seminole chiefs refused to sign the treaty, leading to the outbreak of the Second Seminole War." The NPS puts that war at 1835 to 1842. The Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History says the Seminole removal resulted from wars "and several questionable treaties," and that about 3,612 Seminole were in Indian Territory by 1842.

This is context rather than a court finding. The sources here describe the dispute over how the treaties were made, but no ruling on whether the 1832 and 1833 documents were valid.
