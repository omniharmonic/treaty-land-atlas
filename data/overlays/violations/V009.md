The Treaty of Payne's Landing (May 9, 1832; 7 Stat. 368) had the Seminole "relinquish to the United States, all claim to the lands they at present occupy in the Territory of Florida" and agree to move to the Creek country west of the Mississippi. The preamble made this depend on a trip: chiefs would examine the western land, and the agreement would bind only if they were satisfied with it. Article 7 set removal "within three (3) years after the ratification." The Senate proclaimed the treaty in April 1834.

On March 28, 1833, at Fort Gibson, the delegation signed a statement accepting the western land (7 Stat. 423), which the United States treated as satisfying the condition. Wikipedia reports that most of those chiefs later denied signing it, said they had been forced, or said they had no power to decide for all Seminole. It also quotes Army Maj. Ethan Allen Hitchcock, who wrote that Payne's Landing "was a fraud upon them." That is a single tertiary source here.

The Second Seminole War followed. These sources do not give the war's dates or casualty figures, so none are stated. The Seminole Tribe of Florida and the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma are the listed successor nations.
