A 1832 treaty on the Tippecanoe River reserved twenty-two sections of land in Indiana for Potawatomi bands. On August 5, 1836, near the Yellow River, Indiana Indian commissioner Abel C. Pepper and three Potawatomi chiefs and headmen signed a treaty. In Article 1 they "cede to the United States twenty two sections of land reserved for them" in 1832. Article 3 said the bands would "remove to the country west of the Mississippi river" within two years. The United States agreed to pay $14,080 in specie and settle just debts out of that sum.

Wikipedia reports that Chief Menominee refused to sign this treaty and refused to leave. It says Indiana Governor David Wallace authorized Gen. John Tipton to raise a militia, and that on August 30, 1838, the militia surrounded people at Twin Lakes. The Citizen Potawatomi Nation's news page says that from Sept. 4 to Nov. 4, 1838, "the United States forcibly removed a band of 859 Potawatomi" from northern Indiana to present-day Kansas. It says this was to make way for squatters who wanted Potawatomi lands. It gives the trip as 660 miles and says 41 died.

The sources read here show no court case or compensation claim. Today the Potawatomi nations descended from these bands hold the memory. The Potawatomi Trail of Death Association holds a remembrance every five years, according to the Citizen Potawatomi Nation.
