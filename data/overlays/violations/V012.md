The Treaty of Buffalo Creek (Jan. 15, 1838; 7 Stat. 550) was made between commissioner Ransom H. Gillet and "the chiefs, head men and warriors of the several tribes of New York Indians." The tribes gave up their Green Bay lands and the United States set aside land in Indian Territory as a "future home" for the Senecas, Onondagas, Cayugas, Tuscaroras, Oneidas, St. Regis, Stockbridges, Munsees and Brothertowns. The treaty also recites that Thomas L. Ogden and Joseph Fellows, assignees of Massachusetts, had bought Seneca claims to certain lands, with the price given as two hundred and two thousand dollars.

The Seneca sale covered four New York reservations, the 1842 treaty says: Buffalo Creek, Cattaraugus, Allegany and Tonawanda. That treaty records "divers questions and differences" over the sale. It let the Seneca Nation keep the Cattaraugus and Allegany reservations, and granted Buffalo Creek and Tonawanda to Ogden and Fellows. The 1857 Tonawanda Band treaty says that "for divers reasons and differences" the earlier treaties "remain unexecuted" for Tonawanda. In it the band gave up its claims to western land and removal, the United States agreed to pay $256,000, and the band was allowed to buy back its reservation from Ogden and Fellows.

This is context about a sale that the government itself later said was never carried out at Tonawanda, not a court ruling. No source here describes how the 1838 treaty was negotiated. The database lists the Tonawanda Band of Seneca, Seneca Nation of Indians and Seneca-Cayuga Nation as successors.
