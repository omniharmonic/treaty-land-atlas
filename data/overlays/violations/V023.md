The Cheyenne and Arapaho Indian Reservation in Indian Territory (now Oklahoma) was created by an executive order dated August 10, 1869. This was a reservation set up by the President, not a new treaty, so the "promise" here is that set-aside of land.

About fourteen years later, President Chester Arthur approved a request from his Secretary of War to carve a military reservation for Fort Reno out of it. A history page on Fort Reno says this became "a 9,493-acre permanent military reservation on land that had been part of the Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes' reservation." The Secretary's letter said the Interior Department reported no objection from the Indian Office. The Army used the land, including as a horse and mule remount station through 1948. Today it houses a U.S. Department of Agriculture research center.

The tribes contend the land was not part of earlier payments, because it was set aside as a military installation, and should return to them now that the military has left. Rep. Frank Lucas, whose district includes the site, says the tribes were paid in the 1890s and in 1964. Reporting says a 1999 Interior opinion found the tribes had a credible equitable claim; a 2009 appeals ruling upheld a decision that the statute of limitations had expired. In March 2026 the House Agriculture Committee advanced a farm bill draft, 34-17, that would remove the end date on a bar to USDA transferring the land.
