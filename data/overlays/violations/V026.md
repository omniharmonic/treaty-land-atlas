In the Treaties of Traverse des Sioux and Mendota (1851), the Sisseton, Wahpeton, Wahpekute and Mdewakanton bands of Dakota transferred millions of acres to the United States in exchange for reservations and annual annuity payments. The Minnesota Historical Society (MNHS) says the 1858 Treaty of Washington then confined the Dakota to a smaller area than first promised.

MNHS writes that the US government "failed to fulfill its treaty obligations" and that in summer 1862 the Dakota on the reservation were starving as annuity payments were late and traders refused credit. War broke out in August 1862. A military commission sentenced 303 Dakota men to death; Lincoln pardoned all but thirty-eight, who were hanged at Mankato on December 26, 1862, the largest mass hanging in American history. About 1,600 non-combatants were held at Fort Snelling that winter. Then Congress passed the Act of February 16, 1863, which declared the Dakota treaties "abrogated, and annulled" as to any future US obligation and forfeited the Dakota's lands in Minnesota and their annuities to the United States. Its preamble blames the bands for the war, and it applied to whole bands rather than to individuals found guilty.

According to MNHS, the Dakota were removed in spring 1863 to a reservation in Dakota Territory and later Nebraska, and the government allowed a few who had supported peace to stay.
