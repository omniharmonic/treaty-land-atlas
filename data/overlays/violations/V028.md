According to Cultural Survival, the 1851 Fort Laramie Treaty designated 12,500,000 acres between the Yellowstone and Missouri rivers for the Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara, and later government actions reduced this to 640,000 acres by 1910. That smaller Fort Berthold Reservation held the tribes' farms and towns along the Missouri River bottomlands.

The Flood Control Act of 1944 (Public Law 78-534) authorized the Garrison Dam on the Missouri River as part of a basin-wide plan. A 1945 tribal resolution called the land the tribes' "inherent property from time immemorial." The Center of the West writes that at every hearing the Indian people "simply refused to sell their land," and the government told them to "either sell your land or it will be condemned." Public Law 437 (1949), known as the Taking Act, provided for acquiring about 155,000 acres of Fort Berthold land. In 1948 Tribal Chairman George Gillette said, as quoted by Cultural Survival, that the tribes' "Treaty of Fort Laramie" and constitution were being "torn to shreds" by the contract. The gates closed in 1953 and some 349 families had to relocate.

A Senate report says a federal advisory committee later concluded the 1950s compensation "was inadequate." In 1992 Congress created a $149.2 million trust fund for the Three Affiliated Tribes and a $90.6 million fund for Standing Rock. The Center of the West puts the tribes' land loss at approximately 95 percent, a figure I could not confirm from a second source.
