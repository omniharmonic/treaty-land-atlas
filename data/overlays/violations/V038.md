The 1855 treaty set aside a large reservation for the Nez Perce. In 1863, at Lapwai, the United States negotiated a new treaty. The treaty text says the Nez Perce relinquished "the lands heretofore reserved for the use and occupation of the said tribe," except for a new, smaller reservation described in Article II. The ceded land was to be surveyed and appraised, and the United States agreed to pay $262,500, mainly for moving costs, farm equipment, schools and buildings.

The National Park Service says Lawyer and other pro-treaty headmen signed on June 9, 1863. Chief Joseph, Chief White Bird and other "non-treaty" headmen did not sign and did not consider themselves bound, but "the US government assumed they had reached an agreement with all Nez Perce." The treaty was ratified in 1867. The Park Service states that the 1863 treaty "planted the seeds of conflict that would eventually grow to the 1877 Nez Perce War."

The 1877 war and the later exile of Chief Joseph's people are well documented, but this entry's sources did not give the war's details, the reservation's acreage, or the present status of the land. Readers should consult the Nez Perce Tribe's own history pages for those.
