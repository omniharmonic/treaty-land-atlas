The 1868 treaty with the Ute reserved most of western Colorado for their "absolute and undisturbed use and occupation." As Colorado Newsline quotes it, the treaty said no outsiders "shall ever be permitted to pass over, settle upon, or reside in the territory described."

That promise lasted about four years. Prospectors trespassed in the San Juan Mountains, and under pressure from Colorado's leaders the Ute were pushed to cede about 3.7 million acres in the Brunot Agreement of 1873, according to Colorado Newsline. After the 1879 White River conflict, the government obtained an 1880 agreement that directed the Uncompahgre and White River Utes to leave Colorado for the Uintah and Ouray reservations in Utah. In September 1881 troops under Gen. Ranald Mackenzie and Gen. Wesley Merritt moved to enforce it. The Aspen Journalism account says the government paid the Utes $75,000 in total for their surrendered lands.

Land opened in Utah later shrank too. In Hagen v. Utah (1994), the Supreme Court held 7-2 that Congress diminished the Uintah Reservation, so the town of Myton is not Indian country.
