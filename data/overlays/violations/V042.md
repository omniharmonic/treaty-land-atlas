In 1872 an executive order created a Chiricahua Reservation in southeastern Arizona after Gen. Oliver Howard negotiated peace with Cochise. It rested on an informal agreement, not a ratified treaty.

The Fort Sill Apache Tribe's history, reported by Deep Roots magazine, states that the Chiricahua were forcibly removed in 1876 to San Carlos in eastern Arizona. Geronimo surrendered in September 1886, and the government then sent Geronimo and about 450 Apache men, women and children to prisons in Florida. HistoryNet adds that Army scouts who had served the United States were also arrested and sent to Florida. The prisoners were later held in Alabama and in 1894 moved to Fort Sill, Oklahoma. HistoryNet says their numbers fell from 506 to 257 over nearly three decades, and that children born in captivity were also counted as prisoners.

Both accounts say they were released after an Act of Congress. HistoryNet dates the move to 1913, while Deep Roots says the tribe was released from prisoner-of-war status on March 7, 1914. About 190 went to the Mescalero reservation in New Mexico and 67 stayed in Oklahoma. In 2011 the Interior Department granted the Fort Sill Apache Tribe a reservation in southern New Mexico.
