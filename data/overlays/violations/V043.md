The United States owed the Seminole Nation payments under its treaties, including the 1866 treaty. In Seminole Nation v. United States (1942) the Nation sued over five items of treaty funds, alleging the United States paid money to tribal officers who defrauded members, with federal officials knowing it. The opinion quotes a commission report on the governments in Indian Territory, which called them "wholly corrupt, irresponsible, and unworthy to be longer trusted" with the Indians' money.

The United States argued that paying the tribe's council discharged its duty. The Court disagreed in part. It held that the government is "something more than a mere contracting party" and that its conduct "should therefore be judged by the most exacting fiduciary standards." Payments made to a council do not end the obligation if officials knew the council was misusing the money.

The Court affirmed the denial of recovery on three items, finding for example that the 1866 treaty released the diverted funds in item one. For items two and five, which involved per capita trust payments and post-Curtis Act payments to the tribal treasurer, it sent the case back to the Court of Claims for findings on corruption and official knowledge.
