The Five Tribes (Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Muscogee (Creek) and Seminole) had treaties that exempted them from the 1887 Dawes Act and kept their governments and communal land. Their treaty texts were not opened for this entry.

Congress passed the Curtis Act in 1898 because the Dawes Commission had made little progress in two years of negotiating. According to the Oklahoma Historical Society, the law let the Commission take over functions of tribal government such as law enforcement and deciding citizenship. It also "called for the dissolution of tribal government" and "allowed allotment to proceed even though the elected tribal governments refused to consent." Individuals could be enrolled as members without tribal consent.

A House report states that most Five Tribes lands were allotted between 1897 and 1902. The society adds that the act effectively ended the remaining tribal courts, governments and land claims in Indian Territory, which helped Oklahoma become a state in 1907. The Society's page says the tribes lost control of about 90 million acres of communal land, but that number was not found in the opened page, so treat it as unconfirmed. In McGirt v. Oklahoma (2020) the Court later held that Congress never disestablished the Creek Reservation.
