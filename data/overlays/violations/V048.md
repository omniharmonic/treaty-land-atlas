The question in this case was who may prosecute non-Indians who commit crimes against Native victims in Indian country. Before the ruling, Oklahoma argued it could, and the federal government and tribes had long treated that role as federal.

In Oklahoma v. Castro-Huerta, the Supreme Court held that the State may prosecute non-Indians who commit crimes against Indians in Indian country, alongside federal prosecutors. This was a court decision interpreting federal statutes, not a new act of Congress. The majority concluded that federal law allows state prosecution here.

The Court decided the case 5-4 on June 29, 2022. Justice Kavanaugh wrote for the majority. Justice Gorsuch dissented, joined by three others, and began: "Where this Court once stood firm, today it wilts." Whether the ruling conflicts with particular treaties is argued by the dissent and by tribes. This entry does not cite a court holding that it does. The dissent invited Congress to respond. This case followed McGirt (V047), which had confirmed the Creek reservation still exists for the Major Crimes Act.
