This is a context entry, not an adjudicated treaty violation. The 1852 Treaty with the Apache, signed at Santa Fe, promised "perpetual peace and amity" between the Apache and the United States. In Article 9 the United States agreed to "designate, settle, and adjust their territorial boundaries." Apache Stronghold, a group that has fought the transfer, argues that treaty and religious freedom protect Oak Flat. Those arguments are not independently checked.

In 2014 Sen. John McCain added a provision to a defense bill that let the federal government swap 2,422 acres of the Tonto National Forest to Resolution Copper. KJZZ reports that the Supreme Court rejected an appeal from Apache Stronghold, a nonprofit group that tried to block the transfer. Eos adds that Apache Stronghold had argued the mine would violate the First Amendment rights of Indigenous people who consider Oak Flat a religious site. KJZZ says Oak Flat is a site the San Carlos Apache Tribe considers sacred, and that a federal appeals court denied tribes' and activists' request for an injunction three days before the transfer.

On March 16, 2026, KJZZ reported that more than 2,400 acres had been transferred to Resolution Copper. The Sierra Club and other opponents say the legal fight is not over. Because litigation is ongoing and a later development could change this picture, readers should check current news.
