This is a context entry, not an adjudicated treaty violation. The 1852 Treaty with the Apache, signed at Santa Fe, promised "perpetual peace and amity" between the Apache and the United States. In Article 9 the United States agreed to "designate, settle, and adjust their territorial boundaries." Apache Stronghold, a group that has fought the transfer, argues that treaty and religious freedom protect Oak Flat. This entry does not verify those arguments.

In 2014 Sen. John McCain added a provision to a defense bill that let the federal government swap 2,422 acres of the Tonto National Forest to Resolution Copper. KJZZ reports that the Supreme Court rejected an appeal from Apache Stronghold in 2025. Three days before the transfer, a federal appeals court denied tribes' and activists' request for an injunction.

The U.S. Forest Service transferred the land on March 16, 2026. KJZZ puts it at more than 2,400 acres. Opponents have continued to ask the courts to reconsider. Because litigation is ongoing and a later development could change this picture, readers should check current news.
