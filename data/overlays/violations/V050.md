Taos Pueblo has long depended on the mountain watershed around Blue Lake. A National Archives account says that in 1906 President Theodore Roosevelt made a national forest withdrawal permanent, so the land, "and thus Blue Lake, were now a national forest." Nixon later put the amount taken at about 48,000 acres. The Pueblo's petitions to get the land back were rejected in 1912 and 1918.

Because the Pueblo Lands Board could only pay for taken land, the Pueblo and the government argued over money versus return. In 1965 the Indian Claims Commission ruled the Pueblo entitled to nearly $300,000, but the Pueblo proposed giving up that award in exchange for about 50,000 acres around Blue Lake. The government was reluctant to set that precedent.

Congress acted in 1970. The Senate passed H.R. 471 on December 2, 1970, by a vote of 70 to 12, and President Nixon signed it on December 15, 1970 (Public Law 91-550). The law declares the lands "held by the United States in trust for the Pueblo de Taos" and recites that the Pueblo depends on them for water, forage, wood and timber, and as the scene of certain religious ceremonials. Nixon said "in 1906 an injustice was done," and that Congress "returns that land to whom it belongs."
