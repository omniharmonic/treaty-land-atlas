Washington Territory Governor Isaac Stevens negotiated the Treaty of Medicine Creek with several Puget Sound peoples, including the Nisqually and Puyallup. The tribes ceded land by boundary description. They received reserved tracts, such as "a square tract containing two sections, or twelve hundred and eighty acres," and $32,500. Article 3 secured "The right of taking fish, at all usual and accustomed grounds and stations."

The reservations were widely seen as inadequate. HistoryLink says Nisqually leader Leschi was "Outraged by the inadequate reservation imposed by the treaty." HistoryLink says historical evidence indicates not all those named in the treaty actually signed, largely because they objected to the inadequate reservations. A war followed. The government later set up "New and significantly larger Puyallup and Nisqually reservations" in 1857.

Leschi was convicted of murder after two trials and "hanged at Steilacoom" on February 19, 1858. In 2004 "a specially formed historical court exonerated Chief Leschi of the crime for which he was executed."
