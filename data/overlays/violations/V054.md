In 1851 and 1852 federal commissioners negotiated 18 treaties with California Indian groups. The Mechoopda Tribe's history says the treaties reserved "more than eight million acres of land." Tribes agreed to give up claims to land outside the reservations in return for the promised land and goods.

The Mechoopda history says the California State Senate and the Governor objected to reserving lands for Indians that "might be of either agricultural or gold bearing value," and that the U.S. Senate "secretly rejected the treaties on July 8, 1852." The Bunker Hill exhibit also dates the closed-door vote to July 8, 1852. The treaties were then placed under a seal of secrecy. "Not until 1905, over fifty years later, would the injunction of secrecy be removed." Tribes had been told they would have reservations, but the treaties were never ratified.

The Bunker Hill exhibit says officials in California kept acting as if the treaties were in force and the tribes were apparently never told their status had changed. It says the reservations that came to exist covered roughly one-hundredth of the state, not the promised one-seventh. The Mechoopda history adds that not until 1928 were California Indians permitted to sue the federal government for compensation.
