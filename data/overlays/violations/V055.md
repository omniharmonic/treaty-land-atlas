The Siletz tribes' own history site traces the story in steps. It lists the Western Oregon treaties of 1853 to 1855, the creation of the Coast (Siletz) Reservation, the end of the wars and removal, an "Executive Order, December 21, 1865," and "Changing Federal Policy & Reservation Reduction, 1875." It then lists the Siletz allotments in 1891 and a "Surplus Lands" "Agreement" in 1892.

A KPTV report says the people's territory "shrunk to what was supposed to be, by U.S. treaty, 1.1 million acres as a permanent home." The reservation had already been cut by then. In 1954, KPTV says, the Western Oregon Termination Act ended the tribes' federal status, and "Along with their land, their federal recognition as a tribe was also taken."

The Oregon History Project describes the 1954 policy this way: "the federal government unilaterally dissolved its relationship with certain groups that had existed since the signing of the treaties in the 1850s." It says more than half of the 109 tribes and bands terminated had ancestral homelands in Oregon.

Restoration was slow. The Oregon History Project says a tribe had to find a champion in Congress and prove it had been a tribe continuously since before white contact. The Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians was restored to tribal status in 1977 and received a 3,730-acre reservation in 1980. That is small beside the 1.1 million acres KPTV mentions.
