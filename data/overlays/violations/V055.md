After the Rogue River Wars, Western Oregon peoples were moved onto the Coast Reservation, created by executive order in 1855. A KPTV report says their territory "shrunk to what was supposed to be, by U.S. treaty, 1.1 million acres as a permanent home." The Siletz Tribes' own history site lists later steps, including an "Executive Order, December 21, 1865," and "Changing Federal Policy & Reservation Reduction, 1875."

The reservation shrank in those years, and allotment came in the 1890s. Then, in 1954, Congress passed the Western Oregon Indian Termination Act. KPTV reports that "Along with their land, their federal recognition as a tribe was also taken." The Oregon History Project describes the 1954 policy as one where "the federal government unilaterally dissolved its relationship with certain groups."

A group of Siletz members lobbied Congress, which restored the Confederated Tribes of Siletz Indians in 1977. The Oregon History Project says the tribe received a 3,730-acre reservation in 1980. That is a small fraction of the original reserve.
