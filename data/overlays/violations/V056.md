In the 1864 Treaty with the Klamath, Modoc and Yahooskin Band of Snake Indians (signed October 14, 1864; ratified July 2, 1866; proclaimed February 17, 1870), the tribes ceded a large territory in south-central Oregon and northern California and agreed to live on a reservation around Klamath Lake. The treaty gave the tribes "the exclusive right of taking fish in the streams and lakes" there, and annual payments over 15 years. A Senate committee report says the treaty required the United States to "pay less than $300,000 for over one million acres of aboriginal land."

In 1954 Congress passed the Klamath Termination Act (Pub. L. 83-587). The Senate report says it led to "the termination of the Federal government's trust relationship with the Klamath Tribes" and set up a process for selling part of the reservation land. 

The Klamath Indian Tribe Restoration Act of 1986 restored the federal relationship, but the Senate report states that it "did not restore the Klamath Tribes' former reservation lands," and that "efforts to regain their tribal land base continue to this day."
