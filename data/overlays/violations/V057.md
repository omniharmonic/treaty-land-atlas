The Catawba held a reservation of 144,000 acres in South Carolina, which the Supreme Court describes as "comprising 144,000 acres and 225 square miles." Under the federal Nonintercourse Act, the Court quotes, a conveyance "shall be of any validity in law or equity" only if made by treaty or convention under the Constitution.

In 1840 South Carolina made the Treaty of Nation Ford with the Catawba. In the Supreme Court's dissent, the agreement is described as one "which the United States never joined or approved." In 1959 Congress passed a law ending federal services to the Tribe.

The Tribe sued. In 1986 the Supreme Court held that the 1959 Act "requires the application of the state statute of limitations to the Tribe's claim" and sent the case back. In 1993 Congress passed a settlement act. The statute refers to $50,000,000 "obligated for payment to the Tribe by Federal, State, local, and private parties" and limits an expanded reservation to 3,000 acres. The Catawba Indian Nation was restored to federal recognition and its land claim was settled rather than decided in court.
