The Passamaquoddy and Penobscot Nations' claim began with a 1794 land treaty with Massachusetts, which then governed Maine. The Christian Science Monitor reports that under the 1790 Indian Nonintercourse Act "all land deals between whites and Indians in the Eastern United States had to be ratified by Congress." The tribes argued that the deals they had made with Massachusetts and Maine were never ratified.

The Monitor says the tribes sued eight years before 1980, and that they could have sought for return of 12.5 million acres and $25 billion in damages, a threat that hung over the state's property owners. The claims covered about two-thirds of Maine.

President Carter signed the Maine Indian Claims Settlement Act in October 1980. The tribes gave up their claims in exchange for a "$27 million development fund and $54.5 million to buy land from willing private land owners," with "300,000 acres returned to their tribes." The National Library of Medicine timeline records "an out-of-court settlement of $81.5 million in reparation for land taken from tribes." Tribal counsel Thomas Tureen said, "The slate is effectively wiped clean." Later debate about the settlement's effect on tribal sovereignty was not covered by these sources.
