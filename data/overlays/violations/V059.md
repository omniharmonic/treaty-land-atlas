The 1825 Treaty with the Kansa, signed at St. Louis, had the Kansa cede "all the lands lying within the State of Missouri" and a large area west of it. The United States agreed to pay "three thousand five hundred dollars per annum, for twenty successive years." A reservation was set aside along the Kansas River.

In 1846 a second treaty had the Kansa cede "two millions of acres of land" for "two hundred and two thousand dollars," and also gave up the balance of the 1825 reservation. Kansapedia says an 1859 treaty "reduced the reserve to 80,000 acres" amid settlers "settling on Kaw territory illegally," and that a federal act of May 1872 provided for removal of the Kaw Nation. The Kaw Nation's history says the government "forced the tribe to cede all lands in Kansas" and the people were moved to "a roughly 100,000 acre site in Indian Territory."

Kansapedia gives that reservation as 100,137 acres in present Kay County, Oklahoma. The Kaw Nation's history says the new land was later "split up and allotted to individual families," and that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers later flooded part of its Oklahoma lands for a reservoir, including the old town of Washunga.
