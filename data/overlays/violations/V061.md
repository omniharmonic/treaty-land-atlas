This is context, not a ruling that a U.S. treaty with a Native nation was broken. The Kingdom of Hawaii's government was overthrown on January 17, 1893. A 1993 Congressional resolution (Pub. L. 103-150) "apologizes to Native Hawaiians on behalf of the people of the United States for the overthrow of the Kingdom of Hawaii on January 17, 1893 with the participation of agents and citizens of the United States."

A treaty of annexation was signed in 1897 but fell short of the two-thirds Senate vote. The National Archives says "only 46 senators were willing to vote for annexation" by February 1898. Supporters turned to a joint resolution, which needs only a simple majority in each chamber. The Newlands Resolution had the Republic of Hawaii give up "all rights of sovereignty of whatsoever kind," and the islands were annexed on July 7, 1898.

The 1993 resolution states that "Nothing in this Joint Resolution is intended to serve as a settlement of any claims against the United States." In Hawaii v. Office of Hawaiian Affairs (2009) a unanimous Supreme Court held that it "did not strip Hawaii of its sovereign authority to alienate the lands." The 1993 resolution itself says that "the indigenous Hawaiian people never directly relin- quished their claims to their inherent sovereignty as a people." This entry does not speak for Native Hawaiians.
