# I5. Content Design and Voice

**Design Agent I, Treaty Land Atlas.** Working title "What Was Promised" (I1). This file is the writing guide: voice, language, content warnings, the structure of the four main page types, and the microcopy for the places where wording carries the most risk (donations, disclaimers, legal status, uncertainty).

**Tags.** `[verified]` = stated in a research file in this repo (as tagged there); `[from-memory]`, `[unverified]` as usual. **Recommendation** = decision made here. Bracketed text such as [Nation] is a slot to fill from the data; nothing in this file is real record data. Sample sentences are for tone and structure, not for publication.

**Editorial reference.** *The Elements of Indigenous Style* by Gregory Younging (Brush Education; second edition 2025) is adopted as the editorial reference, per G3 s.6 `[verified as G3's recommendation; the book itself was not read in this work]`. Where this file and that book differ, the book and the nation's own usage win.

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Who is speaking

The project is a small team, not an institution and not a Native voice. The site says so (About page and positionality statement, G3 s.5.4). Therefore:

- The voice is that of **a careful record-keeper**: plain, specific, sourced, unhurried.
- The project never claims to speak for a nation, never says "we" when it means "Native people", and does not use first-person plural to blur who is acting.
- Where a nation has spoken, the site quotes the nation, labels it, and lets it speak first (I4 s.4.4).
- Attribution is **Benjamin Life (@omniharmonic)**. No other organization is named as author or publisher.

### 1.2 Five attributes

| Attribute | Means | Sounds like | Does not sound like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | Short sentences, common words, terms defined at first use | "Congress passed a law in 1887 that split communal reservation land into individual parcels." | "The allotment regime effected a fundamental restructuring of tenure." |
| **Specific** | Names, dates, instruments, cites; ranges where unsure | "Signed [date], ratified [date], proclaimed [date]." | "Long ago, treaties were made." |
| **Even** | Facts without alarm or triumph; the evidence sets the temperature | "A court found that this was a taking that required payment." | "A shocking betrayal." |
| **Accountable** | Says who acted, says how sure we are, says who has not been asked | "The 1877 Act, passed by Congress, ended the reservation boundary at the location described." "This page has not yet been reviewed by [Nation]." | "Land was lost." "Mistakes were made." |
| **Present** | Nations are here now | "Nation today: [endonym]." | "The tribe once lived here." |

### 1.3 Agency

G4 s.2 principle 7. Native nations are given verbs of agency: negotiated, refused, petitioned, sued, purchased, restored, organized. The United States and its agencies are given precise verbs: ratified, enacted, ordered, withheld, surveyed. **Avoid the passive when it hides the actor.**

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Land was ceded." | "[Nation] signed a treaty that transferred [what] to the United States; U.S. records call this a 'cession'." (Gloss on first use. See s.2.) |
| "The tribe was removed." | "The U.S. government forced [Nation] to leave [place] in [year]." (Only when the evidence supports "forced"; else "moved under the [instrument]".) |
| "Boundaries shrank." | "Congress reduced the reservation boundaries in [year] by the [Act]." |
| "Rights were lost." | "A court ruled in [year] that the right ended / did not end." |

### 1.4 Emotion

The record is hard. The writing does not add to it, and does not look away.

- No adjectives of horror, outrage or sentiment ("devastating", "heartbreaking", "shameful", "tragic"). Describe the act and let the reader respond.
- No guilt appeals, no "you are standing on". Guilt is not a call to action (G4 s.2).
- No heroics for outsiders ("help save", "give back", "restore" as something the reader does).
- No ending on loss. Every page that records harm has, on the same page, what the nation does or did next, or says "We have not yet recorded this" (G4 four strands).
- No sentimental or romantic language about Native peoples ("spiritual", "ancient wisdom", "one with the land"). Describe specific practices only in the nation's own words.

### 1.5 Legal precision without legalese

- Use the narrowest accurate word (B1 label rules; s.2.4).
- Give the plain meaning first and the legal term second, with the citation in a source line, not in the sentence.
- Mark what a court decided, what it did not decide, and the date.
- Never state legal outcomes as present fact unless the panel has a sign-off date. Say "as of [date]".
- "Would have" and "as described" in counterfactual views; never "rightfully" or "should be" (G2 s.7).

## 2. Language guide

### 2.1 Names of nations and peoples

1. **Endonym first.** The nation's own name for itself is the primary label. The name used in the treaty or in Royce appears second: "Historical name in the record: [name]". Example from G3: Ohkay Owingeh, which was named San Juan de los Caballeros in 1598 and officially reverted in 2005 `[verified in G3]`.
2. **Spell it the nation's way**, with diacritics and special letters, and mark Indigenous-language text with `lang` (J1 ADR-013). Never fold, strip or "simplify" a name in display. Slugs are folded; display is not.
3. **When the endonym is not confirmed**, show the best available name with the line "endonym not yet confirmed", and the historical name "as named in the treaty" (J1 ADR-013). Do not fill an endonym from memory.
4. **Do not invent pronunciations or audio.** Audio only with permission (G3).
5. **One nation, one page.** Do not merge nations into regional or cultural groupings ("Plains tribes") except where a record does, and then quote it.

### 2.2 Terms

| Use | Avoid | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Native**, **Native nations**, **Native people** | "Natives" as a noun; "Indians" except in quoted names and legal terms | "Native" is a general descriptor in the U.S. `[verified in G3 s.6]`. Use the term a community uses for itself when known |
| **Indigenous** | Using it as a noun ("Indigenous"); using it where "Native" is the community's usage | Used for international context and in organization names. Adjective only |
| **American Indian**, **Alaska Native**, **Native Hawaiian** | Collapsing them as one legal category | Alaska Native corporations, Alaska villages and Native Hawaiians have distinct legal relationships `[from-memory]`; each gets its own labeled context (F4, G3). Confirm scope with the legal reviewer |
| **nation** (default) | "tribe" as a generic substitute | Use "tribe" where the nation or the law does ("federally recognized tribe"). "Nation" and "tribe" carry different meanings for many people `[verified in G3 s.6]` |
| **federally recognized** (as a legal category, labeled) | "real" vs "unrecognized" | State-recognized and unrecognized nations are not "less real" (G3 s.5.3). The label says legal category |
| **Native nations today**, present tense | "vanished", "extinct", "disappeared", "lost tribes", "the last of" | Present tense for living nations; if past tense is required, add the date or context (G3 s.6.7) |
| **transferred by treaty (called a "cession" in U.S. records)** | "ceded" as a plain fact; "sold" when coercion is alleged | "Cession" is the record's word and is glossed on first use per page (G3 s.6.10). Prefer "transfer", "alteration" or "taking" depending on the evidence flag |
| **took** / **taking** | "stole" / "stolen" | Use "taken" only for recognized title (B1). "Stolen" only in a direct quote, attributed, or in analysis where scholars use "dispossession" with attribution (G4 s.3.3) |
| **treaty** | "agreement" as a euphemism for a treaty | Use "agreement" only where the record does (the TLA-A series) |
| **unratified treaty** | "broken treaty" for a treaty never ratified | Unratified is not broken (B1) |
| **did not carry out its promise**, **failed to honor** | "broke the treaty" as a general claim | "Broke" only for non-performance with an evidence flag (B1) |
| **abrogated** | "cancelled" or "voided" | Only when a statute or court ended the obligation (B1) |
| **diminished** / **disestablished** | "shrunk", "dissolved" | Boundary language; add "for purposes of federal criminal jurisdiction" when citing the cases (B1) |
| **terminated** | "ended" for 1950s and 1960s federal policy acts | Termination only for those acts (B1) |
| **Land Back** | "Land Back movement" as a single entity; any claim about what Land Back organizations want | A diverse set of efforts; NDN Collective frames LANDBACK as a political, organizing and narrative framework `[verified in G2 s.6]`. Attribute positions to the organization's own words |
| **Indian country**, **Indian title**, **Indian Claims Commission** | Removing the quotation marks or the gloss | Legal terms of art; retain in quotes with a short gloss at first use (G3 s.6.6) |
| **settlers**, **colonial governments** | "pioneers", "discoverers", "the West was settled" | Avoid "discovered", "wilderness", "prehistoric" `[from-memory, G3 s.6.9]` |
| **massacre** | "incident", "skirmish", "battle" when the record or nation calls it a massacre | Use the name the sources and the nation use, with a content warning |

### 2.3 Generalizing

- Never "all Native Americans". Use "most", "some" or name the nation (G3 s.6.8).
- No pan-Indian voice. Each nation's page has its own shape; do not reuse boilerplate that implies sameness (G4 s.2, principle 6).
- No positions attributed to a nation unless its own statement is cited. Say what documents say and what the nation has published (G3 s.5.2).

### 2.4 Violation and alteration vocabulary

From B1. The label shown must not be stronger than the evidence flag allows (I1 principle 10).

| Evidence flag (G2) | Label the interface may use |
|---|---|
| E0 Ratified, no recorded procedural contest | "Alteration" |
| E1 Ratified, signatory authority contested | "Alteration (authority contested)" |
| E2 Unratified | "Unratified instrument" |
| E3 Consent requirement not met | "Violation" allowed, with the reason |
| E4 Unilateral act | "Violation" allowed, with the act named |
| E5 Compensation found inadequate or unfair by a court | "Violation" allowed, with the court cited |
| E6 Taking found compensable | "Taking" |
| E7 Tribe-asserted account | "[Nation] states that ..." in the tribal-account lens, never as the project's finding |

Context codes (B1: infrastructure siting, federal land disposal, protective designation rollback, annexation) carry the label **"Context: not an adjudicated breach."**

### 2.5 Numbers and dates

- Dates: ISO in data; display "Month D, YYYY" in English. Three dates for an instrument: signed, ratified, proclaimed. "In effect from [date]" uses proclamation or enactment (G2).
- Acreage: only from a cited source, as a range or as "about", with the source in the line. Never a sum computed from polygons (E3 4.3).
- Money: the amount, year and source; never "worth today" conversions unless labeled as estimates.
- "As of [date]" on every 2025 and 2026 item (C3).

### 2.6 Interface strings

From G4 s.3.3 and tokens:

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| "Instrument signed" | "Land lost" |
| "Altered by [Act of ___]" | "Stolen by" |
| "Returned", "Restored" as first-class labels | Omit |
| "Nation today: [endonym]" in every popup | Omit |
| "Federal record as of [year]" | "Reality in [year]" |
| "Documents recognized through [year]" | "What they should have kept" |
| "Legal status today" | "Verdict", "Ruling on this land" |
| "Tribal review status" | "Approved by" |

### 2.7 Consistent action verbs

One word per action across the whole site (the vocabulary of a product is how people learn it).

| Action | Word | Result message uses the same word |
|---|---|---|
| Show a thing on the map | **Show on map** | "Showing [x] on the map." |
| Open a full page | **Open page** | |
| See details | **Details** (tab and button) | |
| Copy a citation | **Cite** | "Citation copied." |
| Pass along | **Share** | "Link copied." |
| Report a problem or add a statement | **Report an error** (anyone) / **Add a statement** (nation or office) | "Report sent. We will confirm by [channel]." |
| Leave for the organization's own page | **Go to [domain]** | |
| Hide / show a layer | **Show** / **Hide** | |
| Start a tour | **Start this tour** | |
| Skip a sensitive step | **Skip this step** | "Skipped. You can return to it." |
| Return to the present | **Today** | |

Avoid "Submit", "Learn more" without an object, "Click here", "Donate now" (the site takes no donations: "Go to [org]'s donation page").

## 3. Content warnings

### 3.1 Policy `[verified as G3 s.10; levels and wording are the design]`

Warn before pages and steps that describe massacres, forced marches, boarding schools or other violence. Warn in plain language. Provide a skip. No graphic imagery, no photographs of dead or human remains. Tours with sensitive material are opt-in. Pace tours so loss alternates with resistance, survival and return. Offer a pause and Native-led support resources (to be vetted).

### 3.2 Levels

| Level | When | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **None** | Instrument texts, administrative changes | No box |
| **Note** | Events involving death, force or removal, described briefly | One line at the start of the Overview |
| **Warning** | Massacres; boarding schools; forced removal marches; child separation; sexual violence | A labeled box before the narrative, with "Skip details" that collapses to neutral facts |
| **Opt-in tour** | A tour in which most steps are Warning level | Warning page before the tour; steps flagged in the list; "Skip sensitive steps" control |

### 3.3 Sample copy

Note (one line):
> Content note: this page describes a forced removal.

Warning box:
> **Content warning.** This section describes [a massacre / the forced removal of children / a forced march] of [Nation] people. It is described factually and without images. [Skip details] [Continue]

Skip result:
> Details are hidden. What remains: date, instrument, source. [Show details]

Tour opening:
> **Before you start.** This tour covers [x]. Some steps describe violence against Native people. You can skip sensitive steps, pause at any time, or leave. Each sensitive step is marked in the list. [Skip sensitive steps] [Continue with all steps]

Support line (after warning pages):
> If this brings up hard feelings, [link to a Native-led resource] [placeholder: resources to be vetted before launch].

### 3.4 Never

- Never place a warning behind a click that hides the page's purpose.
- Never use alarm colors; use the neutral notice style.
- Never use "trigger".
- Never write a warning that describes the act in more detail than the page does.

## 4. Tribal-review status badge

Appears at the top of every nation page and on every tour, and compactly on treaty, violation and case pages when they name a nation. Statuses from G3 s.3.3 `[verified]`; glyphs from the sprite (`rv-...`); publication rules from I2 s.4.

### 4.1 Badge anatomy

```
(ring glyph)  Tribal review: [status text]   [What this means]
```

Always a button that opens a short explanation. Text, not color, carries the status. The badge has an accessible name: "Tribal review status: Not yet contacted."

### 4.2 Status text and explanation

| Status | Badge text | One-line explanation (appears under the badge) |
|---|---|---|
| Not yet contacted | "Not yet contacted" | "We have not yet asked [Nation] to review this page. It shows public records and map shapes only. It does not speak for the nation." |
| Invited | "Invited, [date]" | "We have invited [Nation] to review this page. Until they respond, it shows public records and map shapes only. No reply is not agreement." |
| Edits requested | "Edits requested, in progress" | "[Nation] has asked for changes. We are making them. Some text on this page may change." |
| Reviewed | "Reviewed by [office], [date]" | "[Office] reviewed this page on [date]. A review means they read it and we made their corrections. It is not an endorsement." |
| Co-authored | "Co-authored with [office]" | "Parts of this page were written by [Nation]. They are marked with the nation's name and shown in a separate frame." |
| Declined | "Declined to participate" | "[Nation] chose not to take part. This page shows the instrument and its sources only." |
| Withdrawn | "Content withdrawn at [Nation]'s request" | "We removed content at [Nation]'s request on [date]." |

### 4.3 Rules

1. "Reviewed" never appears without office and date.
2. "Silence is not consent" appears as a standing sentence on the review explanation page.
3. A nation can ask that no tombstone be shown; then the page carries no mention of the withdrawal.
4. The review explanation links to `/for-tribal-reviewers/` and `/corrections/`.

## 5. Page structures

All pages carry the same top and bottom furniture, in this order: (1) title, (2) type and date line, (3) "Nation today" line where a nation is concerned, (4) tribal-review badge, (5) content, (6) sources, (7) "As of" and "Last reviewed" lines, (8) Report an error / Add a statement, (9) cite and share. Every claim has a source line.

### 5.1 Treaty page (`/treaties/<id>/`)

1. **Title:** the treaty's name as the record gives it; the endonym of the nation in the subtitle.
2. **Today panel:** "Nation today: [endonym]" with the official website, and Legal status today (S-codes) with sign-off date. Present first.
3. **At a glance:** three dates (signed, ratified, proclaimed), parties, place, status (ratified / unratified / agreement / EO), ID.
4. **What it said:** plain summary of what the instrument recognized or reserved; quotation from the text in the source-quote style; link to the primary text.
5. **How we know where:** geometry tier (T1 to T4), lineage line, plain uncertainty text, map thumbnail with the legend line.
6. **What followed:** list of violations and alterations tied to this instrument, each with the B1 term, evidence flag, date; link to the violation page.
7. **Resistance, law and persistence:** cases, petitions, statements.
8. **Returns and continuity:** returns, restorations, programs, present uses (only reviewed or documented).
9. **Tribal account** (if any): framed block.
10. **Support** (scoped to nation or region): F4 panel.
11. **Related:** related treaties, cases, tours.
12. **Sources, as-of, correction links.**

Required: strands 2 and 3 and 4 present or an explicit "We have not yet recorded this" line (G4 principle 2). A page with only strand 2 does not publish.

### 5.2 Violation or alteration page (`/violations/<id>/`)

1. **Title:** plain label ("Reservation boundaries reduced by the [Act], [year]") with the family icon.
2. **Header:** B1 term and definition; evidence flags; confidence (high, medium, low with the reason); date; nation today; review badge.
3. **What happened:** one paragraph, active voice, actor named.
4. **What the instrument required:** the clause, quoted, with the consent requirement if any.
5. **Evidence:** the flag in plain words ("Consent requirement not met: the treaty required [x]; sources record [y]"). Sources listed; the sources the evidence rests on are marked as official, scholarly or tribal.
6. **What courts and Congress did later:** cases, restoration or settlement acts, with dates.
7. **Tribal account:** if reviewed, in the tribal-statement frame, beside the above, with a line when it differs.
8. **What we don't know:** gaps in the record.
9. **Related violations on the same instrument; instrument link.**
10. **Content warning** if applicable, placed before section 3.

Label rules: s.2.4; B1.

### 5.3 Case page (`/cases/<slug>/`)

1. **Title:** the case name as verified, with year. Never invent a case or a citation; unverified cites are tagged and held.
2. **Header:** court, date, citation with pin-cites, doctrine tags (C3), direction chip: "Advanced tribal rights", "Limited tribal rights", "Mixed", with text, not color.
3. **In plain words:** what the case was about, in three sentences.
4. **What the court decided** and **What it did not decide.**
5. **Why it matters here:** nations and instruments affected; links.
6. **Both directions:** earlier cases it relied on or limited; later cases that applied, limited or overruled it in effect (C3 edge types, in words).
7. **As of [date]**, with a "later developments may apply" line, especially for 2021 to 2026 items.
8. **Not legal advice** line.
9. **Sources.**

### 5.4 Nation page (`/nations/<slug>/`)

1. **Title:** the endonym, with the historical name(s) as secondary; legal context label (federally recognized tribe, Alaska Native village or corporation, Native Hawaiian organization, state-recognized, other) with a short gloss.
2. **Tribal-review badge** and one-line explanation (s.4).
3. **Nation today:** the official website, the nation's own statement (framed, attributed), present-day boundary map (Today layer), a short factual line about the current land base from a cited source if available. If the nation has provided nothing: "[Nation]'s own website is the best place for [Nation]'s current story. [Link]".
4. **Legal status today:** the S-codes that apply to this nation's reservations or territories, with sign-off.
5. **Four strands**, with equal headings: (1) Instruments and promises, (2) Alterations and violations, with evidence, (3) Resistance, law and persistence, (4) Returns and continuity.
6. **Map:** the nation's features in the map, with the "Show on map" button.
7. **Tours that include this nation.**
8. **Support**, scoped. For Alaska and Hawaii, distinct legal-context labels (F4).
9. **What this page does not say.** A standing section: "This page shows public records and map shapes. It does not describe [Nation]'s culture, priorities or positions unless [Nation] has said so in its own words." (until a review state allows more).
10. **Sources, last reviewed, corrections.**

**Not-contacted and invited pages** show only items from classes P and D (and names, class N, marked unreviewed). They must not contain project-written text about the nation's culture, meaning or priorities. A placeholder reads: "We have not yet invited [Nation] to add their own account. [Learn how reviews work]".

### 5.5 Tour page

1. Title, one-line promise, time ("about 15 minutes"), review badge.
2. Content warning panel (s.3.3).
3. Steps list with sensitive steps marked.
4. Each step: heading, 2 to 4 short paragraphs, record link, map state, a question for discussion (teacher mode).
5. Last step: "Where this is now" and Support, scoped.
6. "What we don't know" and sources.

## 6. Microcopy

### 6.1 "What this map is not" (the standing disclaimer)

Short line, always visible beside the map:
> Illustrative data. Not a boundary. Not legal advice. [What this map is not]
(In production, drop "Illustrative data" once real data is loaded: "Shapes are approximate. Not a legal boundary. Not legal advice.")

Full dialog (matches the prototype):
> **What this map is not**
> - It is not legal advice, and it is not a statement about any current legal claim.
> - It is not an official boundary. Shapes come from documents and published datasets and are drawn only as precisely as those sources allow.
> - It does not show where anyone lives or owns property. It will never show a parcel or an address.
> - It does not speak for any Native nation. Where a nation has not reviewed a page, the page says so.
> - It is not a proposal about anyone's home.

The statement "The Atlas does not propose evicting private landowners" (G2 s.6) is held back until counsel and advisors have reviewed the wording (G2 and G3 both ask for this).

### 6.2 Uncertainty

- Legend: "Edges are soft where boundaries rest on landmarks."
- Drawer line by tier: T1 "Surveyed or statutory line." T2 "Described by identifiable features." T3 "Landmarks moved or ambiguous; edges are soft." T4 "No usable geometry; shown as an icon only." Plus: "Source geometry is generalized from maps made around 1899 and is not a legal description." `[Royce dating from-memory; verify]`
- Deep zoom: "Historical shapes are generalized from maps at about 1:2,000,000 and cannot show a lot or an address."
- After 1894: "Cession geometry ends in 1894. Later changes are shown as events, not areas." (J1 ADR-006)
- Overlap: "Hatching means more than one nation's documented claim. Darker means more overlapping records, not more land ceded. This map does not adjudicate between nations."

### 6.3 Honored mode

First-use notice:
> **Honored through year.** This view asks one question: what had the United States already promised by this year, before later changes? It is a model built from documents. Later changes appear as outlines with their evidence. It does not say what should happen now. Legal status today is shown beside it.

Caption: "Documents recognized through [year]." Chip: "A model."

Legal status bar: "Legal status today: [S-codes]. Read."

Panel footer:
> Reviewed by [role], signed off [date]. This panel summarizes legal history for education. It is not legal advice and is not a statement about any current legal claim.

Empty: "No status has been assigned to this shape yet. We do not guess."

### 6.4 Donations and Support

All of this extends F4 s.6; where F4's draft policy text exists it is reused as-is, not rewritten, so the policy has one source of truth.

Footer (short; F4 6.1):
> **How we handle donations.** This site does not collect money. We link to organizations that we have checked: we confirm their legal status, look for Indigenous leadership, and open the donation page ourselves. We prefer organizations led by the Native nations and people whose land is involved. Last checked: [date]. Spot a problem? [contact].

Panel header:
> **Support.** This site does not take money. Links go to each organization's own page. [How we choose]

Panel step 1:
> **Start with the nation.** [Nation]'s official website: [link]. Many tribal governments do not run public giving programs. Ask what would help before you give.

Tier labels (words, not color):
- Tier 1: "Listed"
- Tier 2: "Listed with caveats"
- Tier 3: "Information only. No giving link."

Type line (first line of every listing): the recipient type, followed by the sentence for allies: "A gift to an ally is not a gift to a tribe."

Leadership line values: "Verified: [names and nations]." / "Self-described. We could not confirm it." / "Not verified."

Link button: "Go to [domain]" (opens the organization's own page; no tracking parameters).
First-use line: "You are leaving this site. [Organization] handles all gifts. We take no payment, commission or data."

Other ways to help:
> **Other ways to help.** Money is not the only way. Some Indigenous hosts ask local residents to pay a voluntary rent or land tax, for example Real Rent Duwamish in Seattle and the Shuumi Land Tax in the East Bay. Others ask for goods, volunteers or land. Learn what each nation or organization is asking for before you act.

Empty state:
> We have not verified any giving options connected to [Nation]. Start with [Nation]'s own website, or see other ways to help.

Delisted:
> This listing was removed on [date]. [Reason in neutral words, if the maintainers decide to publish one.]

Never: "Donate now", "Give today", "Help save", "Your gift will", "Every dollar returns X acres", countdowns, goals thermometers, images of need.

A note on the full policy page: the long policy uses F4 s.6.2 text. Replace "Treaty Land Atlas" with the final product name when chosen. Counsel review of the donation page language is an F4 operational rule `[F4: have a lawyer licensed in the project owner's state review]`.

### 6.5 Corrections and tribal review

Form intro:
> **Report an error or add a statement.** If something on this page is wrong, missing, or should be said differently, tell us. If you are writing for a Native nation or office, say so, and we will put your request first. You do not need an account.

After sending:
> Report sent. We will confirm by [channel] within [time]. [Nation] requests about a nation's own page are applied first, unless they conflict with the public record; in that case we publish the nation's statement beside the record.

For tribal reviewers page (opening):
> This page is for people who work for or belong to a Native nation. You are not our audience for learning this history. You are the people who can tell us where we are wrong. You can correct a fact, add a statement in your own words, change how your nation's name is written, ask us to withhold something, or ask us to remove a page. You do not have to explain why.

Withdrawal:
> We will remove or withhold content you ask us to remove. By default we leave a short note that content was withdrawn, so readers are not misled by silence. If you prefer no note, tell us.

### 6.6 Place lookup

Intro: "Choose your state and county. We will show which nations' treaty records touch the area. We do not ask for your address."
Result opener: "Records in our data that touch [County], [State]:"
Caveat: "These shapes are approximate. A record touching this county does not mean that a nation has a claim to any particular place, and it is not a legal finding."
Never: "You are on stolen land", "Your home", "violations near you".

Errors say what happened and what to do. They do not apologize and do not blame the reader.

### 6.8 Share cards and exports

Every exported or shared image carries: product name, year and mode, the "Federal record as of" or "A model: documents recognized through" line, the lineage line, the legend line for uncertainty, the Legal status today line (in Honored mode), the URL and "Not legal advice. Not a boundary." (G2, G4). No image is created without these.

Share text:
> [Treaty or nation] on What Was Promised: [one factual sentence]. Record and sources: [URL].

### 6.9 Alt text and live regions

- Alt text for a map image states what is shown, the year, and the uncertainty: "Map of [region], [year], showing [n] recognized territories and [m] reservations. Edges are approximate." It never describes people.
- Live-region announcements: "[year]. [n] features in view. [m] alterations." On selection: "[Title], [type], [year]."

## 7. AI and automation in the writing

G3 s.12 `[verified as G3's recommendation]`:

1. No nation-specific narrative is published on model output alone. A human writes or edits, a human with source access checks, and the review status governs publication.
2. AI assistance in drafting is labeled in the page's "How this page was made" line.
3. No machine translation of Indigenous-language text and no generated Indigenous-language audio.
4. No chatbot. Search is Pagefind over published pages.
5. Citations, case names and URLs are never accepted from a model without a human opening the source.

This document was drafted with AI assistance (Claude). It is a draft for the project owner's review and for the tribal advisory conversations, not a finished style guide.
