## 2. Historic and 2019-2024 returns

### 2.1 Tuluwat (Duluwat) Island, Wiyot Tribe, California
- **What:** Eureka City Council voted unanimously on Oct. 21, 2019 to return about 200 acres (the city still owned roughly 202 acres) on Humboldt Bay's largest island, "without condition"; Mayor Susan Seaman and Wiyot Chair Ted Hernandez signed the deed about 90 minutes after the vote. The council had declared the island surplus on Dec. 4, 2018. `[verified]` (North Coast Journal).
- **Note (V3):** The source article gives both "200 acres" (lede) and "roughly 202 acres" (later passage); 200 is kept as the rounded figure.
- **Earlier steps:** In 2000 the tribe raised over $100,000 to buy 1.5 acres including the Tuluwat village site; in 2004 the city gave another 40 acres (NCJ, citing the tribe's earlier open-market purchase).
- **Mechanism:** C (city gift). Experts told the Journal there was no precedent for a municipality returning hundreds of acres without a sale or lawsuit settlement.
- **Name note:** The tribe's own spelling is Tuluwat; the island is also called Duluwat or Indian Island. Use the tribe's spelling; flag for tribal review.
- **Source:** https://community.northcoastjournal.com/NewsBlog/archives/2019/10/21/duluwat-island-is-returned-to-the-wiyot-tribe-in-historic-ceremony
- **Confidence:** high. (A Wiyot film appears in the KPBS October 2026 documentary "The Land Returns," per the preview.)

### 2.2 National Bison Range, Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, Montana
- **What:** About 18,800 acres (DOI: 18,800.22 acres) restored to the tribes.
- **Dates by stage (they differ across sources):**
  - Dec. 27, 2020: omnibus spending bill signed; the restoration was part of the Montana Water Rights Protection Act, which also settled CSKT water rights (Sen. Daines release). Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021, Pub. L. 116-260 `[verified]` (DOI release).
  - Jan. 15, 2021: Interior Secretary David Bernhardt signed the paperwork for the transfer (Daines release) `[verified]`.
  - DOI "Interior Transfers National Bison Range Lands in Trust for the CSKT": the release is dated June 23, 2021 (confirmed), moving about 18,800.22 acres from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to BIA to be held in trust for the CSKT; it gives no separate signing date; a two-year management transition was set `[verified]`. Sen. Daines' Jan. 15, 2021 release says Interior Secretary Bernhardt signed the transfer papers and calls it an 18,800-acre refuge.
- **Mechanism:** A and B. Tribal voice: Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary Bryan Newland said the Range's creation "reduced the Salish and Kootenai peoples' homeland by thousands of acres."
- **Sources:** https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/interior-transfers-national-bison-range-lands-trust-confederated-salish-and-kootenai ; https://www.daines.senate.gov/2021/01/15/bison-range-officially-transferred-to-cskt
- **Confidence:** high on the fact and acreage; medium on the trust-transfer date.

### 2.3 Pe' Sla, Black Hills, South Dakota (2012)
- **What:** 1,942 acres, "officially returned to the Oceti Sakowin (Great Sioux Nation)" on Nov. 30, 2012 in Rapid City. The purchase price was $9 million; ILTF's affiliate Indian Land Capital Company provided $900,000 in fast financing so the tribes could lock in the initial purchase agreement; the public raised money through LastRealIndians and ILTF `[verified]` (UCLA American Indian Studies Center repost of the ILTF release). The Rosebud Sioux Tribe's lead role is inferred from search headlines (for example "Rosebud signs deal for Pe' Sla") that were not opened `[unverified]`.
- **Status 2025-26:** The Lakota People's Law Project is running a campaign about drilling and mining permits at Pe' Sla (lakotalaw.org) `[verified]` as an advocacy claim; details not independently checked.
- **Source:** https://main.aisc.ucla.edu/?p=89
- **Confidence:** medium.

### 2.4 Adler Ranch (Little Sur River), Esselen Tribe of Monterey County, California
- **What:** 1,199 acres in Big Sur; Western Rivers Conservancy moved to buy the ranch, then the Esselen Tribe of Monterey County purchased it from the Conservancy using the grant; release dated July 27, 2020 (it says the sale was finalized "on Friday", about July 24, 2020; the day is inferred). $4.5 million California Natural Resources Agency grant. Tribal Chairman Tom Little Bear Nason: "It is with great honor that we become stewards of these sacred indigenous lands once again." The Land Trust Alliance blog calls it the tribe's first ancestral homeland regained `[verified]`.
- **Mechanism:** D with C funding. Release names Big Sur Land Trust and Conservancy for the Range of the Condor as supporters, not parties.
- **Sources:** https://www.westernrivers.org/press/pr/in-big-sur-the-first-lands-ever-returned-to-the-esselen-tribe-mean-conservation-and-cultural-resilience ; https://landtrustalliance.org/blog/returning-ancestral-lands
- **Confidence:** high.

### 2.5 Land beside Bear Butte (Mato Paha), Cheyenne River Youth Project (2024)
- **What:** Nearly 40 acres in the Black Hills adjoining Bear Butte State Park, announced April 11, 2024 by the Native-led nonprofit; price not disclosed; executive director Julie Garreau cited distance and cost as barriers to ceremonial access. AP also notes the Lakota refuse the $105 million awarded for the Black Hills because the land was not for sale `[verified]` (AP via valuethemarkets mirror).
- **Mechanism:** E (Indigenous-led nonprofit holds title; not tribal government). Sacred-site caution: Bear Butte is publicly known; do not map the exact parcel.
- **Source:** https://www.valuethemarkets.com/news/native-american-led-nonprofit-says-it-bought-40-acres-in-the-black-hills-of-south-dakota
- **Confidence:** medium.

### 2.6 Penobscot Nation, 735 acres (Oct. 2020)
- **What:** The Elliotsville Foundation and Penobscot Nation announced the return of more than 700 acres (headline: 735) in Piscataquis County, Maine, Oct. 30, 2020, "through a land stewardship agreement." Chief Kirk Francis quoted `[verified]` (Atowi reposting Bangor Daily News).
- **Open question:** Whether fee title or a stewardship instrument transferred is not clear. Flag the mechanism as G with `title_unclear`.
- **Source:** https://www.atowi.org/press/735-acres-returned-to-penobscot-nation
- **Confidence:** medium.

## 5. Blocked, contested, or conceptually different

### 5.1 Fort Reno, Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes (Oklahoma): blocked
About 6,700 acres west of Oklahoma City, held by USDA (Oklahoma and Central Plains Agricultural Research Center; part with DOJ for the El Reno prison). Other figures: about 6,900 acres in a 1949 bill; Gov. Reggie Wassana cited "a discrepancy of about 10,000 acres" `[disputed]`. The tribes say they were never compensated and that the land should have reverted when the Army left in 1948; an Interior opinion called the claim "credible, equitable." The Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 (H.R. 7567) would remove the sunset on the bar against disposal, making it permanent; the House Agriculture Committee approved it 34-17 in early March 2026. The current bar was extended through Sept. 30, 2025 by the American Relief Act of 2025. **Status after March 2026 not checked.** `[verified]` (NonDoc, Mar. 20, 2026). Source: https://nondoc.com/2026/03/20/farm-bill-draft-would-permanently-block-return-of-fort-reno-to-cheyenne-and-arapaho-tribes/

### 5.2 Black Hills (He Sapa), Oceti Sakowin: ongoing claim
*United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians*, 448 U.S. 371 (1980), decided June 30, 1980: the 1877 Act effected a taking; compensation owed, with 5% interest on a $17.1 million principal from 1877 `[verified]` (Cornell LII). The Sioux have refused the award (AP: about $105 million) since the land was not for sale `[verified]` (AP mirror). NDN Collective's LANDBACK Campaign (2020) demands Mount Rushmore's closure and return of Black Hills public lands `[verified]`. Acreage is not stated in the sources read. Do not state a Black Hills acreage on the site until a source is read.

### 5.3 Menominee Legend Lake buybacks (Wisconsin): contested
WPR, June 29, 2026: the Wisconsin Supreme Court ruled 4-3 that the Legend Lake Property Owners Association cannot sue to block the tribe's buybacks; Justice Susan Crawford wrote that "Tribal sovereign immunity is the rule, not the exception." WPR says 40 parcels; a law professor says 40 acres; unit unclear `[verified]` (single source). https://www.wpr.org/news/wisconsin-supreme-courts-ruling-menominee-land-buyback

### 5.4 Land Buy-Back Program for Tribal Nations (2012-2022): concluded; different in kind
Nearly 3 million acres in 15 states restored to tribal trust through purchases of fractionated interests from willing individual owners; about $1.69 billion paid; $135.2 million of the $285 million administrative allocation went to buy 100,000 more interests; $60 million to the Cobell Education Scholarship Fund; more than 50 tribes; appropriations ended November 2022; conclusion announced Dec. 4, 2023 `[verified]` (DOI). This consolidates ownership of existing reservation allotments and does not return ceded land.

### 5.5 Santa Ynez Band of Chumash Indians, Camp 4: unverified
Wikipedia states 1,390 acres were placed into trust but gives conflicting years (2017 vs 2019 in the page itself). `[unverified]`. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ynez_Band_of_Chumash_Indians
