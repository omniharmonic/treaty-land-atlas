# PSL TREC 2024, Kiowa/Comanche (part B) and Kiowa Apache: findings

Benjamin Life (@omniharmonic). Research-assistant draft.
Source: People of the Sacred Land, "The Legal and Political History of Colorado Tribes" (2024), PDF pp. 61-92. Structured rows: `02-kca-b-apache.csv` (PSL-KCA-B-001 to 032).

Chunk note: p.78 is blank and the KCA endnotes stop partway through note 33 (p.77). Endnotes 34-45, which cover most ICC docket citations, the Section 22 citation, and the buffalo and interest figures, are not in this chunk.

## 1. Summary (10 lines)

1. Medicine Lodge 1867 Art. 12 voided any cession not signed by three-fourths of adult males occupying the reservation (p.64).
2. The Jerome Agreement (1892-10-06) claimed 456 of 562 men (81%). PSL says many said they did not sign or were deceived, and A'piatan alleged fraud (pp.66-67).
3. C. Blue Clark, quoted at p.67, says the agreement "did not contain sufficient signatures, even with forgeries."
4. Congress waited 8 years, then passed it as the Act of June 6, 1900 (31 Stat. 677), changing "almost every article". The changes were never put back to the tribes (pp.66, 72).
5. Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock (1903) upheld the cession under plenary power and sidestepped Art. 12 (pp.67-68).
6. ICC Docket 32: claimed $16,268,664. Found 2,033,583 acres worth $4,067,166 ($2/acre, 1900 value), less $2,000,000 already paid, for an award of $2,067,166. A $1.5M depredation offset was rejected. Amended Final Award 1957-03-29 (pp.71-73).
7. ICC Dockets 257 and 259-A: "recognized title" under the 1865 Little Arkansas treaty to about 39,680,000 acres, Royce 510 and 511 (NW Texas, W Kansas, SE Colorado). Award $35,060,000, approved 1974-04-17. No land was returned and no interest was paid (pp.73-74).
8. ICC Act Sec. 22 makes payment a full discharge. PSL says the government could appeal but the tribes were barred (pp.73, 91-92).
9. The Kiowa Apache (Apache Tribe of Oklahoma) are a distinct nation with ties to SE Colorado, the Front Range and the Arkansas Valley. At Medicine Lodge they had no Apache-language interpreter of their own (pp.81, 85).
10. There are 11 TREC recommendations to Colorado, including support for reopening the ICC settlement, water-rights and trespass compensation, public-land use rights, return of abandoned rail lands, and a real-estate fee fund (pp.89-90).

## 2. What the atlas likely gets wrong or flattens

- Royce 510 and 511 shown as plainly "ceded" in 1867. The ICC itself found recognized title, and the claim was closed by money ($35.06M in 1974), not by consent or land return.
- The 1900 KCA cession (the post-Jerome surplus) shown as a negotiated agreement. In fact the signature count is contested, there are alleged forgeries, the text was changed by Congress without re-consent, and the treaty's own Art. 12 consent rule was not met (per Clark).
- The 1867 Medicine Lodge text treated as agreed terms. The author says most articles (6-10, 11's railroad and military clauses, 13, 14, 16) do not appear in the council transcripts.
- The Kiowa Apache merged into "Kiowa" or into Cheyenne-Arapaho. PSL stresses that they are a distinct nation and that this merging risks erasing them.
- ICC outcomes should carry the per-acre valuation ($2.00 per PSL for Docket 32, against up to $11 nearby) and the Sec. 22 "full discharge" bar, so that ICC payment does not read as fair purchase.

## 3. Land promised or reserved (mappable detail given)

- Medicine Lodge Art. 11: hunting right on "any lands south of the Arkansas river, so long as the buffalo may range thereon". No white settlement on the old 1865 Little Arkansas reservation for three years (p.63).
- Medicine Lodge Art. 6: individual selections of up to 320 acres per head of family and 80 acres for single adults over 18. The Jerome Commission cut this to 160 acres (pp.61, 67).
- Little Arkansas 1865 (KC, RIT 343, 14 Stat. 717): about 39,680,000 acres, Royce areas 510 and 511 on Royce map 57; NW Texas, W Kansas, SE Colorado (p.73).
- Little Arkansas 1865 (Cheyenne-Arapaho-Apache): 320-acre grants to leaders of bands killed at Sand Creek, and 160 acres to each widow or orphan, within a new reservation that was never set up (p.83).
- Jerome Agreement: about 2.5 million acres of "surplus" for $2M (Kracht, p.86). The ICC ceded area was 2,033,583 acres (p.72).
- Post-allotment loss: over 65% of individual holdings and about 93% of tribal pasture (p.69).

## 4. Quotes worth keeping

- "The contents of this treaty are all fabrications by the US commissioners and their staff. None of this is in the treaty transcripts." (p.62)
- "The commission supposedly obtained 456 signatures from among the 562 males to more than meet the three-fourths requirement of the 1867 Medicine Lodge Treaty." (p.66)
- "did not contain sufficient signatures, even with forgeries" (Clark, quoted p.67)
- "The changes were never taken back to the Tribes for approval." (p.66)
- "The Indian Claims Commission resolved that offsets for depredations were contrary to the purpose of the proceedings." (p.72)
- "said Apache speeches were translated into Arapaho by an Apache-speaking member of Little Raven's tribe" (Jones 1966, quoted p.85)
- "the treaty marked the unwilling loss, under duress, of their traditional nomadic lifeways" (p.86)

## 5. Open questions and claims to verify

- Signature figures: is 456/562 the commission's own count? How many signatures were disputed or forged? (Clark and the Kansapedia figure point in different directions.)
- The Docket 32 timeline: PSL gives Additional Findings 1955-12-13 and the Amended Final Award 1957-03-29, but Kansapedia says "nearly $2 million in 1960 after 11 years." This needs reconciling.
- Per acre: PSL says $2.00 (p.72) and also "$1.00 an acre" (p.74). The Docket 257 award works out to about $0.88/acre.
- The "largest award ever" claim for $35,060,000 needs checking against other ICC awards.
- Buffalo value: $48B (p.74) vs $46B (p.92). The $12B interest figure has no shown working.
- The Little Arkansas Treaty "never ratified" (p.84): the Cheyenne-Arapaho-Apache treaty is generally recorded as ratified with Senate amendments removing the reservation (14 Stat. 703). Clarify. Page 86 wrongly calls the 1866 $39,050 appropriation a "Medicine Lodge Treaty of 1866" item.
- The Apache Medicine Lodge signing date: PSL says 1867-10-25; it is usually 1867-10-21 (15 Stat. 589).
- p.69 says "southeastern Oklahoma" for the KCA reservation, which was in the southwest.
- Agent "James F. Ranflett" (p.86) is probably James F. Randlett.
- Lone Wolf has no U.S. Reports cite in the source (the standard cite is 187 U.S. 553).
- The recommendation claims (School Fund 4M acres, 200+ USFS sacred sites, the 1924 Act "protectorate clause", Gov. Evans's liability) are uncited.
