## 1. Summary (10 lines)

1. In 1878 the Indian Bureau settled the Northern Arapaho permanently on the Shoshone's 1868 "absolute and undisturbed" reservation. Per Shoshone attorney Tunison (pp.135-136), this was done without Shoshone approval.
2. The source contradicts itself here. Stamm (p.132) says the Arapaho came "only with the consent of the Shoshones." Tunison and Fowler (p.135) say the Shoshone did not approve.
3. The 1896 Hot Springs agreement made the Arapaho "equal partners" in the reservation (p.132). The 1878 placement was turned into co-ownership through a land-cession deal.
4. Shoshone Tribe v. US, 299 U.S. 476 (1937), and US v. Shoshone Tribe, 304 U.S. 111 (1938), found the 1878 placement was a compensable taking. The Shoshone half interest (1,171,770 acres) was valued at $1,581,889.50, timber and minerals included (p.137).
5. The 1939 Act paid about $4.2 million for 61 years of Arapaho presence, with $1M earmarked to buy land back (p.136). The remedy was money, and it also confirmed the Arapaho's right to stay.
6. In the 1904 McLaughlin agreement, 202 of 247 Shoshone men signed but only 80 of 237 Arapaho men. It passed on a pooled majority (p.134).
7. Lone Bear protested in 1905 that the ratified text "not agree with original treaty signed by tribe" (p.134). Congress never promised a price; it only brokered sales. Payment fell far short (projected $2.2M, perhaps $500K received).
8. Wyoming v. EPA, 875 F.3d 505 (10th Cir. 2017), cert. denied 2018, held the 1905 Act diminished the reservation. The Interior Solicitor, EPA and Judge Lucero's dissent disagreed, and about 75% of the opened land is still in trust.
9. Dr. Joseph Robertson's 2023 overlay finds the 1859 Doty map (given to the Senate for the 1863 treaty) and Royce area 520 diverge by 40-50 miles toward Steamboat Springs, Colorado (p.143).
10. TREC recommends putting Vermillion Canyon and Browns Park (NW Colorado) into Eastern Shoshone trust. It also recommends recognizing hunting, water and mineral rights in ceded Colorado land, paying for 1834-1868 trespass, and returning abandoned railroad lands (pp.148, 161-163).

## 2. What the atlas likely gets wrong or flattens

- **Wind River as one static reservation.** The 1868 polygon hides the 1878 administrative transfer of a half interest to another nation. That transfer was never a Shoshone cession, and the Supreme Court later treated it as a taking.
- **"Joint cession" framing of 1896 and 1905.** Both agreements counted consent across the two tribes combined. In 1905 only 34% of Arapaho men signed. In 1896 the interpreter certified that duplicate names on the roll were different people.
- **1905 as a sale.** It was a trust brokerage with no guaranteed payment. Congress altered it after signing (per Lone Bear). Revenue went partly to the Riverton/Midvale project for settlers, built with Indian appropriations (p.141).
- **1905 boundary.** Whether the reservation was diminished is still contested. The Tenth Circuit majority said yes; Interior, EPA and the dissent said no. The map should show both boundaries rather than pick one silently.
- **1897 amendment.** Congress amended the 1896 agreement to grant one square mile at the hot spring directly to Wyoming. The chunk shows no tribal consent to that amendment.
- **Royce 520 in Colorado.** Its boundary may be off by 40-50 miles east of the Green River and north of the Yampa (single-source analysis).
- **Reserved rights on "ceded" land.** Courts have upheld off-reservation hunting rights under the 1868 treaty: Herrera 2019 (Crow analog) and Northwestern Band v. Wooten 2023. TREC claims residual water and mineral rights in Colorado. A plain "ceded" fill erases all of this.

## 3. Land promised or reserved (mappable detail)

- **1868 Wind River reservation:** 3,054,182 acres "definitely described," for "absolute and undisturbed use and occupation," and no outsiders to "ever be permitted to pass over, settle upon, or reside in" it (304 U.S. at 113; p.137). It replaced a 44,672,000-acre 1863 area in CO, UT, ID and WY (the Court's figure).
- **Reductions to overlay:**
  - 1872 Lander purchase, the south part (18 Stat. pt. 3, 291-292).
  - 1878 Arapaho half interest, 1,171,770 of 2,343,540 acres. This is an interest, not a drawn area.
  - 1896 Hot Springs, about 100 sq mi at the NE corner. The 1 sq mi state grant is described as: start 1/4 mile due east of the main spring, then 1/2 mile N, 1 mile W, 1 mile S, 1 mile E, 1/2 mile N back to the start (p.131).
  - 1905 cession, about 1,438,633.66 acres. It covers everything north of the Wind River down to the mouth of the Popo Agie, plus the SE corner from the mouth of the north fork of the Popo Agie to the southern boundary (p.132). A WyGISC map is cited (endnote 105).
- **Vermillion Canyon / Browns Park NWR / Irish Canyon / Vermillion Falls, Moffat County, CO:** proposed for return to trust. The source gives no boundary; the FWS 2022 refuge map is cited.

## 5. Open questions and claims needing verification

- **Consent in 1878.** The source contradicts itself (pp.132 vs 135). Check Stamm 1999 and Fowler 1982 directly. Shoshone tradition reportedly describes a temporary welcome by Washakie.
- **Date of the jurisdictional act.** The source says Congress passed it in 1928; it is commonly dated March 3, 1927.
- **Missing statute cites.** The source gives no Stat. cites for the 1905 Act (likely 33 Stat. 1016) or the 1939 Act (possibly 53 Stat. 1128). Verify both.
- **Big Horn citation errors.** The source prints "49 U.S. 406" (should be 492 U.S. 406, affirmed by an equally divided Court). It also labels 835 P.2d 273 as both Big Horn II and Big Horn III.
- **Overstated holding.** The source says the 10th Circuit held Riverton "belongs to the State of Wyoming." The holding concerns reservation status, not land ownership.
- **Browns Park manager.** The source says the area is BLM-run "as a National Wildlife Refuge." Browns Park NWR is run by FWS; nearby Vermillion Canyon is largely BLM.
- **Recommendation 1 errors.** It says "northeastern Colorado" (the Shoshone land is northwestern) and cites "Article 11" of the 1834 Act (it is a section).
- **Missing results.** The source reports no result for the 2004 Federal Circuit breach-of-trust case (364 F.3d 1339), and no award for ICC Docket 63 in this chunk.
- **Royce numbers.** None are given for the 1896 and 1905 cessions; look them up in the Royce schedule.
- **Robertson map.** It is not reproducible from the text. Obtain it from Mato Ohitika Analytics before changing Royce 520.
- **Author opinion.** The author's view that the 10th Circuit and the Supreme Court made "a biased mistake" (p.139) is opinion, not evidence.
