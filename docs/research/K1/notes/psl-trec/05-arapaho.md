# PSL TREC 2024: Arapaho chapter (PDF pp. 165-202)

Benjamin Life (@omniharmonic). Research-assistant draft.
Source read: People of the Sacred Land, "The Legal and Political History of Colorado Tribes" (2024), chapter "Expulsion of the Arapahos from Colorado, 1851-1864" (pp. 167-198) and "Northern Arapaho Tribe TREC Recommendations" (pp. 199-202). Findings CSV: `05-arapaho.csv` (PSL-ARA-001 to 028).

Scope note: the chapter's narrative stops in 1865. It contains no account of Northern Arapaho removal to Wind River, the 1868 treaties, or ICC proceedings beyond one endnote. Sand Creek is covered in a few sentences. The chapter itself says it relies on published non-Native sources and that Arapaho oral history would be needed to complete it (p. 167).

## 1. Ten-line summary

1. The 1851 Fort Laramie treaty recognized Royce 426 (North Platte to Arkansas, mountains to the Santa Fe crossing / Platte forks) as Cheyenne and Arapaho territory, with rights to other lands expressly preserved (pp. 171-172). PSL-ARA-001.
2. Denver, Auraria, St. Charles, Montana Town (1858) and Boulder City (1859) were founded on that unceded land by squatters invoking an 1844 Townsite Act that did not apply; ~100,000 trespassers came 1858-61 and none was removed under the 1834 Intercourse Act (p. 175). PSL-ARA-008.
3. Kansas Territory declared the land "Arapahoe County" in 1855 although the Kansas-Nebraska Act excluded Indian lands until tribal assent (p. 175). PSL-ARA-007.
4. Fort Wise (Feb. 18, 1861) was marked by four Southern Arapaho band leaders (Little Raven, Storm, Shave-Head, Big Mouth). No Northern Arapaho signed or attended; Left Hand was not contacted; signers said they spoke only for their bands (p. 182). PSL-ARA-010, 011.
5. Fort Wise was tainted by hunger, no competent interpreter, and self-dealing: agent Boone's ranch on usurped land, and 640-acre grants to the interpreters' families in a postscript (pp. 182-183). PSL-ARA-012, 013.
6. Article 11 (struck by the Senate) would have had the tribes sell the Denver townsite at $1.25/acre, which Unrau reads as an admission that Indian title there was not extinguished (p. 181). PSL-ARA-009.
7. In 1862 US Attorney S.E. Browne concluded white settlement north and west of the South Platte was unlawful; Commissioner Dole agreed, then reversed himself under pressure. Unrau: "defiance of federal law ... as a fait accompli" (pp. 184-185). PSL-ARA-015, 016.
8. The Sand Creek reserve (Royce 477) was never furnished; agent Colley stole annuities and leased part of the reserve to whites (pp. 186, 189). PSL-ARA-014, 018.
9. Congress "legalized" Denver lots in May 1864, months before Sand Creek and the 1865 cession, and the Supreme Court (Cofield v. McClelland, 1872) later called the site "public domain" (p. 194). PSL-ARA-022.
10. The Northern Arapaho say they never ceded Colorado land, seek compensation for gold, water, railroad lands and removal, and want ICC Docket "329 D" reopened as biased (pp. 199-201). PSL-ARA-024, 025, 026.

## 2. What the atlas most likely gets wrong or flattens

- **Royce 426 shown as "ceded 1861."** It reads as a consensual cession by "the Arapaho." The record in this chapter supports: signed by four Southern band leaders only; no Northern Arapaho party; Article 6 says non-joiners get no benefits, which PSL and Unrau read as meaning non-signers were not bound; a US Attorney in 1862 found the north/west South Platte area not lawfully open. At minimum flag 426 as "signatory legitimacy contested; Northern Arapaho never signed."
- **Time gap 1851-1861.** The atlas shows nothing between recognition and cession. Denver, Boulder, Golden and the mining districts were built in that gap on treaty land. A 1858-1861 trespass layer is needed.
- **1851 ratification status.** The atlas cites 11 Stat. 749 as if fully ratified. PSL says it was "never legally ratified" (p. 174) and Royce's schedule says "never ratified," while the Senate amended the annuity term (50 to 10 years). Treat as contested, Senate-amended.
- **Denver title.** The May 1864 relief acts and the 1872 Supreme Court decision recast unceded land as "public domain." The map should show that legal fiction as an event, not as a background fact.
- **Fort Wise reserve (Royce 477)** appears as a reserve later ceded in 1865. It was never delivered (no houses, mill, tools), was partly leased to whites, and was the scene of Sand Creek.
- **"Arapaho" as one party.** The chapter repeatedly distinguishes Northern and Southern Arapaho; Fort Wise itself names only "Arapahoe and Cheyenne of the Upper Arkansas."

## 3. Land promised or reserved, with mappable boundaries

**1851 Fort Laramie, Cheyenne and Arapaho territory (Royce 426)** (p. 171-172, quoting Kappler II):
"commencing at the Red Bute, or the place where the road leaves the north fork of the Platte River; thence up the north fork of the Platte River to its source; thence along the main range of the Rocky Mountains to the head-waters of the Arkansas River; thence down the Arkansas River to the crossing of the Santa Fe road; thence in a northwesterly direction to the forks of the Platte River, and thence up the Platte River to the place of beginning." Plus: nations "do not hereby abandon or prejudice any rights or claims they may have to other lands" and keep hunting, fishing and passage rights.

**1861 Fort Wise, Sand Creek reserve (Royce 477)** (pp. 178-179, quoting RIT 315):
"Beginning at the mouth of the Sandy Fork of the Arkansas River and extending westwardly along the said river to the mouth of Purgatory River; thence along up the west bank of the Purgatory River to the northern boundary of the Territory of New Mexico; thence west along said boundary to a point where a line drawn due south from a point on the Arkansas River, five miles east of the mouth of the Huerfano River, would intersect said northern boundary of New Mexico; thence due north from that point on said boundary of the Sandy Fork to the place of the beginning." Eastern portion assigned to the Arapaho (p. 179). PSL notes the NM line then ran just south of the Arkansas.

**Fort Wise P.S. grants** (p. 182): 640 acres to Robert Bent at the Sulphur Spring, north side of the Arkansas about five miles below the Pawnee Hills; 640 acres to Jack Smith, seven miles above Bent's Old Fort, north side of the Arkansas.

**Proposed Northern Arapaho reserve, Cache la Poudre** (p. 185): discussed with Little Owl, Medicine Man and Friday, never created. No boundaries given.

**Northern Arapaho claimed water area** (p. 199): "the watershed from the continental divide, from the headwaters of the North Platte River to the confluence with the South Platte River and back to the continental divide." Loose; not mappable precisely without tribal input.

## 4. Quotes worth preserving

- "Whites invaded illegally, Indigenous people resisted, the U.S. government responded years later." (p. 169)
- "To Arapahos and Cheyennes, speeches of the council after a smoke were viewed as the actual 'treaty,' and White Antelope later insisted that the treaty obligated troops to protect, not attack, Natives." (p. 171, Fowler 2015)
- "The Arapaho system of governance did not have a 'head chief.'" (p. 173)
- "Arapahos wanted the settlers to understand that they could take the gold but not the land." (p. 176, Fowler 2015)
- "Only a few tribal leaders with questionable authority made verbal promises to exchange their princely domain for government annuities..." (p. 178, Unrau 1964)
- "the cession clauses of the Fort Wise agreement were of little value as a means of nullifying tribal claims to the upper South Platte basin" (p. 181, Unrau 1964)
- "No Northern Arapahos were present. Important Southern Arapahos, especially Left Hand, were not present." (p. 182)
- Boone made a treaty "over their heads." (p. 183, Fowler 2015)
- "defiance of federal law and recognition of the whole affair as a fait accompli." (p. 185, Unrau 1964)
- Left Hand "insisted that it was not valid without his consent." (p. 186, Roberts 1984)
- "At negotiations over the 1861 Treaty of Fort Wise, the US government threatened us with starvation unless our leaders agreed to stay on a tiny piece of barren land in Southeastern Colorado." (p. 196, Cheyenne and Arapaho descendants, History Colorado exhibit)
- "The Arapaho were never compensated for the gold taken from 1858 to the present on the land that they never signed a treaty and ceded." (p. 201, Northern Arapaho TREC)

## 5. Open questions and claims needing verification

- **1851 ratification.** PSL says never legally ratified; atlas cites 11 Stat. 749. Check whether the Arapaho assented to the Senate amendment and how Kappler and NARA list it.
- **Little Raven as Fort Wise signer.** p. 177 says he left the 1860 meeting; p. 182 lists him as marking the 1861 treaty; p. 184 says he repudiated it. Reconcile with RIT 315 signature list.
- **Northern Arapaho assent.** p. 189 says Evans ordered Northern chiefs to sign assent before receiving food. Did any sign? Any such document would be a food-duress instrument.
- **Denver relief act date.** Text says May 24, 1864; the quoted Cofield opinion says May 28, 1864, plus a general act of May 23, 1864. Verify in 13 Stat.
- **ICC docket.** Source names "329 D". Verify docket number, award amount, valuation date and acreage (ICC Docket 329 and related Cheyenne-Arapaho dockets).
- **Railroad grant size.** p. 195 says "ten square miles on each side of the tracks for each mile"; Pacific Railway Act grants were in alternate sections. Likely imprecise.
- **Black Hawk** as the 1858 discovery site (p. 175) looks loose; the Gregory Gulch strike was 1859.
- **Shoshone "not invited"** at Horse Creek (p. 170) is a single uncited parenthetical.
- **Not covered here:** Sand Creek details, Northern Arapaho removal to Wind River, 1865 Little Arkansas and 1867 Medicine Lodge, 1868 Fort Laramie. These need other PSL chapters or sources.
