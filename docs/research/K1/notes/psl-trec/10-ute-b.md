# PSL TREC 2024, Ute Tribes chapter, second half (PDF pp. 412-466)

Research-assistant draft for Benjamin Life (@omniharmonic), Living Law, Living Land.
Source: People of the Sacred Land, TREC report "The Legal and Political History of Colorado Tribes" (2024). Findings CSV: `10-ute-b.csv` (PSL-UTE-B-001 to 045).

Scope note: the chunk starts mid-way through the 1868-1874 period. So it covers the Brunot Agreement (San Juan Cession, Royce 566) as well as Meeker, the 1880 agreement and removal, allotment, the Hunter Act, the ICC, and the TREC recommendations. There is no separate Ute Mountain Ute section; Towaoc is mentioned only briefly.

## 1. Ten-line summary

1. Royce 566 (San Juan Cession, 1873/74) was taken over by illegal mining from 1870 to 1873. Federal commissioners admitted the trespass breached the 1868 treaty and offered cession as the cure (pp. 412-414).
2. Brunot used Ouray's captive son as leverage for the cession. The son was never delivered. Brunot wrote that "no presents were given", yet Article 6 paid Ouray $1,000 a year (pp. 414-418).
3. The Utes meant to sell only the mining districts. They kept the valleys, Uncompahgre Park and hunting rights. The southern bands, whose land made up most of the cession, said they had been tricked (pp. 416-419).
4. Uncompahgre Park was expressly reserved in 1873. A surveyor's boundary moved it into the cession. Grant restored it in 1876, then the Act of May 14, 1884 took it. The ICC excluded it from Docket 327 because it "was not included in the cession" (pp. 420, 455-456).
5. The three-fourths adult-male consent for the 1880 agreement (Royce 616) was obtained only after commissioner Otto Mears paid $2 per signature. Secretary Kirkwood dropped the charges and reimbursed Mears for the bribes (p. 438, citing Conetah).
6. Ouray signed the March 1880 version but died without signing the ratification (Conetah) (p. 438).
7. The Uncompahgre were promised land at the Grand/Gunnison confluence. Mears falsely reported it unsuitable. In September 1881 about 1,400 people were marched 350 miles to Utah under Mackenzie's troops, given 24 hours to leave (pp. 437-439, 455). In ICC Docket 349 the US argued it "had no obligation" to provide the reservation; the award was $300,000.
8. The Uncompahgre were punished for White River's acts even though 1868 Treaty Art. 17 protects bands that stay at peace (p. 435).
9. Docket 327 valued Royce 566 at $8.5M less $507,662.84, against a Ute appraisal of $40,773,533. The Southern Ute claim for 230,547.44 acres in Royce 617 (taken by the Hunter Act, 1895) was barred as res judicata (402 U.S. 159, 1971) under a 1950 consent judgment. The 1969 ICC finding said that judgment was never meant to cover 617 (pp. 455-458).
10. In Utah, the Uintah opening (1903-05) went ahead with 82 of 282 signatures, well short of three-fourths. Belt (1890) said Uncompahgre consent was unnecessary (pp. 451-452).

## 2. What the atlas likely gets wrong or flattens

- **Royce 566 drawn as a clean consensual cession.** It was preceded by years of tolerated trespass and obtained with leverage (a captive son, a chief's salary). The southern bands rejected it. Ute understanding was a mines-only sale with retained hunting rights. Flag it as DURESS and contested, and overlay retained hunting rights.
- **Uncompahgre Park (10,240 acres) colored as part of 566.** It was reserved in 1873 and restored by executive order in 1876. It was taken by the Act of May 14, 1884, never by cession, and the ICC excluded it from the 566 award. Map it as a separate NEVER_CEDED / statutory taking.
- **Royce 616 (1880) shown as a ratified cession.** Consent was bought. The Uncompahgre were punished collectively in breach of 1868 Art. 17. The promised Colorado reservations (Grand/Gunnison for the Uncompahgre, La Plata for the Southern Utes) were never delivered. The removal itself was done at gunpoint.
- **Royce 617 (Southern Ute strip, 15 x 110 miles) shown as ceded.** It was the Southern Ute reservation remnant, opened by the Hunter Act (Feb 20, 1895). The Southern Utes got nothing for 230,547.44 acres because of a 1950 stipulation. The 1971 Supreme Court read the 1880 Act as ceding all non-allotted land; the ICC had found the stipulation was never meant to reach 617.
- **ICC money read as closure.** Luebben (quoted pp. 454-455): the ICC could never return land and may have foreclosed quiet-title actions.
- **"Meeker Massacre" / "Colorow's War" framing.** PSL documents a fabricated assault claim and troops entering the reservation at Milk Creek. It also documents a state posse / National Guard attack in 1887 that killed eight Utes, including a child and an infant.

## 3. Land promised or reserved (mappable descriptions)

- **1873 Brunot reserved areas** (Brunot report, H.R. Exec. Doc. 1, 43d Cong. 1st Sess. p. 454): "The Uncompagre Park, the Gunnison and San Miguel Rivers, and the agricultural and grazing lands on the south end of the present reservation are reserved to the Indians." The cession itself is "over 60 by 100 miles" of mountain country.
- **Uncompahgre Park**: 10,240 acres (ICC). The 1876 executive order restored "sixteen square miles" (p. 420). Returned to public domain by the Act of May 14, 1884.
- **1880 Uncompahgre promise**: "agricultural lands on Grand River, near the mouth of the Gunnison River, in Colorado, if a sufficient quantity... if not then upon such other unoccupied agricultural lands as may be found in that vicinity and in the Territory of Utah" (p. 455). This is roughly the Grand Junction vicinity; it was never surveyed as a reservation.
- **1880 Southern Ute promise**: "unoccupied agricultural lands on the La Plata River, in Colorado", or in its vicinity in New Mexico (pp. 437-438, 448).
- **Royce 617**: a strip "15 miles wide and 110 miles long" between the southern Brunot line and the New Mexico border (quoting 402 U.S. 159, p. 457).
- **1878 proposed consolidated White River reservation**: about 728,320 acres (Hayt). Never created.
- **1888 proposed Southern Ute Utah reservation**: boundaries "similar to" S. 1916 (Bowen), in San Juan County, Utah. Defeated.

## 4. Quotes worth preserving

- Ouray, Sept 13, 1873: "We are perfectly willing to sell our mountainland... We do not want they should go down into our valleys, however, and kill or scare away our game." (p. 416)
- Ouray, quoted 1873: "They are measuring, and whenever they find a mine, they take a little piece more of our country." (p. 416)
- Commissioner Hayt, 1879: "the Ute Indians have a good and sufficient title to 12,000,000 acres of land in Colorado... these treaties have been made and must be lived up to, whether convenient or otherwise." (p. 436)
- Ouray to the House committee, 1880: "I think that Meeker wanted all the time to get them out of the land—wanted to make some row in order to get them off the land." (p. 436)
- Pitkin to the House committee: the Utes "were occupying very valuable mineral land which was of no use to them" (pp. 435-436)
- Acting Commissioner Belt, 1890: "the Government may not be in duty bound to submit the question for the consent of the Indians" (p. 451)
- Chief Happy Jack (H. Doc. 33, 58th Cong.): "You are just like a storm from the mountains when the flood is coming down the stream, and we can't get help or stop it." (p. 452)
- US in ICC Docket 349: "it had no obligation to furnish such a reservation." (p. 455)
- Colorado HB 178 (1881): a $25 reward "for each Indian or skunk scalp produced" (p. 439)

## 5. Open questions and claims needing verification

- **Docket 349**: the $300,000 award and the 1965 findings are not footnoted. Verify in the ICC decisions.
- **Docket 327 figures** ($40,773,533; $8.5M; $507,662.84 offset): not footnoted. Confirm the net award and the decision volume.
- **Attorney's name**: PSL names "Dr. Charles Wilkinson" as the Ute attorney in the 1950 stipulation. This is probably Ernest L. Wilkinson. Verify.
- **Royce 566 size**: about 4 million acres in the narrative (p. 416) versus 3.7 million acres in the Supreme Court quote (p. 457).
- **Approval date**: PSL says the Senate "ratified" the Brunot Agreement on April 29, 1874. It was approved by an act of Congress (18 Stat. 36).
- **Uncompahgre Park boundary**: called a surveyor's "error" on p. 420 and "deliberately" included on p. 455. The source contradicts itself.
- **Southern Ute removal, 1888**: 274 of 242 required signatures (p. 449) sits beside a newspaper piece saying the commission failed to get a treaty. Clarify.
- **Final 1880 signature count**: not given, nor whether three-fourths was actually reached.
- **Unsourced claims**: the HB 178 outcome (passed or not); the militia "kill all Indians off the reservation" order; Agent Adams's Little Annie sale (one unnamed newspaper); the dendrochronology claim; the Ouray "We are Uncompahgre" quote (PSL itself says there is no citation).
- **Dockets 44 and 45** are named in the recommendations endnotes but not discussed in this chunk. They may be covered in Ute part A.
- **Statute cites**: PSL does not cite the Act of June 15, 1880 or the Act of Feb 20, 1895. My CSV adds 21 Stat. 199 and 28 Stat. 677 from general knowledge; these are labeled as not given by PSL and should be verified.
