# K2. Nations' own accounts (method and coverage)

K1 showed where the federal record (Royce, Kappler, NARA) misleads. K2 asks what Native nations say themselves about their land, the treaties and agreements affecting it, and how that land was lost or kept. It draws only on sources a nation, or its own people, published or authorized.

**The findings themselves are not in this public repository.** Everything K2 gathered about a nation is a draft for that nation's review, marked "DRAFT FOR [NATION] REVIEW - NOT FOR PUBLICATION", and is kept in the owner's private repository. Nothing has been sent to any nation. Nothing from K2 appears on landback.land until the nation concerned has reviewed it (see `docs/governance/K2-nation-review-protocol.md` and `docs/governance/K2-site-display-rules.md`). Silence is never consent.

## Rules every research assistant followed

- Sources in priority order: the nation's official website, constitution and statements; its historic preservation office, cultural center, museum, tribal college and newspaper; public-domain testimony by its citizens (attributed by name and nation, noting the federal interpreter or recorder); oral-history collections made public with permission; scholarship by its citizens (cited and described, never reproduced).
- Quotes are 40 words or fewer, exact and attributed, with a link. No images, maps, songs or ceremonial knowledge. Where a site's terms forbid reuse, only the citation and link were kept.
- Anything marked sacred, restricted or not for public use, and anything locating burials or sacred sites, was skipped. Only the fact that such material exists was noted.
- A nation's position is reported as its own ("The [Nation] states that..."), in its own terms and spellings. It is not adopted as the project's finding.
- Where a nation's account differs from the federal record, both are recorded side by side. Where nations disagree with each other, each nation's own source is named, and the atlas does not rule between them.
- Nothing was inferred. Where a nation has published no position, the drafts say so.
- An author's nation is recorded only as the author states it.

## Limits

- No nation has reviewed anything.
- The shared web-search budget ran out during the pass; later work used direct fetches of nations' official domains. Several official sites were unreachable, blocked automated access, or forbade reuse. Several lookalike or hijacked domains were found and not used (listed in the private drafts).
- Oral history is thin (17 rows) by design: only collections already published with permission were used.

## What K2 changes in the atlas, once nations review it

- A "What the nation says" section on nation, treaty and area pages, shown only after review. Until then the section reads "No nation has reviewed this yet."
- New candidate records (treaties absent from Kappler, land returns, post-1894 events) are held as candidates, not published, until the nation concerned confirms them.
