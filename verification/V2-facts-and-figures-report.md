### The most serious errors found

1. **Hodel v. Irving (D3 section 7.1) is misquoted.** D3 says the 439 owners of a $1,080 tract each received no more than $1 a year. The opinion says one-third receive under $.05 and two-thirds under $1, while the largest holder receives $82.85 a year (481 U.S. at 713). Wrong in the direction that understates the owners' shares.
2. **Medicine Creek payment term (V051 and B2).** "$32,500 over 10 years" should be 20 years. The treaty's own schedule runs 20 years and adds up to $32,250, not the stated $32,500.
3. **Treaty citations in V047 (McGirt) are off by one page in three places.** The 1833, 1856 and 1866 Creek instruments begin at 7 Stat. 417, 11 Stat. 699 and 14 Stat. 785, not 418, 700 and 786. The repo already carries a crosswalk note about the 785/786 split; 785 is right.
4. **Bears Ears (V062, C2, B2, B3, statutes seed).** The primary text is now available: Proclamation 11043 (91 FR 45169), signed July 13, 2026, published July 17, modifies the monument to about 121,096 acres and excludes about 1,238,904 acres. It does not revoke the monument (Native News Online overstates), and Wikipedia's "Proclamation 9558" label is wrong. The seed's "numbers not located" and "sources conflict" can be closed. Grand Staircase is Proclamation 11044 and 181,541 acres, not 181,500.
5. **Bosque Redondo return date (V040).** "Let them return in May 1868" cannot be right when the treaty was signed June 1, 1868. Wikipedia carries the same error. The usual June 18, 1868 date for the return march was not verified here.
6. **Unsupported 2026 item in B1/B3.** The Penobscot "about 11,000 acres, Sept. 17, 2026" return is tagged "[verified in F3]", but F3 contains no such item, and the cited Atowi page is a 2020 return of 735 acres.
7. **Ratification-date labels (T-170 Black Hawk Purchase, T-189 Chicago 1833).** The "senate-consent" dates equal the proclamation dates printed in the Statutes at Large margins. The dates are fine as proclamation dates; the label is not supported.

Everything in the headline D3 numbers checked out: 575 entities (91 FR 4102, Jan. 30, 2026), 56 million surface and 60 million subsurface trust acres, 370 ratified and at least 45 unratified treaties (BIA FAQ), 417 boarding school institutions and 973 deaths (DOI Volume II), Cobell $3.4 billion, and the Wilson Table VII Dawes-era figures.

## 2. Method and limits

- **WebSearch was exhausted**, so every check used WebFetch on direct URLs. Reached and used: govinfo.gov (Statutes at Large page PDFs, Federal Register, Public Laws), federalregister.gov (including its JSON API), archives.gov, bia.gov, doi.gov, Justia, Cornell LII, Library of Congress (catalog record only), UNL Treaties Portal, Berkeley (Heizer 1972), Wikipedia, HistoryLink, Harvard EELP, and several news pages.
- **Blocked or failed:** the Oklahoma State University Kappler site (robots.txt), congress.gov (robots.txt), npr.org, and several doi.gov pages (client errors). Because OSU is blocked, **Kappler page citations were not independently verified**, except the Fort Laramie 1851 Kappler page via Wikipedia.
- **Treaty checks.** The Statutes at Large PDFs on govinfo carry the treaty text and the marginal notes (date signed, "Proclamation", sometimes "Ratified" or "Consent of Senate"). I used those for the date signed, the Stat. page, and the ratification event. Each govinfo PDF can bundle neighboring treaties, so for ambiguous pages I re-queried for the full list of treaties on the file.
- **The NARA-index comparison is circular.** The treaty seed was built from the NARA index, so all 99 NARA-numbered rows in the seed (including the 61 checked here) match it exactly on RIT number, date signed and NAID. I therefore also compared the NARA index with the live NARA catalog-links table for RITs 8, 19A, 143A, 199, 281, 289, 323, 364-366, 369-371 and 374 (all match). The index has 376 rows (374 numbered plus 19A and 143A), consistent with A1's 368 federal-era count once 143A (a document set) is excluded.
- **Priority of sources.** Where Wikipedia was the only source I say so (single-source). Where Wikipedia conflicts with a primary text, the primary text wins and the conflict is reported.

## Confidence & Gaps

Overall confidence: **medium to high** for the 61 treaty rows (primary Statutes at Large pages for almost all), **medium** for the violation entries (many secondary sources remain), **high** for the D3 headline numbers and the Bears Ears primary facts.

- Kappler citations, the Senate Executive Journal, and congress.gov were not reachable.
- Govinfo page PDFs sometimes bundle neighboring treaties and the summarizing tool occasionally misread OCR (for example, "June 9, 1833" for the 1863 Nez Perce margin and "Dec. 2" for Greenville). I treated any OCR-looking oddity as unresolved rather than as a finding, except where two sources agreed.
- Wikipedia conflicts with primary sources in at least two places (Bears Ears proclamation number; Navajo return month). Seed rows that lean on Wikipedia only are flagged single-source.
- The 30-row sample is random but small; the clean result on dates and Stat. pages (no signing date or Stat. page contradicted among the 61 rows; the 11 Stat. 749 page for Fort Laramie 1851 could not be read) is encouraging but does not prove the remaining 74 rows are clean. The full-seed NARA cross-check (internal consistency only) found zero mismatches.
- Statuses count claims, not severity; see section 1 for the serious ones.
